# The California legal system

**Why this file exists.** Most essay subjects default to "general application", but some essays test California rules every time. You also need to know which California authority binds which court to read a California case correctly. This file gives the structure. The doctrine itself lives in each subject's `ca-distinctions.md` (Phase 4).

Read [us-legal-system.md](us-legal-system.md) first for federalism, precedent and the federal courts.

---

## 1. The California Constitution

California's constitution is long, detailed and frequently amended, often directly by voters. The articles you will meet:

| Article | Content | Where it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Art. I** (Declaration of Rights) | Individual rights, which can be broader than federal rights. § 16: jury trial (in a civil cause, three-fourths of the jury may render a verdict). § 28: victims' rights, incl. **Truth-in-Evidence** [S-145]. | Evidence and Criminal Procedure essays (§ 28); Civ Pro jury questions (§ 16) |
| **Art. II** (Voting, Initiative and Referendum, and Recall) | § 8: the **initiative** power (voters enact statutes and constitutional amendments). § 10(c): the Legislature cannot amend an initiative statute without voter approval unless the initiative permits it. | Explains why key evidence and tort rules are hard to change (§5 below) |
| **Art. VI** (Judicial) | § 1: judicial power vested in the Supreme Court, Courts of Appeal and superior courts. § 2: Chief Justice and six associate justices. § 10: superior courts' original jurisdiction. § 11: appellate jurisdiction. § 16: election and retention of judges. [S-146] | Court structure (below) |

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Constitution is not applied directly by courts (no judicial review of legislation). California courts apply the California Constitution as enforceable law every day, and voters can amend it by ballot (approx.).

## 2. Court structure

| Court | Number / composition | Jurisdiction | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supreme Court of California** | **7 justices**: the Chief Justice + 6 associate justices | Mostly **discretionary** review by petition for review. Grounds include securing uniformity of decision and settling an important question of law (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 8.500(b)). **Automatic, direct appeal in death-penalty cases** (Cal. Const. art. VI, § 11(a); Pen. Code § 1239(b)). | [S-141] [S-146] |
| **Courts of Appeal** | **6 appellate districts**. The First, Second and Fourth are subdivided into divisions, **19 divisions** in all. Each district or division has a presiding justice and two or more associate justices; cases are decided by panels. | Appeals from the superior courts (unlimited civil cases, felonies, juvenile and other matters), except death-penalty judgments | [S-140] |
| — your local court | **Fourth District, Division One**, sits in San Diego and generally hears appeals from San Diego and Imperial counties. | — | [S-142] |
| **Superior courts** | **58**, one per county | General trial jurisdiction over all causes not given to other courts (Cal. Const. art. VI, § 10). Civil cases are **limited** (amount in controversy **$35,000 or less**, exclusive of attorney's fees, interest and costs) or **unlimited** (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 85, 88). The superior court's **appellate division** hears appeals in limited civil and misdemeanor cases. | [S-141] [S-147] |

**Federal courts in California** are a separate system: four district courts (N.D., E.D., C.D. and S.D. Cal.; San Diego is in S.D. Cal.) under the Ninth Circuit. See [us-legal-system.md](us-legal-system.md) §2.

**Judges.** Superior court judges are elected by county. Appellate justices are appointed by the Governor, confirmed, and then face yes/no retention elections (Cal. Const. art. VI, § 16).

## 3. The codes that matter on the bar

California organizes its statutes into named **codes** (the Legislature's site lists them all [S-148]). On essays you may cite a code section by number if you know it. The points come from a correct rule applied to the facts, not from the number ([grader-expectations.md](../00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md) §1).

| Code | Abbrev. (exam shorthand) | What it governs on the exam | Subject folder |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Code of Civil Procedure** | CCP | California civil procedure: jurisdiction (§ 410.10, the long-arm statute "on any basis not inconsistent with" the state and federal constitutions); limited vs unlimited cases (§ 85); fact pleading (§ 425.10); demurrer (§ 430.10); sanctions (§ 128.7); anti-SLAPP (§ 425.16); summary judgment (§ 437c); discovery incl. initial disclosures (§ 2016.090 [S-113]). Tested with the FRCP [S-084]. | [civil-procedure](../03-subjects/civil-procedure/README.md) |
| **Evidence Code** | CEC / Evid. Code | California evidence: relevance (§ 210), discretionary exclusion (§ 352), character (§§ 1101, 1103, 1108, 1109), hearsay (§ 1200 and exceptions), privileges. Compare and contrast with the FRE [S-083]. | [evidence](../03-subjects/evidence/README.md) |
| **Civil Code** | Civ. Code | Contracts (e.g., liquidated damages § 1671; statute of frauds § 1624), general duty of care (§ 1714), several liability for non-economic damages (§ 1431.2), punitive damages (§ 3294), property and recording | contracts · torts · real-property · remedies |
| **Commercial Code** | Com. Code | California's enactment of the UCC; numbering drops the hyphen (Com. Code § 2207 = UCC § 2-207). The MBE uses the UCC Official Text instead. | [contracts](../03-subjects/contracts/README.md) |
| **Penal Code** | Pen. Code | Crimes and criminal procedure: murder (§§ 187–189), insanity and abolition of diminished capacity (§ 25), suppression motions (§ 1538.5) | [criminal-law-procedure](../03-subjects/criminal-law-procedure/README.md) |
| **Probate Code** | Prob. Code | Wills (§§ 6110, 6111), intestacy (§§ 6401, 6402), anti-lapse (§ 21110), omitted spouse and child (§§ 21610, 21620), community property at death (§ 100), trusts (§§ 15200, 15400, 15401, 16047) [S-094] [S-093] | [wills-succession](../03-subjects/wills-succession/README.md) · [trusts](../03-subjects/trusts/README.md) |
| **Family Code** | Fam. Code | Community property: definition (§ 760), separate property (§ 770), quasi-community property (§ 125), transmutation (§ 852), equal division (§ 2550), reimbursement (§ 2640) [S-086] | [community-property](../03-subjects/community-property/README.md) |
| **Business and Professions Code** | B&P | State Bar Act: attorney duties (§ 6068, incl. confidentiality § 6068(e)), written fee agreements (§§ 6147, 6148) [S-082]. Also noncompetes (§ 16600). | [professional-responsibility](../03-subjects/professional-responsibility/README.md) |
| **Corporations Code** | Corp. Code | Corporations (Title 1); partnerships, LLCs and other entities (Title 2) [S-148]. E.g., interested-director transactions (§ 310), derivative suits (§ 800). BA essays use general principles unless the call asks for California law. | [business-associations](../03-subjects/business-associations/README.md) |

## 4. Precedent in California

| Rule | Authority |
|---|---|
| Decisions of the **Supreme Court of California** bind every California court. | Vertical stare decisis |
| A **published** Court of Appeal decision binds **every superior court in the state**, not just the courts in its own district. | *Auto Equity Sales, Inc. v. Superior Court* (1962) 57 Cal.2d 450, 455 |
| When published Court of Appeal decisions **conflict**, a superior court may choose which to follow. | *Auto Equity*, *supra*, 57 Cal.2d at p. 456 |
| A Court of Appeal panel is **not bound** by another district's or division's decision (no horizontal stare decisis among Courts of Appeal). In practice panels usually follow one another absent good reason to disagree. | *Sarti v. Salt Creek Ltd.* (2008) 167 Cal.App.4th 1187, 1193, as followed in *Cohen v. Superior Court* (2024) [S-1301] [S-1302] |
| **Unpublished** Court of Appeal opinions "must not be cited or relied on by a court or a party in any other action". Exceptions: law of the case, res judicata, collateral estoppel, or a criminal or disciplinary action involving the same defendant. | Cal. Rules of Court, rule 8.1115(a)–(b) [S-143]. A 2026 proposal would allow limited citation in petitions for review [S-144] [VERIFY V-042]. |
| A published Court of Appeal opinion under **Supreme Court review** has no binding effect while review is pending. It may be cited for persuasive value only, unless the Supreme Court orders otherwise. | Rule 8.1115(e) [S-143] |
| **U.S. Supreme Court** decisions bind California courts on federal questions. Decisions of **lower federal courts, including the Ninth Circuit**, are persuasive but not binding, even on federal law. | *People v. Bradley* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 80, 86 |

**Why it matters for you**
- When a subject file cites a California Court of Appeal case, it is binding statewide on trial courts. That is why a handful of Court of Appeal decisions are "the California rule".
- On the essay, case names are optional. A few California cases **are** the rule, and naming them signals command of California law. Examples: *Li v. Yellow Cab Co.* (1975) 13 Cal.3d 804 (pure comparative negligence); *Thing v. La Chusa* (1989) 48 Cal.3d 644 (bystander emotional distress).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: an unpublished California opinion is roughly like a PRC judgment that is not a guiding case. It decides the parties' dispute but cannot be invoked as authority in later cases (approx.).

## 5. Initiatives and propositions that matter on the exam

Voters enact statutes and constitutional amendments by **initiative** (Cal. Const. art. II, § 8). Some of the best-known California distinctions on the bar were enacted this way, and a voter-enacted rule can be changed only as the initiative or constitution allows.

| Proposition | What it did | Exam use |
|---|---|---|
| **Proposition 8 (1982), "Victims' Bill of Rights"**, including the **Right to Truth-in-Evidence**, Cal. Const. art. I, § 28(f)(2) [S-145] | **Relevant evidence shall not be excluded in any criminal proceeding.** Exceptions: (1) existing statutory rules of privilege and hearsay; (2) Evid. Code §§ 352, 782 and 1103; (3) a later statute passed by a two-thirds vote of each house; (4) exclusion **required by the U.S. Constitution**. Under (4), Prop 8 permits excluding unlawfully obtained evidence only where federal law requires it (*In re Lance W.* (1985) 37 Cal.3d 873, 890). Applies only in criminal proceedings. The provision was originally numbered § 28(d) and was later redesignated § 28(f)(2), when Proposition 9 (2008) restructured § 28 [S-1303] [S-145]. | **Every criminal-case Evidence essay under California law**: start CEC analysis by asking whether Prop 8 admits evidence the CEC would otherwise exclude (e.g., character evidence beyond the preserved exceptions). |
| Same measure: **prior felony convictions**, Cal. Const. art. I, § 28(f)(4) [S-145] | Prior felony convictions may be used "without limitation" for impeachment or sentence enhancement in criminal proceedings. The Supreme Court limits impeachment to felonies involving **moral turpitude**, and Evid. Code § 352 still applies (*People v. Castro* (1985) 38 Cal.3d 301). | Impeachment in California criminal cases |
| Same measure: **Pen. Code § 25** | (a) Abolished the diminished-capacity defense. (b) Insanity: the defendant must prove by a preponderance that they were incapable of knowing or understanding the nature and quality of the act **or** of distinguishing right from wrong. The statute says "and"; the Supreme Court reads it as the disjunctive *M'Naghten* test (*People v. Skinner* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 765). [S-150] | Criminal Law, only if the call asks for California law |
| **Proposition 51 (1986)**, Civ. Code § 1431.2 | In comparative-fault actions, each defendant's liability for **non-economic** damages is several only, in proportion to fault. **Economic** damages remain joint and several. | Torts essays under California law. The MBE default is joint and several liability [S-040]. |

**Trap: two different "Proposition 8"s.** A 2008 measure on marriage also carried the number Proposition 8. On an evidence essay, write "Proposition 8 (1982), the Truth-in-Evidence provision (Cal. Const. art. I, § 28(f)(2))".

## 6. Which essays test California law and which test general principles

**Default instruction (printed on every exam):** "Unless a question expressly asks you to use California law, you should answer according to legal theories and principles of general application" [S-077]. The State Bar's scope statements then pull California law into specific subjects.

| Subject | What law the essay tests | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence** | FRE **and** California Evidence Code; be prepared to "compare and contrast" | [S-083] |
| **Civil Procedure** | FRCP **and** California Code of Civil Procedure | [S-084] |
| **Professional Responsibility** | California Rules of Professional Conduct, relevant B&P sections and leading case law; the content outline also lists the ABA Model Rules and Model Code. PR issues can appear in **any** essay. | [S-082] |
| **Community Property** | California community property law (Family Code; the outline cites California cases) | [S-086] |
| **Wills and Succession** | California Probate Code (the outline is keyed to Probate Code sections) | [S-094] |
| **Trusts** | California Probate Code (trust law) | [S-093] |
| Contracts, Torts, Real Property, Constitutional Law, Criminal Law and Procedure, Business Associations, Remedies | **General principles** unless the call expressly asks for California law | [S-077] |

The 13 written-section subjects are listed on the State Bar's scope page [S-041]. How this plays out against the MBE and the PT is in [how-the-bar-exam-tests-law.md](how-the-bar-exam-tests-law.md).
