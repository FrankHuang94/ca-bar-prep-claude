# Contracts — primer

## Why this subject matters for you
Contracts is worth **25 scored MBE questions** [S-007]. It has appeared on **16 of 31** California essay administrations since 2012, often paired with Remedies ([`../../06-past-exams/frequency-analysis.md`](../../06-past-exams/frequency-analysis.md)). Your capital-markets and M&A background helps: you already read contracts for a living. What is new is the **common-law method**. Rules come from cases and the Restatement rather than a code, and the exam rewards arguing both sides of each issue.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Civil Code's contract book gives you one coherent statute; US contract law is a patchwork of case law (summarized in the Restatement), a statute for goods (UCC Article 2) and a few state statutes (approx.).

## The map: one contract, six questions
Every Contracts problem walks the life of a deal. The NCBE outline [S-040] follows the same order:

| Stage | Question | Outline category |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Which law? | Goods (UCC Article 2) or everything else (common law)? | Introductory principles |
| 1. Was a contract formed? | Offer, acceptance, consideration (or a substitute) | I Formation |
| 2. Can it be enforced? | Defenses: capacity, duress, mistake, fraud, illegality, statute of frauds | II Defenses |
| 3. What are its terms? | Parol evidence, interpretation, implied terms, warranties | III Content |
| 4. Did someone fail to perform, and is that excused? | Conditions, breach, repudiation, impossibility, discharge | IV Performance |
| 5. What does the injured party get? | Damages, specific performance, rescission, restitution | V Remedies |
| 6. Can outsiders sue or take over the contract? | Third-party beneficiaries, assignment, delegation | VI Third parties |

On an essay you walk this map **in order**, stopping only where the facts raise a real question.

## How the pieces connect
1. **Two bodies of law run in parallel.** For **goods** (things you can move), UCC Article 2 governs. It is a statute, more flexible and more business-friendly:
   - no mirror-image rule (§ 2-207);
   - modifications need no consideration (§ 2-209);
   - missing terms are filled in (§§ 2-305 to 2-310);
   - the buyer can reject for **any** defect (perfect tender, § 2-601).

   For **services, land and employment**, the common law governs. The first heading of every essay says which applies (R-CTR-001).
2. **Formation is objective.** Courts ask what a reasonable person would understand, not what a party privately intended (R-CTR-003). Offers are freely revocable until accepted, unless one of four doctrines makes them irrevocable (R-CTR-007 to R-CTR-011). That surprises civil-law lawyers.
3. **Consideration is the gatekeeper.** A promise is enforceable only if something was bargained for in exchange (R-CTR-022). A pure gift promise is not, unless the promisee reasonably relied (promissory estoppel, R-CTR-027).
4. **Writing rules are narrow but strict.** Only the "MY LEGS" contracts need a signed writing (R-CTR-042). Once a final writing exists, the parol evidence rule keeps out earlier side deals that contradict it (R-CTR-049). Both rules have many exceptions, and California applies the parol evidence rule liberally (R-CTR-052).
5. **Performance is about conditions and materiality.**
   - An **express condition** must be met exactly (R-CTR-062).
   - The parties' **promises** are measured by **materiality**. A small breach gives damages only; a material breach lets the other side stop and sue (R-CTR-066).
   - For goods, the test is **perfect tender**, softened by cure and revocation rules (R-CTR-072 to R-CTR-075).
6. **Excuse is rare.** Impossibility, impracticability and frustration discharge a duty only for unforeseen events that destroy the basis of the deal. Higher costs are not enough (R-CTR-082 to R-CTR-084).
7. **Remedies aim at the "benefit of the bargain."**
   - The default is **money damages** measured by expectation (R-CTR-089).
   - They are limited by foreseeability (*Hadley v. Baxendale*), certainty and mitigation (R-CTR-092 to R-CTR-095).
   - **Specific performance** is the exception, for land and unique goods (R-CTR-104).
   - **Punitive damages** are not available for breach (R-CTR-098).

## The ideas that matter most
1. **UCC or common law?** Decide this first, always.
2. **Revocability.** An offer can be revoked until accepted unless there is an option, a merchant's firm offer, part performance of a unilateral offer, or reliance.
3. **Mailbox rule.** Acceptance is effective when sent; everything else when received.
4. **Consideration and its substitutes.** Pre-existing duty, past consideration, promissory estoppel.
5. **Statute of frauds and parol evidence.** Know the categories, the writing requirements, and the exceptions.
6. **Material breach vs. minor breach; perfect tender vs. substantial performance.** This is where most essay points sit.
7. **Expectation damages with Hadley foreseeability and mitigation.** Every essay asks what someone can recover.
8. **Equitable remedies.** Specific performance, rescission and reformation, because Contracts + Remedies is a top crossover pair.

## Where California differs (essays only)
California essays are answered under general principles unless the call asks for California law [S-077]. The few California points worth a sentence:
- Civil Code § 1671 (liquidated damages presumed valid);
- *PG&E* and *Riverisland* (liberal parol evidence; fraud);
- Civil Code § 1624 (statute of frauds list);
- Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600 (noncompetes void);
- *Drennan* (subcontractor bids);
- *Rattagan* (fraud during performance).

See [`ca-distinctions.md`](ca-distinctions.md).
