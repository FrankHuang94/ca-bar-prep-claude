# Contracts on the MBE

## 1. What NCBE tells you
- **25 scored questions** per exam [S-007].
- **Assumption:** examinees assume the Official Text of UCC Articles 1 and 2 has been adopted and is in effect, and apply it where appropriate. **About one-quarter** of Contracts questions are based on it [S-040].
- **Distribution:** about **half** of the questions come from **categories I (formation) and IV (performance, breach, discharge)**. The rest come from II (defenses), III (content and meaning), V (remedies) and VI (third-party rights) [S-040].
- **Default law:** answer under generally accepted principles. In practice, follow the Restatement (Second) of Contracts where it states the majority or modern rule, and the UCC for goods. California law is irrelevant on the MBE.

| Category | Approx. share of 25 | Your priority |
|---|---|---|
| I Formation (assent, definiteness, consideration, reliance, modification) | ~6–7 | Highest |
| IV Performance, breach, discharge (conditions, breach, repudiation, warranties, UCC performance, excuse, discharge) | ~6 | Highest |
| II Defenses (capacity, duress, mistake, fraud, illegality, SOF) | ~3–4 | High |
| V Remedies (expectation, foreseeability, LDs, mitigation, equitable, reliance, restitution) | ~3–4 | High |
| III Content and meaning (parol evidence, interpretation, implied terms) | ~2–3 | Medium |
| VI Third-party rights (beneficiaries, assignment, delegation) | ~2–3 | Medium |

The per-category numbers are estimates derived from NCBE's "about half / about one-quarter" notes, not NCBE figures. See [`../../05-mbe/subject-weighting.md`](../../05-mbe/subject-weighting.md).

## 2. The first question to ask on every Contracts MCQ
1. **Goods or not?** If goods, switch to UCC rules: no mirror-image rule (§ 2-207), firm offers (§ 2-205), modification without consideration (§ 2-209), perfect tender (§ 2-601), gap fillers.
2. **Merchant(s)?** That changes §§ 2-201(2), 2-205, 2-207(2), 2-314 and 2-509(3).
3. **What exactly does the call ask?**
   - "Is there an enforceable contract?" — formation and defenses.
   - "Is X liable for breach?" — conditions, materiality, excuse.
   - "What is the measure of damages?" — formula selection.
   - "Which of the following is Y's best argument?" — pick the argument that would actually win, not merely a true statement.

## 3. Black-letter rules the MBE favors

### I Formation
- An offer stated to be open for a period is **revocable** unless there is an option (consideration), a merchant's signed firm offer, a unilateral offer partly performed, or foreseeable reliance (R-CTR-007 to R-CTR-011).
- **Revocation is effective on receipt; acceptance on dispatch** (R-CTR-015). An option acceptance is effective on receipt.
- A **counteroffer** terminates the power of acceptance; an **inquiry** does not (R-CTR-006).
- **Death** of the offeror terminates an ordinary offer even without notice (R-CTR-006).
- **Unilateral contract.** Beginning performance creates an option (R2d § 45); mere preparation does not (R-CTR-010).
- **§ 2-207.** Between merchants, an additional term is in unless it materially alters, the offer limits acceptance, or the offeror objects. Different terms knock out (R-CTR-013, R-CTR-014).
- **Shipment of nonconforming goods** is acceptance and breach unless sent as an accommodation (R-CTR-016).
- **Consideration.** The pre-existing duty rule kills the "pay me more for the same work" promise at common law, unless unforeseen circumstances make it fair and equitable (R-CTR-024, R-CTR-028). Under the UCC, a good-faith modification needs no consideration (R-CTR-029).
- **Past consideration** is no consideration, but watch for the **§ 86 material benefit** answer choice in rescue cases (R-CTR-026).
- **Promissory estoppel** is the right answer when there is a gratuitous promise plus foreseeable, substantial reliance (R-CTR-027).
- **Requirements/output contracts** are not illusory; demands must be in good faith and not unreasonably disproportionate (R-CTR-021).

### II Defenses
- **Minor** disaffirms; the adult cannot. Necessaries mean liability for reasonable value (R-CTR-030).
- **Mutual mistake** turns on **who bore the risk**: an "as is" clause or conscious ignorance defeats rescission (R-CTR-034).
- **Unilateral mistake** works only if the other party knew or should have known, or enforcement is unconscionable (R-CTR-035).
- **Statute of frauds:**
  - the one-year clause asks whether performance is **possible** within a year **from the making** (R-CTR-044);
  - suretyship requires a collateral promise to the creditor, subject to the main purpose rule (R-CTR-045);
  - for goods, the merchant confirmation and the three exceptions (R-CTR-047);
  - a modification must satisfy the SOF if the modified contract falls within it (R-CTR-048).
- **Misrepresentation:** a material misrepresentation, even an innocent one, justifies rescission (R-CTR-037).

### III Content and meaning
- **Parol evidence:** classify the evidence first.
  - **Prior or contemporaneous** and contradicting a final writing: excluded.
  - **Subsequent** agreement: not barred.
  - Proof of **fraud, mistake or a condition precedent**: admissible (R-CTR-049, R-CTR-050).
  - A **merger clause** is strong evidence of complete integration (R-CTR-049).
- **Course of performance > course of dealing > usage of trade**, and express terms beat all three (R-CTR-054).

### IV Performance, breach, discharge
- **Express conditions need strict compliance.** Excuse comes through waiver, prevention, or forfeiture (R-CTR-062, R-CTR-064).
- **Substantial performance** applies to services and construction, not goods (R-CTR-065).
- **Material breach** lets the other party suspend and then terminate. A **minor** breach gives damages only (R-CTR-066).
- **Anticipatory repudiation** must be unequivocal. The aggrieved party may sue at once, but it may not keep performing to increase damages (R-CTR-068, R-CTR-095).
- **Adequate assurance:** a written demand under the UCC; failure to respond within 30 days is a repudiation (R-CTR-070).
- **Perfect tender** with cure (R-CTR-072, R-CTR-073); **installment** contracts use substantial impairment (R-CTR-076).
- **Acceptance** of goods by using them; **revocation** requires substantial impairment (R-CTR-074, R-CTR-075).
- **Risk of loss order:** agreement → breach → carrier (shipment/destination) → bailee → merchant (receipt) / non-merchant (tender) (R-CTR-078, R-CTR-079).
- **Warranties:**
  - merchantability requires a merchant seller;
  - fitness requires reason to know of the purpose and reliance;
  - disclaiming merchantability requires the word "merchantability" (conspicuous if written), or "as is" (R-CTR-057 to R-CTR-060).
- **Impracticability:** cost increases do not excuse; destruction of a specific source the parties contemplated does (R-CTR-083).
- **Accord:** while it is executory, the original debt is suspended; the debtor's breach revives the creditor's option to sue on either (R-CTR-087). A **substituted contract** immediately discharges the original debt (R-CTR-088).

### V Remedies
- Pick the **measure that matches the facts**: cover vs. market for buyers; resale vs. market vs. lost volume vs. price for sellers; cost of completion vs. diminution in value for construction (R-CTR-090, R-CTR-099 to R-CTR-103).
- **Consequential damages** require foreseeability at contracting (*Hadley*). The seller has no consequential damages under Article 2 (R-CTR-092).
- **Liquidated damages:** reasonable relative to anticipated or actual loss and the difficulty of proof; otherwise a penalty (R-CTR-096).
- **Mitigation:** employees need not take inferior jobs; a lost-volume seller is not penalized for reselling (R-CTR-095, R-CTR-100).
- **Specific performance:** land and unique goods yes; personal services no (R-CTR-104).
- **Restitution for a breaching party:** benefit conferred minus damages caused; UCC § 2-718(2) for deposits (R-CTR-109).

### VI Third-party rights
- A **named, intended** beneficiary may sue the promisor. The promisor can use defenses **from the contract** against it. Modification is allowed before **vesting** (R-CTR-110, R-CTR-111).
- An **anti-assignment** clause without "void" language does not invalidate an assignment of rights; prohibiting assignment of "the contract" bars only delegation (R-CTR-113).
- An **obligor who pays the assignor before notice** is discharged (R-CTR-114).
- A **delegating party stays liable**; the delegate is liable only on assumption (R-CTR-116).

## 4. Recurring MBE fact patterns
| Pattern | What it tests | Usual answer |
|---|---|---|
| Builder midway demands more money; owner agrees, then refuses to pay | Pre-existing duty; § 89 | No consideration unless an unforeseen difficulty made the modification fair |
| Offer "held open 30 days" with no payment; offeror revokes on day 5 | Revocability | Revocation effective (no option, not a merchant firm offer) |
| Merchant's signed letter "price good until June 30" for goods | § 2-205 | Irrevocable up to 3 months |
| Offeree mails acceptance; offeror's revocation arrives before the acceptance does | Mailbox | Contract formed on dispatch |
| Offeree mails rejection, then phones acceptance that arrives first | § 40 sequencing | Contract (acceptance arrived first) |
| Rescuer injured saving the promisor's life; later promise of a pension | Past consideration vs. § 86 | Most likely enforceable under § 86 in a modern jurisdiction |
| Uncle promises $X; nephew buys a car in reliance | § 90 | Enforceable to the extent of reliance |
| Oral employment for one year beginning next month | One-year SOF | Within the statute |
| "Lifetime" oral employment | One-year SOF | Outside the statute |
| Father tells a store "give my son credit; if he doesn't pay, I will" | Suretyship | Within the SOF; unenforceable without a writing |
| Written contract with merger clause; oral promise made the same day | Parol evidence | Excluded if it contradicts or the writing is complete; admissible to prove fraud or a condition precedent |
| Oral agreement two weeks after signing | Parol evidence | Not barred (subsequent); check the NOM clause and § 2-209 |
| Goods arrive with a minor defect before the delivery deadline | Perfect tender + cure | Buyer may reject; seller may cure by the deadline |
| Seller (merchant) tells buyer to pick up; goods destroyed first | § 2-509(3) | Risk on the merchant seller |
| F.O.B. seller's city; goods lost in transit | § 2-509(1)(a) | Risk on the buyer if the goods conformed |
| Wrong goods shipped F.O.B. seller; lost in transit | § 2-510(1) | Risk stays on the seller |
| Harvest destroyed on the specified field | Impracticability | Excused |
| Supplier's costs double | Impracticability | Not excused |
| Contractor stops after the owner fails to make a progress payment | Material breach / constructive condition | Depends on materiality; non-payment of a progress payment is often material |
| Builder uses a different but equivalent brand | Substantial performance | Recover the price minus (nominal) diminution in value |
| Employee fired; refuses an offered job in another field | Mitigation | Damages not reduced |
| Buyer repudiates; dealer with unlimited supply resells the item | Lost volume | Lost profit (§ 2-708(2)) |
| Clause: "$1,000,000 for any breach" in a $50,000 deal | Penalty | Unenforceable; actual damages |
| Assignment despite "no assignment of rights" clause without "void" | § 322 | Assignment effective; obligor may claim damages |
| A and B modify the contract before C learns of it | TPB vesting | Modification binds C |

## 5. Distractor patterns to recognize
- **"Because the offer said it would remain open"** — wrong unless there is an option or a merchant's firm offer.
- **"Because there was no consideration"** in a sale-of-goods modification — wrong under § 2-209.
- **"Because the contract was not in writing"** where the SOF does not apply (possible within a year; goods under $500) or an exception applies.
- **"Because the buyer accepted the goods"** given as the reason the buyer has no remedy — wrong; an accepting buyer keeps a damages claim (§ 2-714) if it gave notice.
- **"Substantial performance"** offered in a goods case — wrong (perfect tender).
- **"Punitive damages"** for a willful breach — wrong absent an independent tort.
- **The "fairest" outcome** instead of the rule-based outcome. The MBE rewards the black-letter answer.
- **Two right-sounding rules, one inapplicable to the facts.** For example, "mutual mistake" offered where the parties consciously gambled on value.
