# Contracts — traps

Each item gives the trap, the correct rule, and the rule ID.

## Formation
1. **"The offer said it would stay open, so it can't be revoked."**
   - Wrong at common law without consideration (R-CTR-007).
   - Only an option (R-CTR-008), a merchant's signed firm offer (R-CTR-009), begun unilateral performance (R-CTR-010) or reliance (R-CTR-011) makes an offer irrevocable.
2. **Firm offer from a non-merchant, or an unsigned one.** § 2-205 needs a **merchant** and a **signed writing** (R-CTR-009).
3. **Firm offer beyond three months.** It is irrevocable only for three months without consideration (R-CTR-009).
4. **Mailbox rule applied to revocations or rejections.** Only acceptances are effective on dispatch; option acceptances are effective on receipt (R-CTR-015).
5. **Rejection sent first, then acceptance.** No dispatch rule here; whichever **arrives first** wins (R-CTR-015).
6. **Counteroffer vs. inquiry.** "Would you take $9,000?" is not a rejection (R-CTR-006).
7. **Death of the offeror.** It terminates an ordinary offer even if the offeree didn't know, but not an option (R-CTR-006, R-CTR-008).
8. **Mirror image applied to goods.** Under the UCC, a reply with new terms can still be an acceptance (§ 2-207) (R-CTR-013).
9. **"Expressly conditional" read loosely.** A bare "subject to our terms" is not enough; the clause must clearly condition acceptance on assent (R-CTR-013).
10. **Additional terms between a merchant and a non-merchant.** They are only proposals; they do not become terms automatically (R-CTR-013).
11. **Preparation treated as part performance of a unilateral offer.** Buying materials is not beginning performance (§ 45). Use § 87(2) reliance instead (R-CTR-010, R-CTR-011).
12. **Advertisements treated as offers.** Usually invitations, unless specific and limited ("first come, first served") (R-CTR-005).

## Consideration and modification
13. **Inadequate consideration = no consideration.** Courts do not weigh adequacy; $1 can support a promise if actually bargained for (R-CTR-022).
14. **Past consideration.** A promise to pay for a past benefit has no consideration. The MBE answer may be § 86 material benefit in a modern jurisdiction, so read the answer choices (R-CTR-026).
15. **Pre-existing duty under the UCC.** For goods, no consideration is needed for a good-faith modification (R-CTR-029). For services, consideration is needed unless § 89 applies (R-CTR-028).
16. **"No oral modification" clauses.** Enforceable under UCC § 2-209(2), often waived at common law. **CA** § 1698(c) applies "unless the contract otherwise expressly provides" (R-CTR-028, R-CTR-029).
17. **Promissory estoppel without a promise.** Negotiations or statements of intention are not promises (R-CTR-027).

## Defenses
18. **Statute of frauds measured from the start of performance.** Measure from the **making** of the contract (R-CTR-044).
19. **"Lifetime" contracts treated as over a year.** They can be performed within a year (death), so they are **outside** the statute (R-CTR-044).
20. **Primary promise treated as suretyship.** "Send it to my son and bill me" is the promisor's own debt, outside the statute (R-CTR-045).
21. **UCC writing with the wrong quantity.** The contract is enforceable only up to the quantity **stated** (R-CTR-047).
22. **Merchant confirmation binds the sender.** It removes the SOF defense of the **recipient**. The sender, who signed, was already bound (R-CTR-047).
23. **Mutual mistake about value.** Value mistakes are not basic-assumption mistakes. An "as is" clause or conscious ignorance puts the risk on the complaining party (R-CTR-034).
24. **Unilateral mistake without the other party's knowledge.** Relief also needs unconscionability or the other party's reason to know (R-CTR-035).
25. **Innocent misrepresentation = no remedy.** A **material** innocent misrepresentation supports rescission; tort damages need scienter (R-CTR-037).
26. **Duress = any pressure.** It must be an **improper** threat leaving **no reasonable alternative** (R-CTR-032).
27. **Noncompetes on a California essay.** Give the majority reasonableness test, but note California voids them (B&P § 16600) (R-CTR-041).

## Content and meaning
28. **Parol evidence rule applied to later agreements.** It covers only **prior** and **contemporaneous** agreements (R-CTR-050).
29. **Merger clause = automatic exclusion of everything.** Evidence of fraud, mistake, a condition precedent or ambiguity still comes in (R-CTR-050). In California, fraud evidence comes in even if contradictory (*Riverisland*) (R-CTR-052).
30. **Trade usage overriding express terms.** The hierarchy runs express > course of performance > course of dealing > usage (R-CTR-054).

## Performance and breach
31. **Substantial performance in a goods case.** Goods use perfect tender (with cure and installment exceptions) (R-CTR-065, R-CTR-072).
32. **Substantial performance of an express condition.** Express conditions need strict compliance; look for an excuse instead (R-CTR-062, R-CTR-064).
33. **Minor breach treated as permission to stop performing.** Terminating for a minor breach is itself a material breach (R-CTR-066).
34. **Ambiguous doubts treated as repudiation.** "I may have trouble delivering" is not unequivocal; demand assurance (R-CTR-068, R-CTR-070).
35. **Continuing performance after repudiation.** The added costs are not recoverable (R-CTR-068, R-CTR-095).
36. **Acceptance ends all remedies.** An accepting buyer can still sue for damages (§ 2-714) if it gave timely notice (R-CTR-074, R-CTR-103).
37. **Revocation for any defect.** It requires **substantial impairment** (R-CTR-075).
38. **Installment contract and perfect tender.** The test is substantial impairment of the installment, or of the whole (R-CTR-076).
39. **Risk of loss follows title.** It does not; follow § 2-509 / § 2-510 (R-CTR-078, R-CTR-079).
40. **Merchant seller and tender.** Risk passes on the buyer's **receipt**, not on the seller's tender (R-CTR-078).
41. **Nonconforming goods in transit under a shipment contract.** Risk stays on the seller (§ 2-510(1)) (R-CTR-079).
42. **"As is" disclaims the title warranty.** It does not (R-CTR-056).
43. **Price increase = impracticability.** Usually not (R-CTR-083).
44. **Executory accord treated as an immediate discharge.** Until satisfaction, the original debt is only suspended. A substituted contract discharges immediately (R-CTR-087, R-CTR-088).

## Remedies
45. **Punitive damages for a willful breach.** Not without an independent tort (R-CTR-098, R-CTR-117).
46. **Consequential damages for the seller under the UCC.** Article 2 gives sellers incidental damages only (R-CTR-092, R-CTR-099).
47. **Foreseeability judged at the time of breach.** It is judged at **contracting** (R-CTR-092).
48. **Cost of completion where it is economic waste.** Use diminution in value (R-CTR-090).
49. **Mitigation requires an employee to take any job.** Only substantially similar work counts (R-CTR-091, R-CTR-095).
50. **Lost-volume seller's resale reduces its damages.** It does not (R-CTR-100).
51. **Labels decide liquidated damages.** Calling a clause "liquidated damages" does not save a penalty. **CA** § 1671(b) presumes validity and places the burden on the challenger (R-CTR-096).
52. **Specific performance of personal services.** Never; at most a negative injunction, which California allows only under Civ. Code § 3423(e) (R-CTR-104).
53. **A breaching party gets nothing.** Restitution of the net benefit is available (R-CTR-109).

## Third parties
54. **Incidental beneficiary suing.** No rights (R-CTR-110).
55. **Beneficiary rights vest at contracting.** They vest only on assent, suit or reliance (R-CTR-111).
56. **"No assignment" clause voids the assignment.** Only if it says the assignment is void; otherwise it gives damages (R-CTR-113).
57. **Delegation releases the delegator.** Only a novation does (R-CTR-116).
58. **Obligor pays the assignor after notice.** After notice, only payment to the assignee discharges (R-CTR-114).

## Essay-writing traps (California)
59. **Skipping governing law.** Selected answers lead with UCC vs. common law; write it even when obvious (R-CTR-001).
60. **One-sided damages.** If the call asks what "each party" can recover, give each party its own heading.
61. **California law when not asked.** Give general principles first; add California as a one-line supplement [S-077].
62. **Forgetting conclusions.** End each issue with "likely" or "probably."
