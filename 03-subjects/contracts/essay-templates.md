# Contracts — essay templates

**Basis.** The Phase 3 index of 17 Contracts essays (16 administrations, Feb 2012 – Jul 2026) shows four recurring call types [VERIFY V-071]:
1. "Did either party breach, and what damages can each recover?" (Feb 2015 Q1, Jul 2016 Q3, Jul 2018 Q1).
2. "Was a contract formed / who prevails?" with defenses (Jul 2022 Q1, Jul 2019 Q5, Feb 2026 Q2, Feb 2024 Q5).
3. UCC goods disputes: warranty, rejection, revocation, risk of loss (Feb 2016 Q6, Feb 2021 Q2, Jul 2022 Q1).
4. Remedies-focused calls: specific performance, rescission, reformation, restitution (Jul 2014 Q1, Jul 2017 Q3, Feb 2018 Q1, Feb 2020 Q3, Jul 2023 Q5).

The templates below are skeletons; fill them with the rules from [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md). Selected answers open with **governing law** and use **party-by-party** or **call-by-call** headings (`00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §5).

**Timing (60 minutes).** About 15 minutes reading and outlining, 45 minutes writing. Spend the most words on the issues the facts develop most (dates, dollar figures, quoted statements).

---

## Template 1 — "Did either party breach, and what can each recover?" (two-sided breach and damages)

```
I. Governing law — UCC Article 2 vs. common law (R-CTR-001; merchants R-CTR-002)
   Rule → facts (goods? services? predominant purpose) → conclusion.

II. Was there a valid contract?  (one short paragraph unless formation is disputed)
   Offer / acceptance / consideration (R-CTR-003, -012, -022); defenses only if the facts raise them.

III. [Party A]'s claim against [Party B]
   A. Did B breach?
      1. B's duty and its timing (express terms; conditions R-CTR-062/-063)
      2. Breach — material vs. minor (R-CTR-066) / perfect tender (R-CTR-072)
         or anticipatory repudiation (R-CTR-068)
      3. B's defenses / excuses (prior breach by A; impracticability R-CTR-083; waiver R-CTR-064)
      → Conclusion
   B. A's damages
      1. Expectation (R-CTR-089; formula with the facts' numbers; UCC R-CTR-099–103)
      2. Consequential — foreseeability (R-CTR-092) and certainty (R-CTR-093)
      3. Incidental (R-CTR-094)
      4. Mitigation (R-CTR-095)
      5. Liquidated damages clause, if any (R-CTR-096; CA § 1671(b) one-liner)
      → Conclusion with a dollar figure if the facts permit

IV. [Party B]'s claim against [Party A]  (same structure: breach → damages)

V. Conclusion — who recovers what, net.
```

**Paragraph pattern for "material breach":**
> *Rule.* A breach is material if it deprives the injured party of a substantial part of the benefit it reasonably expected (R2d § 241); a minor breach gives only damages. *Application.* [Pro-material facts]. However, [anti-material facts: % complete, cure offered, delay short, no time-of-essence clause]. *Conclusion.* The breach was likely [minor/material], so [termination was / was not] justified.

**Chronology tip.** If both sides breached, analyze in date order. The first material breach decides who was entitled to stop performing.

## Template 2 — "Was a contract formed?" (formation and defenses)

```
I. Governing law (R-CTR-001) + merchant status (R-CTR-002)

II. Formation
   A. Offer (R-CTR-004/-005) — commitment, definite terms, communicated
   B. Termination before acceptance? (revocation R-CTR-007; rejection/counteroffer, lapse,
      death R-CTR-006); irrevocable? (option / firm offer / reliance R-CTR-008–011)
   C. Acceptance (R-CTR-012) — manner; timing (mailbox R-CTR-015);
      goods: § 2-207 (R-CTR-013/-014)
   D. Consideration (R-CTR-022) — or promissory estoppel (R-CTR-027)
   → Conclusion: contract formed on [date] on [terms]

III. Defenses to enforcement
   A. Statute of frauds (R-CTR-042 → category; R-CTR-043/-047 → writing; exceptions R-CTR-046–048)
   B. Mistake (R-CTR-034/-035) or misunderstanding (R-CTR-036)
   C. Misrepresentation (R-CTR-037/-038)
   D. Other: capacity, duress, unconscionability (only if raised)
   → Conclusion on each

IV. Terms — parol evidence (R-CTR-049/-050; CA R-CTR-052) and interpretation (R-CTR-053)

V. Result / remedy (if asked)
```

**Paragraph pattern for "statute of frauds":**
> *Within the statute?* The contract is for the sale of goods priced at $[x], at or above $500, so UCC § 2-201 applies. *Satisfied?* The only writing is [describe]; it [is/is not] signed by [defendant] and [does/does not] state a quantity. *Exception?* [Merchant confirmation / specially manufactured / admission / part performance]. *Conclusion.* The contract is [enforceable / unenforceable], though [Party] may recover in restitution for [benefit].

## Template 3 — UCC goods: nonconforming goods, warranty, risk of loss

```
I. UCC Article 2 applies; merchant status
II. Formation and terms (only if disputed: § 2-207, gap fillers)
III. Seller's performance
   A. Tender and delivery terms — shipment vs. destination; F.O.B. (R-CTR-077)
   B. Conformity — express warranty (R-CTR-057), merchantability (R-CTR-058),
      fitness (R-CTR-059), disclaimers (R-CTR-060)
IV. Buyer's response
   A. Rejection — perfect tender, timely, notice (R-CTR-072)
   B. Seller's cure (R-CTR-073)
   C. Acceptance (R-CTR-074) → revocation (R-CTR-075)?  Installments (R-CTR-076)?
   D. Notice of breach (R-CTR-061)
V. Risk of loss (if goods lost/damaged) — agreement → breach (R-CTR-079) → carrier/bailee/
   merchant (R-CTR-078); casualty to identified goods (R-CTR-080)
VI. Remedies
   Buyer: cover/market (R-CTR-102), accepted goods (R-CTR-103), incidental/consequential;
          § 2-719 limits (R-CTR-097); specific performance for unique goods (R-CTR-104)
   Seller: resale/market/lost volume/price (R-CTR-099–101)
```

## Template 4 — Specific performance (often a Contracts + Remedies crossover)

```
I. Valid contract with definite terms (formation in one paragraph; SOF if land/goods ≥ $500)
II. Plaintiff's conditions satisfied / ready, willing, able
III. Inadequate legal remedy — land (CA Civ. Code § 3387), unique chattel (§ 2-716),
     speculative damages
IV. Feasibility — personal services? (no SP; negative injunction?) supervision?
V. Defenses — laches, unclean hands, unconscionability / inadequate consideration
   (CA § 3391), mistake, BFP, SOF
VI. Interim relief if asked — TRO / preliminary injunction (see Remedies folder)
VII. Conclusion; alternative damages remedy
```
Mnemonic **CCIFD**: Contract, Conditions, Inadequate remedy, Feasibility, Defenses (R-CTR-104).

## Template 5 — Rescission and reformation (misrepresentation / mistake)

```
I. Governing law
II. Rescission
   A. Ground — misrepresentation (R-CTR-037: assertion, fraudulent or material,
      inducement, justified reliance) / nondisclosure (R-CTR-038) / mistake (R-CTR-034/-035)
   B. Requirements — prompt notice, restore benefits (CA Civ. Code § 1691) (R-CTR-105)
   C. Defenses — affirmation, delay/laches, "as is" or merger clause (PER fraud exception;
      CA Riverisland R-CTR-052), unclean hands
   D. Restitution on rescission
III. Reformation (if the writing departs from the agreement) (R-CTR-106)
   A. Prior agreement + writing differs + mutual mistake in expression or fraud
   B. Clear and convincing proof; not barred by PER/SOF
   C. Defenses — BFP, laches
IV. Alternative — damages (fraud tort: CA R-CTR-117; punitive Civ. Code § 3294)
V. Conclusion
```

## Template 6 — Damages-only call ("What damages can X recover?")

```
I. Governing law (1 short paragraph)
II. Breach (1 paragraph — the call assumes or asks it)
III. Damages
   A. Expectation — formula + numbers (R-CTR-089; construction R-CTR-090;
      employment R-CTR-091; UCC R-CTR-099–103)
   B. Consequential — Hadley two limbs; what Seller knew at contracting (R-CTR-092)
   C. Certainty — lost profits; new business (R-CTR-093)
   D. Incidental (R-CTR-094)
   E. Mitigation (R-CTR-095)
   F. Liquidated damages / limitation clauses (R-CTR-096/-097)
   G. Punitive / emotional distress — not recoverable (R-CTR-098)
   H. Alternatives — reliance (R-CTR-107), restitution (R-CTR-108)
   I. Equitable — specific performance (R-CTR-104)
IV. Breaching party's recovery, if relevant (R-CTR-109)
V. Conclusion (dollar figure if possible)
```

**Damages calculation line (write it out):**
> Expectation = contract price $[x] − market/cover price $[y] = $[z] direct loss; plus incidental $[a]; plus consequential $[b] if foreseeable; minus costs saved $[c] → total $[t].
