# Contracts — issue checklist

**How to use it.** Read the call, then run this list **in order** against the facts. Each line names the issue, its **trigger facts**, and the rule ID to write. Threshold issues come first, because California selected answers open with governing law and formation before breach (see `00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §5).

**Frequency key.** ★★★ = tested repeatedly on California essays; ★★ = tested; ★ = rare or MBE-mostly (Phase 3 tags, indicative only [VERIFY V-071]).

## 0. Read the call
- [ ] Does the call name parties ("Can Buyer recover against Seller?"), list questions (formation / breach / damages), or ask only "who prevails"? Organize by the call.
- [ ] Is it a crossover? Contracts + Remedies (specific performance, rescission, reformation), Contracts + PR, Contracts + Torts (fraud, products), Contracts + BA (entity liability).
- [ ] Does the call say "under California law"? If not, use general principles plus a "California" sentence where it matters [S-077].

## 1. Threshold: governing law ★★★
- [ ] **UCC Article 2 vs. common law.** Triggers: goods, equipment, inventory, "purchase order," a mixed contract (installation, design plus plants, a cooler built and installed). → R-CTR-001
- [ ] **Merchant status.** Triggers: a business dealing in the goods; a professional; "both parties are businesses." → R-CTR-002

## 2. Formation ★★★
- [ ] **Offer.** Triggers: an ad, quote, catalog, "I'd sell for…", a bid, an auction, a reward. → R-CTR-004, R-CTR-005
- [ ] **Termination before acceptance.**
  - Revocation: "I've changed my mind," a sale to someone else.
  - Rejection or counteroffer: "I accept if…".
  - Lapse: time passes, "open until."
  - Death or incapacity of a party.
  - → R-CTR-006, R-CTR-007
- [ ] **Irrevocability.**
  - Option: paid to keep the offer open.
  - Merchant's firm offer: a signed written "will hold."
  - Unilateral performance begun.
  - Reliance: subcontractor bids (*Drennan*).
  - → R-CTR-008 to R-CTR-011
- [ ] **Acceptance.**
  - Manner (promise vs. performance; shipment).
  - Mirror image (new terms).
  - Silence.
  - Timing — the mailbox rule: letters crossing, a revocation arriving after the acceptance was sent.
  - → R-CTR-012, R-CTR-015 to R-CTR-017
- [ ] **Battle of the forms** (goods). Triggers: purchase order vs. acknowledgment; terms on an invoice; a "conditional" acceptance; performance despite mismatched forms. → R-CTR-013, R-CTR-014
- [ ] **Definiteness / gap fillers.** Triggers: price "to be agreed," quantity missing, "requirements," "all we need." → R-CTR-019 to R-CTR-021
- [ ] **Consideration.**
  - Gratuitous promise; "in recognition of past help."
  - Promise to pay more for the same work.
  - Settlement of a doubtful claim.
  - Illusory "as we wish."
  - → R-CTR-022 to R-CTR-026
- [ ] **Promissory estoppel** (fallback whenever consideration fails). Triggers: the promisee quit a job, moved, spent money in reliance. → R-CTR-027
- [ ] **Modification.** Triggers: mid-contract price increase, extra pay, a "no oral modification" clause. → R-CTR-028, R-CTR-029
- [ ] **Implied-in-fact contract / quasi-contract.** Triggers: services rendered without agreement on price; no valid contract but a benefit conferred. → R-CTR-018

## 3. Defenses ★★★
- [ ] **Statute of frauds.** Triggers:
  - an oral deal;
  - goods of $500 or more;
  - land;
  - "for two years";
  - "if he doesn't pay, I will";
  - a broker's commission;
  - an email or text, a letterhead, a signed confirmation;
  - specially made goods, part payment, an admission.
  - → R-CTR-042 to R-CTR-048
- [ ] **Parol evidence.** Triggers: a signed writing plus an earlier or same-day oral promise; a merger clause; "the seller had told me…"; an ambiguous term; an oral condition. → R-CTR-049 to R-CTR-052 (California: *PG&E*, *Masterson*, *Riverisland*)
- [ ] **Mistake / misunderstanding.** Triggers: both wrong about the nature or authenticity of the goods; a bid error; "as is"; two meanings of one word. → R-CTR-034 to R-CTR-036
- [ ] **Misrepresentation / nondisclosure.** Triggers: a false statement about condition, history or zoning; concealed defects; "best ever" (puffery). → R-CTR-037, R-CTR-038, R-CTR-117
- [ ] **Duress / undue influence.** Triggers: "sign or I won't deliver"; a threat; a confidential relationship; pressure at an odd time. → R-CTR-032, R-CTR-033
- [ ] **Capacity.** Triggers: age 17; dementia; intoxication. → R-CTR-030, R-CTR-031
- [ ] **Illegality / unconscionability / public policy.** Triggers: unlicensed contractor; adhesion form; a harsh clause; a noncompete; a release. → R-CTR-039 to R-CTR-041

## 4. Terms and meaning ★★
- [ ] **Interpretation.** Triggers: a disputed word ("chicken," "standard"); trade usage; past dealings; course of performance. → R-CTR-053, R-CTR-054
- [ ] **Implied terms / good faith.** Triggers: exclusive dealing; discretion; satisfaction clauses; a termination right exercised to grab a benefit. → R-CTR-055, R-CTR-071
- [ ] **Warranties** (goods). Triggers: a seller's statements, a sample, a defect, a buyer's stated purpose, an "as is" or disclaimer clause. → R-CTR-056 to R-CTR-061

## 5. Performance and breach ★★★
- [ ] **Conditions** — express (strict compliance) vs. constructive (order of performance). Triggers: "subject to," financing, architect approval, a satisfaction clause, progress payments. → R-CTR-062, R-CTR-063
- [ ] **Excuse of conditions.** Triggers: waiver (accepting late performance), prevention, forfeiture. → R-CTR-064
- [ ] **Breach — material vs. minor; substantial performance; time of the essence.** Triggers: late or incomplete work; termination; defects; "time is of the essence" (or its absence). → R-CTR-065 to R-CTR-067
- [ ] **Anticipatory repudiation / assurance / retraction.** Triggers:
  - "I won't perform";
  - a sale of the subject matter to another;
  - a demand for new terms;
  - doubts about performance;
  - "I've changed my mind back."
  - → R-CTR-068 to R-CTR-070
- [ ] **UCC performance.**
  - Perfect tender, rejection, cure.
  - Acceptance and revocation.
  - Installments.
  - Delivery terms and risk of loss (F.O.B., destruction in transit or in the warehouse).
  - Title.
  - → R-CTR-072 to R-CTR-081

## 6. Excuse and discharge ★★
- [ ] **Impossibility / impracticability / frustration.** Triggers: fire, flood, death, new law, war, a pandemic closure, cost spikes, a cancelled event. → R-CTR-082 to R-CTR-084
- [ ] **Discharge.** Triggers: "let's call it off," a release, a "paid in full" check, a substituted agreement, a new party taking over. → R-CTR-085 to R-CTR-088

## 7. Remedies ★★★ (always, for each party)
- [ ] **Expectation damages.** Formula, then numbers. → R-CTR-089 to R-CTR-091; UCC → R-CTR-099 to R-CTR-103
- [ ] **Consequential / incidental damages; foreseeability; certainty.** Triggers: lost profits, lost resale contracts, "Seller knew Buyer needed it by May 1," a new business. → R-CTR-092 to R-CTR-094
- [ ] **Mitigation.** Triggers: the injured party kept working, waited, or refused a substitute. → R-CTR-095
- [ ] **Liquidated damages / limitation of remedies.** Triggers: "$X per day," a forfeited deposit, "repair or replace only." → R-CTR-096, R-CTR-097
- [ ] **Punitive / emotional / fees.** Triggers: a willful breach, distress, a fee clause. → R-CTR-098, R-CTR-117
- [ ] **Reliance / restitution.** Triggers: uncertain profits, a losing contract, an unenforceable contract, part performance. → R-CTR-107, R-CTR-108
- [ ] **Equitable remedies.**
  - Specific performance: land, a unique chattel, an artist's services (denied).
  - Rescission: fraud, mistake.
  - Reformation: a typo, an omitted term.
  - → R-CTR-104 to R-CTR-106
- [ ] **Breaching party's recovery.** Triggers: deposit forfeiture, partial work by the breacher. → R-CTR-109

## 8. Third parties ★
- [ ] **Third-party beneficiary.** Triggers: "pay my debt to C"; a gift to a relative; neighbors or the public benefiting. → R-CTR-110, R-CTR-111
- [ ] **Assignment / delegation.** Triggers: "assigned the contract," a bank financing receivables, a substitute performer, an anti-assignment clause. → R-CTR-112 to R-CTR-116

## 9. Before you finish
- [ ] A conclusion on **every** issue ("likely," "probably").
- [ ] **Both parties'** claims, if the call is two-sided.
- [ ] A California sentence where it matters: § 1671, *PG&E*/*Riverisland*, § 1624, B&P § 16600, *Rattagan*.
- [ ] Tort or PR crossover issues flagged under their own headings.
