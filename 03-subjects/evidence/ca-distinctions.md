# Evidence — California distinctions

**Why this file matters.** Evidence is one of only two subjects (with Civil Procedure) where California essays test **both** a federal code and a California code. The State Bar's Evidence outline asks applicants to know the Federal Rules of Evidence **and** the California Evidence Code and to compare and contrast them [S-083]. The **call names the governing law** and switches between exams: the California Evidence Code governed Feb 2016, Jul 2018, Feb 2020, Feb 2024, the Feb 2025 retest and Jul 2026; the FRE governed Feb 2012, Jul 2014, Feb 2017, Feb 2019 and Feb 2023 [VERIFY V-071]. The **MBE** tests only the FRE "as currently in effect" [S-040].

**Decision rule for the essay.**
1. Read the call. "Under the Federal Rules of Evidence" → write the FRE. "Under the California Evidence Code" / "California law" → write the CEC. "Compare" or no law named → write both where they differ.
2. Under the CEC, ask at once: **is this a criminal case?** If so, Truth-in-Evidence (Prop 8) applies (section 2 below).
3. Label each rule by source: "Under FRE 803(4)…; the CEC has no general equivalent, so…".
4. Where the rules are the same, write one rule and cite both ("FRE 403; § 352").

**Where the systems agree (one rule, two citations).** Relevance definition (FRE 401 / § 210, except the "disputed" fact point); limiting instructions (105 / § 355); personal knowledge (602 / § 702); leading questions (611(c) / § 767); impeachment by bias and capacity; excited utterance / spontaneous statement (803(2) / § 1240); state of mind (803(3) / § 1250); recorded recollection (803(5) / § 1237); former testimony against the same party (804(b)(1) / § 1291); coconspirator statements (801(d)(2)(E) / § 1223 — same elements, but a different foundation standard, see below); habit (406 / § 1105); liability insurance (411 / § 1155); attorney-client privilege elements; psychotherapist-patient privilege; authentication standard (901(a) / § 1400); the Confrontation Clause (federal constitutional law in both).

## Proposition 8 / Right to Truth-in-Evidence in criminal cases and its exceptions

**Text and effect.** Proposition 8 (June 1982) added the "Right to Truth-in-Evidence" to the California Constitution (originally art. I, § 28(d); now § 28(f)(2)): except as provided by a statute enacted by a **two-thirds vote** of each house, **relevant evidence shall not be excluded in any criminal proceeding**, including pretrial and post-conviction motions and hearings and juvenile criminal proceedings; it does not affect any existing statutory rule of evidence relating to **privilege or hearsay**, or Evidence Code **§§ 352, 782 or 1103**, or the rights of the press [S-145]. Section 28(f)(4) adds that a prior **felony conviction** shall be used **without limitation** for impeachment or sentence enhancement [S-145].

| Rule | Survives Prop 8 in a criminal case? | Why | What to write |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hearsay rule and exceptions | **Yes** | Expressly preserved | Do the full hearsay analysis |
| Privileges | **Yes** | Expressly preserved | Do the full privilege analysis |
| § 352 balancing | **Yes** | Expressly preserved | Always balance |
| § 782 (sexual conduct to attack credibility) and § 1103 (victim character; rape shield) | **Yes** | Expressly preserved; AB 2556 extends them [VERIFY V-030] | Apply as written |
| § 1101 (character to prove conduct) | **Yes** | Re-enacted in 1986 by a two-thirds vote (*People v. Ewoldt* (1994) 7 Cal.4th 380) [S-505] | Apply § 1101 and §§ 1108–1109 |
| § 787 (no specific-act impeachment) | **No** | Not preserved | Moral-turpitude conduct admissible, subject to § 352 (*Wheeler*) [S-502] |
| § 788 limits on convictions | **Displaced** by § 28(f)(4) | "Without limitation", but due process limits to moral turpitude (*Castro*) | Moral turpitude + § 352 |
| Exclusion of illegally seized evidence beyond federal law | **No** | California excludes only where the **federal** Constitution requires (*In re Lance W.* (1985) 37 Cal.3d 873) | Use the federal Fourth Amendment analysis (see `03-subjects/criminal-law-procedure/ca-distinctions.md`) |
| Secondary evidence rule, §§ 1108–1109, § 352.2 and other later statutes | Depends on enactment by two-thirds vote | Check each statute [VERIFY V-412] | Note the issue in one line |
| Federal constitutional rules (Confrontation, Miranda, due process) | **Always** | Supremacy Clause | Analyze independently |

**Civil cases.** Prop 8 does not apply. The ordinary Evidence Code rules govern (e.g., § 787 bars specific-act impeachment; § 788 permits felony convictions, subject to § 352).

**Essay paragraph (write this).** "Because this is a criminal proceeding in California, the Truth-in-Evidence provision of Proposition 8 (Cal. Const. art. I, § 28(f)(2)) makes all relevant evidence admissible, subject to exceptions for the hearsay rule, privileges, §§ 352, 782 and 1103, and later statutes passed by a two-thirds vote. [Apply.]"

## Discretionary exclusion: California balancing vs. FRE 403

| Point | FRE 403 | CEC § 352 |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Probative value **substantially outweighed** | Probative value **substantially outweighed** |
| Dangers | Unfair prejudice; confusing issues; misleading the jury; undue delay; wasting time; needlessly cumulative | Undue consumption of time; substantial danger of undue prejudice; confusing issues; misleading the jury |
| Role in criminal cases | One of many exclusionary rules | Survives Prop 8; the main tool for excluding relevant evidence [S-145] |
| Special statutes | 609(a)(1)(B) (reverse for defendant), 609(b), 412(b)(2), 703, 107 | § 352.2 creative expression (presumed minimal literal-truth value; consider racial bias; hearing outside jury) [S-116]; §§ 1108, 1109 expressly subject to § 352 |
| Stipulations | *Old Chief*: must accept a stipulation to felon status | § 210 "disputed fact" can make evidence on a conceded issue irrelevant |

**What to write.** "Under FRE 403 / § 352, the court may exclude evidence whose probative value is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair prejudice. Here, [probative value], but [danger]. On balance, …" The analysis is the same; in a California criminal case add that § 352 survives Truth-in-Evidence. If the evidence is a defendant's rap lyrics, poems or other creative work, apply § 352.2.

## Hearsay definitions and exceptions: California vs. federal differences

| Privilege | Federal (FRE 501, common law) | California (statute) |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Reason and experience; state law for state-law claims in civil cases | Statute only (§ 911); applies in all proceedings (§ 910) |
| Lawyer-client | Upjohn; crime-fraud; survives death (Swidler) | §§ 950–962; Chadbourne; § 956 crime-fraud; § 956.5 death/bodily harm; holder must exist [VERIFY V-414]; no in camera review (§ 915; *Costco*) [S-517] |

| Physician-patient | None | §§ 990–1007; not in criminal cases (§ 998); patient-litigant (§ 996) |

| Clergy-penitent | Recognized | §§ 1030–1034 (penitent and clergy both hold) |
| Counselors (sexual assault, DV) | Not generally | §§ 1035.8, 1037.5 |
| Journalist | No grand-jury privilege (Branzburg) | Shield law: immunity from contempt (Cal. Const. art. I, § 2(b); § 1070) |

**What to write.** "Under the CEC, privileges are statutory (§ 911). [Name the privilege and section.] The holder is …; the communication was confidential because …; it was made for … ; no exception applies because …; the privilege was/was not waived (§ 912)." In a PR crossover, add the broader duty of confidentiality (Bus. & Prof. Code § 6068(e)(1); CRPC 1.6).

## Lay and expert opinion: California vs. federal standards

| Point | FRE | CEC |
|---|---|---|
| Lay opinion | 701: perception, helpful, not specialized | § 800: perception, helpful |
| Expert qualification | 702 (any of five sources) | § 720 (special knowledge, skill, experience, training or education) |
| Proper subject | Helpful (702(a)) | Sufficiently beyond common experience that the opinion would assist (§ 801(a)) |
| Reliability | Daubert/Joiner/Kumho; 702 (2023): each element by a preponderance [S-104] | Sargon gatekeeping (§§ 801(b), 802); Kelly general acceptance for new scientific techniques; Leahy [S-510] |
| Basis | 703: reasonable reliance; inadmissible facts disclosed only if probative value substantially outweighs prejudice | § 801(b): type reasonably relied on; Sanchez bars case-specific hearsay related as true |
| Disclosure of basis | 705: opinion first allowed | § 802: court may require basis first |
| Ultimate issue | 704(a) allowed; 704(b) mental-state bar in criminal cases | § 805 allowed; Pen. Code § 29 mental-state bar [S-1554] |
| Cross with publications | Learned treatise substantive (803(18)) | § 721(b) only to test opinion [S-512] |
| Court-appointed | 706 | § 730 |

**What to write.** "Under § 801(a), expert testimony is proper because [subject] is beyond common experience. Under *Sargon*, the court must ensure the opinion is not speculative and is supported by the material relied on; if the technique is new, *Kelly* requires general acceptance. (Under FRE 702, as amended in 2023, the proponent must show each requirement by a preponderance.)"

## Secondary evidence rule (California) vs. best evidence rule (FRE)

| Point | FRE (best evidence rule) | CEC (secondary evidence rule) |
|---|---|---|
| Basic rule | Original required to prove content (1002) | Content may be proved by original (§ 1520) **or** otherwise admissible secondary evidence (§ 1521) |
| Copies | Duplicates admissible unless genuine question of authenticity or unfairness (1003) | Secondary evidence admissible unless genuine dispute about material terms **and** justice requires exclusion, or admission unfair (§ 1521(a)) [S-507] |
| Oral testimony of content | Allowed once an excuse is shown (1004); no degrees | Barred unless the proponent has no copy and the original was lost/destroyed without fraudulent intent, was not reasonably procurable, or is collateral; or numerous accounts (§ 1523) [S-506] |
| Criminal cases | Same rules | § 1522: additional exclusion where the proponent has the original and did not make it available [VERIFY V-409] |
| Electronic data | Printout is an "original" (1001(d)) | Printouts and digital images presumed accurate (§§ 1552–1553); *Goldsmith* [S-509] |
| Authentication | 901 | Secondary evidence must be authenticated (§ 1401) |
| Summaries | 1006 (2024): whether or not underlying evidence admitted [S-105] | § 1523(d) (numerous accounts); § 352 |

**What to write.** "California replaced the best evidence rule with the secondary evidence rule. The photocopy is admissible under § 1521 unless there is a genuine dispute about its material terms and justice requires exclusion, or admission would be unfair; here … . [If oral testimony:] Oral testimony of the contents is inadmissible under § 1523 unless … ." Under the FRE: "The BER applies because the content of the [writing] is being proved; a duplicate is admissible under 1003 unless … ."
