# Evidence — MBE rules

## 1. NCBE's assumptions
- **Law tested:** "the Federal Rules of Evidence, as currently in effect" [S-040]. No state law, no California rules.
- **No lag policy** for amendments has been found [VERIFY V-027]. For July 2027 the FRE include the Dec. 1, 2023 (106, 615, 702), Dec. 1, 2024 (107, 613(b), 801(d)(2), 804(b)(3), 1006) and Dec. 1, 2026 (801(d)(1)(A)) amendments [S-104] [S-105] [S-101]. Items are written well in advance, so a question may avoid a just-changed point; if it clearly turns on it, apply the current rule.
- **25 scored Evidence questions** among 175 scored (plus pretest items) [S-007].
- **Weighting (NCBE outline) [S-040]:**

| Category | Share | ≈ Scored questions | Highest-yield points |
|---|---|---|---|
| I Presentation of evidence | ~1/4 | ~6 | Impeachment (609, 608(b), 613), refreshing vs. recorded recollection, lay opinion, judicial notice, leading questions |
| II Relevancy and exclusions | ~1/3 | ~8 | 403, character (404(a), 405), 404(b), habit, 413–415, authentication, expert (702–704) |
| III Privileges and policy exclusions + IV Writings | ~1/6 together | ~4 | Remedial measures, settlement, medical expenses, pleas, rape shield, spousal and attorney-client privilege, BER |
| V Hearsay | ~1/4 | ~6 | Definition (not for truth), party admissions, prior statements, 803(1)–(6), 804 with unavailability, Crawford |

## 2. How the MBE asks Evidence questions
- Stem: a fact pattern, often ending "Is the evidence admissible?" or "Should the court sustain the objection?"
- Four answers, each pairing **yes/no** with a **reason**. Usually two answers say "admissible" and two "inadmissible", and each pair has one right and one wrong reason. **Choose the reason first**, then the yes/no.
- The decisive fact is usually the **purpose** (truth vs. notice; propensity vs. intent) or **one element** of an exception (was the declarant under stress? was the record made in the regular course? is the declarant unavailable?).
- "Admissible, because it is relevant" is almost always wrong when a specific exclusionary rule is in play.
- "Inadmissible, because it is hearsay" is wrong when an exception plainly applies or the statement is not offered for its truth.

## 3. Black-letter rules the MBE favors
**Presentation (I)**
1. Anything may refresh memory; the adverse party may introduce it; the proponent may not (612) (R-EVD-012).
2. Recorded recollection is read, not received, unless the adverse party offers it (803(5)) (R-EVD-098).
3. Lay opinion: perception + helpful + not specialized (701) (R-EVD-013).
4. Judicial notice: conclusive in civil cases, permissive in criminal cases (201(f)) (R-EVD-014).
5. Civil presumptions shift the burden of production only (301) (R-EVD-015).
6. Leading questions: allowed on cross and with hostile/adverse witnesses (611(c)) (R-EVD-019).
7. 609: felonies — 403 for witnesses, reverse balancing for the accused; crimen falsi automatic; 10-year rule (R-EVD-025).
8. 608(b): specific acts of untruthfulness on cross only; no extrinsic evidence; take the answer (R-EVD-027).
9. Bias is not collateral; extrinsic evidence allowed, subject to 403 (R-EVD-024).
10. Prior inconsistent statement: no need to show it first, but extrinsic proof only after an opportunity to explain or deny (613, 2024) (R-EVD-023); substantive use under amended 801(d)(1)(A) (R-EVD-085) [S-101].
11. 806: a hearsay declarant may be impeached as if a witness (R-EVD-031).
12. 606(b): jurors may testify only about extraneous information, outside influence, or a form error (R-EVD-011).

**Relevance and character (II)**
13. 403: "substantially outweighed" — admission is favored (R-EVD-003).
14. Character to prove conduct is barred; the accused opens the door to his own and the victim's pertinent trait; prosecution responds; reputation/opinion only (404(a), 405(a)) (R-EVD-035 to R-EVD-039).
15. Homicide first-aggressor: prosecution may show the victim's peacefulness (404(a)(2)(C)) (R-EVD-038).
16. "Have you heard" / "did you know" cross of character witnesses — no extrinsic proof (R-EVD-040).
17. 404(b): MIMIC; Huddleston sufficiency (acquittal no bar — Dowling); 403 (R-EVD-042).
18. 413–415: propensity allowed in sexual-assault and child-molestation cases, civil and criminal (R-EVD-043).
19. Habit = specific, repeated, semi-automatic; routine practice of a business (R-EVD-041).
20. Authentication = sufficient to support a finding; voice ID from any familiarity; handwriting lay familiarity not acquired for litigation (R-EVD-048, R-EVD-049).
21. 702 (2023): each element by a preponderance; judge is gatekeeper (Daubert/Kumho) (R-EVD-054, R-EVD-059).
22. 703: inadmissible bases OK if reasonably relied on; disclosure by proponent only on reverse-403 (R-EVD-056).
23. 704(b): no expert opinion on a criminal defendant's mental state element (R-EVD-058).

**Privileges and policy (III)**
24. 407: post-accident repairs barred for negligence and product defect; admissible for ownership, control, feasibility if disputed, impeachment (R-EVD-074).
25. 408: needs a disputed claim; statements in negotiations barred, including to impeach (R-EVD-075).
26. 409: offer to pay medical expenses barred; accompanying admissions admissible (R-EVD-076).
27. 410: withdrawn guilty pleas, nolo pleas, plea-discussion statements to a prosecutor barred (R-EVD-077).
28. 411: liability insurance barred for fault; admissible for bias, ownership, control (R-EVD-073).
29. 412: rape shield with three criminal exceptions; reverse balancing in civil cases (R-EVD-045).
30. Spousal privileges: Trammel (witness-spouse holds testimonial privilege in criminal cases); communications privilege survives divorce (R-EVD-068, R-EVD-069).
31. Attorney-client: confidential communication for legal advice; Upjohn; crime-fraud; survives death (R-EVD-063 to R-EVD-065).

**Writings (IV)**
32. BER applies only when proving content; duplicates admissible unless authenticity genuinely questioned or unfair; 1004 excuses (R-EVD-078, R-EVD-079).
33. 1006 summaries need not have the underlying records admitted (2024) (R-EVD-081).
34. 106: completeness covers oral statements and overrides hearsay (2023) (R-EVD-082).

**Hearsay (V)**
35. Not hearsay if offered for notice, effect on listener, verbal acts, or impeachment (R-EVD-084).
36. Party admissions need no personal knowledge or against-interest quality; employee statements within scope during employment (R-EVD-088 to R-EVD-091).
37. Coconspirator: during and in furtherance; statement plus independent evidence (R-EVD-091).
38. PSI vs. excited utterance; state of mind (Hillmon; not memory — Shepard); medical diagnosis includes cause but not fault (R-EVD-093 to R-EVD-097).
39. Business records: regular course, regular practice, near the time, person with knowledge under a duty; Palmer v. Hoffman (R-EVD-099).
40. Public records: no police observations against the accused; factual findings in civil cases (R-EVD-100).
41. Learned treatise: read, not received; needs an expert (R-EVD-101).
42. 804 needs unavailability: former testimony (similar motive), dying declaration (homicide or civil only), against interest (corroboration in criminal cases), forfeiture (R-EVD-102 to R-EVD-106).
43. Crawford: testimonial + no cross + not unavailable-with-prior-cross → barred; primary-purpose test (R-EVD-110, R-EVD-111).

## 4. Recurring fact patterns
| Pattern | Usual answer |
|---|---|
| Statement offered to show the defendant **knew** of a danger | Not hearsay (notice) |
| Bystander yells "Look out, that car's going to hit the boy!" | Excited utterance or PSI |
| Victim tells doctor "I was hit by a truck that ran the light" | "Hit by a truck" in (803(4)); "ran the light" out |
| Police report offered by prosecution | Inadmissible under 803(8)(A)(ii); multiple hearsay |
| Hospital record with the patient's account | Business record + 803(4) for each layer |
| Witness forgets; counsel hands her a memo | Refreshing; memo not evidence; adverse party may introduce |
| Witness forgets even after refreshing; she wrote the memo at the time | Recorded recollection; read, not received |
| Defendant calls character witness for honesty in a fraud case | Allowed; prosecution may ask "have you heard…"; may call rebuttal witnesses |
| Prosecution offers D's prior burglary with identical unusual method | 404(b) identity (signature) |
| Plaintiff offers defendant's post-accident repair | Excluded for negligence; admitted if D disputes feasibility |
| "I'll pay your medical bills; I'm sorry I ran the stop sign" | Offer out (409); admission in (party statement) |
| Prior conviction 12 years old | 609(b) — substantially outweighs + notice |
| Witness's misdemeanor shoplifting | Not 609(a)(1); shoplifting usually not crimen falsi; maybe 608(b) inquiry only if dishonest |
| Wife asked to testify against husband in federal criminal trial | She may refuse or choose to testify (Trammel) |
| Former spouse asked about a confidential statement during marriage | Communications privilege survives divorce |
| Grand jury testimony offered against the defendant at trial | Not former testimony against the defendant (no cross); testimonial |
| Declarant's statement against penal interest implicating D | Only self-inculpatory part; corroboration; Crawford if testimonial |
| Lab certificate without analyst | Crawford / Melendez-Diaz violation |
| Prior consistent statement made after motive to lie arose | Not admissible under 801(d)(1)(B)(i) (Tome) |
| Prior inconsistent unsworn statement to police (after Dec. 1, 2026) | Substantive under amended 801(d)(1)(A), subject to 403 [S-101] |

## 5. Answer-elimination tips
- Distinguish **"not hearsay"** (no exception needed) from **"hearsay, but an exception applies"**; the MBE uses both as reasons.
- For 804 answers, check **unavailability** first; for 803 answers, unavailability is irrelevant.
- For impeachment, identify the **method** before the rule (bias? conviction? specific act?), because extrinsic-evidence permission depends on it.
- For character, identify the **purpose** and **who offered it first**.
- For privilege, identify the **holder** — many wrong answers let the wrong person waive.
- Ignore California law entirely on the MBE. See [`traps.md`](traps.md) for distractor patterns.
