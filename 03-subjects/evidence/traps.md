# Evidence — traps

## A. Hearsay traps
1. **"Hearsay" when it is not offered for truth.** Warnings, notice, threats, contract words, and statements explaining a listener's conduct are not hearsay. Ask "offered to prove what?" first (R-EVD-084).
2. **Party admissions need no personal knowledge and need not be against interest.** Distractors add "because the declarant lacked personal knowledge" — wrong for 801(d)(2) (R-EVD-088).
3. **Party admission vs. statement against interest.** A party's own statement offered against him is 801(d)(2); 804(b)(3) is for **non-parties** and needs **unavailability** (R-EVD-088, R-EVD-104).
4. **804 exceptions without unavailability.** If the declarant is available, former testimony, dying declarations and statements against interest fail (R-EVD-102).
5. **Dying declaration in a non-homicide criminal case (FRE).** Attempted murder or robbery: not allowed under 804(b)(2); allowed in California (§ 1242) only if the declarant died (R-EVD-105).
6. **Medical-diagnosis statements assigning fault.** "The driver ran the red light" is not pertinent to treatment (R-EVD-097). And under the CEC there is **no** general medical-diagnosis exception.
7. **Recorded recollection received as an exhibit.** It is read to the jury; only the adverse party may introduce the document (R-EVD-098). Do not confuse with refreshing, where the document is not evidence at all (R-EVD-012).
8. **Business records with outsiders' statements.** The record layer is fine; the customer's or bystander's statement inside it needs its own exception (R-EVD-092, R-EVD-099).
9. **Police reports against the accused.** Excluded under 803(8)(A)(ii); also often testimonial (R-EVD-100, R-EVD-110).
10. **Prior consistent statements made after the motive arose.** Fail Tome (R-EVD-086).
11. **The old 801(d)(1)(A) answer.** "Admissible only to impeach because not made under oath" was right before Dec. 1, 2026; under amended FRE 801(d)(1)(A), an unsworn inconsistent statement of a testifying witness is substantive [VERIFY V-028] (R-EVD-085).
12. **Coconspirator statements after arrest.** Confessions to police are not "in furtherance" (R-EVD-091).
13. **Statement against interest — collateral parts.** "X and I robbed the bank" — only "I robbed" is against X's interest (*Williamson*; *Leach*) (R-EVD-104).
14. **Hearsay exception ≠ confrontation compliance.** A testimonial statement can fit § 1370 or 804(b)(3) and still be barred by Crawford (R-EVD-110).
15. **CEC labels.** Under the CEC, party admissions are **exceptions** (§ 1220), not "not hearsay". Mislabelling costs credibility on a CEC essay (R-EVD-083).

## B. Character and policy traps
16. **Prosecution opens with the defendant's character.** It cannot; only the defendant opens the door (R-EVD-037).
17. **Specific acts to prove the accused's good character.** Reputation or opinion only (FRE 405(a); § 1102) (R-EVD-039).
18. **Victim's specific acts.** FRE: reputation/opinion only (unless known to the defendant — then offered for fear). CEC § 1103(a): specific acts allowed (R-EVD-038).
19. **Habit vs. character.** "He is a careful driver" is character; "he always stops fully at this crossing" is habit (R-EVD-041).
20. **404(b) labels without reasoning.** Saying "intent" is not enough if the only route to intent is "he did it before, so he is the kind of person who…" (R-EVD-042).
21. **Acquitted conduct under 404(b).** Admissible — the standard is sufficiency for the jury, not proof beyond reasonable doubt (*Huddleston*) (R-EVD-042).
22. **Subsequent repairs offered for feasibility when feasibility is conceded.** Exclude — the exception applies only if feasibility is disputed (R-EVD-074).
23. **Repairs in California strict liability.** § 1151 does not apply (*Ault*) (R-EVD-074).
24. **Offers to pay medical bills with admissions.** FRE 409 excludes only the offer; the admission comes in. Contrast 408, where negotiation statements are excluded too (R-EVD-075, R-EVD-076).
25. **408 without a dispute.** "I owe you $5,000 but can only pay $3,000 now" — no dispute about validity or amount, so 408 does not apply (R-EVD-075).
26. **Plea statements to police.** 410 protects discussions with a **prosecutor** (or someone with authority), not general police interviews (R-EVD-077).
27. **Insurance offered for ownership or bias.** Admissible for those purposes (R-EVD-073).

## C. Impeachment and witness traps
28. **608(b) extrinsic evidence.** You must take the answer — no documents, no other witnesses (R-EVD-027). Bias and contradiction of material facts are different (R-EVD-024, R-EVD-033).
29. **609 reverse balancing applies only to the accused.** Other witnesses get ordinary 403 (R-EVD-025).
30. **609(a)(2) — no discretion.** Crimes of dishonesty must be admitted, even misdemeanors and even for the accused (R-EVD-025).
31. **The 10-year clock** runs from conviction **or release from confinement, whichever is later** (R-EVD-025).
32. **California civil cases have no specific-act impeachment** (§ 787), while California criminal cases allow moral-turpitude conduct (Wheeler) (R-EVD-028).
33. **Character for truthfulness supported before attack.** Not allowed (R-EVD-029).
34. **Leading the friendly witness.** Leading is barred on direct; it is allowed when the witness is hostile or identified with the adverse party (R-EVD-019).
35. **Refreshing in California.** Pre-testimony use triggers mandatory production under § 771 (R-EVD-012).
36. **Lay opinion that needs expertise.** "The fire started in the wiring" from a homeowner — specialized (R-EVD-013).

## D. Expert, privilege and writings traps
37. **"General acceptance is required."** Not under FRE 702/Daubert; yes under Kelly for new scientific techniques in California (R-EVD-059, R-EVD-060).
38. **Expert on the defendant's intent.** Barred by 704(b) / Pen. Code § 29; testimony about what most couriers know is not an opinion about the defendant (R-EVD-058).
39. **Expert as conduit.** Relaying case-specific hearsay as true — Sanchez in California; Smith v. Arizona for confrontation (R-EVD-057).
40. **Wrong privilege holder.** Spousal testimonial privilege (federal) belongs to the **witness-spouse**; attorney-client to the **client**; work product (California) to the **attorney** (R-EVD-063, R-EVD-066, R-EVD-068).
41. **Facts are not privileged.** A client cannot shield a fact by telling it to a lawyer (R-EVD-063).
42. **Physician-patient privilege in federal court or California criminal cases.** None (R-EVD-070).
43. **Best evidence rule applied to everything.** It applies only when proving **content**. A witness who saw the event may testify though it was also recorded (R-EVD-078).
44. **"California best evidence rule".** Repealed — say "secondary evidence rule" (R-EVD-080).
45. **Handwriting familiarity acquired for litigation.** Not allowed for a lay witness; allowed for voice identification (R-EVD-049).
