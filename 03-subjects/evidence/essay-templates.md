# Evidence — essay templates

**How California Evidence essays work.** The call lists items — statements, exhibits, testimony, or numbered points in a transcript — and asks whether each was properly admitted or what objections should have been made. Selected answers go **item by item**, with a heading for each item and, under it, the objections in a fixed order [VERIFY V-070]. The call names the **FRE** or the **California Evidence Code**; read it before anything else ([`ca-distinctions.md`](ca-distinctions.md)).

**Timing (60 minutes).** 10–12 minutes reading and outlining; divide the rest by the number of items, giving more time to the hearsay-heavy items. With 5–6 items, that is about 8–9 minutes each; write tight rules from [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md) and spend words on **facts**.

**Grading reminders** (`00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §§ 4–5): answer the call; one heading per item; rule then facts then a hedged conclusion for every issue; argue both sides where the facts allow.

## Template 1 — The universal item (FRE call)
Use for: Feb 2012 Q3, Jul 2014 Q2, Feb 2017 Q3, Feb 2023 Q5 type calls ("Did the court properly admit…? Answer under the FRE").

```
1. [Name of item, e.g., "Mary's statement to the officer"]

Logical relevance
  Rule (FRE 401–402). Facts: tends to show [fact] → of consequence because [element].
Legal relevance
  Rule (FRE 403). Probative value vs. [specific danger]. Conclude.
[Specific exclusionary rule(s) triggered — only those the facts raise]
  Character (404/405) | Policy (407–411) | Privilege (501) | Opinion (701/702)
Hearsay
  Definition (801(c)). Statement? Out of court? Offered for truth of [what]?
  [If non-truth purpose: name it; limiting instruction.]
  Exclusion: 801(d)(1) / 801(d)(2) — elements applied.
  Exception: 803 / 804 (unavailability first) / 807 — elements applied.
  Multiple hearsay (805): second layer → its own exception.
[Writings: Authentication (901/902) → BER (1002–1004)]
Limiting instruction (105)
Conclusion: The court [properly / improperly] admitted [item] [for what purpose].
```

**Model paragraph (original).** *Hearsay.* Hearsay is an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted (FRE 801(c)). Ann's words, "The SUV is weaving all over the road," were spoken in the car, not at trial, and P offers them to prove the SUV was weaving — hearsay unless an exception applies. Under FRE 803(1), a statement describing an event made while the declarant perceived it is admissible. Ann described the SUV as she watched it, so the statement fits. Alternatively, if her voice was raised and she was frightened, it is an excited utterance (803(2)). The court properly admitted it.

## Template 2 — California criminal item (CEC + U.S. Constitution)
Use for: Jul 2018 Q2 (911 call, witness, printout), Feb 2024 Q4, Jul 2026 Q5.

```
Preliminary: Truth-in-Evidence (one paragraph, at the top of the answer)
  Cal. Const. art. I, § 28(f)(2): relevant evidence not excluded in criminal cases,
  except hearsay, privilege, §§ 352, 782, 1103, and two-thirds statutes (§ 1101 survives — Ewoldt).

1. [Item]
Relevance (§ 210) → § 352 (survives Prop 8)
Character, if raised: § 1101(a)/(b); § 1108 or § 1109 propensity; § 1103 victim
Hearsay: § 1200 definition → CEC exception (e.g., § 1240 spontaneous; § 1370 threats; § 1220 party)
  → each layer (§ 1201)
Confrontation Clause (Sixth Amendment)
  Testimonial? (primary purpose: ongoing emergency vs. past events) → declarant testifies? →
  unavailable + prior cross? → forfeiture (Giles)? → conclusion
Authentication / secondary evidence (if a recording, photo, printout)
  § 1400–1401; §§ 1552–1553 presumptions; § 1521 / § 1523
Limiting instruction (§ 355)
Conclusion
```

**Model paragraph (original).** *Confrontation.* The Sixth Amendment bars testimonial hearsay of a declarant who does not testify unless she is unavailable and the defendant had a prior opportunity to cross-examine her (*Crawford*). Statements are nontestimonial when their primary purpose is to meet an ongoing emergency (*Davis*). Vera called 911 while Deb was still in the apartment and asked for help; the dispatcher's questions aimed at sending officers. The call is likely nontestimonial, so the Confrontation Clause does not bar it even though Vera did not testify.

## Template 3 — Witness and impeachment under California law
Use for: Feb 2020 Q4 (prior forgery conviction), Feb 2025 retest Q1 (witness's prior convictions).

```
[Witness] — testimony
  Competency (§§ 700–701), personal knowledge (§ 702), opinion (§ 800) — only if raised
  Form objections (leading § 767; speculation; narrative) — only if raised

Impeachment of [witness]
  a. Prior felony conviction(s)
     Criminal case: Cal. Const. art. I, § 28(f)(4) + Castro — moral turpitude (least adjudicated elements)
       → apply to each conviction (forgery = dishonesty → MT; simple possession → not MT)
       → § 352 factors: honesty, remoteness, similarity, defendant's decision to testify
     Civil case: § 788 felony + § 352 (Robbins v. Wong)
     Compare FRE 609 in one or two sentences if the call invites it
  b. Misdemeanor conduct (criminal only): Wheeler — conduct, not the conviction record (hearsay)
  c. Specific acts: § 787 (civil) vs. Prop 8 (criminal)
  d. Prior inconsistent statements: § 770 foundation; § 1235 substantive
  e. Bias (§ 780(f)): plea deal, leniency
Rehabilitation: § 790 character; § 791 prior consistent statement timing
Conclusion for each conviction / statement
```

## Template 4 — Writings, recordings and photographs
Use for: Feb 2012 Q3 (photocopy of notes), Jul 2018 Q2 (computer printout), Feb 2023 Q5 (photocopied ad), Feb 2024 Q4 (photograph bearing a signature; ATM records).

```
[Exhibit]
Relevance → 403 / § 352
Authentication
  FRE 901(a) / CEC §§ 1400–1401: sufficient to support a finding it is what proponent claims
  Method: witness with knowledge; distinctive characteristics; handwriting; process/system;
          presumptions §§ 1552–1553 (printouts, digital images); self-authentication (902)
Best evidence / secondary evidence
  FRE: content being proved? → original (1002) / duplicate (1003: genuine question? unfair?) / excuse (1004)
  CEC: § 1521 copy (genuine dispute on material terms + justice; unfair) / § 1523 oral testimony
       § 1522 (criminal, proponent holds original)
Hearsay of the contents
  Machine-generated? → not a statement (Goldsmith)
  Human entries → business record (803(6) / § 1271: who, when, regular course, trustworthiness)
                → recorded recollection (803(5) / § 1237: read, not received)
                → party admission / prior identification (signature on a photo array → § 1238)
  Each layer (805 / § 1201)
Limiting instruction
Conclusion
```

## Template 5 — Opinion, experts and learned treatises
Use for: Feb 2023 Q5 (buyer's value opinion, expert on a painting's age, treatise excerpt, expert's own article).

```
[Witness opinion]
Lay or expert?
  Lay (701 / § 800): perception; helpful; not specialized → owner's value opinion usually allowed
Expert
  Qualification (702 / § 720) — subject-specific
  Helpfulness (702(a) / § 801(a) beyond common experience)
  Reliability
    FRE 702 (2023): proponent shows by a preponderance sufficient facts, reliable method, reliable application;
      Daubert factors; Kumho; Joiner gap
    CEC: Sargon (speculation; support); Kelly (new scientific technique → general acceptance)
  Basis (703 / § 801(b); Sanchez case-specific hearsay)
  Ultimate issue (704 / § 805; 704(b), Pen. Code § 29 in criminal cases)
Treatise / article
  FRE 803(18): established as reliable authority; called to expert's attention or relied on; read not received
  CEC § 721(b): cross-examination only — not substantive
  Expert's own article: party admission if expert is a party? Otherwise hearsay; 803(18) if established;
    prior consistent/inconsistent statement analysis if used to impeach
Conclusion
```

## Template 6 — Privilege (often with Professional Responsibility)
Use for: Jul 2017 Q2 (compel lawyer to testify and produce memo), Feb 2022 Q4 (lawyer's testimony about client's statement and about turning over physical evidence), Feb 2020 Q4 (privilege claim).

```
I. Evidence: [Can the court compel X / was testimony properly admitted?]
  Governing law: FRE 501 (federal common law; state law for state-law civil claims) / CEC § 911
  Attorney-client privilege (§ 954 / Upjohn)
    Holder; lawyer acting as lawyer; communication; confidential (§ 917 presumption); legal advice purpose
    Entity client: Upjohn / Chadbourne
    Exceptions: crime-fraud (§ 956); § 956.5; breach of duty; joint client; deceased client
    Waiver (§ 912 / FRE 502)
    Physical evidence: not privileged; communication about it is
  Work product (FRCP 26(b)(3) / CCP § 2018.030): memo → opinion work product absolute in CA
  Spousal / physician / psychotherapist privileges, if raised
  Conclusion: what must be disclosed, what is protected

II. Professional Responsibility: [ethical violations]
  Duty of confidentiality (CRPC 1.6; Bus. & Prof. Code § 6068(e)) — broader than privilege
  Physical evidence, candor, withdrawal — see 03-subjects/professional-responsibility (CA and ABA)
```

## Template 7 — "What objections should have been made, and how should the court have ruled?"
Use for: Feb 2016 Q5, Jul 2026 Q5 (six numbered trial points under the CEC and the U.S. Constitution).

```
[Numbered point 1] — [what happened, in 5 words]
  Objection(s): name each precisely ("hearsay"; "leading"; "improper character evidence";
                "lack of foundation — authentication"; "Confrontation Clause"; "motion to strike")
  Timing / preservation: timely, specific (§ 353 / FRE 103); offer of proof if evidence excluded (§ 354)
  Analysis: rule → facts → both sides
  Ruling: "The court should have sustained/overruled…"; remedy (strike, limiting instruction, mistrial,
          motion to suppress, motion for severance under Bruton)
[Repeat for each numbered point]
```

Tip: the constitutional points (Confrontation, Miranda, suppression) follow federal law; Truth-in-Evidence explains why California does not add stricter state exclusion (see `03-subjects/criminal-law-procedure/ca-distinctions.md`).

## Template 8 — "Compare" call or no law specified
Use for: Jul 2012 Q3 type calls, and whenever the call says "under California law and the Federal Rules".

```
[Item]
State the rule once where the codes agree ("FRE 403; § 352").
Where they differ, use a two-line split under the same heading:
  Under the FRE: [rule → application → result].
  Under the CEC: [rule → application → result].
Highest-value differences to look for: Prop 8 (criminal); 609 vs. moral turpitude; 608(b) vs. § 787/Wheeler;
  employee admissions; PSI; medical diagnosis; § 1251; business-record opinions; learned treatise;
  § 1230 social interest; § 1242 any case; § 1370; residual exception; 407 vs. § 1151 (Ault);
  §§ 1152, 1160; physician privilege; spousal privilege scope; Kelly/Sargon/Sanchez; secondary evidence rule.
Conclusion under each code.
```
