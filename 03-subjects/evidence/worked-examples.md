# Evidence — worked examples

All fact patterns are original. Each states the governing law, then rule → application → conclusion, with the rule ID from [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md).

## A. Hearsay

**1. The warning sign (FRE).** Shopper P slips on a wet floor in Mart. P offers testimony by stock clerk C that, an hour before the fall, C told the manager, "There's a big puddle in aisle 5."
- *Rule:* A statement offered to show its effect on the listener (notice) is not hearsay (R-EVD-084).
- *Application:* Offered to prove Mart **knew** of the puddle, the statement is relevant whether or not it was true — not hearsay. Offered to prove the puddle **existed**, it is hearsay, but C is an employee speaking about a matter within the scope of his job during employment, so it is a party-opponent statement under 801(d)(2)(D) (R-EVD-090).
- *Conclusion:* Admissible for notice, and for truth as an employee admission. Under the CEC, the notice purpose works; the truth purpose needs § 1222 authority or another exception.

**2. "That truck is flying" (CEC vs. FRE).** Passenger W, calmly looking out the window, says to her husband, "That red truck is going way over the limit." Seconds later the truck hits P's car. W has died; P offers the husband's testimony.
- *Rule:* FRE 803(1) admits a statement describing an event made while the declarant perceived it (R-EVD-093). The CEC has no general present sense impression exception; § 1240 needs stress of excitement (R-EVD-094).
- *Application:* Under the FRE, the statement is contemporaneous and descriptive. Under the CEC, W was calm before the crash and was describing someone else's conduct, so § 1241 does not apply; § 1240 fails unless the speeding itself startled her.
- *Conclusion:* Admissible under the FRE; likely inadmissible under the CEC.

**3. The 911 call (CEC criminal).** In Deb's battery prosecution, the People play a 911 call in which Vic, crying, says, "Deb just hit me with a lamp — she's still here, please hurry." Vic refuses to testify.
- *Rule:* Spontaneous statement (§ 1240) (R-EVD-094); statements to meet an ongoing emergency are nontestimonial (R-EVD-111).
- *Application:* Vic was crying, the attack had just happened, and she described an event she perceived — § 1240 is met. The primary purpose was to get help while Deb remained present, so the call is nontestimonial (*Davis*). Prop 8 does not displace the hearsay analysis but makes relevant evidence otherwise admissible (R-EVD-004). Authenticate the recording by the dispatcher or custodian (R-EVD-051).
- *Conclusion:* Admissible; no Confrontation Clause bar.

**4. The station-house statement (confrontation).** Same case: two hours later, at the police station, Vic gives a signed statement describing the attack. Vic is in another state and cannot be found.
- *Rule:* Crawford bars testimonial statements of an absent declarant without prior cross (R-EVD-110). § 1370 admits an unavailable declarant's statement of physical injury made to police (R-EVD-109).
- *Application:* Even if § 1370's elements are met (unavailable, at or near the time, to law enforcement, in writing), the statement was made to prove past events for prosecution — testimonial. Deb never cross-examined Vic, and nothing suggests Deb procured her absence.
- *Conclusion:* Inadmissible under the Confrontation Clause despite § 1370.

**5. The ER nurse (FRE vs. CEC).** P, injured in a crash, tells the ER nurse, "My neck has hurt since the other car slammed into me; that driver was texting."
- *Rule:* FRE 803(4) covers history, symptoms and general cause, not fault (R-EVD-097). CEC § 1253 is limited to child-abuse victims; § 1250 covers then-existing pain (R-EVD-095).
- *Application:* FRE: neck pain and "slammed into me" are pertinent to treatment; "texting" is fault. CEC: only "my neck hurts" (present pain) comes in under § 1250.
- *Conclusion:* Different results under the two codes; under the FRE, redact the fault statement.

**6. The ATM records (CEC).** In a robbery prosecution, custodian Chet testifies that the bank's ATM logs record each withdrawal automatically at the time it happens and are kept in the regular course of business. The People offer a log showing a withdrawal from V's account five minutes after the robbery.
- *Rule:* § 1271 business records (R-EVD-099); machine-generated data is not hearsay (R-EVD-051).
- *Application:* Chet is a qualified witness who explained the mode of preparation; entries are made at the time; the automatic process indicates trustworthiness. Alternatively, a computer-generated log is not a "statement" by a person at all (*Goldsmith*); the printout is presumed accurate (§ 1552).
- *Conclusion:* Admissible; nontestimonial (routine business record).

**7. The accident report (FRE).** Bus Co.'s safety manager writes a report three days after a crash, at its lawyer's request, concluding the driver was not at fault. Bus Co. offers it.
- *Rule:* 803(6) excludes records whose source or circumstances indicate untrustworthiness (R-EVD-099); *Palmer v. Hoffman*.
- *Application:* Prepared for litigation, self-serving, and offered by its maker.
- *Conclusion:* Inadmissible as a business record.

**8. The co-defendant's notes (CEC).** In D's drug-distribution trial, the People offer notebook pages written by co-defendant X (who has pleaded guilty and refuses to testify even after being held in contempt) listing "D — 2 kilos — paid".
- *Rule:* Declaration against interest, § 1230; only specifically disserving portions (*Leach*; *Duarte*) (R-EVD-104); coconspirator statements, § 1223 (R-EVD-091); unavailability § 240 (R-EVD-102).
- *Application:* The entries expose X to criminal liability, but the parts naming D shift some responsibility to D and are collateral to X's own exposure — arguably inadmissible under § 1230. As coconspirator records kept during and in furtherance of the distribution ring, the entries fit § 1223 if evidence independent of the notebook makes a prima facie showing of the conspiracy and D's participation (R-EVD-091). Business-like ledgers kept in furtherance are nontestimonial. X is "unavailable" for § 1230 because he persists in refusing to testify despite the contempt finding (§ 240(a)(6)); a court order alone would not be enough under the CEC (R-EVD-102).
- *Conclusion:* Likely admissible under § 1223; § 1230 is weaker for the portions naming D.

**9. The employee's apology (FRE vs. CEC).** Delivery driver E, after hitting P, tells P, "I'm so sorry — I ran the stop sign because my boss makes us rush." P sues E's employer only.
- *Rule:* FRE 801(d)(2)(D) (R-EVD-090); CEC §§ 1222, 1224 (R-EVD-090); § 1160 sympathy (R-EVD-076).
- *Application:* FRE: E was an employee speaking about driving within the scope of his job while employed — admissible against the employer. CEC: E had no authority to speak (§ 1222); § 1224 may apply because the employer's liability is derivative of E's negligence (*Labis*) [VERIFY V-401]. "I'm so sorry" is excluded under § 1160; the statement of fault is not excluded by § 1160.
- *Conclusion:* Admissible under the FRE; arguably admissible under § 1224 in California, minus the apology.

**10. Prior inconsistent statement (after Dec. 1, 2026).** Eyewitness W told a detective on the night of the shooting, "D fired the gun." At trial she testifies she did not see who fired. The detective's notes are offered.
- *Rule:* Amended FRE 801(d)(1)(A) and CEC § 1235 make a testifying witness's inconsistent statement substantive (R-EVD-085); foundation under 613(b) / § 770 (R-EVD-023).
- *Application:* W testifies and can be cross-examined about the statement. Ask her about it first (613(b)); in California, it is enough that she has not been excused. Before the amendment, the unsworn statement would have been impeachment only.
- *Conclusion:* Admissible for its truth (subject to 403) [S-101]; the notes are read or the detective testifies.

## B. Impeachment

**11. Prior forgery conviction (CEC criminal).** D, charged with drug sales, testifies. The People offer his eight-year-old felony forgery conviction.
- *Rule:* Felonies involving moral turpitude, subject to § 352 (*Castro*; art. I, § 28(f)(4)) (R-EVD-026).
- *Application:* Forgery involves dishonesty — moral turpitude. It is dissimilar to drug sales (low risk of propensity misuse) and strongly bears on credibility; eight years is not too remote.
- *Conclusion:* Admissible to impeach. (Under FRE 609(a)(2), automatically admissible.)

**12. Misdemeanor shoplifting (FRE vs. CEC).** A prosecution witness has a misdemeanor shoplifting conviction from two years ago.
- *Rule:* FRE 609 admits misdemeanors only if dishonesty is an element (R-EVD-025); CEC criminal: moral-turpitude misdemeanor conduct, § 352 (*Wheeler*) (R-EVD-026, R-EVD-028).
- *Application:* FRE: theft is not ordinarily a crimen falsi offense, so 609(a)(2) does not apply; 608(b) inquiry is possible only if the court finds it probative of untruthfulness. CEC: theft involves moral turpitude, so the conduct may be proved (not by the conviction record, which is hearsay unless an exception applies).
- *Conclusion:* Probably excluded under the FRE; admissible conduct under the CEC subject to § 352.

**13. The lying résumé (FRE).** On cross, counsel asks plaintiff's expert whether he falsely claimed a Ph.D. on his résumé. He denies it. Counsel offers the university registrar's letter.
- *Rule:* 608(b) permits inquiry but bars extrinsic evidence of specific acts to show untruthfulness (R-EVD-027).
- *Application:* The question was proper (probative of untruthfulness). The letter is extrinsic. But the expert's qualifications are a non-collateral matter (the basis for admitting his opinion), so the letter may be admissible as contradiction (R-EVD-033).
- *Conclusion:* Barred as 608(b) character evidence; likely admissible to contradict qualifications.

## C. Relevance, character, policy

**14. Prior burglaries (FRE).** D is charged with burglarizing a jewelry store by cutting through the roof and disabling a specific alarm model. The prosecution offers two prior burglaries with the same roof-cutting and alarm technique.
- *Rule:* 404(b) identity requires a signature (R-EVD-042).
- *Application:* Roof entry plus defeating the same alarm model is distinctive. Huddleston: sufficient proof D committed them. 403: probative of identity, which D disputes; prejudice reduced by an instruction.
- *Conclusion:* Admissible for identity with a limiting instruction.

**15. Self-defense and the victim's violence (CEC).** D, charged with assault, claims V attacked first. D offers testimony that V punched a bartender last year.
- *Rule:* § 1103(a) allows specific instances of the victim's character; § 1103(b) opens the door to D's violence (R-EVD-038).
- *Application:* Admissible under § 1103(a) to show V acted violently. The People may now prove D's violent character, including D's prior fights. (Under the FRE, D could use only reputation or opinion, unless D knew of the punch — then it shows D's reasonable fear.)
- *Conclusion:* Admissible; door opened to § 1103(b) rebuttal.

**16. The post-accident guardrail (FRE vs. CEC).** P is injured when his car hits a defective guardrail made by G Co. After the accident, G Co. redesigns the guardrail. P sues in strict products liability.
- *Rule:* FRE 407 bars repairs to prove product defect (R-EVD-074); CEC § 1151 does not apply to strict liability (*Ault*).
- *Application:* Federal court: excluded unless G Co. disputes feasibility. California court: admissible to prove defect.
- *Conclusion:* Opposite results; a classic compare-and-contrast point.

**17. Settlement talk (FRE).** After a contract dispute arises, D's CEO writes, "We know we shipped late, but we'll pay $20,000 to end this." P offers the letter at trial.
- *Rule:* 408 bars offers and statements in compromise negotiations of a disputed claim (R-EVD-075).
- *Application:* A dispute existed about the amount; the admission of late shipment was made in negotiations.
- *Conclusion:* Inadmissible to prove liability or amount, or to impeach.

## D. Witnesses, experts, privilege, writings

**18. Refreshing with a police report (CEC).** Officer O reviews his report the night before testifying but does not bring it to court. Defense demands it.
- *Rule:* § 771 — production mandatory whether used before or while testifying; otherwise testimony stricken (R-EVD-012).
- *Application:* O used the writing to refresh about the subject of his testimony. The report is in the People's control.
- *Conclusion:* Must be produced, or O's testimony on that subject is stricken. (FRE 612: discretionary for pre-testimony use.)

**19. The value of the painting (FRE).** Buyer B testifies that the painting he bought for $200,000 is "worth maybe $5,000" because he has seen similar prints at flea markets.
- *Rule:* Lay opinion must be rationally based on perception and not specialized (R-EVD-013); owner-value testimony is commonly allowed.
- *Application:* B owns the painting, so an owner's value opinion is usually admitted; but valuing fine art may require specialized knowledge, and his basis (flea-market prints) is thin.
- *Conclusion:* Arguably admissible as owner opinion; weight for the jury. A strong answer argues both sides.

**20. The novel dating technique (CEC vs. FRE).** Expert X dates the painting using a new chemical-aging test she developed last year, not yet peer-reviewed.
- *Rule:* FRE 702 (2023) + Daubert factors (R-EVD-054, R-EVD-059); CEC Kelly general acceptance for new techniques (R-EVD-060).
- *Application:* FRE: untested, no peer review, unknown error rate — the proponent likely cannot show reliability by a preponderance. CEC: a new scientific technique without general acceptance fails Kelly.
- *Conclusion:* Likely excluded under both.

**21. Treatise on cross (FRE vs. CEC).** Plaintiff reads to the defense expert a passage from a standard art-authentication treatise the expert admits is authoritative.
- *Rule:* FRE 803(18) — substantive, read not received (R-EVD-101); CEC § 721(b) — impeachment only.
- *Conclusion:* Substantive evidence under the FRE; limited to testing the opinion under the CEC.

**22. The bloody pants (privilege; CEC).** Client C gives his lawyer L a pair of bloody pants and says, "These are from that night." L turns the pants over to the prosecution. The People call L to testify where he got the pants.
- *Rule:* Lawyer-client privilege protects confidential communications, not physical evidence (R-EVD-063).
- *Application:* C's words "these are from that night" are a confidential communication — privileged. The pants themselves are not privileged and must not be concealed. To avoid revealing the communication, courts typically let the prosecution prove the item's origin without identifying the lawyer as the source (for example, by stipulation) [VERIFY V-409].
- *Conclusion:* The statement is privileged; the pants are admissible with a neutral account of their source. In a PR crossover, discuss L's duties regarding physical evidence and confidentiality.

**23. Divorced spouse (FRE).** In a federal fraud trial of H, the prosecution calls his ex-wife W to testify (a) that she saw H shredding documents, and (b) that H told her privately during the marriage, "I cooked the books."
- *Rule:* Testimonial privilege ends with the marriage; communications privilege survives (R-EVD-068, R-EVD-069).
- *Application:* (a) Observation of conduct, not a communication, and no marriage exists now — no privilege. (b) Confidential marital communication — H may block it.
- *Conclusion:* (a) admissible; (b) privileged.

**24. The photocopied ad (FRE vs. CEC).** Buyer offers a photocopy of a gallery advertisement bearing the gallery's logo, claiming it promised authenticity. The gallery says it never ran the ad.
- *Rule:* Authentication (R-EVD-048, R-EVD-049); FRE 1003 duplicates (R-EVD-079); CEC § 1521 (R-EVD-080).
- *Application:* Authentication: buyer's testimony that he received it, plus the logo and contents (distinctive characteristics), suffices for a jury finding. FRE: a photocopy is a duplicate, but the gallery raises a genuine question about authenticity of the original — the court may require the original or a 1004 excuse. CEC: § 1521 excludes the copy only if there is a genuine dispute about **material terms** and justice requires exclusion; the dispute here is whether the ad existed, which is an authentication question for the jury.
- *Conclusion:* Likely admissible under the CEC; contested under FRE 1003. The ad's statements are not hearsay when offered as the gallery's own representation (verbal act / party statement).

**25. Gruesome photographs (CEC criminal).** In a murder trial where D concedes the victim died from stab wounds and claims self-defense, the People offer twelve close-up autopsy photographs.
- *Rule:* § 352 survives Prop 8 (R-EVD-003, R-EVD-004).
- *Application:* Cause of death is conceded (§ 210 "disputed fact"), but the number and location of wounds may bear on self-defense. Twelve photographs are cumulative and inflammatory.
- *Conclusion:* Court should admit a limited number showing wound patterns and exclude the rest.
