# Torts — issue checklist

Use this in the first 10–15 minutes of an essay. Work through it **for each plaintiff against each defendant**, in this order. Selected answers organize by party pair and then by tort, with a conclusion for each [VERIFY V-070].

## 0. Threshold: build the grid from the call
- [ ] **Who sues whom?** List every Π–Δ pair. Include hidden pairs: a passenger, a bystander, an estate, a spouse (consortium), an employer (vicarious), a hospital (vicarious or nondelegable duty), an insurer.
- [ ] **What law?** General principles unless the call says California [S-077]. Note any statute in the facts.
- [ ] **Is anyone dead?** Then write separate **survival** (estate) and **wrongful death** (heirs) headings (R-TRT-059, R-TRT-060).
- [ ] **Is any Δ a public entity or official?** Then immunity (R-TRT-075). Is any Δ an employer or hirer? Then vicarious liability (R-TRT-063, R-TRT-064).
- [ ] **Is a product involved?** Then the products sequence (§ 4 below).
- [ ] **Is there a remedies call?** Then hand off to the Remedies templates after liability (R-REM-006).

## 1. Intentional torts (trigger: deliberate acts, fights, detentions, threats, entries, taking things)
- [ ] **Battery.** Triggers: a punch, shove, kiss, spit, grabbing an object from a hand, poison, a trap (R-TRT-002).
- [ ] **Assault.** Triggers: a raised fist, a pointed gun, a swing that misses, a lunge, a threat with conduct. Check whether Π was aware (R-TRT-003).
- [ ] **False imprisonment.** Triggers: locked doors, a store detention, threats to stay, withholding a purse, "you're under arrest". Check for escape routes (R-TRT-004).
- [ ] **IIED.** Triggers: humiliation, harassment, threats to a vulnerable person, abuse of position, repeated calls, conduct in front of family (R-TRT-005).
- [ ] **Trespass to land.** Triggers: entering a yard, staying after being told to leave, throwing things over a fence, a structure over the line, dust or particles (R-TRT-006).
- [ ] **Trespass to chattels or conversion.** Triggers: borrowing, using, damaging, keeping or selling another's property (R-TRT-007, R-TRT-008).
- [ ] **Transferred intent.** Triggers: the wrong victim was hit, or the wrong tort occurred (R-TRT-001).
- [ ] **Defenses:**
  - consent (sports, surgery, games) (R-TRT-009);
  - self-defense and defense of others (who started it? proportional?) (R-TRT-010, R-TRT-011);
  - defense of property (deadly force? device?) (R-TRT-012);
  - recapture and the shopkeeper's privilege (R-TRT-013);
  - parental discipline (R-TRT-014);
  - arrest (R-TRT-015);
  - necessity (fire, storm, emergency) (R-TRT-016, R-TRT-017).

## 2. Negligence (trigger: careless conduct, accidents, injuries, failures to warn or protect)
- [ ] **Duty** (R-TRT-018 to R-TRT-024):
  - Is Π foreseeable? (*Palsgraf*)
  - **CA:** § 1714 and *Rowland*.
  - Is this a failure to act? Look for a special relationship (hospital–patient, school–student, business–customer), Δ creating the peril, or an undertaking.
  - Is there a third-party attacker? Look for a special relationship, then the *Brown* two-step.
  - Is there a therapist? Then *Tarasoff* and § 43.92.
  - Is there a rescuer or firefighter?
- [ ] **Land:** entrant status, then duty; attractive nuisance; *Rowland* in California; recreational use; crime on the premises (R-TRT-047 to R-TRT-053).
- [ ] **Standard of care and breach** (R-TRT-025 to R-TRT-036):
  - Is Δ a child, a person with a disability, a professional or a carrier?
  - Is there an emergency?
  - Hand formula.
  - Custom.
  - **Statute in the facts?** Then negligence per se (Evid. Code § 669 in California), plus excuse.
  - **Unknown cause?** Then res ipsa.
  - **Spill or hazard?** Then notice.
  - **Medical procedure?** Then informed consent.
- [ ] **Actual cause:** but-for; substantial factor; multiple Δs; unidentifiable Δ (*Summers*, *Sindell*) (R-TRT-037 to R-TRT-040).
- [ ] **Proximate cause:** type of harm; unforeseeable Π; eggshell plaintiff; intervening acts (doctor, criminal, rescuer, second accident) (R-TRT-044 to R-TRT-046).
- [ ] **Damages:** actual harm. Is it purely emotional (NIED: zone, bystander *Thing*, direct victim *Burgess*)? Purely economic (no duty unless a special relationship)? Fear of disease (*Potter*)? A spouse (consortium)? A death (wrongful death and survival)? (R-TRT-054 to R-TRT-062).
- [ ] **Defenses** (R-TRT-069 to R-TRT-076):
  - Π's own carelessness: pure comparative fault (*Li*), or as stated;
  - release (express assumption of risk);
  - sport or recreation (*Knight* primary or secondary; coaches under *Kahn*);
  - immunity (family, public entity);
  - limitations period.
- [ ] **Apportionment** (R-TRT-041 to R-TRT-043): multiple Δs → joint and several liability; **CA:** Prop 51 for non-economic damages; contribution and indemnity; settlement.

## 3. Liability for others (trigger: employee, agent, contractor, partner, child, driver of another's car)
- [ ] Employee or independent contractor? Scope of employment; frolic or detour; an intentional tort by an employee (R-TRT-063).
- [ ] Independent contractor exceptions: nondelegable duty (hospital food, public premises), inherently dangerous work, the hirer's own negligence, apparent agency (R-TRT-064).
- [ ] Negligent hiring, supervision or entrustment (R-TRT-067).
- [ ] Partners or joint enterprise; vehicle owner; parent; alcohol provider (R-TRT-065, R-TRT-066, R-TRT-068).

## 4. Strict liability and products (trigger: explosives, chemicals, wild or vicious animals, dog bites, any manufactured product)
- [ ] Abnormally dangerous activity: § 520 factors; kind of harm (R-TRT-077).
- [ ] Animals: wild; domestic with known propensity; **CA:** § 3342 for dog bites (R-TRT-078, R-TRT-079).
- [ ] **Products, in this order:**
  1. Strict liability: commercial seller in the chain (manufacturer, retailer, component maker, lessor) (R-TRT-085).
  2. The defect: manufacturing (R-TRT-082) / design, both tests and the *Barker* burden in California (R-TRT-083) / warning, including knowability and the learned intermediary (R-TRT-084).
  3. Causation and damages, including bystanders and the economic loss rule (R-TRT-086).
  4. Negligence (R-TRT-088).
  5. Warranty: express, merchantability, fitness; privity, disclaimer, notice (R-TRT-089).
  6. Misrepresentation: § 402B, fraud (R-TRT-090).
  7. Intentional tort, briefly (R-TRT-091).
  8. Defenses: misuse, alteration, comparative fault, assumption of risk, state of the art, regulatory compliance and preemption (R-TRT-080, R-TRT-087).

## 5. Other torts
- [ ] **Nuisance.** Triggers: noise, smoke, odors, lights, vibrations, a business next to homes, "moved in later". Private (substantial + unreasonable) / public (special injury) / defenses (coming to the nuisance) / remedies (injunction vs. damages) (R-TRT-092 to R-TRT-095).
- [ ] **Defamation.** Triggers: false statements to others (emails, posts, references, news stories) (R-TRT-096 to R-TRT-100):
  1. elements;
  2. libel or slander, and per se;
  3. public or private Π and public or private concern, then the fault and damages grid;
  4. truth or opinion;
  5. privileges (§ 47);
  6. retraction and anti-SLAPP (California).
- [ ] **Privacy.** Triggers: photos used in ads, hidden cameras, revealing medical facts, misleading portrayals (R-TRT-101 to R-TRT-104).
- [ ] **Malicious prosecution or abuse of process.** Triggers: a baseless suit or charge that ended in Π's favor; misuse of a subpoena or attachment (R-TRT-105, R-TRT-106).
- [ ] **Misrepresentation.** Triggers: a sale of a house, business or product with false statements or hidden defects; advisors' reports (R-TRT-107 to R-TRT-109):
  - fraud (scienter);
  - concealment (duty to disclose; *Rattagan* if there is a contract);
  - negligent misrepresentation (§ 552 class).
- [ ] **Interference.** Triggers: a rival poaching a contract partner or customer; false statements to customers (R-TRT-110 to R-TRT-112):
  - contract vs. prospective relationship;
  - privilege or improper means;
  - **CA:** an independently wrongful act.

## 6. Damages and close
- [ ] Compensatory damages: economic and non-economic; collateral source; mitigation (R-TRT-113).
- [ ] Punitive damages: malice, oppression or fraud; clear and convincing evidence in California (R-TRT-114).
- [ ] California limits: Prop 51; MICRA (R-TRT-115); Prop 213 (uninsured drivers).
- [ ] **A conclusion for every issue** ("likely", "probably"), then an overall conclusion for each Π–Δ pair.
