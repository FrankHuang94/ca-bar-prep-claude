# Torts — traps

Each trap names the mistake, the correct rule and the rule ID.

## Intentional torts and privileges
1. **"No injury, so no battery."** Wrong: battery needs only a harmful **or offensive** contact; nominal damages are available (R-TRT-002).
2. **"P was asleep, so no battery."** Battery needs no awareness. Assault does (R-TRT-002, R-TRT-003). Related: **Words alone as assault.** Usually not enough without an act showing imminent intent; future threats never are (R-TRT-003).
3. **False imprisonment with an open door P knew about.** A reasonable, known means of escape defeats confinement (R-TRT-004).
4. **IIED for a single rude insult.** Not outrageous. Look for abuse of power, vulnerability, repetition (R-TRT-005).
5. **"D honestly thought it was his land."** Mistake is no defense to trespass to land or conversion (R-TRT-006, R-TRT-008).
6. **Trespass to chattels without damage.** Intermeddling needs actual harm; brief harmless use is not actionable (R-TRT-007).
7. **Transferred intent applied to IIED or conversion.** It works only among assault, battery, false imprisonment, trespass to land and trespass to chattels (R-TRT-001).
8. **Consent to a fight is "void because illegal."** The majority treats consent to mutual combat as effective; the exception is statutes protecting a class (R-TRT-009).
9. **Deadly force to protect property.** Never privileged, including by spring guns (R-TRT-012). Related: **Reasonable mistake in recapturing chattels or defending property.** Not protected, unlike self-defense and the shopkeeper's privilege (R-TRT-012, R-TRT-013).
10. **Private necessity = no liability.** Incomplete privilege: pay actual damage. Public necessity = no liability (R-TRT-016, R-TRT-017).
11. **Private citizen's arrest on reasonable suspicion of a felony that never happened.** Not privileged; the felony must actually have occurred (R-TRT-015).

## Negligence
12. **Negligence per se = automatic liability.** It establishes duty and breach only; causation and damages remain, and excuse is available (R-TRT-033, R-TRT-034).
13. **Statute aimed at a different class or harm.** No per se effect. Analyze ordinary negligence (R-TRT-033). Related: **Compliance with a statute or custom = no negligence.** Evidence only (R-TRT-032, R-TRT-034).
14. **Mentally ill adult judged by a "reasonable mentally ill person".** No: the ordinary adult standard. Physical disability, by contrast, is taken into account (R-TRT-025). Related: **Child driving a car judged as a child.** The adult standard applies to adult activities (R-TRT-026).
15. **Res ipsa = directed verdict for P.** It is a permissible inference; the jury may still find for D (majority). In California, a presumption affecting production (R-TRT-035).
16. **"D could not foresee the extent of harm."** The extent and manner need not be foreseeable; eggshell plaintiff (R-TRT-044, R-TRT-045).
17. **Treating every intervening act as superseding.** Medical malpractice, rescue, foreseeable negligence and foreseeable crimes are not superseding (R-TRT-046).
18. **Bystander who arrives after the accident.** No recovery under *Thing* or most majority tests (R-TRT-055). Related: **"Close relative" stretched to fiancés or cohabitants in California.** *Elden* excludes them (under review in *Sanchez v. Rojas*) [VERIFY V-604] (R-TRT-055).
19. **Recovering purely economic loss in negligence from a stranger.** Generally no duty (R-TRT-061).
20. **Licensee vs. invitee confusion.** Social guests are licensees (no duty to inspect); customers are invitees (inspect). In California, use *Rowland* after stating the categories (R-TRT-049 to R-TRT-051). Related: **Attractive nuisance applied to a natural pond or obvious danger.** The doctrine covers artificial conditions the child cannot appreciate (R-TRT-048).
21. **Employer liable for every employee tort.** Only within scope; frolic is out; use negligent hiring as the backup theory (R-TRT-063, R-TRT-067). Related: **Hirer always immune for independent contractors.** Not for nondelegable duties, inherently dangerous work or the hirer's own negligence (R-TRT-064).
22. **Parents vicariously liable for children.** Not at common law; only for their own negligence, or by statute (R-TRT-066).

## Defenses and apportionment
23. **Barring a 60%-at-fault plaintiff on the MBE.** The default is pure comparative fault: P recovers 40% (R-TRT-070). Related: **Using last clear chance in a comparative jurisdiction.** It is abolished there (R-TRT-069).
24. **Applying Prop 51 on the MBE.** The MBE default is joint and several liability; Prop 51 is California only, and only for non-economic damages (R-TRT-041, R-TRT-042).
25. **Comparative negligence reducing an intentional tortfeasor's liability.** Generally not (R-TRT-070).
26. **Release covering gross negligence.** Unenforceable in California and most states (R-TRT-071).
27. **California sports injury analyzed only as implied assumption.** Use *Knight*: primary (no duty for inherent risks) vs. secondary (comparative fault); co-participants and coaches need intent or recklessness (R-TRT-073).

## Strict and products liability
28. **Strict liability for an ordinary dog's first bite (MBE).** Not without known propensity; in California, § 3342 changes the result for bites (R-TRT-079).
29. **Contributory negligence as a defense to strict liability.** Failing to discover the danger is no defense; a knowing assumption of risk is (R-TRT-080).
30. **Retailer escapes because it could not detect the defect.** Strict liability applies to all commercial sellers; the retailer seeks indemnity (R-TRT-085).
31. **Foreseeable misuse as a complete defense.** Only unforeseeable misuse defeats liability (R-TRT-087).
32. **Privity required for strict liability.** No; users, consumers and bystanders recover (R-TRT-086). Privity issues belong to warranty (R-TRT-089). Related: **State-of-the-art defense to a manufacturing defect.** Irrelevant; it matters for design and warning (R-TRT-082, R-TRT-087).
33. **Recovering lost profits from a product that broke only itself, in strict liability.** Economic loss goes to warranty (*Seely*) (R-TRT-086).

## Other torts
34. **Truth as a defense to public disclosure.** No: the facts are true by definition (R-TRT-103).
35. **Actual malice = spite.** It means knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard (serious doubts), not ill will (R-TRT-098).
36. **Opinion label = protected.** Not if it implies provably false facts (*Milkovich*) (R-TRT-100).
37. **Slander without proof of special damages.** Required unless per se (R-TRT-097).
38. **Coming to the nuisance as a complete bar.** It is only a factor (R-TRT-094). Related: **Public nuisance suit by a private person with only the public's common harm.** Special injury different in kind is required (R-TRT-093).
39. **Contributory negligence as a defense to fraud.** No; but the reliance must be justifiable (R-TRT-107). It is a defense to negligent misrepresentation (R-TRT-109).
40. **Assuming the economic loss rule bars fraud claims between contracting parties in California.** Not fraud in the inducement, and not concealment during performance under *Rattagan* (R-TRT-108).
