# Torts — essay templates

Seven templates, built from the shapes of past California Torts essays: negligence against several defendants (Jul 2014 Q6, Jul 2017 Q5, Jul 2021 Q3, Jul 2025 Q2); intentional torts (Feb 2016 Q2, Jul 2025 Q2); products (Jul 2023 Q2); nuisance and trespass with remedies (Feb 2018 Q3, Feb 2022 Q3); defamation and IIED (Feb 2020 Q1); fraud with remedies (Feb 2014 Q6, Jul 2024 Q5) [VERIFY V-071]. Never copy State Bar text; these are original skeletons.

**Universal paragraph (IRAC per element).**
- **Rule** in 1–2 sentences, from [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md).
- **"Here,"** then the specific facts, argued both ways where they cut both ways.
- **"Therefore,"** a hedged conclusion ("likely", "probably").

Spend more words on the contested elements and fewer on the obvious ones ("Duty is clear: drivers owe other motorists reasonable care.").

**Timing (60 minutes).** About 15 minutes to read and outline (build the Π × Δ grid first), about 40 minutes to write, 5 minutes to check conclusions.

## Template 1 — Negligence against one or more defendants
Use for: "Under what theories may P sue A, B and C for negligence?"

```
I. P v. A
  A. Negligence
     1. Duty — general duty to foreseeable plaintiffs (Palsgraf, Cardozo); CA: Civ. Code § 1714 / Rowland.
        [Special duty rules if triggered: failure to act → special relationship; third-party crime →
         Brown two-step; land → entrant category, then "California rejects the categories (Rowland)".]
     2. Standard of care — reasonable person (or child / professional / carrier).
     3. Breach — risk-utility; custom; negligence per se (statute: class, harm, excuse; CA Evid. Code § 669);
        res ipsa if the cause is unknown.
     4. Actual cause — but-for / substantial factor.
     5. Proximate cause — foreseeable type of harm; intervening acts (foreseeable? superseding?).
     6. Damages — injuries listed; eggshell plaintiff.
  B. Defenses
     1. Comparative negligence — pure (Li); compute if numbers are given.
     2. Assumption of risk — express / implied; CA Knight primary vs. secondary.
     3. Immunity or statutory limits if a public entity is involved.
  C. Conclusion for P v. A
II. P v. B (vicarious liability)
  A. Respondeat superior — employee? scope (frolic/detour)?
  B. Independent contractor — general rule; exceptions (nondelegable duty, inherently dangerous, own negligence)
  C. Direct negligence — hiring/supervision/entrustment
III. P v. C …
IV. Apportionment (if called) — joint and several (majority); CA Prop 51: economic joint, non-economic several;
    contribution/indemnity; CCP § 877 settlement.
```

## Template 2 — Intentional torts and defenses (fights, detentions, sports)
Use for: "What tort claims do P and D have against each other?"

```
I. P v. D
  A. Battery — act, intent (purpose/substantial certainty; transferred intent), harmful or offensive contact, causation.
  B. Assault — reasonable apprehension of imminent contact; awareness.
  C. False imprisonment — confinement, no reasonable escape, awareness or harm.
  D. IIED — outrageous, intent/recklessness, severe distress (CA Christensen if third party).
  E. Defenses
     1. Consent (express/apparent; sports scope — Hackbart; CA Knight co-participant rule)
     2. Self-defense / defense of others (reasonable belief, proportional force, aggressor)
     3. Shopkeeper / arrest / property privileges as triggered
  F. Damages — nominal, compensatory (eggshell), punitive (malice; CA § 3294; CA needs actual damages)
II. D v. P (counterclaims — the person who struck back may also be liable)
III. Negligence in the alternative (if intent is doubtful)
```

## Template 3 — Products liability (party by party, theory by theory)
Use for: "What products liability claims may P bring against M (and R)?" (Jul 2023 Q2 order)

```
I. P v. Manufacturer
  A. Strict products liability
     1. Commercial seller; product reached P without substantial change
     2. Defect
        a. Manufacturing — departure from intended design
        b. Design — consumer expectations test; risk-benefit test (CA Barker: burden on Δ; Soule limits)
        c. Warning — known/knowable risk (CA Anderson); not obvious; adequacy; heeding
     3. Actual and proximate cause
     4. Damages — personal injury / other property (no pure economic loss — Seely)
  B. Negligence — duty to foreseeable users (MacPherson); breach in design/testing/warning; causation; damages
  C. Warranty
     1. Express (§ 2-313) — label/ads "safe" = affirmation of fact
     2. Implied merchantability (§ 2-314)
     3. Fitness for a particular purpose (§ 2-315) — only if reliance on seller's skill for a particular purpose
     4. Privity, disclaimer, notice (§ 2-607)
  D. Misrepresentation — R2d § 402B; fraud (scienter); negligent misrepresentation
  E. Intentional tort — battery requires substantial certainty; likely fails
  F. Defenses — misuse (foreseeable?), alteration, comparative fault (Daly), assumption of risk, state of the art,
     regulatory compliance/preemption
II. P v. Retailer — strictly liable (Vandermark); warranty; indemnity from manufacturer
III. Damages and punitive damages (§ 3294 if conscious disregard)
```

## Template 4 — Land use: nuisance and trespass (with remedies)
Use for: "What claims may P assert against D, and what remedies?" (Feb 2018 Q3, Feb 2022 Q3)

```
I. Private nuisance
   A. P's possessory interest
   B. Substantial interference (normal person)
   C. Unreasonable (gravity vs. utility; locality; who was first)
   D. Defenses — coming to the nuisance (factor), statutory authorization (CA § 3482), zoning compliance
II. Public nuisance — interference with a public right; P's special injury
III. Trespass to land — intentional entry (mistake no defense); intangibles → nuisance unless physical (CA Wilson)
IV. Remedies (hand off to the Remedies template)
   A. Damages — permanent vs. continuing; diminution in value; nominal for trespass
   B. Injunction — inadequate legal remedy, feasibility, balancing of hardships, public interest, defenses
      (laches, unclean hands); tailored relief (hours limits); Boomer damages in lieu
V. Conclusion
```

## Template 5 — Defamation (plus privacy and IIED)
Use for: "What tort claims does P have against D for D's statements?" (Feb 2015 Q5, Feb 2020 Q1)

```
I. Defamation — statement by statement if several
   A. Defamatory statement of fact (not opinion — Milkovich)
   B. Of and concerning P
   C. Publication (to whom; repeaters)
   D. Type and damages — libel (presumed; CA § 45a on its face) / slander (per se categories, CA § 46; else special)
   E. Constitutional overlay
      1. P's status: public official / public figure (all-purpose, limited) / private
      2. Public or private concern
      3. Fault required (actual malice / negligence) and what damages follow (Gertz; Dun & Bradstreet)
   F. Defenses — truth; absolute privilege; qualified privilege (CA § 47(c)) and abuse; retraction (CA § 48a);
      anti-SLAPP (CCP § 425.16) in one line
II. False light / public disclosure (if the facts are true but private, or misleading)
III. IIED — outrageous conduct; severe distress; First Amendment limits for public figures (Hustler)
IV. Conclusion and damages
```

## Template 6 — Misrepresentation (fraud, concealment, negligent misrepresentation)
Use for: house sales, business sales, advisor reports; often with a remedies call (Feb 2014 Q6, Jul 2024 Q5).

```
I. Intentional misrepresentation
   A. Misrepresentation of material fact (words, conduct, half-truth; opinion exceptions)
   B. Scienter
   C. Intent to induce reliance
   D. Actual and justifiable reliance
   E. Damages — out-of-pocket vs. benefit of the bargain (CA § 3343 for property transactions); punitive
II. Fraudulent concealment / nondisclosure — duty to disclose (fiduciary, half-truth, latent defect);
    CA: Rattagan if the parties had a contract (economic loss rule exception)
III. Negligent misrepresentation — § 552 business context, limited class (CA Bily), comparative fault
IV. Defenses — no justifiable reliance, puffery, economic loss rule, limitations (CCP § 338(d))
V. Remedies — damages; rescission and restitution (hand off to Remedies); constructive trust if property traced
```

## Template 7 — Death cases and damages (survival, wrongful death, consortium)
Use for: any fact pattern with a death or a seriously injured spouse.

```
I. Liability (Templates 1–3 as applicable)
II. Survival action — Estate of V v. D (CCP §§ 377.20, 377.30, 377.34)
   A. V's own claim survives
   B. Damages: pre-death medical costs, lost earnings to death, punitive damages; no pain and suffering
      in actions filed from Jan 1, 2026
III. Wrongful death — heirs v. D (CCP § 377.60)
   A. Who may sue (spouse, children, dependents)
   B. Damages: lost support and services, funeral costs, loss of love/companionship; no grief; no punitive
   C. Defenses: V's comparative fault reduces recovery
IV. Loss of consortium — spouse of injured P (not cohabitant — Elden; not child — Borer)
V. Limits — Prop 51 (non-economic several); MICRA cap if a health care provider (R-TRT-115)
```
