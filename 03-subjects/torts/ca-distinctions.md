# Torts — California distinctions

**The governing-law rule for Torts essays.** California Torts essays are graded on **general common-law principles** unless the call asks for California law [S-077]. Unlike Evidence or Civil Procedure, no California code is expected as the primary law. So:
1. **State the majority rule first**, in the Restatement or common-law form. That is what the grader looks for.
2. **Add the California rule in one sentence** where it differs and the facts raise the point: "In California, …". Selected answers that did this were rewarded, not penalized [VERIFY V-070].
3. If the call says "under California law", lead with the California rule.

**The MBE** never tests California law. Its defaults are joint and several liability with pure comparative negligence, and wrongful-death and survival actions are available [S-040].

Where the rules are the same (intent, battery, assault, false imprisonment, conversion, the elements of negligence, res ipsa basics, proximate cause, defamation's constitutional rules), write one rule.

## Strict liability, liability for acts of others, and immunities
The State Bar essay outline groups these three topics at the end of its Torts outline [S-092] [VERIFY V-024]. Majority rules are in [`outline.md`](outline.md) (III; II.F; II.G Immunities). California adds the following.

**Strict liability.**
- **Abnormally dangerous activities:** California follows R2d §§ 519–520 (*Luthringer v. Moore* (1948) 31 Cal.2d 489, fumigation). Comparative fault reduces recovery (*Daly v. General Motors Corp.* (1978) 20 Cal.3d 725, by extension).
- **Animals:**
  - The common-law rules govern wild animals and domestic animals other than dog bites.
  - **Dog bites:** strict liability under Civ. Code § 3342, with no need to prove the dog's past viciousness. Exceptions for police and military dogs; comparative fault applies.
  - Kennel and veterinary workers may face primary assumption of risk (*Priebe v. Nelson* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 1112 [VERIFY V-611]).
- **Products:** see the table above (*Greenman*, *Cronin*, *Barker*, *Soule*, *Anderson*).
- **Statutory product rule:** Civ. Code § 1714.45 (no liability for inherently unsafe common consumer products known to be unsafe by ordinary consumers, such as sugar or butter) [VERIFY V-609].

**Liability for acts of others.**

| Issue | Majority | California | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope of employment | Kind of work, time and space, purpose to serve | Engendered by or a foreseeable outgrowth of the employment | *Lisa M.* (1995) 12 Cal.4th 291; *Mary M.* (1991) 54 Cal.3d 202 |
| Hirer's liability to contractor's employees | Peculiar-risk liability in many states | **No** liability (*Privette*), unless the hirer's retained control affirmatively contributed, or a concealed hazard went unwarned | *Privette* (1993) 5 Cal.4th 689; *Hooker* (2002) 27 Cal.4th 198; *Kinsman* (2005) 37 Cal.4th 659 |
| Vehicle owners | No vicarious liability (except family purpose) | Owner liable for a permissive user, up to statutory limits | Veh. Code §§ 17150–17151 [VERIFY V-609] |
| Parents | No vicarious liability | Joint and several liability with a minor for willful misconduct, capped | Civ. Code § 1714.1 [VERIFY V-609] |
| Employer punitive damages | Complicity or managerial-capacity rule | Officer, director or managing agent: advance knowledge and conscious disregard, authorization, or ratification | Civ. Code § 3294(b) [S-451] |
| Alcohol | Dram-shop statutes | Statutory non-liability with narrow exceptions | Civ. Code § 1714(b)–(d) |

**Immunities.**

| Issue | Majority | California | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interspousal | Abolished | Abolished | *Self v. Self* (1962) 58 Cal.2d 683; *Klein v. Klein* (1962) 58 Cal.2d 692 |
| Parent–child | Abolished or limited; often retained for parental discretion | Abolished; reasonable-parent standard | *Gibson* (1971) 3 Cal.3d 914 |
| Charitable | Mostly abolished | Abolished | *Malloy v. Fong* (1951) 37 Cal.2d 356 |
| Public entities | FTCA (federal); state waivers vary; governmental vs. proprietary | Liability only by statute; vicarious liability; dangerous condition of public property; no punitive damages; claim within six months | Gov. Code §§ 815, 815.2, 818, 835, 911.2 [S-719] [S-1658] [S-1660] [VERIFY V-607] |
| Public employees | Qualified immunity for discretionary acts | Immunity for discretionary (basic policy) decisions, not operational acts | Gov. Code § 820.2 [VERIFY V-607]; *Johnson v. State* (1968) 69 Cal.2d 782 |
| Police protection | Public-duty doctrine (no duty to individuals) | No liability for failing to provide or for insufficient police protection | Gov. Code § 845 [VERIFY V-607] |

**What to write.** When Δ is a public hospital or agency (Jul 2021 Q3), add a short "Governmental immunity" heading:
1. Liability exists only by statute (§ 815).
2. The entity is vicariously liable for employees (§ 815.2).
3. The employee is immune only for discretionary policy decisions (§ 820.2), not operational ones.
4. Note the dangerous-condition statute (§ 835) where property is involved.

## Wrongful death and survival in California
California-specific points for the essay outline's wrongful death and survival topic [S-092]:
- **Who sues:** wrongful death belongs to the CCP § 377.60 heirs. Survival belongs to the personal representative or successor in interest (§ 377.30).
- **Damages:**
  - Wrongful death: the heirs' economic loss and loss of the decedent's love, companionship, comfort, care, assistance, protection, affection, society and moral support; no grief or sorrow; generally no punitive damages (narrow felony-homicide exception, Civ. Code § 3294(d)) [VERIFY V-1000].
  - Survival: the decedent's pre-death economic loss plus punitive damages.
  - Pain, suffering and disfigurement are not recoverable in survival actions filed on or after Jan 1, 2026 (§ 377.34; the SB 447 window closed) [S-704] [S-705] [VERIFY V-602].
- **Caps and limits:** in medical negligence cases, separate MICRA caps apply in wrongful-death cases (on the AB 35 schedule, $700,000 in 2027) [S-703]. A plaintiff barred by Prop 213 cannot recover non-economic damages (Civ. Code § 3333.4) [VERIFY V-608].
