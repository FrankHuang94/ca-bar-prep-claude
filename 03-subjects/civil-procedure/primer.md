# Civil Procedure — primer

## 1. What this subject is about
Civil Procedure is the rulebook for a private lawsuit from filing to appeal. In the United States there are **two parallel court systems**: the federal courts (one national system, divided into 94 districts) and the courts of each State (in California, one superior court per county). The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (**FRCP**) govern procedure in federal court; Title 28 of the U.S. Code supplies jurisdiction, venue and removal rules. California's state courts follow the Code of Civil Procedure (**CCP**). The MBE tests only the federal system; California essays test both and ask you to compare them.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: Think of the PRC Civil Procedure Law as a single national code applied by one court hierarchy; the US has two hierarchies (federal and state) side by side, and a case must first find the right one (approx.).

## 2. The map: a lawsuit in seven stages
| Stage | Question the court asks | NCBE category | Key rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Choosing a court | Does this court have power over the **type of case** (SMJ), over the **defendant** (PJ), proper **notice**, and is it the right **location** (venue)? | I | 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331, 1332, 1367, 1391, 1404, 1406, 1441–1447; FRCP 4 |
| 2. Choosing the law | Federal court hearing a state-law claim: which law decides each issue? | II | Erie; Hanna; Rules Enabling Act |
| 3. Framing the case | What must the complaint say? How does the defendant respond? Who can be added? | III.A–D | FRCP 7–15, 11, 13, 14, 18–24 |
| 4. Gathering facts | What must be disclosed and what can be demanded? | III.E | FRCP 26–37, 45 |
| 5. Ending early | Dismissal, default, summary judgment | III.F, V.A | FRCP 12, 41, 55, 56 |
| 6. Trial | Who decides facts (jury or judge)? How are verdicts tested? | IV, V.B–C, VI.A–C | Seventh Amendment; FRCP 38–52, 59, 60 |
| 7. After judgment | What is the judgment's effect on later suits? When can a party appeal? | VI.D, VII | Preclusion; 28 U.S.C. §§ 1291, 1292 |

## 4. How the pieces connect
- **Jurisdiction is layered.** A federal court needs SMJ *and* PJ *and* proper service *and* venue. SMJ protects the federal–state division of power, so the parties cannot waive it. PJ, service and venue protect the defendant, so the defendant can waive them by not objecting promptly (Rule 12(h)(1)).
- **Removal is a defendant's choice of forum.** It works only if the federal court would have had original jurisdiction, and it has strict timing (30 days) and unanimity rules.
- **Erie follows diversity.** Once a state-law claim is in federal court, the court must decide which law governs each issue: state law for substance, federal Rules for procedure.
- **Pleading and joinder build the case.** Each new claim or party must independently satisfy SMJ (often through § 1367), PJ and the joinder Rule.
- **Discovery feeds the dispositive motions.** Summary judgment and JMOL ask the same question at different times: could a reasonable jury find for the non-moving party on this evidence?
- **Judgments have consequences beyond the case.** Claim preclusion stops a party from splitting claims; issue preclusion stops it from relitigating issues it lost.
- **Appeals usually wait.** Federal appellate courts hear appeals from final judgments, with a short list of exceptions.

## 5. The ideas that matter most
1. **SMJ first, always.** On every essay set in federal court, state that federal courts have limited jurisdiction, then test federal question (well-pleaded complaint) and diversity (complete diversity + more than $75,000), then supplemental jurisdiction for the leftover claims (§ 1367(a) and (b)). (R-CIV-001 to R-CIV-012)
2. **Minimum contacts is a three-step test.** Purposeful availment → relatedness ("arise out of or relate to") → fairness. General jurisdiction is limited to where a defendant is "at home." (R-CIV-017 to R-CIV-025)
3. **Waiver rules are the MBE's favourite trap.** PJ, venue and service defenses are lost if left out of the first Rule 12 response; failure to state a claim survives to trial; SMJ is never waived. (R-CIV-047)
4. **Erie is a sorting exercise.** Federal Rule on point? Apply it if valid (Hanna, Shady Grove, Berk v. Choy). Otherwise ask whether ignoring state law would encourage forum shopping (twin aims). (R-CIV-037 to R-CIV-040)
5. **Discovery = relevance + proportionality, minus protections.** Then apply work product, privilege, expert rules and Rule 35. On essays, handle each request separately. (R-CIV-064 to R-CIV-073)
6. **The same "reasonable jury" standard runs through summary judgment and JMOL**, and a renewed JMOL requires a pre-verdict motion. New trials allow the judge to weigh evidence. (R-CIV-085 to R-CIV-090)
7. **Preclusion needs a final judgment on the merits between the same parties.** Federal law uses the transactional test; California uses primary rights. (R-CIV-094 to R-CIV-098, R-CIV-114)
8. **California changes the vocabulary, not the logic.** Demurrers instead of 12(b)(6), cross-complaints instead of counterclaims, disclosures on demand, three-fourths verdicts, enumerated appealable orders. (R-CIV-103 to R-CIV-120)

## 6. Common-law habits this subject requires
- **Deadlines are substantive.** 14, 21, 28, 30 and 90 days each carry legal consequences; learn them with the Rule number.
- **Motions are the unit of analysis.** An essay asks "how should the court rule on the motion?" State the motion, the standard, apply facts, conclude.
- **The jury is a constitutional actor.** Much of the subject protects the jury's fact-finding role from the judge (Seventh Amendment), which has no direct counterpart in PRC practice.
- **Party-driven fact-gathering.** In US discovery the parties, not the court, compel disclosure from each other; the court intervenes only on motion.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC courts investigate and collect evidence to a degree; US courts are passive, and the adversarial system relies on discovery to give each side access to the other's evidence (approx.).
