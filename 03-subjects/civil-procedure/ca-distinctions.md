# Civil Procedure — California distinctions

**Why this file matters.** Unlike most subjects, Civil Procedure essays are written on **both** the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and the California Code of Civil Procedure; the State Bar's scope statement asks applicants to compare and contrast them [S-084]. The MBE tests **only** federal law [S-040]. The recent essays with a visible forum (Jul. 2021, Feb. 2023, Feb. 2026) were set in federal court, so the default is: **answer under the FRCP, then add the California rule where the call or facts invite it.** If the case is in a California superior court, flip the default.

**Decision rule for the essay.**
1. Read the call: does it name a court ("the federal district court", "the superior court") or a body of law?
2. Federal court → FRCP and Title 28 (and Erie for state substantive law). A California comparison sentence earns credit only if invited or clearly relevant.
3. California court → CCP rules below; cite the federal counterpart in one line if the call says "compare".
4. Never mix them silently: label each rule "Under the FRCP…" / "Under the CCP…".

## California court subject-matter jurisdiction (superior courts; limited vs. unlimited civil cases)
- **One trial court:** each county has a superior court of general jurisdiction (Cal. Const. art. VI, § 10). State courts may hear federal claims unless Congress made federal jurisdiction exclusive (patents, copyrights: 28 U.S.C. § 1338(a)).
- **Limited civil case:** amount in controversy **$35,000 or less**, exclusive of attorney's fees, interest and costs (CCP § 85 [S-147]); otherwise unlimited (§ 88).
- **Consequences of "limited":** economic-litigation procedures and restricted discovery (CCP §§ 90–100; § 94: as to each adverse party, 35 combined interrogatories, inspection demands and RFAs, and one deposition [S-1409] [VERIFY V-978]); eight-person juries are constitutionally permitted and used in expedited jury trials, which have been mandatory in many limited civil cases (CCP § 630.01 et seq.) [S-1411] [VERIFY V-153]; appeals to the appellate division of the superior court (§ 904.2); recovery capped at the jurisdictional limit unless reclassified (§ 403.040).
- **Small claims:** up to **$12,500** for natural persons and **$6,250** for others (CCP §§ 116.220, 116.221 [S-270]).
- **Essay sentence:** "Unlike the federal court, which must find a statutory basis for jurisdiction, the superior court has general jurisdiction over this $50,000 claim, which is an unlimited civil case."

## California personal jurisdiction (long-arm statute) and service of process
- **Long-arm:** CCP § 410.10 — "any basis not inconsistent with" the California or U.S. Constitution. The analysis is the same as federal due process (R-CIV-017 to R-CIV-026). California's corporate-registration statute (Corp. Code § 2105) requires consent to service of process, not to general jurisdiction (AM Trust v. UBS AG (9th Cir. 2017) (unpublished)), so Mallory rarely helps a California plaintiff [S-1405].
- **Service methods:**

| Method | CCP § | Key condition |
|---|---|---|
| Personal delivery | 415.10 | Complete on delivery |
| Substituted service | 415.20 | After reasonable diligence to serve personally (for individuals); leave at home with competent household member 18+ or at office with person apparently in charge, then mail; complete 10 days after mailing |
| Notice and acknowledgment by mail | 415.30 | Complete when the acknowledgment is signed and returned; non-returning defendant may be charged the cost of other service |
| Out-of-state mail | 415.40 | First-class mail, return receipt requested; complete 10 days after mailing |
| Publication | 415.50 | Court order; defendant cannot be served by other methods with reasonable diligence |
| Corporations | 416.10 | Designated agent; officers and managers listed in the statute |

- **Timing:** summons must be served within 3 years (CCP § 583.210); the defendant has 30 days to respond.
- **Motion to quash** (CCP § 418.10): lack of PJ or defective service; filed on or before the last day to plead; may be joined with a demurrer or motion to strike without a general appearance; order granting it is appealable (§ 904.1).
- **Federal comparison:** Rule 4(e)(1) lets a federal court in California use these methods; Rule 4(d) waiver is the federal analogue of § 415.30.

## California venue rules
- **General rule** (CCP § 395(a)): county where any defendant resides at commencement. If no defendant resides in California, any county the plaintiff designates (subject to transfer).
- **Personal injury, death, injury to personal property:** also the county where the injury occurred (§ 395(a)).
- **Contracts:** also the county where the contract was entered into or was to be performed (§ 395(a)); consumer-contract special rules exist.
- **Corporations and associations** (§ 395.5): county where the contract is made or performed, where the obligation or liability arises, where the breach occurs, or the principal place of business.
- **Real property** (§ 392): county where the property is situated.
- **Transfer** (§ 397): wrong county; impartial trial impossible; convenience of witnesses and ends of justice; no qualified judge. The defendant moves for transfer for wrong venue at the time of its first response (§ 396b).
- **Forum non conveniens** (§ 410.30; Stangvik): suitable alternative forum, then private and public interests; stay rather than dismiss a California resident's suit.
- **Federal comparison:** federal venue is by district under § 1391; transfer under §§ 1404/1406 moves cases between federal districts, which California courts cannot do with other States.

## California joinder and cross-complaints vs. federal counterclaims and crossclaims
- **Everything is a cross-complaint.** A defendant's claims against the plaintiff, a co-defendant or a new party are all cross-complaints (CCP § 428.10).
- **Compulsory:** a defendant who answers must plead any **related cause of action** (same transaction, occurrence or series) it has against the **plaintiff**, or lose it (CCP §§ 426.10(c), 426.30 [S-257]). Exceptions: court lacked PJ; defendant did not answer (§ 426.30(b)); § 426.40 exceptions. Relief: leave to file an omitted cross-complaint in good faith (§ 426.50).
- **Permissive:** cross-complaints against co-defendants or third persons (the California version of crossclaims and impleader) when they arise from the same transaction or concern the same property (§ 428.10(b)).
- **Timing:** a cross-complaint against the plaintiff is filed with the answer; others before the trial date is set, or later with leave (§ 428.50).
- **Joinder of parties:** permissive joinder (§§ 378, 379), compulsory joinder (§ 389, modeled on Rule 19), intervention (§ 387), interpleader (§ 386), class actions (§ 382, with Rule 23 used as guidance).

| | Federal | California |
|---|---|---|
| Claim v. opposing party, same transaction | Compulsory counterclaim (13(a)) | Compulsory cross-complaint (§ 426.30) |
| Claim v. opposing party, unrelated | Permissive counterclaim (13(b)) | Permissive cross-complaint (§ 428.10(a)) |
| Claim v. co-party | Crossclaim (13(g)), never compulsory | Permissive cross-complaint |
| Claim v. non-party who may be liable to defendant | Impleader (14), derivative liability only | Cross-complaint against new party (§ 428.10(b)) — broader (any claim from the same transaction) |
| Jurisdiction check | SMJ for each claim (§ 1367) | None (general jurisdiction) |

## California discovery differences (including initial disclosures on demand)
- **Scope:** relevant to the **subject matter** and admissible or reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence (CCP § 2017.010) — broader than the federal claim-or-defense + proportionality test.
- **Initial disclosures:** only on **demand** of any party; due within **60 days**; cover witnesses, documents/ESI the party may use, and insurance; exempt actions include unlawful detainer, small claims, Family Code and Probate Code proceedings and actions with § 36 trial preference, and the section does not apply to a party not represented by counsel (CCP § 2016.090 [S-111] [S-113] [S-269] [S-1410] [S-1835]; exact scope wording and full exempt list [VERIFY V-159]); sunset removed in 2025 [S-112] [VERIFY V-029].
- **Limits:** 35 specially prepared interrogatories (§ 2030.030) and 35 RFAs except genuineness of documents (§ 2033.030), unless a declaration for additional discovery is served; Judicial Council form interrogatories outside the limit; depositions generally capped at 7 hours [VERIFY V-157].
- **Physical exam by demand:** one exam of a personal-injury plaintiff, not painful, protracted or intrusive, within 75 miles of the plaintiff's residence (§ 2032.220 [S-265]); **mental exam** by leave for good cause (§§ 2032.310, 2032.320 [S-266]); if the plaintiff stipulates to no unusual emotional distress, only exceptional circumstances justify one.
- **Experts:** simultaneous exchange of expert witness information on demand (§ 2034.210 et seq.).
- **Work product:** CCP § 2018.030 — absolute for attorney impressions, qualified otherwise; belongs to the attorney; witness statements obtained by counsel protected at least qualifiedly (Coito).
- **Privilege objections:** response must give enough factual information to evaluate the claim, including a privilege log if necessary (§ 2031.240).
- **Motions to compel further responses:** within **45 days** after the verified response, with a meet-and-confer declaration (§§ 2016.040, 2030.300, 2031.310).
- **Sanctions:** misuse of discovery (§§ 2023.010, 2023.030 — monetary, issue, evidence, terminating, contempt); **mandatory $1,000** sanction for specified bad-faith conduct (§ 2023.050 [S-111]).
- **Limited civil cases:** reduced discovery — as to each adverse party, any combination of 35 interrogatories, inspection demands and RFAs (no subparts) plus one deposition; more only by stipulation or court order (§§ 94, 95 [S-1409] [VERIFY V-978]).
- **Essay sentence:** "Under the CCP the request would be judged by the broader subject-matter relevance standard, and the injured plaintiff could not resist a single physical examination demanded within 75 miles of her home."

## California summary judgment and summary adjudication
- **Standard:** no triable issue as to any material fact and moving party entitled to judgment as a matter of law (CCP § 437c(c)); court considers all evidence and reasonable inferences but may not grant SJ on inferences contradicted by other inferences or evidence raising a triable issue.
- **Defendant's burden:** show one or more elements cannot be established or a complete defense (§ 437c(p)(2)); may rely on factually devoid discovery responses showing the plaintiff lacks and cannot reasonably obtain evidence (Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield).
- **Procedure:** separate statement of undisputed material facts (the "golden rule": facts not in the separate statement do not exist); **81 days'** notice, opposition **20 days** and reply **11 days** before the hearing, no new evidence in reply (AB 2049, eff. Jan. 1, 2025 [S-114]); hearing at least 30 days before trial.
- **Summary adjudication** (§ 437c(f)(1)): only if it completely disposes of a cause of action, an affirmative defense, a claim for punitive damages, or an issue of duty.
- **Review:** order granting SJ → appeal from the judgment; order denying SJ or summary adjudication → writ petition within **20 days** after service of notice of entry (§ 437c(m)(1) [S-263]).
- **Federal comparison:** Rule 56 (Celotex burden-shifting; any time until 30 days after discovery closes; partial SJ on part of a claim).

## Claim preclusion: California primary rights theory vs. federal transactional test; issue preclusion
- **California claim preclusion:** a cause of action = a **primary right**, the corresponding duty and the breach; one injury, one cause of action, whatever the theory (Slater v. Blackwood [S-272]; Crowley v. Katleman; Mycogen; Boeken [S-273]). Different primary rights from one transaction may be sued separately; in tort, injury to the person and injury to property from one act have traditionally been treated as separate primary rights [VERIFY V-976], but a single breach of contract or warranty gives one cause of action for all resulting damages, personal and property (Holmes v. David H. Bricker, Inc. (1969) 70 Cal.2d 786) [S-274] [S-1404].
- **Federal claim preclusion:** transactional test (Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 24) bars all claims from the same transaction.
- **Finality:** in California a judgment is not final for claim or issue preclusion while an appeal is pending or the time to appeal has not expired (Samara v. Matar [S-267]; Sandoval [S-268]; CCP § 1049); federal majority treats a judgment as final when entered.
- **California issue preclusion:** identical issue, actually litigated, necessarily decided, final and on the merits, against a party or privy (Lucido), plus public-policy check; no mutuality required (Bernhard); only against parties to the first case (DKN Holdings).
- **Which law applies?** A later court applies the rendering court's preclusion law: California state judgment → California law (28 U.S.C. § 1738); federal diversity judgment entered in California → federal common law ordinarily adopting California law (Semtek); federal-question judgment → federal law.

| Scenario | Federal transactional test | California primary rights |
|---|---|---|
| Contract judgment, then tort suit for the same harm | Barred | Barred (same harm, same primary right) |
| Personal-injury judgment, then property-damage suit from the same crash (tort) | Barred | Possibly not barred (traditional separate-primary-rights view) [VERIFY V-976] |
| Warranty judgment for car damage, then personal-injury suit for the same breach | Barred | Barred: one breach of contract, one cause of action (Holmes) [S-1404] |
| Declaratory judgment, then damages for the same breach | Barred (unless reserved) | Barred (Mycogen) |
| First judgment on appeal | Preclusive now (majority) | Not yet final |

## California jury trial right, jury size and verdict rules
- **Source:** Cal. Const. art. I, § 16 — the right is inviolate; in a civil cause **three-fourths** of the jury may render a verdict; **12 jurors** unless the parties agree in open court to fewer [S-260]; the Legislature may provide 8 jurors in limited civil cases, and expedited jury trials use 8 [S-1411] [VERIFY V-153].
- **Scope:** actions triable at common law when the Constitution was adopted (1850); equitable actions (e.g., promissory estoppel — C & K Engineering) carry no jury right; the "gist" of the action controls.
- **Order of trial:** the trial court has discretion over the order of trial and may try equitable issues first, without a jury; if those findings dispose of the legal issues, nothing remains for the jury (Raedeke v. Gibraltar Sav. & Loan Assn. (1974) 10 Cal.3d 665; Hoopes v. Dolan (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 146) [S-1407] — the reverse of Beacon Theatres. If the jury decides a common issue first, the court is bound by that finding when ruling on equitable relief (Hoopes).
- **Waiver:** no pre-dispute contractual jury waivers (Grafton Partners); waiver by statutory methods including failure to demand or to deposit advance jury fees (CCP § 631).
- **Selection:** voir dire; six peremptory challenges per party in a two-party case, challenges allocated by side and divided among the parties on a side in multiparty cases (CCP § 231(c) [S-271] [S-1406]); CCP § 231.7 bias-based challenge procedure applies in civil trials from Jan. 1, 2026 [S-365]; one peremptory challenge to the assigned judge (CCP § 170.6).
- **Federal comparison:** Seventh Amendment (1791), 6–12 jurors, unanimity unless stipulated (Rule 48), three peremptories (§ 1870).

## California post-trial motions and appeals (final judgment rule; writs)
- **Nonsuit** (§ 581c) after plaintiff's opening statement or evidence; **directed verdict** (§ 630) after all evidence; **JNOV** (§ 629) whenever a directed verdict should have been granted "had a previous motion been made" — no prior motion needed.
- **New trial** (§ 657): seven statutory grounds; written specification of grounds and reasons; insufficiency/damages grounds only if the court, weighing the evidence, is convinced the jury clearly should have reached a different verdict; additur or remittitur (§ 662.5).
- **Deadlines:** notice of intention within **15 days** after service of notice of entry of judgment or **180 days** after entry, whichever is earliest (§ 659 [S-261]); the court's power expires **75 days** after the clerk's mailing or a party's service of notice of entry, whichever is earlier (or 75 days after the first notice of intention if no notice was given), and an unruled motion is denied by operation of law (§ 660 [S-262]); the deadlines are jurisdictional.
- **Juror evidence:** Evid. Code § 1150 (overt acts, not mental processes).
- **Appealable orders** (§ 904.1 [S-258]): judgments; orders granting a motion to quash or an FNC stay/dismissal; orders granting a new trial or denying JNOV; injunction orders; anti-SLAPP orders (§ 904.1(a)(13)); orders after an appealable judgment. Orders denying arbitration (§ 1294(a)); class-certification denials (death-knell doctrine, Daar).
- **Time to appeal:** 60 days after service of notice of entry, or 180 days after entry (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 8.104).
- **Writs:** mandate (§ 1085) and prohibition (§ 1102) when no adequate remedy at law — the usual route for discovery orders on privilege and denials of summary judgment (§ 437c(m)(1), 20 days). A trial court may note that an interlocutory ruling presents a controlling question of law suitable for writ review (CCP § 166.1) [VERIFY V-160].
- **Limited civil cases:** appeals to the appellate division (§ 904.2).
- **Federal comparison:** Rules 50, 59, 60; § 1291 finality; § 1292; collateral orders; 30-day appeal deadline (R-CIV-087 to R-CIV-101).

## Arbitration waiver after Quach
- Federal: waiver of the right to arbitrate is judged by ordinary waiver principles, without a prejudice requirement (Morgan v. Sundance (2022)).
- California: *Quach v. California Commerce Club* (2024) adopted the same approach for the California Arbitration Act and abrogated the former prejudice requirement [S-131]; the party asserting waiver must prove it by clear and convincing evidence [VERIFY V-977].
- Write: "Under Quach, D's 13 months of litigation are conduct inconsistent with the right to arbitrate; P need not show prejudice." (R-CIV-120)
