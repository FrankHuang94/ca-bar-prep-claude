# Civil Procedure — essay templates

**Calibrated to the 13 past Civil Procedure essays** (Phase 3 index; issue tags indicative [VERIFY V-071]). Calls are almost always phrased as rulings or motions: "Did the court properly deny…?", "How should the court rule on…?". Organize by the call; inside each call, use the template for that issue type. Every template ends each sub-issue with a conclusion ("likely", "probably"). Rules: [rule-statements.md](rule-statements.md). Grading rubric: `../../00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §4.

**Timing (one hour):** about 15 minutes to read and outline, 40–45 minutes to write. Allocate words by the facts: the issue the facts develop most gets the most space.

---

## Template 1 — Federal subject-matter jurisdiction (tested in 7 of 13 essays)
**Use when:** "Does the federal court have subject-matter jurisdiction?"; "Did the court properly deny the motion to dismiss for lack of SMJ / the motion to remand?"

```
I. Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
   Rule: federal courts are courts of limited jurisdiction; SMJ cannot be waived (R-CIV-001).
   A. Federal Question
      Rule: arising under; well-pleaded complaint (R-CIV-002).
      Application: is P's claim created by federal law? Is the federal issue only a defense?
      Conclusion.
   B. Diversity
      Rule: complete diversity + more than $75,000, measured at filing (R-CIV-004).
      1. Citizenship of each party — one short paragraph per party
         (individual domicile R-CIV-005; corporation R-CIV-006; LLC R-CIV-007; alien R-CIV-005)
      2. Complete diversity? (list the pairs)
      3. Amount in controversy (good faith, legal certainty, aggregation) (R-CIV-008)
      Conclusion.
   C. Supplemental Jurisdiction over the remaining claim(s)
      Rule: § 1367(a) common nucleus; § 1367(b) limits in diversity cases; § 1367(c) discretion (R-CIV-010 to -012).
      Application claim by claim.
      Conclusion.
   D. [If removed] Removal and remand
      Rule: § 1441(a); forum-defendant rule; § 1446 timing and unanimity; § 1447 remand (R-CIV-013 to -015).
      Application; Conclusion.
   [CA comparison, if invited: superior court has general jurisdiction; limited vs. unlimited (R-CIV-103).]
```
**Paragraph pattern (citizenship):** "D Corp. is a citizen of every State where it is incorporated and of the State of its principal place of business, its nerve center (Hertz). D is incorporated in Delaware and its officers direct the company from Phoenix; although most employees work in California, its nerve center is Arizona. D is therefore a citizen of Delaware and Arizona."

## Template 2 — Personal jurisdiction (and service)
**Use when:** "Did the court properly rule on D's motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction?"; "Was D properly served?" — one heading **per defendant**.

```
II. Personal Jurisdiction over [Defendant]
   Rule: statute + due process; CCP § 410.10 → constitutional limit (R-CIV-017).
   A. Traditional bases (presence/tag, domicile, consent, waiver) (R-CIV-018, -019)
   B. General jurisdiction — "at home" (R-CIV-020)
   C. Specific jurisdiction (R-CIV-021 to -025)
      1. Purposeful availment (stream of commerce: both Asahi views; effects test)
      2. Relatedness — arise out of or relate to (Ford; BMS)
      3. Fair play and substantial justice — five factors
   Conclusion for this defendant.
III. Service of Process
   Rule: notice reasonably calculated (Mullane); Rule 4(e)/(h) methods or state-law methods; 90 days (R-CIV-028 to -030).
   Application: who served, how, where, on whom; waiver request?
   Conclusion; note that defects are waived if not raised in the first Rule 12 response (R-CIV-047).
```
**Tip:** argue both sides on purposeful availment — e.g., "D will argue it merely sold to a national distributor; P will respond that D designed the product for, advertised in and staffed a sales office in California."

## Template 3 — Venue, transfer and forum non conveniens
**Use when:** "Was venue proper in the Eastern District of California?"; "Should the court transfer or dismiss?"

```
IV. Venue
   Rule: § 1391(b)(1)–(3) (R-CIV-031); residence rules (R-CIV-032); removed cases → § 1441(a).
   A. Residence of all defendants — same State? which district?
   B. Substantial part of events or property
   C. Fallback (only if no district qualifies)
   Conclusion.
V. Transfer / Dismissal
   Proper venue → § 1404(a) factors, transferor's law follows (R-CIV-033); forum clause → Atlantic Marine (R-CIV-035)
   Improper venue → § 1406(a) dismiss or transfer (R-CIV-034)
   Foreign or state forum → forum non conveniens (R-CIV-036; CA: § 410.30, Stangvik)
   Conclusion.
```

## Template 4 — Erie / choice of law in federal court
**Use when:** "May the federal court apply California law to…?"; a state procedural requirement conflicts with federal practice.

```
VI. Applicable Law (Erie)
   Rule: federal court applies state substantive law and federal procedural law (R-CIV-037).
   Step 1: Federal statute or Rule on point? Sufficiently broad? Valid under the REA? → apply it (Hanna; Shady Grove; Berk v. Choy) (R-CIV-038).
   Step 2: If none — outcome-determinative in a way that encourages forum shopping or inequitable administration? (York; Hanna) (R-CIV-039)
   Step 3: Countervailing federal interests (Byrd); Gasperini balance.
   Step 4: Which State's law? Klaxon (forum's choice-of-law rules); Van Dusen after § 1404 transfer (R-CIV-040).
   Conclusion.
```

## Template 5 — Pleadings, motions to dismiss and joinder
**Use when:** "Is the fraud claim sufficiently pleaded?"; "Did the court properly deny the motion to dismiss for failure to join a party?"

```
VII. Sufficiency of the Complaint
   Rule: Rule 8(a) plausibility (Twombly/Iqbal) (R-CIV-044); Rule 9(b) for fraud (R-CIV-045).
   Application: identify conclusory vs. factual allegations; who/what/when/where/how.
   Conclusion; leave to amend (R-CIV-048).
   [CA: fact pleading; general/special demurrer; anti-SLAPP if speech/petition (R-CIV-106 to -108).]
VIII. Joinder
   A. Is the absent person required? Rule 19(a)(1)(A)/(B) (R-CIV-054)
   B. Is joinder feasible? (service; SMJ)
   C. If not, Rule 19(b) factors (R-CIV-055)
   D. Alternatives: impleader (R-CIV-056), permissive joinder (R-CIV-053), counterclaims/crossclaims (R-CIV-052)
   E. Jurisdiction over any added claim/party (§ 1367(b) check)
   Conclusion.
```

## Template 6 — Discovery: motion to compel (tested in 5 of 13 essays)
**Use when:** "How should the court rule on P's motion to compel…?" — **one heading per requested item**.

```
IX. Motion to Compel — General Standards (state once)
   Meet-and-confer certification (R-CIV-073); scope = relevance + proportionality (R-CIV-064).
   [If in California: CCP § 2017.010 subject-matter scope; 45-day deadline; $1,000 sanction (R-CIV-110, -111).]
   A. Item 1: [e.g., witness statement]
      1. Relevance and proportionality
      2. Privilege? (attorney–client; log) (R-CIV-065)
      3. Work product? anticipation of litigation → substantial need + undue hardship; opinion WP protected (R-CIV-066)
         [CA: CCP § 2018.030; Coito (R-CIV-112)]
      Conclusion.
   B. Item 2: [e.g., expert report]
      Testifying (disclose report; deposition) vs. consulting (exceptional circumstances) (R-CIV-067)
      Conclusion.
   C. Item 3: [e.g., physical or mental examination]
      Rule 35: motion, good cause, condition in controversy (Schlagenhauf) (R-CIV-070)
      [CA: one physical exam by demand; mental exam by leave (R-CIV-110)]
      Conclusion.
   D. Expenses and sanctions
      Rule 37(a)(5) mandatory expenses unless substantially justified; Rule 37(b)/(c)/(e) as relevant (R-CIV-071, -073)
      Conclusion.
```
**Paragraph pattern (work product):** "The statement was taken by D's insurer two days after the accident, when D anticipated suit, so it is work product. P can obtain it only by showing substantial need and inability to obtain the substantial equivalent without undue hardship. Because W has since died, P cannot depose her, and the statement is a contemporaneous account; P likely meets the standard. The adjuster's notes on liability, however, are opinion work product and remain protected."

## Template 7 — Trial and post-trial (jury, JMOL, new trial, Rule 60)
**Use when:** "Which issues are triable to a jury?"; "Was seating the juror error?"; "Was denial of the directed verdict correct?"; "How should the court rule on the motion for new trial?"

```
X. Right to Jury Trial (R-CIV-078 to -080)
   Seventh Amendment: legal vs. equitable, claim by claim; Beacon sequencing; timely demand (Rule 38).
   [CA: art. I, § 16; 1850 test; three-fourths verdict (R-CIV-116).]
XI. Jury Selection (R-CIV-082, -092)
   Challenge for cause (actual/implied bias) → was it erroneously denied? preserved? peremptories exhausted?
   Later-discovered non-disclosure → McDonough test.
XII. Directed Verdict / JMOL (R-CIV-087, -088)
   Standard: reasonable jury, evidence for non-movant, no credibility weighing.
   Marshal the evidence each side presented; conclusion.
   Renewal requires a pre-verdict motion; 28 days.
XIII. New Trial / Relief from Judgment (R-CIV-089, -090)
   Rule 59 grounds (28 days); newly discovered evidence; misconduct of opposing party.
   Rule 60(b)(2)/(3) if beyond 28 days (reasonable time, one-year cap).
   Discovery-violation sanctions (Rule 37(c)(1)) as an alternative.
   [CA: § 657 grounds, 15/180/75 days, JNOV without prior motion (R-CIV-117).]
   Conclusion.
```

## Template 8 — Preclusion and appeals
**Use when:** "Can D use the first judgment to bar the second suit?"; "How should the court of appeals rule?"

```
XIV. Claim Preclusion (R-CIV-094; CA R-CIV-114)
   Which law? rendering court (§ 1738; Semtek) (R-CIV-098)
   1. Valid final judgment on the merits (CA: not final pending appeal — R-CIV-115)
   2. Same claim — transactional test; [CA: primary right]
   3. Same parties or privies (Taylor v. Sturgell) (R-CIV-097)
   Conclusion.
XV. Issue Preclusion (R-CIV-095, -096)
   Same issue; actually litigated and decided; essential; full and fair opportunity; mutuality/Parklane.
   Conclusion.
XVI. Appealability (R-CIV-099 to -102)
   Final judgment? 54(b)? § 1292(a)(1) injunction? § 1292(b)? collateral order? mandamus?
   Timeliness (FRAP 4: 30 days; tolling motions).
   Standard of review for each ruling.
   [CA: § 904.1 list; writs; 60 days (R-CIV-118).]
   Conclusion for each appeal.
```

Under the CCP, [state the California rule — e.g., discovery extends to matters relevant to the subject matter (§ 2017.010), and initial disclosures are owed only on demand (§ 2016.090)]. Therefore [conclusion]."
