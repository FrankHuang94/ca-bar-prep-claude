# Civil Procedure — outline

**Scope and law.** The MBE assumes the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure as currently in effect and the Title 28 sections on jurisdiction, venue and transfer; about two-thirds of the scored questions come from categories I, III and V [S-040]. California essays test the FRCP **and** the California Code of Civil Procedure [S-084]; the recent essays with a visible forum were in federal court (`../../06-past-exams/frequency-analysis.md`). Headings follow NCBE numbering. Each topic cites its rule blocks in [rule-statements.md](rule-statements.md) (R-CIV-###) and ends with **How it is tested**. California points appear as **CA note:** lines; the full comparison is in [ca-distinctions.md](ca-distinctions.md).

**Current-law checkpoints (July 2027).** FRCP 16.1 (MDL) and the 16(b)/26(f) privilege-log amendments took effect Dec. 1, 2025 [S-107]; there were no civil-rule amendments on Dec. 1, 2026 [S-103]; the proposed Dec. 1, 2027 amendments to Rules 7.1, 26, 41, 45 and 81 are **not** testable [S-110]. No amendment to 28 U.S.C. §§ 1332, 1391 or 1441 has been found since 2011 [VERIFY V-027]. Recent Supreme Court decisions: Mallory (2023) and Royal Canin (2025) [VERIFY V-035]; Berk v. Choy [S-250], Coney Island [S-252] and Hain Celestial [S-251] (2026); Chevron v. Plaquemines (2026) [S-254] [VERIFY V-034]; GEO Group v. Menocal (2026) [S-1400].

## I Jurisdiction and venue

A court needs **four things** before it can bind a defendant: subject-matter jurisdiction (power over the type of case), personal jurisdiction (power over this defendant), proper notice and service, and proper venue (the right location within the system). Analyze them in that order. SMJ cannot be waived; PJ, service and venue can.

### I.A Federal subject-matter jurisdiction (federal question, diversity, supplemental, and removal)

**State Bar essay outline.** This section is also the home of the essay outline's "Federal court subject-matter jurisdiction" category (diversity; federal question; supplemental; removal).

#### I.A.1 Limited jurisdiction (R-CIV-001)
- Article III, § 2 lists the outer limits; Congress must grant jurisdiction by statute. Presumption **against** jurisdiction; the proponent bears the burden.
- Never waived, forfeited or created by consent; the court must raise it on its own (FRCP 12(h)(3); Mottley). A final judgment is generally safe from collateral attack if jurisdiction was or could have been litigated (Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 12).
- **CA note:** superior courts have general jurisdiction (Cal. Const. art. VI, § 10); the only "SMJ" questions are exclusive federal jurisdiction and limited/unlimited classification (R-CIV-103).

#### I.A.2 Federal-question jurisdiction — 28 U.S.C. § 1331 (R-CIV-002, R-CIV-003)
- **"Arising under."** Most cases: federal law creates the cause of action (American Well Works).
- **Well-pleaded complaint rule** (Mottley): look only at what the plaintiff must plead. Federal defenses (preemption, First Amendment, federal statute excuse) and federal counterclaims do not count.
- **Declaratory judgments:** ask whether the coercive action the declaratory defendant could bring would arise under federal law (Skelly Oil).
- **Embedded federal issue** (Grable; Gunn): necessarily raised, actually disputed, substantial (to the federal system), and consistent with the federal–state balance. Merrell Dow: no private federal right of action weighs against.
- **Complete preemption** (LMRA § 301, ERISA § 502(a)): the state claim is recharacterized as federal; removable.
- No amount-in-controversy requirement. Most federal claims are **concurrent** (state courts may hear them); patent and copyright are **exclusive** (§ 1338(a)).
- **Federal common law** claims arise under federal law (Illinois v. City of Milwaukee) — see II.B.

#### I.A.3 Diversity jurisdiction — 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a) (R-CIV-004 to R-CIV-008)
**Requirements:** (1) complete diversity and (2) amount in controversy **exceeding** $75,000, exclusive of interest and costs.

| Party | Citizenship | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| U.S. citizen individual | State of domicile (presence + intent to remain indefinitely); one domicile at a time | § 1332(a)(1); Mas v. Perry |
| U.S. citizen domiciled abroad | "Stateless" — destroys diversity | Newman-Green |
| Alien | Alienage under § 1332(a)(2)/(3); green-card holder not diverse from a citizen of the same State of domicile | § 1332(a)(2) |
| Corporation | **Every** State/foreign state of incorporation **+** principal place of business (nerve center) | § 1332(c)(1); Hertz |
| Partnership, LLC, union, association | **Every member**, traced through layers | Carden |
| Estate representative; guardian of minor/incompetent | Citizenship of the decedent/represented person | § 1332(c)(2) |
| Class action (non-CAFA) | Named representatives only | Ben-Hur |
| Traditional trust | Trustee's own citizenship (if trustee controls assets) | Navarro |

- **Complete diversity** (Strawbridge): no plaintiff shares citizenship with any defendant. Alignment follows real interests; the court may realign.
- **Timing:** at filing (Grupo Dataflux); a later move neither creates nor destroys diversity. For removal, diversity must exist at filing and at removal.
- **Cure:** dismissing a dispensable non-diverse party under Rule 21 (Newman-Green) or a cure completed before judgment (Caterpillar v. Lewis) saves the judgment; a court's **erroneous** dismissal of a properly joined non-diverse defendant does **not** — vacate and remand (Hain Celestial (2026) [S-251]).
- **Devices to manufacture or defeat diversity:** collusive assignment (§ 1359) is ignored; fraudulent joinder of a non-diverse defendant with no possible claim is disregarded.
- **Amount in controversy** (R-CIV-008): good-faith demand controls unless legally certain it is ≤ $75,000 (St. Paul Mercury). Exactly $75,000 fails.
  - Aggregation: one plaintiff v. one defendant — aggregate all claims, related or not. Multiple plaintiffs — no aggregation of separate claims (Snyder v. Harris) unless common undivided interest. One plaintiff v. multiple defendants — only if jointly liable.
  - Count punitive damages if recoverable; count attorney's fees only if recoverable by statute or contract; exclude interest and costs.
  - Injunctions: value of the object of the litigation (courts differ on viewpoint; plaintiff's-viewpoint rule is common).
  - Counterclaims do not count toward the plaintiff's amount for removal.
- **Exceptions:** domestic relations (divorce, alimony, custody decrees — Ankenbrandt) and probate (probating a will, administering an estate — Marshall v. Marshall). Tort suits between family members are fine.
- **CAFA** (R-CIV-009): ≥ 100 members, > $5,000,000 aggregate, minimal diversity; home-state and local-controversy exceptions (mandatory at ≥ 2/3 forum citizens; discretionary between 1/3 and 2/3); § 1453 removal by any defendant without consent, no forum-defendant rule, no one-year limit.
- **Statutory interpleader** (§ 1335): minimal diversity between two adverse claimants, $500 (III.D).

#### I.A.4 Supplemental jurisdiction — 28 U.S.C. § 1367 (R-CIV-010 to R-CIV-012)
- **§ 1367(a):** same Article III case or controversy = common nucleus of operative fact (Gibbs). Includes claims involving added parties.
- **§ 1367(b)** — diversity-only cases. No supplemental jurisdiction over:
  - claims **by plaintiffs** against persons made parties under Rules 14, 19, 20 or 24 (Kroger);
  - claims by persons joined as plaintiffs under Rule 19 or intervening as plaintiffs under Rule 24;
  - when inconsistent with § 1332.
- **What (b) allows:** defendants' claims (counterclaims, crossclaims, impleader); third-party defendant's claims against the plaintiff; Rule 20 co-plaintiffs and Rule 23 class members below the amount if one plaintiff satisfies it and diversity is complete (Exxon Mobil).
- **§ 1367(c)** discretion: novel/complex state issue; state claim predominates; all original-jurisdiction claims dismissed; exceptional circumstances. Courts usually decline once federal claims are dismissed early (Cohill).
- **Royal Canin (2025)** [VERIFY V-035]: plaintiff's post-removal amendment eliminating all federal claims divests supplemental jurisdiction → remand state claims.
- **§ 1367(d):** limitations tolled while pending + 30 days (stop-the-clock: Artis (2018)).

| Claim | Federal question case | Diversity-only case |
|---|---|---|
| P's related state claim v. same D | § 1367(a) | Needs own diversity + amount (aggregation helps) |
| D's compulsory counterclaim | Supplemental | Supplemental |
| D's permissive counterclaim | Needs independent basis (traditional view) | Needs independent basis |
| Crossclaim (same T/O) | Supplemental | Supplemental (claim by defendant) |
| D's impleader of T | Supplemental | Supplemental |
| P's claim v. impleaded T | Supplemental | **Barred** by § 1367(b) (Kroger) |
| T's claim v. P (same T/O) | Supplemental | Allowed (not a claim "by plaintiff") |
| Rule 20 co-plaintiff below $75k | Supplemental | Allowed if complete diversity (Exxon Mobil) |
| Rule 24 intervenor-plaintiff | Supplemental | **Barred** |

#### I.A.5 Removal — 28 U.S.C. §§ 1441–1447, 1453 (R-CIV-013 to R-CIV-016)
- **What:** any civil action within original federal jurisdiction (§ 1441(a)), judged by the complaint as it stood at removal.
- **Who:** defendants only — not a plaintiff facing a counterclaim (Shamrock Oil), not a third-party counterclaim defendant (Home Depot (2019)).
- **Where:** the federal district and division embracing the state court; § 1391 does not apply (Polizzi). **CA note:** San Diego County → S.D. Cal.; Los Angeles → C.D. Cal.; San Francisco → N.D. Cal.; Sacramento → E.D. Cal.
- **Forum-defendant rule** (§ 1441(b)(2)): diversity cases only; any properly joined **and served** forum defendant bars removal. Most courts: procedural, waived if not raised within 30 days.
- **§ 1441(c):** federal-question claim + claims outside original/supplemental jurisdiction → remove all, sever and remand the latter; only defendants on the federal claim must consent.
- **Non-removable:** state workers' compensation claims; FELA suits against railroads (§ 1445).
- **Procedure** (§ 1446): notice signed under Rule 11 with short and plain grounds; 30 days from formal service (Murphy Bros.); each defendant has its own 30 days; unanimity of properly joined and served defendants; "other paper" restarts the clock; one-year cap on diversity removals of cases that became removable only later (§ 1446(b)(3), (c)(1)) unless plaintiff's bad faith (deliberately hiding the amount counts); notice to adverse parties and state court; state court proceeds no further.
- **Amount on removal:** sum demanded in good faith; if state practice bars a specific demand, the notice may allege it and the court decides by a preponderance; plausible allegation suffices in the notice (Dart Cherokee).
- **Remand** (§ 1447): procedural defects — motion within 30 days; SMJ — any time before final judgment; fees only if no objectively reasonable basis (Martin v. Franklin Capital); remand orders not reviewable except § 1442/1443 cases (whole order, BP v. Baltimore (2021)) and CAFA by permission; post-removal joinder of a non-diverse defendant → deny or permit and remand (§ 1447(e)).
- **Federal-officer removal** (§ 1442): "acting under" + "for or relating to" (plausible close relationship to federal duties — Chevron v. Plaquemines (2026) [S-254] [VERIFY V-034]) + colorable federal defense (Mesa). No well-pleaded complaint rule; no unanimity.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — the forum-defendant rule, unanimity, the 30-day clock, citizenship of LLCs and corporations, aggregation, § 1367(b). Essays — "Did the court properly deny the motion to remand?" (Feb. 2014), "Does the court have SMJ?" (Feb. 2019, Feb. 2026), diversity with an alien party (Jul. 2012), SMJ plus supplemental (Jul. 2017). SMJ is the single most-tested Civil Procedure essay issue (7 of 13 essays).

### I.B Personal jurisdiction

**State Bar essay outline.** This section covers the essay outline's "Personal Jurisdiction" category (in personam bases; in rem and quasi in rem; minimum contacts).

#### I.B.1 Framework (R-CIV-017)
1. **Statutory authority:** state long-arm statute (borrowed in federal court by Rule 4(k)(1)(A)) or a federal statute/rule (4(k)(1)(B), (C), 4(k)(2)).
2. **Constitutional limit:** Fourteenth Amendment due process (state courts and federal courts borrowing state law); Fifth Amendment for federal statutes with nationwide service.
- **CA note:** CCP § 410.10 reaches "any basis not inconsistent with" the state and federal constitutions — skip directly to due process, but cite § 410.10.

#### I.B.2 Traditional bases (R-CIV-018, R-CIV-019)
- **Presence ("tag")**: personal service while voluntarily in the forum — Burnham (all nine Justices upheld it, but no majority rationale: Scalia's four-Justice plurality relied on tradition; Brennan's four-Justice concurrence in the judgment on fairness). Fraud/force exception; litigation-attendance immunity (common law).
- **Domicile:** Milliken v. Meyer — general jurisdiction over a domiciliary wherever served.
- **Consent:** forum-selection clauses (Burger King; Carnival Cruise), stipulations, appearance; sanction-based finding of jurisdiction (Insurance Corp. of Ireland).
- **Waiver:** omit PJ from first Rule 12 motion or answer → waived (12(h)(1)).
- **Registration consent:** Mallory (2023) [VERIFY V-035] upholds Pennsylvania's express-consent statute; dormant Commerce Clause question open. **CA note:** California's registration scheme (Corp. Code § 2105) requires consent to service of process, not to general jurisdiction (AM Trust v. UBS AG (9th Cir. 2017) (unpublished)) [S-1405].

#### I.B.3 General ("all-purpose") jurisdiction (R-CIV-020)
- "At home": individual — domicile; corporation — incorporation State and principal place of business (Goodyear; Daimler); "exceptional case" otherwise (Perkins).
- Large sales or many employees in a State are **not** enough (Daimler; BNSF v. Tyrrell).
- Subsidiaries' contacts are not automatically attributed to the parent (Daimler rejected agency shortcut).

#### I.B.4 Specific jurisdiction (R-CIV-021 to R-CIV-025)
**Three steps:**
1. **Purposeful availment / direction** (Hanson; World-Wide Volkswagen; Burger King). Defendant's own deliberate contacts; foreseeability alone insufficient; unilateral acts of others don't count (Hanson; Kulko). A single contract can suffice if it has a substantial connection with the forum (McGee); in Burger King a Michigan franchisee's long-term franchise relationship with a Florida franchisor supported Florida jurisdiction, judged by the negotiations, contemplated future consequences, terms and course of dealing.
2. **Relatedness** — "arise out of **or relate to**" (Ford (2021)): causation not required, but the link must be real; BMS (2017): non-resident plaintiffs' claims lacked a forum link.
3. **Fair play and substantial justice** (Burger King factors; Asahi): burden on defendant, forum's interest, plaintiff's interest, efficiency, shared policy interests; defendant must make a "compelling case."
- **Stream of commerce** (R-CIV-024): Asahi split (O'Connor "plus" vs. Brennan "awareness"); Nicastro plurality (targeting) and Breyer (single sale insufficient). Argue both on essays.
- **Effects test** (R-CIV-025): Calder (express aiming + brunt of harm in forum); Keeton (regular circulation suffices); Walden (plaintiff cannot be the only link).
- **Internet:** no Supreme Court rule; apply purposeful-direction principles (targeting forum users, sales to forum, interactive features); Ford reserved the question.
- **Specific jurisdiction checklist for a manufacturer** (Jul. 2015-type facts): where designed, made, sold, marketed, serviced; distributor agreements; forum-specific features; volume; plaintiff's residence and place of injury.

#### I.B.5 In rem and quasi in rem (R-CIV-026)
- **In rem:** rights of everyone in a thing (quiet title, forfeiture). **Quasi in rem type 1:** settle particular persons' interests in forum property. **Type 2:** attach unrelated property to secure a personal claim.
- Shaffer v. Heitner: all bases must meet International Shoe; type 2 survives only with independent minimum contacts. Recovery limited to the property's value. Rule 4(n) allows federal courts to use state seizure procedures where PJ cannot be obtained.

#### I.B.6 Federal reach — Rule 4(k) (R-CIV-027)
- 4(k)(1)(A) borrow state law; (B) 100-mile bulge for Rule 14 and 19 parties; (C) federal statute (e.g., § 2361 interpleader, nationwide service statutes); 4(k)(2) federal claim + no State has general jurisdiction + national contacts.

#### I.B.7 Raising and waiving PJ
- Rule 12(b)(2) motion or answer; waived under 12(h)(1). A defendant may default and collaterally attack the judgment for lack of PJ in the enforcement forum (risk: loses merits defenses). If it appears and loses on PJ, it is bound (Insurance Corp. of Ireland; issue preclusion).
- **CA note:** motion to quash on or before the last day to plead (CCP § 418.10); a general appearance waives; order granting the motion is appealable (R-CIV-104).
- **How it is tested:** MBE — general vs. specific, stream of commerce, tag, consent, the bulge. Essays — Jul. 2015 (doctor and valve manufacturer), Jul. 2016, Feb. 2026: write the full framework with two-sided application for each defendant.

### I.C Service of process and notice

#### I.C.1 Constitutional notice (R-CIV-028)
- Mullane: notice reasonably calculated under all circumstances to apprise; publication insufficient for known addresses. Jones v. Flowers: additional reasonable steps after known failure.

#### I.C.2 Service under Rule 4 (R-CIV-029, R-CIV-030)
- **Summons** (4(a)–(b)): signed and sealed by clerk; served with complaint (4(c)(1)).
- **Server:** any non-party at least 18 (4(c)(2)); marshal on court order or for IFP plaintiffs (4(c)(3)).
- **Individuals in the U.S.** (4(e)): (1) state-law method of the forum State or State where served; (2)(A) personal delivery; (B) dwelling or usual place of abode with person of suitable age and discretion who resides there; (C) authorized agent.
- **Minors and incompetents** (4(g)): state-law methods.
- **Corporations, partnerships, associations** (4(h)): officer, managing or general agent, or agent authorized by appointment or law (plus mailing if the statute requires), or state-law method.
- **Foreign individuals/entities** (4(f), 4(h)(2)): Hague Service Convention or other internationally agreed means; methods reasonably calculated to give notice; court-ordered alternatives not prohibited by international agreement.
- **United States, agencies, officers** (4(i)): serve U.S. Attorney and Attorney General (+ agency).
- **Waiver** (4(d)): request with 30 days (60 abroad) to return; 60/90 days to answer; cost-shifting for domestic defendants without good cause; waiver preserves PJ and venue objections.
- **Time limit** (4(m)): 90 days after filing; dismiss without prejudice or order service; must extend for good cause; does not apply to service abroad.
- **Proof** (4(l)): server's affidavit; failure to prove does not affect validity.
- **Subpoenas** are served under Rule 45, not Rule 4.
- **Challenges:** 12(b)(4) (defect in the summons itself) and 12(b)(5) (defect in delivery); both waivable.
- **CA note:** California methods (personal, substituted with mailing after reasonable diligence, notice and acknowledgment by mail, out-of-state certified mail, publication by court order) and 30 days to respond (R-CIV-104); a federal court in California may use them through Rule 4(e)(1)/(h)(1)(A).
- **How it is tested:** MBE — substituted service at a dwelling, waiver timing, 90-day limit. Essays — Jul. 2016 and Feb. 2026 ("Was Dan properly served?").

### I.D Venue, forum non conveniens, and transfer

**State Bar essay outline.** This section is the home of the essay outline's "Venue (federal venue in diversity and federal-question cases; state-court venue; transfer); Erie doctrine; forum non conveniens" category; the Erie part is taught in II.A and state-court venue in [ca-distinctions.md](ca-distinctions.md).

#### I.D.1 Original venue — § 1391 (R-CIV-031, R-CIV-032)
- Same rules for diversity and federal-question cases (since 2011).
- (b)(1) residence (all defendants reside in the same State → any district where any defendant resides); (b)(2) substantial part of events or omissions, or property; (b)(3) fallback.
- Residence: individuals — domicile; entity defendants — any district where subject to PJ for this action (multi-district State: treat district as a State, § 1391(d)); non-U.S. residents — any district and ignored for (b)(1).
- Special statutes: § 1397 (interpleader), patent venue, etc. Local action doctrine (real-property claims) is largely absorbed by (b)(2).
- **Waivable** (12(b)(3), 12(h)(1)).

#### I.D.2 Transfer within the federal system (R-CIV-033 to R-CIV-035)
| | § 1404(a) | § 1406(a) |
|---|---|---|
| Original venue | Proper | Improper |
| Standard | Convenience of parties and witnesses + interest of justice (private and public factors) | Dismiss, or transfer if in the interest of justice |
| Where to | District where action might have been brought (venue + PJ, Hoffman v. Blaski) or consented by all | District where it could have been brought |
| Law applied | Transferor's law incl. choice of law (Van Dusen; Ferens) | Transferee's own law (majority) |
| PJ needed in transferor? | Yes | No (Goldlawr) |

- **Forum-selection clauses** (Atlantic Marine): enforced by § 1404(a) (federal forum) or FNC (state/foreign forum); plaintiff's choice gets no weight; private interests deemed to favor the chosen forum; transferor's choice-of-law rules do not follow. Validity: Bremen (fraud, overreaching, strong public policy); Carnival Cruise (form clauses enforceable if fundamentally fair).
- **§ 1631:** transfer to cure want of jurisdiction.
- **Removed cases:** venue fixed by § 1441(a); transfer under § 1404 still available.

#### I.D.3 Forum non conveniens (R-CIV-036)
- Federal common-law doctrine for foreign (or state-court) alternative forums: adequate available forum → private factors (Gulf Oil) → public factors → deference to plaintiff's choice (strong for domestic, less for foreign plaintiffs) → less favorable law is not decisive (Piper) → dismissal may be conditioned (consent to jurisdiction, tolling).
- May be decided before jurisdiction (Sinochem).
- **CA note:** CCP § 410.30 and Stangvik — stay usually preferred for California residents; California venue is by **county** (§§ 392, 395, 395.5, 397) — R-CIV-105.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — § 1391(b) choices, entity residence, § 1404 vs. § 1406, Van Dusen/Ferens, Atlantic Marine. Essays — Jul. 2016 and Jul. 2021 (venue in the Eastern District of California), with transfer as the remedy.

## II Law applied by federal courts

### II.A State law in federal court

**Erie roadmap (R-CIV-037 to R-CIV-040):**
1. Is the claim governed by state law (diversity, supplemental, or state law borrowed in a federal case)? If not, federal law applies.
2. **Is there a federal statute on point?** If valid (constitutional), it controls (Stewart v. Ricoh — § 1404(a)).
3. **Is there a Federal Rule on point?** Read it for a "direct collision" — is it sufficiently broad to answer the question (Walker; Burlington Northern)? If yes, apply it if valid under the Rules Enabling Act, § 2072 (really regulates procedure: Sibbach; Hanna; Shady Grove plurality, which disregards the state law's purpose; Justice Stevens's concurrence would ask whether the state rule is bound up with the state-created right). Berk v. Choy (2026) [S-250]: Rule 8 governs what a complaint must contain, so a state affidavit-of-merit requirement does not apply in federal court.
4. **No federal rule (judge-made practice):** modified outcome-determination — would applying federal practice encourage forum shopping or inequitable administration (Hanna's twin aims)? If yes, use state law (York) — unless the state rule is form-and-mode and an important federal interest outweighs it (Byrd: judge/jury allocation). Gasperini: apply state excessiveness standard in the district court; review for abuse of discretion.

**Standard outcomes**

| Issue | Law in diversity | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Elements, defenses, presumptions of state claim | State | Erie |
| Statute of limitations; when "commenced" for SOL; tolling | State | York; Walker; Ragan line |
| Choice-of-law rules | Forum State's (transferor's after § 1404) | Klaxon; Van Dusen; Ferens |
| Burden of proof on state claim | State | Cities Service v. Dunlap |
| Method of service | FRCP 4 | Hanna |
| Pleading standard / affidavit of merit | FRCP 8 | Berk v. Choy [S-250] |
| Class actions | FRCP 23 | Shady Grove |
| Right to jury; judge/jury allocation | Federal | Byrd; Simler |
| JMOL sufficiency standard | Federal (majority) | — |
| Excessive-verdict standard | State standard, applied by trial judge | Gasperini |
| Transfer / forum-selection clauses | § 1404(a) | Stewart; Atlantic Marine |
| Privileges on state claims | State | FRE 501 |
| Preclusive effect of federal diversity judgment | Federal common law adopting forum State's law | Semtek |
| Appellate sanctions for frivolous appeal | FRAP 38 | Burlington Northern |
| California anti-SLAPP | Applied by Ninth Circuit precedent (Newsham; en banc Gopher Media (2025) assumed without deciding); rejected by several other circuits | Newsham; Gopher Media [S-1402] [VERIFY V-975] |

- **Determining state law:** follow the State's highest court; if none, predict (Bosch); certify if the State permits. Intermediate appellate decisions are data, not binding.
- **"Reverse Erie":** state courts hearing federal claims must apply federal rules that are part of the federal right (mention only).
- **How it is tested:** MBE — classic Erie/Hanna/Byrd sorting, Klaxon, Van Dusen. Essays — Jul. 2017 ("may the federal court apply California law to the real-property contract claim?"), Feb. 2013 (limitations).

### II.B Federal common law
- No "federal general common law" (Erie), but specialized federal common law exists where uniquely federal interests are at stake and state law would significantly conflict (R-CIV-041):
  - proprietary interests of the U.S. (Clearfield Trust; Kimbell Foods — may adopt state law as federal rule);
  - government-contractor defense (Boyle);
  - interstate disputes over water and pollution (Hinderlider; Illinois v. City of Milwaukee);
  - foreign relations (act of state — Sabbatino);
  - admiralty and labor contracts under LMRA § 301 (Lincoln Mills);
  - preclusive effect of federal judgments (Semtek; Taylor v. Sturgell).
- Federal common law binds state courts (Supremacy Clause) and supports § 1331.
- Federal courts in federal-question cases may "borrow" state limitations periods when Congress provides none (the four-year federal catch-all, 28 U.S.C. § 1658, applies to statutes enacted after Dec. 1, 1990).
- **How it is tested:** occasional MBE question asking whether federal or state law governs a federal-interest dispute; rare on essays.

## III Pretrial procedures

### III.A Preliminary injunctions and temporary restraining orders
- **Preliminary injunction** (R-CIV-042): notice required (65(a)(1)); Winter factors; findings (52(a)(2)); consolidation with trial (65(a)(2)); many circuits require a stronger showing for mandatory injunctions that change the status quo ante; a temporary loss of income ordinarily is not irreparable harm (*Sampson v. Murray*, 415 U.S. 61 (1974)).
- **TRO** (R-CIV-043): without notice only on specific facts + attorney certification; ≤ 14 days, one extension; motion to dissolve on 2 days' notice; hearing on preliminary injunction at earliest possible time.
- **Security** (65(c)): in an amount the court considers proper to pay costs and damages of a party wrongfully enjoined; the U.S. is exempt.
- **Contents and scope** (65(d)): reasons; specific terms; describe acts restrained in reasonable detail (no reference to the complaint); binds parties, officers, agents, servants, employees, attorneys, and those in active concert with actual notice.
- **Appeal:** § 1292(a)(1) (R-CIV-100); TROs generally not appealable (Sampson v. Murray).
- **Universal injunctions:** Trump v. CASA (2025) limited universal injunctions [VERIFY V-035] — low exam relevance.
- **CA note:** CCP §§ 526–529 (TRO and preliminary injunction; undertaking required); IT Corp. v. County of Imperial balancing; Remedies subject covers the merits.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — TRO duration and notice, Winter factors, who is bound. Essay — Feb. 2014 (reinstatement injunction, then appeal).

### III.B Pleadings and amended and supplemental pleadings

**State Bar essay outline.** This section covers the essay outline's "Pleadings" category; California pleading devices (complaint, special demurrer, motion to strike, anti-SLAPP, judgment on the pleadings, answers, amendment) are compared in [ca-distinctions.md](ca-distinctions.md).

#### III.B.1 Pleadings allowed (Rule 7(a))
Complaint; answer; answer to counterclaim designated as such; answer to crossclaim; third-party complaint; third-party answer; reply to an answer only if ordered. Motions are not pleadings (7(b)).

#### III.B.2 Complaint (R-CIV-044, R-CIV-045)
- 8(a): jurisdiction statement, claim showing entitlement, demand for relief (may be alternative or different types).
- Plausibility (Twombly; Iqbal): two-step; conclusory allegations disregarded; context-specific, uses judicial experience and common sense.
- 8(d): alternative and inconsistent claims allowed; 8(e): construe to do justice.
- Rule 9: capacity need not be alleged (9(a)); **fraud/mistake with particularity** (9(b)); conditions precedent may be alleged generally, denial must be particular (9(c)); special damages specifically stated (9(g)).
- Rule 10: caption, numbered paragraphs, separate counts; exhibits part of pleading.
- **CA note:** fact pleading under CCP § 425.10; fraud must be pleaded with particularity — how, when, where, to whom and by what means (*Lazar v. Superior Court* (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631); no damages amount in personal-injury complaints; Doe defendants (R-CIV-106, R-CIV-107).

#### III.B.3 Responses (R-CIV-046, R-CIV-047)
- **Timing** (12(a)): 21 days after service; 60 days from request if waived (90 abroad); U.S. 60 days; 14 days after ruling on a Rule 12 motion or service of a more definite statement.
- **Answer** (8(b)): admit, deny, or lack knowledge (= denial); deny in part; failure to deny = admission except damages; general denial only if in good faith.
- **Affirmative defenses** (8(c)): accord and satisfaction, arbitration and award, assumption of risk, contributory negligence, duress, estoppel, failure of consideration, fraud, illegality, injury by fellow servant, laches, license, payment, release, res judicata, statute of frauds, statute of limitations, waiver. Omission → generally waived (cure by amendment).
- **Counterclaims and crossclaims** are pleaded in the answer (III.D).
- **Rule 12 motions:** (b)(1)–(7); 12(e); 12(f); 12(c). Consolidation (12(g)(2)) and waiver (12(h)) — R-CIV-047.

| Defense | When waived |
|---|---|
| 12(b)(2) PJ, (3) venue, (4) process, (5) service | If omitted from the first Rule 12 motion, or from the answer (or amendment as of course) if no motion |
| 12(b)(6) failure to state a claim; 12(b)(7) Rule 19; failure to state a legal defense | Not until end of trial (may be raised in any pleading, by 12(c) motion, or at trial) |
| 12(b)(1) SMJ | Never |

#### III.B.4 Amendments (R-CIV-048, R-CIV-049)
- **As of course** (15(a)(1)); **consent or leave** (15(a)(2)) — Foman factors; after scheduling-order deadline add Rule 16(b)(4) good cause; response to amended pleading — later of remaining time or 14 days (15(a)(3)).
- **During and after trial** (15(b)): (1) on objection, court should freely permit if no prejudice; may grant continuance; (2) issues tried by express or implied consent are treated as pleaded; amendment may be made any time, even after judgment.
- **Relation back** (15(c)): statute permits; same conduct, transaction or occurrence; new party — same conduct + notice within the 4(m) period + knew or should have known of mistake (Krupski). Doe substitutions usually not a "mistake" federally.
- **Supplemental pleadings** (15(d)): events after the date of the pleading; on motion and reasonable notice; allowed even if original pleading was defective.
- **CA note:** CCP §§ 472, 473(a); liberal leave after sustained demurrer (Blank v. Kirwan); Doe relation back (§ 474).
- **How it is tested:** MBE — relation back of new defendants, as-of-course timing, waiver of defenses, 9(b). Essays — Jul. 2017 (fraud pleaded with particularity?), Feb. 2013 (dismissal on limitations grounds).

### III.C Rule 11
- **Scope:** every pleading, written motion and other paper; signed by at least one attorney of record (11(a)); unsigned paper stricken unless promptly corrected. Applies to later "advocating" a paper (11(b)). Does not apply to discovery (11(d)) — see Rule 26(g).
- **Certifications** (R-CIV-050): improper purpose; legally warranted (including nonfrivolous arguments to change law); factual support (or specifically identified as likely after discovery); denials warranted.
- **Standard:** objective reasonableness under the circumstances (time available, reliance on client, complexity); "empty head, pure heart" is not a defense.
- **Procedure and sanctions** (R-CIV-051): separate motion; 21-day safe harbor; court-initiated show-cause order (no safe harbor, but no monetary sanction after voluntary dismissal or settlement unless OSC issued earlier); sanctions limited to deterrence; law firm jointly responsible; no monetary sanctions against represented party for 11(b)(2); court must describe the conduct and explain the basis (11(c)(6)).
- **Other sources:** 28 U.S.C. § 1927 (unreasonable and vexatious multiplication of proceedings — attorneys); inherent power (bad faith); Rule 26(g) and 37 (discovery).
- **Review:** abuse of discretion (Cooter & Gell). Voluntary dismissal does not deprive the court of jurisdiction to impose Rule 11 sanctions (Cooter & Gell).
- **CA note:** CCP § 128.7 mirrors Rule 11 with a 21-day safe harbor (R-CIV-119) [S-264].
- **How it is tested:** MBE favorite — safe harbor, sanctions on represented parties, court-initiated sanctions, factual inquiry.

### III.D Joinder of parties and claims (including class actions)

**State Bar essay outline.** This section covers the essay outline's "Multiple parties and claims" category (joinder of claims and parties; counterclaims; crossclaims; interpleader; impleader; class actions). For every joinder question, check (1) the Rule permits it, (2) SMJ for the new claim (§ 1331/§ 1332/§ 1367), (3) PJ over any new party, (4) venue (usually not re-examined for added claims).

#### III.D.1 Joinder of claims (R-CIV-052)
- **Rule 18(a):** any claims against an opposing party; no relatedness; still need SMJ for each.
- **Rule 13(a) compulsory counterclaim:** same transaction or occurrence (logical-relationship test; overlapping evidence; res judicata test); exceptions (pending action; in rem jurisdiction; no pleading served). Failure → barred later (by rule and estoppel).
- **Rule 13(b) permissive counterclaim:** any other claim; needs independent SMJ under the traditional view.
- **Rule 13(g) crossclaim:** against coparty; same transaction or occurrence or property; includes indemnity claims; **never compulsory**.
- **Rule 13(h):** join additional parties to counterclaims/crossclaims under Rules 19 and 20.
- **Consolidation and separate trials** (Rule 42): consolidate actions with common questions; separate trials for convenience, to avoid prejudice, or to expedite (preserving jury right).

#### III.D.2 Joinder of parties
- **Permissive (Rule 20)** — R-CIV-053: same T/O/series + common question. Misjoinder (Rule 21): drop/add/sever, not dismiss.
- **Required (Rule 19)** — R-CIV-054, R-CIV-055: (a) complete relief, impairment, inconsistent obligations; joinder if feasible (service + no SMJ destruction); (b) equity-and-good-conscience factors; 12(b)(7) motion; waivable only at end of trial. Joint tortfeasors are not required (Temple). **Essay:** Feb. 2023 (motion to dismiss for failure to join a party).
- **Impleader (Rule 14)** — R-CIV-056: derivative liability only; 14 days after answer without leave; third-party defendant's defenses and claims; plaintiff's claims against T (need independent SMJ in diversity cases).
- **Intervention (Rule 24)** — R-CIV-057: of right vs. permissive; timeliness; adequacy of representation.
- **Interpleader** — R-CIV-058: statutory (§§ 1335, 1397, 2361) vs. rule (Rule 22); the two-stage process (whether interpleader is proper; then who owns the stake); injunction against other proceedings (§ 2361).

| | Statutory (§ 1335) | Rule 22 |
|---|---|---|
| SMJ | Minimal diversity among claimants; ≥ $500 | Ordinary § 1331/§ 1332 (complete diversity stakeholder v. claimants; > $75,000) |
| PJ / service | Nationwide (§ 2361) | Rule 4(k) |
| Venue | Where any claimant resides (§ 1397) | § 1391 |
| Deposit | Required (or bond) | Not required |

#### III.D.3 Class actions (R-CIV-059 to R-CIV-061)
- **Prerequisites** 23(a): numerosity, commonality (Dukes), typicality, adequacy (Amchem); implicit ascertainability in many circuits.
- **Types** 23(b): (1)(A) incompatible standards; (1)(B) limited fund; (2) injunctive/declaratory (no individualized damages — Dukes); (3) predominance + superiority (Comcast).
- **Certification** 23(c)(1): at an early practicable time; order defines class and claims and appoints class counsel (23(g)); may be altered before final judgment.
- **Notice** 23(c)(2): (b)(3) — best practicable, individual notice to identifiable members (mail, electronic or other means), opt-out right; plaintiff ordinarily pays (Eisen), though the court may direct the defendant to perform tasks it can do more cheaply, such as identifying members (*Oppenheimer Fund, Inc. v. Sanders*, 437 U.S. 340 (1978)). (b)(1)/(b)(2) — discretionary notice.
- **Settlement** 23(e): notice, hearing, fair/reasonable/adequate; objectors; court may allow second opt-out.
- **Appeal** 23(f): permission, petition within 14 days (45 if U.S. party).
- **Jurisdiction:** named reps' citizenship; at least one named plaintiff over $75,000 (Exxon Mobil); CAFA alternative. PJ over absent plaintiffs not required (Shutts); standing for every member to recover damages (TransUnion).
- **Binding effect:** adequate representation required (Hansberry); a (b)(3) judgment binds members to whom the 23(c)(2) notice was directed and who did not opt out (23(c)(3)(B)) — actual receipt is not the test.
- **Derivative actions** (Rule 23.1): verified complaint; contemporaneous ownership; demand on board or futility — see Business Associations.
- **CA note:** CCP § 382; denial of certification appealable under death-knell doctrine (Daar); cross-complaints replace counterclaims/crossclaims/impleader; compulsory cross-complaint (CCP § 426.30) — R-CIV-109.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — compulsory counterclaims, crossclaims not compulsory, impleader limited to derivative liability, § 1367(b) combinations, Rule 19 joint tortfeasors, interpleader jurisdiction, 23(b)(3) notice. Essay — Feb. 2023 (Rule 19).

### III.E Discovery (including e-discovery), disclosure, and sanctions

Discovery is the second most-tested essay topic (Feb. 2015, Jul. 2019, Jul. 2021, Feb. 2023, Feb. 2026). A typical call: "How should the court rule on the motion to compel?" Organize item by item: (1) procedural prerequisites (meet and confer, timing), (2) scope (relevance + proportionality), (3) privilege / work product / expert protections, (4) the specific device's limits, (5) sanctions or expenses.

#### III.E.1 Required disclosures (R-CIV-062, R-CIV-063)
- **Initial** (26(a)(1)): witnesses, documents/ESI, damages computation, insurance — "may use to support its claims or defenses"; within 14 days after the 26(f) conference; exempt proceedings (26(a)(1)(B)).
- **Experts** (26(a)(2)): identity; written report for retained experts (opinions + basis, facts/data considered, exhibits, qualifications + 10-year publications, 4-year testimony list, compensation); summary for non-retained experts; 90 days before trial; rebuttal 30 days.
- **Pretrial** (26(a)(3)): witnesses, deposition designations, exhibits; 30 days before trial; objections within 14 days or waived (except FRE 402/403).
- **Supplementation** (26(e)): timely, when materially incomplete or incorrect; expert reports and deposition testimony included.
- **Sanction:** exclusion unless substantially justified or harmless (37(c)(1)).
- **CA note:** disclosures only on demand, 60 days (CCP § 2016.090 [S-113]) — R-CIV-111.

#### III.E.2 Scope and limits (R-CIV-064 to R-CIV-067)
- **26(b)(1):** non-privileged + relevant to any party's claim or defense + proportional (six factors). Admissibility not required. The pre-2015 "reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence" phrase is gone federally (but survives in California, CCP § 2017.010).
- **26(b)(2)(B):** ESI not reasonably accessible — may be ordered for good cause, with cost-shifting conditions.
- **26(b)(2)(C):** court must limit cumulative/duplicative, obtainable elsewhere more conveniently, or outside 26(b)(1).
- **Privilege** (26(b)(5)(A)): express claim + description (log). **Dec. 1, 2025 amendment:** the 26(f) discovery plan must state the parties' views on the timing and method for complying with 26(b)(5)(A), and the scheduling order may address it (16(b)(3)(B)) [S-107] [S-276]. **Clawback** (26(b)(5)(B)) + FRE 502(b) (inadvertent, reasonable precautions, prompt rectification) and 502(d) orders. Corporate privilege covers employees' communications to counsel for legal advice (Upjohn); facts are never privileged. In diversity, state privilege law applies to state claims (FRE 501).
- **Work product** (26(b)(3)): anticipation of litigation; by or for party or representative; ordinary (substantial need + undue hardship) vs. opinion (near-absolute); own statement available on request (26(b)(3)(C)); Hickman v. Taylor.
- **Experts** (26(b)(4)): testifying expert — deposition after report; drafts protected; attorney communications protected except compensation, facts/data provided and considered, assumptions provided and relied on. Consulting expert — only Rule 35(b) or exceptional circumstances. Fees paid by the seeking party.
- **CA note:** work product belongs to the attorney; absolute vs. qualified (CCP § 2018.030; Coito) — R-CIV-112.

| Material | Federal rule | Typical essay answer |
|---|---|---|
| Witness statement taken by party's insurer or investigator after accident | Ordinary work product; discoverable on substantial need + undue hardship | If witness now unavailable or memory faded → discoverable; otherwise depose the witness |
| Attorney's notes of interview | Opinion work product (impressions) | Protected |
| Party's own prior statement | Obtainable on request (26(b)(3)(C)) | Produce |
| Routine incident report made in ordinary course | Not work product (not because of litigation) | Discoverable if relevant |
| Report of consulting (non-testifying) expert | 26(b)(4)(D) exceptional circumstances | Usually protected unless object destroyed or no other expert available |
| Report of testifying expert | Must be disclosed (26(a)(2)(B)) | Produce |
| Email to in-house counsel seeking legal advice | Attorney–client privilege (Upjohn) | Protected; log required |

#### III.E.3 Discovery devices (R-CIV-068 to R-CIV-070)
| Device | Who | Limits | Response time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oral deposition (Rule 30) | Parties and non-parties (subpoena) | 10 per side; once per deponent; 1 day of 7 hours | Reasonable written notice |
| Written deposition (Rule 31) | Parties and non-parties | Counts toward 10 | — |
| Interrogatories (Rule 33) | Parties only | 25 incl. discrete subparts | 30 days |
| Production / inspection (Rule 34) | Parties; non-parties by Rule 45 | Possession, custody or control | 30 days |
| Physical/mental exam (Rule 35) | Parties (or persons in their control) | Motion + good cause + condition in controversy; court order | Per order |
| Requests for admission (Rule 36) | Parties only | No numerical limit in the rule | 30 days or deemed admitted |
| Subpoena (Rule 45) | Non-parties (and parties for trial) | 100-mile limits; undue-burden protection | Objections within 14 days |

- **Rule 30 details:** 30(b)(6) organizational deponent; objections stated concisely, non-argumentatively and non-suggestively; instruct not to answer only for privilege, court-ordered limit, or 30(d)(3) motion; deponent may review transcript and sign changes within 30 days (30(e)).
- **Rule 32 use:** party-opponent's deposition for any purpose; unavailable witness (dead; > 100 miles or abroad unless procured; age, illness, infirmity, imprisonment; subpoena failed; exceptional circumstances); impeachment; optional completeness (32(a)(6)); objections to admissibility generally preserved except those curable at the deposition (32(d)(3)).
- **Rule 35 details** (R-CIV-070): Schlagenhauf — affirmative showing; plaintiff placing condition in issue; garden-variety emotional distress usually not enough for a mental exam (majority); report exchange and privilege waiver (35(b)). **Essays:** Feb. 2015 (physical and mental exams), Feb. 2023 (compelled physical exam).
- **Rule 36:** deemed admissions; withdrawal standard; expenses of proof for unjustified denials (37(c)(2)).
- **CA note:** 35 specially prepared interrogatories and 35 RFAs; one physical exam by demand in personal-injury cases within 75 miles; mental exam by leave (CCP §§ 2030.030, 2032.220, 2032.320, 2033.030) — R-CIV-110.

#### III.E.4 Timing, planning and protective orders (R-CIV-072, R-CIV-077)
- No discovery before the 26(f) conference (26(d)(1)); early Rule 34 requests allowed (26(d)(2)), deemed served at the conference.
- 26(f) conference: claims and defenses; settlement; disclosures; preservation; privilege (now including log timing and method); proposed plan; report within 14 days.
- Protective orders (26(c)): good cause + meet-and-confer certification; options include forbidding, conditions, limiting scope, confidentiality, sealed depositions, trade secret protection, simultaneous filing of documents.
- Signing discovery papers (26(g)): certifies completeness and that requests are consistent with rules, not improper, and not unreasonable or unduly burdensome; mandatory sanctions for violation without substantial justification.

#### III.E.5 E-discovery (R-CIV-071)
- Preservation duty arises when litigation is reasonably anticipated (litigation hold).
- Form of production (34(b)(1)(C), 34(b)(2)(E)): requester may specify form; otherwise as ordinarily maintained or reasonably usable form; only one form.
- **37(e):** reasonable steps; irreplaceable loss; (1) prejudice → curative measures; (2) intent to deprive → adverse presumption, instruction, dismissal or default. Negligence or gross negligence is not enough for (e)(2).

#### III.E.6 Enforcement and sanctions (R-CIV-073)
- **Motion to compel** (37(a)): certification of good-faith conferral (Jul. 2019 essay: meet-and-confer prerequisite); evasive = failure; mandatory expenses to the winner unless exceptions; loser's expenses if denied; apportionment if partly granted.
- **Violation of order** (37(b)(2)(A)): (i) facts established; (ii) preclusion; (iii) striking pleadings; (iv) stay; (v) dismissal; (vi) default judgment; (vii) contempt (not for Rule 35 orders). Plus expenses.
- **No order needed** (37(d)): failure to appear for own deposition, to answer interrogatories, or to respond to Rule 34 requests.
- **37(c)(1):** undisclosed information or witness excluded unless substantially justified or harmless; jury may be informed.
- **37(f):** failure to participate in good faith in framing a discovery plan.
- **Severe sanctions:** willfulness, bad faith or fault; consider lesser sanctions; due process nexus (NHL v. Metropolitan Hockey Club; Insurance Corp. of Ireland).
- **Subpoena enforcement** (45(g)): contempt of non-party who fails without adequate excuse.
- **CA note:** 45-day deadline and meet-and-confer declaration for motions to compel further responses; mandatory $1,000 sanction (CCP § 2023.050 [S-111]); misuse sanctions (§§ 2023.010, 2023.030) — R-CIV-110, R-CIV-111.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — scope/proportionality, work product, consulting experts, Rule 35, deposition use, 37(e). Essays — consistently "motion to compel" calls; answer each request separately.

### III.F Adjudication without a trial
- **Voluntary dismissal** (R-CIV-074): 41(a)(1)(A)(i) notice before answer or SJ motion; (ii) stipulation; without prejudice; **two-dismissal rule**; 41(a)(2) court order on terms (often costs, conditions); pending counterclaim protection; 41(c) applies to counterclaims, crossclaims and third-party claims; 41(d) costs of previously dismissed action. Current rule applies; the 2027 proposal is not testable [S-110].
- **Involuntary dismissal** (R-CIV-075): 41(b) — failure to prosecute or comply; adjudication on the merits unless stated or jurisdiction/venue/Rule 19.
- **Default** (R-CIV-076): entry (55(a)), judgment by clerk (sum certain, non-appearing, competent adult) or court (55(b)(2); 7 days' notice if appeared; hearings for damages); limited to what is demanded (54(c)); set aside entry for good cause (55(c)); judgment under 60(b). Default judgment is on the merits for claim preclusion, but issues are not "actually litigated" for issue preclusion.
- **Summary judgment** — see V.A (R-CIV-085, R-CIV-086).
- **Judgment on partial findings** in bench trials — 52(c) (VI.C).
- **Settlement and offers of judgment** (Rule 68): a defending party may serve an offer at least 14 days before trial; if the plaintiff rejects it and the judgment obtained is not more favorable, the plaintiff pays costs incurred after the offer (costs only — attorney's fees only if the underlying statute defines them as costs).
- **Arbitration:** a court must stay (not dismiss) proceedings pending arbitration when a party requests a stay under 9 U.S.C. § 3; a party may waive arbitration by litigation conduct without any prejudice requirement (Morgan v. Sundance (2022); CA: Quach (2024) [S-131]) — R-CIV-120.
- **CA note:** voluntary dismissal before actual commencement of trial (CCP § 581(c)); mandatory dismissal for failure to serve within 3 years or bring to trial within 5 (§§ 583.210, 583.310); relief from default within six months (§ 473(b)); CCP § 998 offers to compromise (cost-shifting, including expert fees).
- **How it is tested:** MBE — notice dismissal timing and the two-dismissal rule, default judgment limits, Rule 68. Essays — less often; dismissals appear as preclusion facts (Jul. 2021).

### III.G Pretrial conference and order
- **Rule 16** (R-CIV-077): purposes (expediting disposition, managing the case, discouraging waste, improving trial quality, facilitating settlement); scheduling order (90/60-day timing; mandatory contents — joinder, amendment, discovery, motions; permissive — disclosure, ESI, **privilege-log timing and method**, FRE 502 agreements, pretrial conference dates); modification for good cause; final pretrial order modified only to prevent manifest injustice; sanctions (16(f)) including Rule 37(b)(2)(A)(ii)–(vii) and mandatory expenses.
- **Rule 16.1 (MDL, eff. Dec. 1, 2025)** [S-107] [S-275]: after transfer by the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation, the transferee court should schedule an initial management conference; it may order the parties to meet and submit a report on matters such as leadership counsel, previously entered orders, how to manage discovery, and related actions; it should then enter an initial management order. Flexible, not mandatory deadlines.
- **Effect of pretrial order:** controls subsequent course; issues omitted are generally waived; evidence and witnesses not listed may be excluded.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — modification standards and effect of the final pretrial order; Rule 16(f) sanctions.

## IV Jury trials

### IV.A Right to jury trial
- **Seventh Amendment** (R-CIV-078): federal courts only; "suits at common law" (1791 analogy) + nature of remedy (decisive); applies to statutory claims seeking legal relief (Curtis v. Loether; Terry) and to civil penalties for fraud-type claims (SEC v. Jarkesy (2024)); public-rights exception.
- **Legal vs. equitable remedies:**

| Legal (jury) | Equitable (judge) |
|---|---|
| Compensatory and punitive damages | Injunctions (TRO, preliminary, permanent) |
| Statutory damages and back pay as damages | Specific performance |
| Replevin, ejectment | Rescission, reformation (usually) |
| Accounting for money owed when not complex (Dairy Queen) | Complex accounting, constructive trust, equitable lien |
| Civil penalties for fraud-type claims (Jarkesy) | Declaratory judgment follows the underlying coercive action |

- **Mixed cases** (R-CIV-079): jury decides legal claims and common issues first (Beacon Theatres; Dairy Queen); derivative suits (Ross v. Bernhard); declaratory judgment actions — look to the nature of the underlying claim.
- **Issues for the judge in jury cases:** questions of law; patent claim construction (Markman); preliminary facts for admissibility (FRE 104(a)); amount of civil penalties (Tull).
- **Demand and waiver** (R-CIV-080): 38(b) 14 days after last pleading on the issue; 38(d) waiver; 39(b) discretion; 81(c)(3) removed cases; advisory jury (39(c)). Essay: Feb. 2015 (timeliness of jury demand).
- **CA note:** California right as at common law in 1850 (Cal. Const. art. I, § 16 [S-260]); the court may try equitable issues first (Raedeke; Hoopes v. Dolan) [S-1407]; no pre-dispute jury waivers (Grafton) — R-CIV-116.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — legal vs. equitable classification, Beacon sequencing, demand timing. Essay — Jul. 2017 ("which issues are triable to a jury?").

### IV.B Selection and composition of juries
- **Qualifications** (28 U.S.C. § 1865): U.S. citizen, 18+, resident of the district for one year, adequate English, no disqualifying mental or physical condition, no pending felony charge or unrestored felony conviction. Jury pools drawn from a fair cross-section (§ 1861).
- **Size** (Rule 48(a)): 6 to 12; all participate unless excused for good cause (47(c)).
- **Voir dire** (Rule 47(a)): court or attorneys; if court examines, it must allow supplemental questions it considers proper.
- **Challenges** (R-CIV-082): for cause (unlimited; actual or implied bias); peremptory — 3 per party (§ 1870), multiple parties may be treated as one or given more; Batson/Edmonson/J.E.B. limits.
- **Post-verdict juror issues** (R-CIV-092): McDonough test for voir dire non-disclosure; FRE 606(b) limits juror testimony.
- **Essay:** Jul. 2024 ("was seating Juror #5 error?") — analyze the challenge for cause (bias by relationship or statements), whether a peremptory was available and used, preservation, and McDonough if facts surfaced later.
- **CA note:** six peremptory challenges per party in a two-party civil case (CCP § 231(c) [S-271]); statutory bias-challenge procedure in CCP § 231.7, civil trials from Jan. 1, 2026 [S-365]; jury of 12 (or fewer by agreement), three-fourths verdict.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — number of jurors, peremptories, Batson in civil cases, for-cause grounds. Essay — Jul. 2024.

### IV.C Requests for and objections to jury instructions
- **Rule 51** (R-CIV-083): written requests at close of evidence (or earlier per order; later requests for issues that could not reasonably have been anticipated); court informs parties of proposed instructions and actions before instructing and before closing arguments; objection on the record, out of the jury's hearing, stating distinctly the matter and grounds; timing; preservation; plain error.
- Instructions may be given before or after closing arguments, or both (51(b)(3)).
- **How it is tested:** MBE — preservation of instructional error and plain-error review.

## V Motions

### V.A Pretrial motions, including motions addressed to face of pleadings, motions to dismiss, and summary judgment motions

**Motions addressed to the pleadings (R-CIV-084, R-CIV-047):**
- 12(b)(1)–(7) pre-answer motions; facial vs. factual SMJ attacks (court may consider evidence on a factual 12(b)(1) challenge); PJ — plaintiff's prima facie showing on affidavits, or evidentiary hearing.
- 12(b)(6): plausibility; materials considered; limitations defense only if apparent on the face (Feb. 2013 essay: motion to dismiss on limitations grounds — apply state limitations law under Erie and ask whether the complaint itself shows untimeliness).
- 12(c) judgment on the pleadings; 12(d) conversion; 12(e) more definite statement; 12(f) strike.
- Leave to amend usually granted after a first dismissal unless futile.
- **CA note:** general demurrer, special demurrer, motion to strike, anti-SLAPP motion (CCP §§ 425.16, 430.10, 435–436, 438) — R-CIV-106 to R-CIV-108.

**Summary judgment (R-CIV-085, R-CIV-086):**
- Standard 56(a): no genuine dispute of material fact + entitled to judgment as a matter of law; may be on part of a claim or defense.
- Burden-shifting (Celotex): movant without trial burden may point to absence of evidence; non-movant must cite record materials; cannot rest on pleadings.
- Evidence: admissible in substance (56(c)(2)); declarations on personal knowledge (56(c)(4)); court need consider only cited materials (56(c)(3)).
- Genuineness: reasonable jury, with substantive standard (Anderson v. Liberty Lobby); no credibility weighing; inferences for non-movant (Tolan); blatant contradiction by record (Scott v. Harris); implausible economic theories (Matsushita).
- Timing: any time until 30 days after close of discovery (56(b)); 56(d) more time for discovery; 56(e) failure to address fact; 56(f) independent of motion; 56(g) facts not genuinely in dispute; 56(h) bad-faith affidavits sanctioned.
- A denial of summary judgment is interlocutory and not appealable; after trial, a sufficiency challenge must be preserved through Rule 50 (Ortiz v. Jordan, 562 U.S. 180 (2011)), though purely legal issues decided at SJ are preserved (Dupree v. Younger, 598 U.S. 729 (2023)) [S-1408].
- **CA note:** separate statement; 81/20/11 days; hearing ≥ 30 days before trial; summary adjudication only of whole causes of action, defenses, punitive-damages claims or duty issues; writ review of denial within 20 days (CCP § 437c [S-114] [S-263]) — R-CIV-113.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — burden-shifting, genuine vs. material, 12(d) conversion, waiver of 12(b) defenses. Essays — Feb. 2019 (motion to dismiss for lack of SMJ), Feb. 2023 (motion to dismiss for failure to join), Jul. 2015 (summary adjudication).

### V.B Motions for judgments as a matter of law (directed verdicts and judgments notwithstanding the verdict)
- **50(a)** (R-CIV-087): after the party has been fully heard; standard = no legally sufficient evidentiary basis for a reasonable jury; any time before submission; specify grounds; review entire record favoring non-movant (Reeves).
- **50(b)** (R-CIV-088): renewed within 28 days after judgment; only grounds raised in 50(a); alternative new-trial request; outcomes; conditional new-trial ruling (50(c)); non-movant's own new-trial motion within 28 days after JMOL judgment (50(d)).
- **Appeals:** no sufficiency review without 50(b) (Unitherm); appellate court may direct entry of JMOL (Weisgram); if JMOL is reversed, the appellate court may order a new trial or remand (50(e)).
- **Seventh Amendment:** JMOL is constitutional (Galloway); but a court may not re-weigh credibility.
- **Essay:** Jul. 2024 ("was denial of the directed verdict correct?") — state the 50(a) standard, marshal the evidence for the non-movant, and conclude whether a reasonable jury could find for it.
- **CA note:** nonsuit (§ 581c), directed verdict (§ 630), JNOV without a prior motion (§ 629) — R-CIV-117.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — timing, renewal requirement, standard, Unitherm.

### V.C Posttrial motions, including motions for relief from judgment and for new trial
- **New trial** (R-CIV-089): grounds; weighing permitted ("clear weight," "miscarriage of justice"); 28 days, no extension (6(b)(2)); court's own initiative within 28 days; partial new trial if issues separable; remittitur (not additur — Dimick); Gasperini for state excessiveness standards; affidavits supporting the motion (59(c)).
- **Alter or amend** (59(e)): 28 days; correct manifest errors of law or fact, newly discovered evidence, intervening change of law; not to relitigate.
- **Relief from judgment** (R-CIV-090): 60(a) clerical; 60(b)(1)–(6); timing (60(c)); void judgments and the reasonable-time limit (Coney Island (2026) [S-252]); independent action and fraud on the court (60(d)); 60(b)(2) evidence must be (i) discovered after trial, (ii) not discoverable with diligence in time for a Rule 59 motion, (iii) material and not merely cumulative or impeaching, and (iv) likely to change the outcome (general formulation); 60(b)(3) misconduct shown by clear and convincing evidence in most circuits and need not be outcome-changing if it prevented a full and fair presentation.
- **Interplay and timing:** motions under 50(b), 52(b), 59 and Rule 60 (if within 28 days) toll the time to appeal (FRAP 4(a)(4)).
- **Essay:** Jul. 2024 (new trial based on hidden crash-test reports) — consider Rule 59 (newly discovered evidence / misconduct within 28 days), Rule 60(b)(2) and (b)(3) (within a year), and Rule 37 sanctions for the discovery violation.
- **CA note:** CCP § 657 grounds with written reasons; notice of intention 15/180 days; 75 days from notice of entry to rule; additur and remittitur (§ 662.5); § 473(b) relief — R-CIV-107, R-CIV-117 [S-261] [S-262].
- **How it is tested:** MBE — 28-day deadlines, remittitur vs. additur, 60(b) categories and one-year limit.

## VI Verdicts and judgments

### VI.A Defaults and involuntary dismissals
- **Default** (R-CIV-076): entry → judgment; clerk vs. court; 54(c) limit; 7-day notice to appearing party; setting aside (good cause vs. 60(b)); void-judgment attacks subject to reasonable time (Coney Island [S-252]); default judgment entered without PJ may be attacked collaterally by a defendant who never appeared.
- **Involuntary dismissal** (R-CIV-075): 41(b); adjudication on the merits unless otherwise stated or jurisdiction/venue/Rule 19; Semtek limits the reach of "on the merits."
- **CA note:** CCP § 473(b) mandatory relief on attorney affidavit of fault; § 473.5 relief where no actual notice; dismissal for delay (§§ 583.210, 583.310, 583.410) — R-CIV-107.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — clerk's authority, setting aside, effect of 41(b) dismissal.

### VI.B Jury verdicts—types and challenges
- **Types** (R-CIV-091): general verdict; special verdict (49(a)); general verdict with answers to written questions (49(b)).
- **Inconsistency:** 49(b)(3) answers consistent with each other but not with verdict — judgment on answers, further deliberation, or new trial; 49(b)(4) answers inconsistent with each other and one with verdict — no judgment; further deliberation or new trial; raise before discharge.
- **Unanimity and polling** (R-CIV-081): 48(b)–(c).
- **Challenges to verdicts:** JMOL (V.B), new trial (V.C), juror misconduct and non-disclosure (R-CIV-092: McDonough; FRE 606(b)); extraneous information or outside influence.
- **Entry of judgment** (Rule 58): separate document; deemed entered after 150 days on the civil docket if no separate document; Rule 54(b) partial judgments; costs (54(d)(1)) and attorney's fees motion within 14 days (54(d)(2)).
- **Stay of execution:** automatic 30 days after entry (62(a)); supersedeas bond or other security for a stay pending appeal (62(b)).
- **CA note:** three-fourths verdicts; juror evidence under Evid. Code § 1150 — R-CIV-116, R-CIV-117.

### VI.C Judicial findings and conclusions
- **Rule 52** (R-CIV-093): findings and conclusions stated separately in bench trials (and with advisory juries); findings required for interlocutory injunctions; not required on Rule 12 or 56 motions; clear-error review with deference to credibility (Anderson v. Bessemer City); 52(b) amendment within 28 days; 52(c) judgment on partial findings.
- **CA note:** statement of decision on request (CCP § 632).
- **How it is tested:** MBE — standard of review for findings; 52(c).

### VI.D Effect; claim and issue preclusion
**Claim preclusion (R-CIV-094):**
- Elements: same claim (transactional — Restatement (Second) of Judgments § 24: facts related in time, space, origin or motivation; convenient trial unit; parties' expectations); same parties/privies; valid final judgment on the merits.
- Merger (plaintiff wins) and bar (defendant wins).
- On the merits: trial judgments, SJ, 12(b)(6), default, 41(b) dismissals; not: jurisdiction, venue, Rule 19.
- Exceptions (Restatement § 26): parties' agreement; court's express reservation; first court's jurisdictional limits; inconsistency with statutory scheme; continuing or recurring wrongs (new claims for later conduct).
- Defendant's preclusion: compulsory counterclaims (Rule 13(a)); common-law defense preclusion is limited (Lucky Brand).
- Federal finality: judgment final when entered, despite pending appeal (majority).
- **Essay:** Jul. 2021 ("can the first judgment be used defensively to bar the second suit?") — state the elements, apply the transactional test, and add the California primary-rights contrast if the first judgment came from a California court.

**Issue preclusion (R-CIV-095 to R-CIV-097):**
- Elements: same issue; actually litigated and determined; valid final judgment; essential to the judgment; full and fair opportunity of the party bound.
- Not actually litigated: defaults, consent judgments (unless intended), admissions; alternative grounds (Restatement: no preclusion absent appellate affirmance on that ground).
- Exceptions (Restatement § 28): no appellate review available; change in law or different claims on pure questions of law; procedural differences; heavier burden in the first action; public interest or unforeseeability.
- Mutuality: defensive non-mutual allowed (Blonder-Tongue); offensive non-mutual discretionary (Parklane factors); not against the U.S. (Mendoza).
- Non-parties: Taylor v. Sturgell's six categories; due process (Hansberry; Martin v. Wilks).

**Interjurisdictional preclusion (R-CIV-098):** § 1738 (state judgments: rendering State's law; Kremer; Marrese); federal-question judgments: federal law; federal diversity judgments: federal common law adopting forum State's law (Semtek).

| Feature | Federal / MBE | California |
|---|---|---|
| Claim definition | Transaction (Restatement § 24) | Primary right (Mycogen; Slater; Boeken) |
| Finality while appeal pending | Final (majority) | Not final, for claim or issue preclusion (Samara; CCP § 1049) |
| Mutuality for issue preclusion | Not required; Parklane limits on offensive use | Not required (Bernhard); fairness/public-policy check (Lucido) |
| Terminology | Claim / issue preclusion | Same, adopted in DKN Holdings |

- **CA note:** R-CIV-114, R-CIV-115.
- **How it is tested:** MBE — transactional test, actually litigated/essential, Parklane factors, non-party preclusion. Essay — Jul. 2021.

## VII Appealability and review

### VII.A Availability of interlocutory review
- **§ 1292(a)(1)** injunction orders (R-CIV-100); practical-effect test (Carson); TROs generally not (Sampson).
- **§ 1292(b)** certified questions: district court certification + court of appeals' permission; 10 days.
- **Rule 23(f)** class-certification appeals (14 days).
- **Collateral order doctrine** (R-CIV-101): Cohen three-part test; narrow category; included — denials of qualified immunity that turn on issues of law (not fact-based sufficiency rulings: *Johnson v. Jones*, 515 U.S. 304 (1995)), sovereign/Eleventh Amendment immunity, double jeopardy, orders requiring security in Cohen; excluded — privilege disclosure orders (Mohawk: appeal after final judgment generally suffices; § 1292(b), mandamus or a contempt appeal also possible), attorney disqualification (Richardson-Merrell), forum-selection-clause denials (Lauro Lines), class-certification denials (Coopers & Lybrand), and denial of a contractor's derivative sovereign immunity (GEO Group v. Menocal (2026) [S-253] [S-1400]).
- **Mandamus** (§ 1651; Cheney): clear abuse or usurpation; no other adequate means; clear and indisputable right.
- **Other statutory routes:** 9 U.S.C. § 16 (orders refusing arbitration); § 1453(c) (CAFA remand orders); § 1447(d) (federal-officer removal remands).
- **CA note:** CCP § 904.1 lists appealable interlocutory orders (anti-SLAPP, injunctions, new-trial grants, motion-to-quash grants, FNC); writs for the rest; "death knell" for class-certification denials — R-CIV-118.
- **Essay:** Feb. 2014 ("how should the court of appeals rule on each party's appeal?") — classify each order: injunction (appealable now under § 1292(a)(1)); denial of remand (interlocutory; reviewable after final judgment, or via § 1292(b)); timeliness under FRAP 4.

### VII.B Final judgment rule
- **§ 1291** (R-CIV-099): final decision ends litigation on the merits (Catlin).
- **Rule 54(b):** multiple claims or parties — express determination of no just reason for delay.
- **Rule 58:** separate-document requirement and 150-day rule.
- **FRAP 4:** 30 days (60 if U.S. party); tolling motions; extension for excusable neglect or good cause on motion within 30 days after the deadline; reopening if notice of judgment not received (4(a)(6)). Statutory deadline is jurisdictional (Bowles).
- **Consolidated cases** retain separate finality (Hall v. Hall); voluntary dismissal to manufacture finality after denial of class certification is not allowed (Microsoft v. Baker).
- **Cross-appeals:** within 14 days after the first notice of appeal or the original deadline, whichever later (FRAP 4(a)(3)).
- **CA note:** one final judgment; 60 days after notice of entry or 180 days after entry (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 8.104); appellate division for limited civil cases (CCP § 904.2).

### VII.C Scope of review for judge and jury
- **Standards** (R-CIV-102): de novo (law, SJ, 12(b)(6), JMOL); clear error (bench findings, Rule 52(a)(6)); abuse of discretion (evidentiary rulings, discovery, sanctions, new-trial rulings, Rule 11 — Cooter & Gell; remittitur — Gasperini); jury verdicts — substantial evidence/reasonable jury, limited by the Seventh Amendment's Reexamination Clause.
- **Harmless error:** 28 U.S.C. § 2111; Rule 61 — disregard errors not affecting substantial rights.
- **Preservation:** contemporaneous objections (FRE 103; Rule 51); plain error for unpreserved instructional errors.
- **Record:** appellate courts review the record made below; new arguments generally not considered.
- **CA note:** substantial-evidence, abuse-of-discretion and de novo standards; prejudicial-error requirement (Cal. Const. art. VI, § 13).
- **How it is tested:** MBE — matching the ruling to the standard; Reexamination Clause.
