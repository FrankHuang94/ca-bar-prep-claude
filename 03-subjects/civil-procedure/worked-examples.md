# Civil Procedure — worked examples

Each example is original. Format: **Facts → Rule → Application → Conclusion**, with the rule ID from [rule-statements.md](rule-statements.md).

### WE-01 — Diversity with an LLC (R-CIV-004, R-CIV-007)
**Facts.** Pia, domiciled in Oregon, sues Brightline LLC in federal court in Oregon for $200,000 in product damages. Brightline's two members are a Delaware corporation headquartered in Texas and an individual domiciled in Oregon.
**Rule.** Complete diversity is required; an LLC takes the citizenship of every member.
**Application.** Brightline is a citizen of Delaware, Texas (through the corporate member) and Oregon (through the individual member). Pia is an Oregon citizen.
**Conclusion.** No diversity jurisdiction; the case must be dismissed (or refiled in state court).

### WE-02 — Amount in controversy and aggregation (R-CIV-008, R-CIV-011)
**Facts.** Two California homeowners, Ana ($90,000) and Ben ($40,000), sue an Arizona contractor in federal court for separate defective-roof contracts on the same street, joined under Rule 20.
**Rule.** Separate claims of multiple plaintiffs cannot be aggregated; but § 1367 supplemental jurisdiction covers a Rule 20 co-plaintiff below the amount if one plaintiff satisfies it and diversity is complete (Exxon Mobil).
**Application.** Ana's claim exceeds $75,000 and anchors jurisdiction. Ben's claim must share a common nucleus with Ana's; two separate contracts on the same street may not form one case or controversy unless the defects arise from the same work or materials.
**Conclusion.** Ana's claim stays. Ben's claim is in federal court only if it shares a common nucleus with Ana's; if it does, Exxon Mobil allows it.

### WE-03 — Supplemental jurisdiction against an impleaded party (R-CIV-011, R-CIV-056)
**Facts.** Kai (Nevada) sues Dune Trucking (Utah) for $300,000 after a crash. Dune impleads Lane (Nevada), its maintenance contractor, for indemnity. Kai then asserts a negligence claim directly against Lane.
**Rule.** In a diversity-only case, § 1367(b) bars supplemental jurisdiction over claims by plaintiffs against persons made parties under Rule 14.
**Application.** Dune's impleader claim is a defendant's claim and is supported by supplemental jurisdiction. Kai's claim against Lane is a plaintiff's claim against a Rule 14 party, and Kai and Lane are both Nevadans.
**Conclusion.** Dune v. Lane proceeds; Kai v. Lane must be dismissed (Kroger).

### WE-04 — Removal by a forum defendant (R-CIV-013, R-CIV-015)
**Facts.** Ravi (Washington) sues Sunrise Inc. (incorporated in Delaware, headquartered in San Diego) in San Diego Superior Court for $500,000. Sunrise removes to the Southern District of California within 20 days. Ravi moves to remand 40 days after removal.
**Rule.** A diversity case cannot be removed if a properly joined and served defendant is a forum citizen; most courts treat this as a procedural defect that must be raised within 30 days of removal.
**Application.** Sunrise is a California citizen (nerve center), so removal violated § 1441(b)(2). But Ravi waited 40 days.
**Conclusion.** Remand will likely be denied as untimely; the court has subject-matter jurisdiction (complete diversity), so it keeps the case.

### WE-05 — Federal defense does not support removal (R-CIV-002, R-CIV-013)
**Facts.** A California employee sues her employer in state court for breach of an employment contract. The employer removes, arguing a federal statute preempts the claim.
**Rule.** Removal requires original jurisdiction based on the well-pleaded complaint; a preemption defense does not create federal-question jurisdiction (unless complete preemption applies).
**Application.** The contract claim arises under state law; ordinary preemption is a defense.
**Conclusion.** Remand is required (unless the statute completely preempts, as LMRA § 301 does for collective-bargaining agreements).

### WE-06 — General vs. specific jurisdiction over a manufacturer (R-CIV-020 to R-CIV-024)
**Facts.** HeartTech, incorporated and headquartered in Minnesota, designs a cardiac valve in Minnesota and sells it through a national distributor. It advertises in medical journals circulated nationwide, staffs a sales representative in Los Angeles, and sold 4,000 valves to California hospitals last year. A valve implanted in Sacramento fails, injuring a California patient, who sues HeartTech in California.
**Rule.** General jurisdiction requires that the corporation be at home; specific jurisdiction requires purposeful availment, relatedness, and fairness.
**Application.** HeartTech is not at home in California (Daimler). But it deliberately serves the California market: a local representative, thousands of sales, and the injury occurred in California to a Californian using that product. That is more than mere awareness under any stream-of-commerce view, and the claim relates to those contacts (Ford). The burden of litigating in California is modest for a national company, and California has a strong interest.
**Conclusion.** Specific jurisdiction exists.

### WE-07 — Tag jurisdiction (R-CIV-018)
**Facts.** Dr. Lee, domiciled in Arizona, is served in San Diego while attending a two-day conference, in a malpractice suit arising from surgery in Arizona.
**Rule.** Personal service on an individual voluntarily present in the forum supports general jurisdiction (Burnham — unanimous result, but no majority rationale: a plurality relied on tradition, a concurrence on fairness).
**Application.** Lee came voluntarily; nothing suggests fraud or force. Under the plurality's tradition test that ends the inquiry; under the concurrence's fairness view, Lee knowingly availed himself of California's protections during the visit and the burden of defending there is modest, so the result is the same.
**Conclusion.** California has PJ, although Lee may seek a transfer or dismissal on forum non conveniens grounds.

### WE-08 — Abode service (R-CIV-029)
**Facts.** A process server leaves the summons and complaint with the doorman of Mia's apartment building in Los Angeles.
**Rule.** Rule 4(e)(2)(B) requires leaving copies at the dwelling with someone of suitable age and discretion **who resides there**; state-law methods are also available under 4(e)(1).
**Application.** The doorman does not reside in Mia's unit. California substituted service requires prior diligent attempts at personal service and a follow-up mailing (CCP § 415.20), which did not occur.
**Conclusion.** Service is insufficient; Mia must raise it in her first Rule 12 response or waive it.

### WE-09 — Venue and transfer (R-CIV-031, R-CIV-033, R-CIV-034)
**Facts.** An Idaho rancher sues an irrigation company whose only office is in Fresno (Eastern District of California) for a pump failure on the rancher's Idaho farm. He files in the Central District of California.
**Rule.** Venue lies where any defendant resides (all residing in one State) or where a substantial part of events occurred; improper venue → dismiss or transfer under § 1406(a).
**Application.** The company resides in the Eastern District (an entity resides where it is subject to PJ, measured district by district in a multi-district State, § 1391(d)). The events occurred in Idaho. The Central District satisfies neither test unless the company's contacts there would support PJ.
**Conclusion.** Venue is improper in C.D. Cal.; the court should transfer to E.D. Cal. or D. Idaho.

### WE-10 — Erie: state affidavit requirement (R-CIV-038)
**Facts.** In a diversity malpractice suit in federal court, the defendant moves to dismiss because the plaintiff did not file the expert certificate of merit that state law requires with the complaint.
**Rule.** A valid Federal Rule that answers the question controls; Rule 8 governs the contents of a complaint (Berk v. Choy).
**Application.** The state requirement adds a filing-stage merits showing that Rule 8 does not require, so the Rule answers the question. Rule 8 really regulates procedure and is valid under the Rules Enabling Act.
**Conclusion.** Motion denied.

### WE-11 — Erie: statute of limitations (R-CIV-040)
**Facts.** State X law says an action is commenced for limitations purposes when the defendant is served. P files a diversity suit in federal court one day before the two-year period expires and serves D 30 days later.
**Rule.** Rule 3 governs when an action commences for federal-rules purposes, but does not displace state tolling rules for state-law claims (Walker).
**Application.** No Federal Rule directly collides; applying a filing date would change the outcome and encourage forum shopping.
**Conclusion.** State law applies; the claim is time-barred.

### WE-12 — Plausibility and fraud particularity (R-CIV-044, R-CIV-045)
**Facts.** A buyer's federal complaint alleges: "Seller defrauded Buyer by misrepresenting the watch." A second count alleges the seller breached the contract by delivering a replica instead of the promised vintage original.
**Rule.** Fraud requires particularity under Rule 9(b); other claims must be plausible under Rule 8(a).
**Application.** The fraud count lacks who, what, when and where. The contract count states the contract, the promise of an original, delivery of a replica and damages — plausible.
**Conclusion.** Dismiss the fraud count with leave to amend; the contract count survives.

### WE-13 — Rule 12 waiver (R-CIV-047)
**Facts.** D moves to dismiss under 12(b)(6). The motion is denied. D's answer then asserts lack of personal jurisdiction and improper venue.
**Rule.** PJ and venue are waived if omitted from a pre-answer Rule 12 motion.
**Application.** Both defenses were available when D filed its 12(b)(6) motion.
**Conclusion.** Both are waived; D may still argue failure to state a claim at trial.

### WE-14 — Relation back of a new defendant (R-CIV-049)
**Facts.** P sues "Coastal Rentals Inc." for an injury on rental equipment, two days before limitations expires. The equipment was owned by its sister company Coastal Equipment LLC, which shares officers and learned of the suit within a month. P amends to name Coastal Equipment four months after filing.
**Rule.** Rule 15(c)(1)(C): same occurrence; notice within 90 days; knew or should have known of the mistake.
**Application.** Same occurrence; notice within a month; shared officers show knowledge that P meant to sue the owner (Krupski).
**Conclusion.** The amendment relates back.

### WE-15 — Rule 11 safe harbor (R-CIV-051)
**Facts.** D serves P with a Rule 11 motion challenging a frivolous count on May 1 and files it on May 15. P withdraws the count on May 18.
**Rule.** A Rule 11 motion may be filed only after 21 days if the paper is not withdrawn.
**Application.** D filed after 14 days; P cured within 21 days.
**Conclusion.** No sanctions.

### WE-16 — Rule 19 joint tortfeasor (R-CIV-054)
**Facts.** A buyer sues a tire manufacturer after a blowout. The manufacturer moves to dismiss for failure to join the dealer that installed the tire.
**Rule.** Joint tortfeasors are not required parties (Temple).
**Application.** Complete relief is available against the manufacturer; the dealer's absence creates no risk of inconsistent obligations.
**Conclusion.** Motion denied; the manufacturer may implead the dealer under Rule 14 if it claims contribution or indemnity.

### WE-17 — Work product and witness statement (R-CIV-066, R-CIV-112)
**Facts.** After a warehouse visitor is hurt when a pallet rack collapses, the warehouse's insurance adjuster records an interview with a forklift driver who saw it. Eight months later the driver has moved abroad and cannot be found. The visitor moves to compel the recording.
**Rule.** Ordinary work product is discoverable on substantial need and inability to obtain the substantial equivalent without undue hardship; mental impressions are protected.
**Application.** The recording was made in anticipation of litigation by the party's insurer. The witness is unavailable and the statement is near-contemporaneous, so substantial need exists. Any adjuster commentary on liability is opinion work product.
**Conclusion.** Compel the factual statement; redact opinions. **CA:** under CCP § 2018.030 and Coito, a statement obtained by an attorney gets at least qualified protection; this one was obtained by an adjuster, so the analysis turns on whether it is attorney work product at all.

### WE-18 — Consulting expert (R-CIV-067)
**Facts.** The warehouse hires an engineer to inspect the collapsed rack and then scraps the debris. It will not call her at trial. The visitor seeks her report.
**Rule.** A consulting expert's facts and opinions are discoverable only on exceptional circumstances.
**Application.** The physical evidence no longer exists, so the visitor cannot obtain the facts by other means.
**Conclusion.** Discovery allowed; the visitor pays a reasonable fee.

### WE-19 — Rule 35 mental exam (R-CIV-070)
**Facts.** In a car-crash suit, P seeks damages for a broken arm and "anxiety." D asks the court to order a psychiatric examination.
**Rule.** The condition must be in controversy and good cause shown (Schlagenhauf).
**Application.** A generic claim for anxiety accompanying a physical injury usually does not place mental condition in controversy; P has not alleged a psychiatric disorder or named an expert.
**Conclusion.** Likely denied; a physical exam of the arm would be granted. **CA:** D could demand one physical exam without court order (CCP § 2032.220), but a mental exam needs leave.

### WE-20 — Summary judgment burden (R-CIV-085, R-CIV-086)
**Facts.** After discovery, D moves for summary judgment on P's toxic-exposure claim, citing P's interrogatory answer that P "has no documents" showing exposure to D's chemical. P files only a declaration repeating the complaint's allegations.
**Rule.** A movant may point to the absence of evidence; the non-movant must cite specific admissible record evidence (Celotex).
**Application.** P's declaration is conclusory and not based on personal knowledge of exposure.
**Conclusion.** Summary judgment for D.

### WE-21 — Jury right in mixed claims (R-CIV-078, R-CIV-079)
**Facts.** A gallery sues a collector in federal court for damages for breach of a consignment contract and for an injunction ordering the return of a consigned sculpture. Both claims turn on whether the collector validly terminated the contract.
**Rule.** Legal claims carry a jury right; equitable claims do not; common issues go to the jury first (Beacon Theatres).
**Application.** Damages are legal; the injunction is equitable. Valid termination is a common factual issue.
**Conclusion.** The jury decides termination on the damages claim first; the judge then decides the injunction consistently with that finding.

### WE-22 — Challenge for cause and McDonough (R-CIV-082, R-CIV-092)
**Facts.** During voir dire in a ladder-defect case, a prospective juror says her husband is an engineer at the defendant manufacturer but she can be fair. The judge denies the plaintiff's challenge for cause; the plaintiff has used all peremptories and the juror sits.
**Rule.** Implied bias may be found from a close relationship to a party; erroneous denial of a for-cause challenge is reversible if a biased juror sits after the party exhausted its peremptories (general rule).
**Application.** A spouse's employment by the defendant gives a direct financial and personal interest; her assurance is not decisive.
**Conclusion.** Likely error requiring a new trial.

### WE-23 — Renewed JMOL without a prior motion (R-CIV-088)
**Facts.** D never moves for JMOL during trial. After a $2 million verdict, D files a Rule 50(b) motion within 10 days.
**Rule.** A renewed motion requires a pre-verdict Rule 50(a) motion on the same grounds.
**Application.** There is no motion to renew.
**Conclusion.** Denied; D may move for a new trial under Rule 59 within 28 days. **CA:** JNOV is available without a prior directed-verdict motion (CCP § 629).

### WE-24 — Concealed evidence after trial (R-CIV-089, R-CIV-090, R-CIV-073)
**Facts.** Two months after a defense verdict in a ladder-collapse case, P learns that D withheld internal load-test failure reports responsive to P's document requests.
**Rule.** Rule 59 motions are due within 28 days; Rule 60(b)(2) (new evidence) and (b)(3) (misconduct) allow relief within a reasonable time, no more than a year.
**Application.** The Rule 59 deadline has passed. D's concealment is misconduct that prevented P from fully presenting its case; the reports could not have been discovered with diligence because D withheld them.
**Conclusion.** Grant a new trial under Rule 60(b)(3) (and possibly (b)(2)); Rule 37 sanctions may also apply.

### WE-25 — Claim preclusion, federal and California (R-CIV-094, R-CIV-114)
**Facts.** P sued D in California superior court for property damage to her car from a collision and won. She now sues D in the same court for personal injuries from the same collision.
**Rule.** Federal: transactional test bars all claims from the same transaction. California: primary rights — in tort, injury to the person and injury to property have traditionally been treated as separate primary rights; but one breach of contract gives one cause of action for all damages (Holmes v. David H. Bricker, Inc.).
**Application.** Because the first judgment is a California judgment, California preclusion law governs (28 U.S.C. § 1738 if refiled in federal court). P's claims sound in tort (negligent driving), and personal injury invades a different primary right from property damage. Had P's first suit been for breach of a warranty covering the car, Holmes would require all damages from that breach in one action.
**Conclusion.** Likely not barred in California [VERIFY V-976]; under the federal transactional test it would be barred.

### WE-26 — Offensive non-mutual issue preclusion (R-CIV-096)
**Facts.** After a jury found that a bus company's driver was negligent in a crash, a second passenger who watched the first trial before filing suit seeks to preclude the company on negligence.
**Rule.** Offensive non-mutual preclusion is discretionary under Parklane.
**Application.** The passenger could easily have joined the first suit and appears to have waited to see the result — a "wait and see" plaintiff. The company had every incentive to litigate the first case.
**Conclusion.** The court will likely deny preclusion because of the wait-and-see factor.

### WE-27 — Appealability of a preliminary injunction and a remand denial (R-CIV-100, R-CIV-015)
**Facts.** After removal, the federal court denies P's motion to remand and grants P, a franchisee, a preliminary injunction barring termination of her franchise. D appeals the injunction seven weeks later; P cross-appeals the remand denial.
**Rule.** Injunction orders are immediately appealable, but a notice of appeal is due within 30 days; denial of remand is interlocutory.
**Application.** D's appeal comes after 30 days. P's cross-appeal from a non-final order is not authorized unless certified under § 1292(b).
**Conclusion.** Both appeals should be dismissed; P can challenge jurisdiction after final judgment (and SMJ is never waived).
