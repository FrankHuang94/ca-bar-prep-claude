# Civil Procedure — traps

Each trap names the wrong instinct, the correct rule and the rule ID in [rule-statements.md](rule-statements.md).

## Jurisdiction
1. **"$75,000 is enough."** The amount must **exceed** $75,000; exactly $75,000 fails. Interest and costs do not count. (R-CIV-008)
2. **"A corporation is a citizen where it does most business."** No — State(s) of incorporation plus the nerve center (headquarters), not where factories or sales are. (R-CIV-006)
3. **Treating an LLC like a corporation.** LLCs and partnerships take every member's citizenship; the State of organization and principal place of business are irrelevant (outside CAFA). (R-CIV-007)
4. **Residence ≠ domicile.** A person living temporarily elsewhere keeps the old domicile until presence and intent to remain indefinitely coincide. (R-CIV-005)
5. **Diversity re-measured after a move.** Citizenship is fixed at filing; later moves do not matter. (R-CIV-004)
6. **Federal defense = federal question.** A federal defense or counterclaim never creates § 1331 jurisdiction (Mottley); it also cannot support removal. (R-CIV-002, R-CIV-013)
7. **Aggregating co-plaintiffs.** Separate claims of different plaintiffs cannot be added together; one plaintiff aggregates against one defendant only. (R-CIV-008)
8. **§ 1367(b) backwards.** It bars claims **by plaintiffs** against Rule 14/19/20/24 parties in diversity-only cases — not claims by defendants, and not in federal-question cases. (R-CIV-011)
9. **"Supplemental jurisdiction must be exercised."** It is discretionary under § 1367(c), except that Royal Canin requires remand when a plaintiff amends away all federal claims after removal and no other basis exists. (R-CIV-012)
10. **Forum-defendant rule in a federal-question case.** It applies only to diversity removals, and only to forum defendants who are properly joined **and served**. (R-CIV-013)
11. **Waiting to raise a procedural removal defect.** Untimeliness, lack of unanimity and the forum-defendant rule must be raised within 30 days of removal; lack of SMJ can be raised any time. (R-CIV-015)
12. **"A plaintiff facing a counterclaim can remove."** Only defendants to the plaintiff's claim may remove (Shamrock Oil; Home Depot). (R-CIV-013)

## Personal jurisdiction, service, venue
13. **"Large sales in the forum = general jurisdiction."** Corporations are at home only in their incorporation State and principal place of business, absent an exceptional case (Daimler; BNSF). (R-CIV-020)
14. **Foreseeability as purposeful availment.** It must be the defendant's own deliberate contacts; foreseeability that a product may travel there is not enough (World-Wide Volkswagen). (R-CIV-021)
15. **Plaintiff's residence as the contact.** The plaintiff cannot be the only link to the forum (Walden). (R-CIV-025)
16. **Thinking the 100-mile bulge applies to all defendants.** Only parties joined under Rule 14 or 19. (R-CIV-027)
17. **Abode service on anyone at the address.** The recipient must reside there and be of suitable age and discretion; doormen and visiting relatives do not qualify. (R-CIV-029)
18. **Waiver of service waives PJ.** It does not; it only saves service costs and gives 60 days to answer. (R-CIV-030)
19. **Venue where the plaintiff lives.** Plaintiff residence is irrelevant under § 1391(b). (R-CIV-031)
20. **Using § 1404 when venue is improper.** Improper venue → § 1406(a); proper venue → § 1404(a). Choice-of-law consequences differ. (R-CIV-033, R-CIV-034)
21. **Ignoring Atlantic Marine.** With a valid forum-selection clause, the plaintiff's choice gets no weight and private-interest factors drop out. (R-CIV-035)

## Erie
22. **"Federal courts in diversity apply state procedure."** They apply state **substantive** law and federal procedure; a valid Federal Rule on point always wins (Hanna; Berk v. Choy). (R-CIV-037, R-CIV-038)
23. **Using the forum's choice-of-law rules after a § 1404 transfer.** The transferee applies the transferor's rules (Van Dusen; Ferens) — unless the transfer enforced a forum-selection clause (Atlantic Marine). (R-CIV-033, R-CIV-040)
24. **Using Rule 3 to save a state-law claim.** State law decides when a state claim is "commenced" for limitations (Walker). (R-CIV-040)

## Pleadings, Rule 11, joinder
25. **Rule 12 waiver traps.** PJ, venue, process and service are waived if omitted from the first Rule 12 motion; 12(b)(6) and Rule 19 defenses survive to trial; SMJ is never waived. (R-CIV-047)
26. **Rule 11 sanctions without the safe harbor.** A motion filed before 21 days after service must be denied; Rule 11 does not apply to discovery papers. (R-CIV-051, R-CIV-050)
27. **"Crossclaims are compulsory."** Only counterclaims arising from the same transaction are compulsory; crossclaims are always permissive. (R-CIV-052)
28. **Impleader for "it's his fault, not mine."** Impleader requires derivative liability (indemnity, contribution); a person solely liable to the plaintiff is a defense, not a third-party claim. (R-CIV-056)
29. **Joint tortfeasors as required parties.** They are permissive parties (Temple). (R-CIV-054)
30. **Relation back focuses on the plaintiff's diligence.** Krupski asks what the new defendant knew or should have known within 90 days. (R-CIV-049)

## Discovery
31. **Old scope language.** The federal test is relevance to a claim or defense **plus proportionality**, not "reasonably calculated to lead to admissible evidence" (that phrase survives in California). (R-CIV-064, R-CIV-110)
32. **All witness statements are opinion work product.** Factual statements are ordinary work product, discoverable on substantial need and undue hardship. (R-CIV-066)
33. **Consulting expert reports are freely discoverable.** Only on exceptional circumstances. (R-CIV-067)
34. **Rule 35 without a motion.** Federal exams always require a court order on good cause; California permits one physical exam by demand. (R-CIV-070, R-CIV-110)
35. **Adverse-inference instruction for negligent ESI loss.** Rule 37(e)(2) requires intent to deprive. (R-CIV-071)
36. **Skipping meet and confer.** A motion to compel without certification fails and forfeits expenses. (R-CIV-073)

## Motions, trial, judgments, appeals
37. **Renewed JMOL without a pre-verdict motion.** No 50(a) motion → no 50(b) motion → no appellate sufficiency review (Unitherm). (R-CIV-088)
38. **Additur in federal court.** Forbidden (Dimick); remittitur is allowed with the option of a new trial. California allows both. (R-CIV-089, R-CIV-117)
39. **Extending the 28-day deadlines.** Rules 50(b), 52(b) and 59 deadlines cannot be extended (Rule 6(b)(2)). (R-CIV-088, R-CIV-089)
40. **Rule 60(b)(1)–(3) after more than one year.** Barred; and even a void-judgment motion must be made within a reasonable time (Coney Island). (R-CIV-090)
41. **Judge decides credibility on summary judgment or JMOL.** Never; only on a new-trial motion may the judge weigh evidence. (R-CIV-085, R-CIV-087, R-CIV-089)
42. **Equitable claim tried first.** In federal court, common issues go to the jury first (Beacon); California often does the reverse. (R-CIV-079, R-CIV-116)
43. **Issue preclusion from a default judgment.** Defaults are on the merits for claim preclusion but nothing was "actually litigated" for issue preclusion. (R-CIV-094, R-CIV-095)
44. **Binding a non-party by "virtual representation".** Rejected in Taylor v. Sturgell. (R-CIV-097)
45. **A pending appeal stops preclusion.** Not in federal court (majority); yes in California. (R-CIV-094, R-CIV-115)
46. **Appealing a privilege ruling as a collateral order.** Not allowed (Mohawk); use mandamus, § 1292(b) or contempt. (R-CIV-101)
47. **Appealing a TRO.** Generally not appealable, unless it operates as a preliminary injunction. (R-CIV-043, R-CIV-100)

## California-specific traps
48. **Using Rule 12(b)(6) language in superior court.** Use "general demurrer" and fact pleading. (R-CIV-106)
49. **Forgetting the compulsory cross-complaint.** A related claim against the plaintiff is lost if not pleaded with the answer (§ 426.30). (R-CIV-109)
50. **Assuming automatic initial disclosures in California.** They are owed only on demand. (R-CIV-111)
51. **Unanimity in California civil juries.** Three-fourths suffices. (R-CIV-116)
52. **Federal summary-judgment timing in California.** 81 days' notice, separate statement, summary adjudication only of whole causes of action. (R-CIV-113)
