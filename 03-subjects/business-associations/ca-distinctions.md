# Business Associations — California distinctions

**Which law the essay wants.** California's written section uses "legal theories and principles of general application" unless a question asks for California law [S-077]. The Business Associations calls in the 2012–2026 index do not show a California-law instruction (e.g., "On what theories can the shareholders sue …?"; "Who is liable for …?") [VERIFY V-700]. So:

1. **Lead with the general rule** (Restatement of Agency, RUPA/ULPA/RULLCA, MBCA, Delaware case law).
2. **Add one California sentence** where the Corporations Code clearly differs, using the last column below. Graders are California lawyers, and selected answers often cite California sections, but an answer built only on California law risks missing the general rule the call asks for [VERIFY V-071].
3. **If a call does say "according to California law,"** flip the order: California rule first, then a sentence on the majority rule where it differs.

**Where California is the same.** Agency (common law plus Civil Code §§ 2295–2356), general partnerships (California's 1994 UPA tracks RUPA section by section), veil-piercing's two-prong test, the business judgment rule, corporate opportunity and federal securities law are substantively the same. Write the general rule and, at most, cite the California section in parentheses.

Rule IDs point to [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md); the full treatment is in [`outline.md`](outline.md).
