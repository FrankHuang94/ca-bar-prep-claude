# Business Associations — essay templates

**How these were built.** The skeletons follow the calls in `06-past-exams/index.csv` (2012–2026) and the selected-answer patterns in `00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §5: headings follow the call; threshold issues first; entity liability before individual liability; a short rule, specific facts, and a hedged conclusion for every issue. Selected-answer headings were not captured in Phase 3, so the structures are my synthesis [VERIFY V-070]. Rule IDs point to [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md).

**Paragraph pattern (every issue).**
> **Heading.** Rule in one or two sentences (cite the authority). Facts that satisfy each element, with the opposing argument where the facts cut both ways. "Therefore, X is likely liable/not liable for …"

**Time.** 60 minutes per essay: about 15 to outline, 45 to write. In liability-chain essays with four or more debts, allocate time by how many facts each debt has.

## Template 1 — "Who is liable for each obligation?" (liability chain)
*Calls like:* "Who is liable for the bank loan, the supplier contract, the second loan, and the customer's injuries?" (Jul 2025 Q3); "On what theories might each owner be personally liable to the landlord?" (Feb 2022 Q5).

```
I. [Obligation 1: Delta loan]
   A. Nature of the business at the time (timeline!)
      1. General partnership formed? (R-BUS-021, R-BUS-022)  → conclusion
   B. Is the business bound?
      1. Partner's authority — ordinary course; notice of limits (R-BUS-025)
      2. (If agent) actual → apparent → ratification (R-BUS-008 to R-BUS-010)
   C. Who is personally liable?
      1. Partners — joint and several; exhaustion (R-BUS-028, R-BUS-029)
      2. Partner who joined later (R-BUS-030)
   D. Conclusion for each defendant (business, A, B, C)
II. [Obligation 2: contract signed before incorporation]
   A. Promoter liability of the signer (R-BUS-054)
   B. Corporation's liability — adoption (express/implied) (R-BUS-055)
   C. Novation? (R-BUS-055)
   D. Co-owners' liability as partners pre-incorporation (R-BUS-021, R-BUS-028)
   E. De facto / estoppel if filing defective (R-BUS-052, R-BUS-053)
   F. Conclusion per defendant
III. [Obligation 3: debt after incorporation]
   A. Corporation bound? officer authority (R-BUS-060)
   B. Shareholders shielded (R-BUS-046) → veil piercing? (R-BUS-047, R-BUS-048)
   C. Directors' liability to creditor? BJR; no duty to creditors (R-BUS-062)
   D. Conclusion
IV. [Tort injury]
   A. Tortfeasor's own liability (R-BUS-019)
   B. Employee? scope of employment (R-BUS-002, R-BUS-014 to R-BUS-016)
   C. Entity liability (respondeat superior / § 16305) (R-BUS-027)
   D. Owners: partners liable vs. corporate shield (R-BUS-028, R-BUS-046)
   E. Conclusion per defendant
```
**Tip.** Put the date of filing at the top of your scratch outline. Every obligation is analyzed under the entity that existed when it arose.

## Template 2 — "Which defendants are liable for the accident?" (tort + firm + partners)
*Call like:* "Which defendants — the attorney, the firm, the partners — might reasonably be held liable, and why?" (Jul 2023 Q1).

```
I. [Tortfeasor] — personal liability
   A. Underlying tort (negligence elements, briefly; cross-reference Torts)
   B. Conclusion
II. [Firm]
   A. Entity type: general partnership vs. LLP vs. corporation (R-BUS-021, R-BUS-044)
   B. Tortfeasor's status: partner (§ 16305) or employee (respondeat superior)
   C. Scope: ordinary course / scope of employment — argue both sides on the policy facts (required phone, errand, commute) (R-BUS-014, R-BUS-015, R-BUS-027)
   D. Conclusion
III. [Each other partner]
   A. Joint and several liability (R-BUS-028) — or LLP shield (R-BUS-045)
   B. Exhaustion (R-BUS-029)
   C. Any direct negligence (supervision, the phone policy) (R-BUS-018)
   D. Conclusion per partner
```

## Template 3 — "On what theories can shareholders sue the directors?" (director by director)
*Calls like:* "On what theories can shareholders sue directors A and B, and director C, and what are the likely results?" (Jul 2024 Q1); dividend decision and a shareholder's suit over a dividend and a donation (Oct 2020 Q2).

```
I. Procedure (once, up front)
   A. Direct vs. derivative (R-BUS-081)
   B. Standing: contemporaneous / continuous ownership; CA § 800(b)(1) (R-BUS-082)
   C. Demand or futility: MBCA universal demand; Zuckerberg; CA § 800(b)(2) (R-BUS-083)
   D. CA security for expenses up to $50,000 (R-BUS-084)
II. Directors A and B (grouped only if their facts are identical)
   A. Duty of care — informed? reasonable inquiry? reliance? (R-BUS-061)
   B. Business judgment rule — presumption; rebutted? (R-BUS-062)
   C. Duty of loyalty — interest? independence? (R-BUS-064)
      1. Interested-director safe harbors: MBCA; CA § 310 just and reasonable (R-BUS-065, R-BUS-066)
      2. Corporate opportunity / waste / donation (R-BUS-058, R-BUS-067, R-BUS-068)
   D. Defenses — exculpation (CA § 204(a)(10) carve-outs); indemnification (R-BUS-070)
   E. Conclusion
III. Director C (the absent or uninformed director)
   A. Care / oversight (Caremark; Francis) (R-BUS-061, R-BUS-063)
   B. Exculpation exclusions: reckless disregard, pattern of inattention
   C. Causation
   D. Conclusion
IV. Remedies — damages, rescission, constructive trust, injunction (R-REM-044, R-REM-055)
```
**Dividend variant (Oct 2020 Q2).** Under II: (1) BJR on whether to pay (R-BUS-079); (2) legality under §§ 500–501 / MBCA § 6.40 (R-BUS-080); (3) director liability for unlawful distributions. Add a separate heading for the **inspection demand** (R-BUS-078) if the call lists it.

## Template 4 — "Can the creditor reach the owners personally?" (veil piercing)
*Calls like:* "On what theories may Examco recover from Testco, Inc. and its owners?" (Feb 2012 Q4); "Can Supplier recover from Art personally?" (Feb 2017 Q4); "What claims does Supplier have, what defenses do the owners and the corporation have?" (Jul 2026 Q4).

```
I. Creditor v. Corporation
   A. Underlying claim (contract breach / tort) — brief
   B. Corporation's liability; defenses
II. Creditor v. Owner 1
   A. Limited liability default (R-BUS-046)
   B. Defective incorporation? (R-BUS-052, R-BUS-053) — if facts suggest
   C. Promoter liability? (R-BUS-054) — if pre-filing contract
   D. Veil piercing (R-BUS-047, R-BUS-048)
      1. Unity of interest — undercapitalization; commingling; formalities; domination (one paragraph per factor, with facts)
      2. Inequitable result — siphoning, evasion; tort vs. contract creditor
      3. CA overlays — § 300(e) close corporations; § 17703.04 LLCs
   E. Own tort / guarantee (R-BUS-050)
   F. Defenses (passive shareholder; creditor assumed risk)
   G. Conclusion
III. Creditor v. Owner 2 (repeat; distinguish active vs. passive)
```

## Template 5 — "Can the insider collect his loan in bankruptcy?" (BUS-PAST-001)
*Call like:* "Can Art collect his $50,000 loan to Retail in bankruptcy?" (Feb 2017 Q4).

```
I. Is the advance a debt or equity? — recharacterization factors (R-BUS-100)
II. Equitable subordination (R-BUS-099)
   A. Rule: 11 U.S.C. § 510(c); Mobile Steel three prongs; Pepper v. Litton; Deep Rock
   B. Insider status (§ 101(31))
   C. Inequitable conduct — undercapitalization + control + self-preference
   D. Harm to creditors / unfair advantage
   E. Consistent with the Code
III. Avoidance of insider repayments within one year (§ 547(b)(4)(B))
IV. Conclusion — likely subordinated/recharacterized; Art paid after outside creditors
```

## Template 6 — "What type of entity, and is the firm bound?" (entity + agency)
*Call like:* "What type of entity is the law firm, what is the status of its associates and senior attorneys, and is the firm bound by an attorney's agreement?" (Feb 2020 Q5).

```
I. Entity type
   A. No filing → general partnership (R-BUS-021)
   B. Not an LLP / law corporation / LLC (California professional limits) (R-BUS-044, R-BUS-108)
   C. Conclusion
II. Status of each group of workers
   A. Associates — employees (no profit share, no control) (R-BUS-002, R-BUS-022)
   B. "Senior attorneys" — partners? (profit share presumption; control; capital) (R-BUS-022)
   C. Partnership by estoppel if held out as "partners" (R-BUS-023)
III. Is the firm bound by the agreement?
   A. If signer is a partner — § 16301 ordinary course; notice (R-BUS-025)
   B. If signer is an employee — actual / apparent authority; ratification (R-BUS-008 to R-BUS-010)
   C. Personal liability of the signer (R-BUS-012, R-BUS-013)
   D. Partners' personal liability for the obligation (R-BUS-028)
```

## Template 7 — "Enjoin the corporation / sue the officer" (ultra vires + duties + close corporation)
*Call like:* validity of a shareholder voting agreement; whether a buyer of shares is bound; theories to enjoin a switch from televisions to bicycles; damages against the president (Jul 2022 Q4) [S-814].

```
I. Voting agreement — valid? specifically enforceable? close corporation rules (R-BUS-076, R-BUS-094)
II. Buyer bound? — transfer restriction / agreement noted on certificate or actual knowledge (R-BUS-077)
III. Injunction against the change of business
   A. Ultra vires — articles purpose clause; who may sue; equitable limits (R-BUS-057)
   B. Fundamental change requiring shareholder vote? (sale of substantially all assets; amendment of articles) (R-BUS-085)
   C. Direct vs. derivative; injunction elements (R-BUS-081; R-REM-055)
IV. Damages against the officer
   A. Care and BJR (R-BUS-061, R-BUS-062)
   B. Loyalty — personal interest in the bicycle business? (R-BUS-064, R-BUS-067)
   C. Derivative procedure (R-BUS-082 to R-BUS-084)
   D. Conclusion
```

## Template 8 — BA + PR crossover (lawyer, client and the firm)
*Call like:* "What ethical violations has the lawyer committed, and what claims does the client have against the lawyer, the LLP and the other partner?" (Jul 2014 Q3; California and ABA authorities).

```
I. Ethics (PR template: duty by duty, California vs. ABA) — trading on client information: loyalty, confidentiality, business transactions (R-PRF-026; see PR essay templates)
II. Client's claims against the lawyer
   A. Securities — misappropriation; Rule 14e-3 (R-BUS-091)
   B. Breach of fiduciary duty (disgorgement, constructive trust) (R-REM-044)
III. Client's claims against the LLP — § 16305 ordinary course (R-BUS-027)
IV. Client's claims against the other partner — LLP shield; own participation or supervision (R-BUS-045)
```
