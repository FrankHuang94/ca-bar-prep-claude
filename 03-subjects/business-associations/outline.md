# Business Associations — outline

**Scope and structure.** This outline follows the State Bar's Business Associations essay outline: I Agency, II General Partnerships, III Limited Partnerships, IV Limited Liability Partnerships, V Corporations, VI Other Business Structures [S-085]. The numbering below the category level (V.A.2, V.C, V.D.3, V.E.5, V.F) follows the matrix rows, whose wording was paraphrased from a search summary and has not been checked against the outline PDF [VERIFY V-024]. Tort liability (respondeat superior) is added under Agency because essays test it, and a section on insider loans in bankruptcy is added for the Feb 2017 essay (`BUS-PAST-001`).

**How to use it.** Each topic gives the rule, the authority, splits between general law and California, and how the topic appears on the essay. The exam-ready wording of each rule is in [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md) (cited as R-BUS-###). California differences are collected in [`ca-distinctions.md`](ca-distinctions.md).

Business Associations calls in the 2012–2026 index do not show a California-law instruction [VERIFY V-700]. Because the State Bar's graders are California lawyers, add one sentence of California law where the Corporations Code clearly differs (cumulative voting, distributions, derivative-suit bond, LP control rule, LLC agency, § 2115). ## I. Agency (formation; principal-agent relationship; termination; contract and tort liability)

Agency is the engine of the whole subject: partners, LLC members, officers and employees bind their businesses only through agency principles. The two great questions are **contract liability** (is the principal bound to the third party?) and **tort liability** (is the principal vicariously liable?).

### I.A Formation of the agency relationship
- **Definition.** Agency = the fiduciary relationship that arises when a principal manifests assent that an agent act on its behalf and subject to its control, and the agent consents (R3d Agency § 1.01 [VERIFY V-701]; R-BUS-001). California: Civ. Code § 2295 ("one who represents another … in dealings with third persons") [VERIFY V-702].
- **Three elements.** (1) Assent (objective manifestations); (2) acting on the principal's behalf; (3) the principal's **right to control**. Compensation, a writing and the word "agent" are unnecessary. A gratuitous agent is still a fiduciary.
- **Capacity.** The principal needs capacity to do the act itself (R3d § 3.04); an agent needs only minimal capacity (§ 3.05) [VERIFY V-701].
- **Formalities.** None, except the **equal dignities rule**: authority to make a contract that must be in writing must itself be in writing (Civ. Code § 2309; R-CTR-048).
- **Employee vs. independent contractor** (R-BUS-002). The right to control the manner and means of the work is decisive; R2d Agency § 220 factors; R3d § 7.07(3) [S-810]; *S.G. Borello & Sons, Inc. v. Department of Industrial Relations* (1989) 48 Cal.3d 341. Labels do not control. California's ABC test (*Dynamex* (2018) 4 Cal.5th 903; Lab. Code § 2775) governs Labor Code status, not common-law respondeat superior.
- **How tested.** Usually embedded: "Is the firm bound by the attorney's agreement?" (Feb 2020 Q5); "Who is liable for Peter's injuries?" (Jul 2025 Q3). Start with "Was X an agent (or employee) of Y?" in one or two sentences, then move to authority or scope.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: 委托代理 in the PRC Civil Code covers the contract side; US agency also covers vicarious tort liability and imposes fiduciary duties by status (approx.).

### I.B Principal-agent relationship: duties owed
**Agent's duties to the principal** (R3d §§ 8.01–8.12 [VERIFY V-701]):

| Duty | Content | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Loyalty | No secret profits/benefits from third parties; no acting as or for an adverse party; no competition during the agency; no use of the principal's property or confidential information for own purposes | R-BUS-003 |
| Care, competence, diligence | Standard of agents in similar circumstances; special skills count | R-BUS-004 |
| Obedience | Act within actual authority; follow lawful, reasonable instructions | R-BUS-004 |
| Disclosure (notice) | Provide information the principal would want | R-BUS-004 |
| Accounting | Account for money and property; no commingling | R-BUS-004 |

- **Consent cures** loyalty problems only if given in good faith after full disclosure and the transaction is fair.
- **After termination** the agent may compete but may not use trade secrets or confidential customer information; *Bancroft-Whitney Co. v. Glen* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 327 (officer may not, while still employed, secretly recruit colleagues for a competitor using confidential data).
- **Remedies**: damages; disgorgement of profits and constructive trust (R-REM-044, R-REM-037); forfeiture of compensation; rescission.

**Principal's duties to the agent** (R-BUS-005): compensation as agreed; reimbursement and indemnification for authorized payments and liabilities (R3d §§ 8.13–8.15 [VERIFY V-701]; Lab. Code § 2802 for employees); good faith and fair dealing.

**Imputed notice** (R-BUS-020): what the agent knows within the scope of the agency is imputed to the principal, except where the agent acts adversely to the principal (R3d §§ 5.01–5.04 [VERIFY V-701]; Civ. Code § 2332).

### I.C Termination of agency
- **Actual authority ends** (R-BUS-006): (1) by agreement (term expires, purpose accomplished); (2) by either party's act — the principal's **power** to revoke and the agent's power to renounce exist even when exercise breaches a contract (remedy is damages); (3) changed circumstances the agent should realize make the act unwanted; (4) operation of law — death (effective when the agent has notice under R3d § 3.07), loss of capacity, impossibility, illegality (R3d §§ 3.06–3.10 [VERIFY V-701]; Civ. Code §§ 2355–2356 [VERIFY V-702]).
- **Irrevocable powers**: a power given as security (power coupled with an interest) survives revocation and the principal's death (R3d § 3.12–3.13).
- **Lingering apparent authority** (R-BUS-007): after termination, the agent may still bind the principal to a third party who reasonably believes authority continues and lacks notice (R3d § 3.11). Cure: actual notice to known dealers; reasonable public notice for others.
- **How tested.** A fired employee or departed partner signs a contract; ask whether the third party had notice.

### I.D Contract liability of principal and agent
Work through the sources of authority **in this order** (see [`flowcharts.md`](flowcharts.md)):

1. **Actual express authority** — words from principal **to agent** (R-BUS-008).
2. **Actual implied authority** — acts reasonably necessary or incidental to express authority, customary for the position, implied by past acquiescence, or needed in an emergency (R3d §§ 2.01–2.02 [S-810]).
3. **Apparent authority** — the principal's manifestation **to the third party** creates a reasonable belief in authority (R3d §§ 2.03, 3.03; Civ. Code §§ 2300, 2317 "ostensible authority") (R-BUS-009). Sources of manifestation: titles and positions, prior course of dealing, documents, failing to correct a known misimpression. **The agent alone cannot create it.** Secret limits do not bind third parties without notice.
4. **Ratification** — affirmance with knowledge of material facts, by express assent or acceptance of benefits; relates back; all-or-nothing; requires capacity at both times (R3d §§ 4.01–4.08; Civ. Code §§ 2307, 2310) (R-BUS-010).
5. **Estoppel and undisclosed principals** — principal who carelessly caused, or failed to correct, a belief in agency is estopped to deny it to a relying third party; an undisclosed principal is liable for an agent's acts within the usual scope of such an agent (R3d §§ 2.05–2.06; *Watteau v. Fenwick* [1893] 1 Q.B. 346; R2d "inherent agency power" §§ 8A, 194) (R-BUS-011).

**Agent's own liability on the contract** (R-BUS-012, R-BUS-013):

| Principal's status at contracting | Principal bound? | Agent bound? |
|---|---|---|
| **Disclosed** (existence and identity known) | Yes, if authorized | No (unless agent agreed) |
| **Unidentified / partially disclosed** (existence known, identity not) | Yes, if authorized | **Yes** |
| **Undisclosed** (third party thinks agent is the principal) | Yes, once discovered (if authorized or under R3d § 2.06) | **Yes** |
| **No authority, no ratification** | No | Yes, for breach of the **implied warranty of authority** (R3d § 6.10) |

- California: Civ. Code § 2343 (agent personally responsible when the agent lacks good-faith belief in authority, or credit was given to the agent personally) [VERIFY V-702].
- **Agent's liability to the principal**: an agent who binds the principal by apparent authority while exceeding actual authority must indemnify the principal for resulting loss (duty of obedience).
- **How tested.** Feb 2020 Q5 (is the law firm bound by an attorney's agreement?); Jul 2025 Q3 (Big Shoe contract signed by one owner); Feb 2022 Q5 (lease signed by one co-owner). Selected answers reach authority issues after first fixing the entity type [VERIFY V-071].

### I.E Tort liability: respondeat superior and independent contractors
**Vicarious liability** (R-BUS-014). An employer is liable for an employee's torts committed within the scope of employment (R3d §§ 2.04, 7.07 [S-810]; R2d §§ 219, 228; Civ. Code § 2338).

- **Step 1 — employee?** Right-to-control test (R-BUS-002).
- **Step 2 — scope of employment?** R2d § 228: (a) the kind of work employed to perform; (b) occurs substantially within authorized time and space limits; (c) actuated at least in part by a purpose to serve the employer; (d) if force is used, not unexpectable. R3d § 7.07(2): work assigned or a course of conduct subject to the employer's control; **outside** scope when an **independent course of conduct not intended to serve any purpose of the employer** [S-810].
- **California formulation.** The risk must be "typical of or broadly incidental to" the enterprise; for intentional torts, the tort must be "engendered by" or arise from the employment, and foreseeable in that sense (*Lisa M. v. Henry Mayo Newhall Memorial Hospital* (1995) 12 Cal.4th 291; *Mary M. v. City of Los Angeles* (1991) 54 Cal.3d 202).
- **Forbidden acts** done in the course of the job remain within scope (*Perez v. Van Groningen & Sons, Inc.* (1986) 41 Cal.3d 962: tractor driver gave a forbidden ride).
- **Frolic vs. detour** (R-BUS-015): minor deviation = within scope; substantial personal departure = outside until return.
- **Going and coming rule**: commuting is outside scope; exceptions for paid travel time (*Hinman v. Westinghouse Electric Co.* (1970) 2 Cal.3d 956), employer-required vehicle (*Moradi v. Marsh USA, Inc.* (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 886 [VERIFY V-727]), special errands, dual-purpose trips.
- **Intentional torts** (R-BUS-016): within scope if motivated in part to serve the employer, or force is natural to the job (bouncers), or (California) engendered by the work.

**Independent contractors** (R-BUS-017). No vicarious liability, except nondelegable duties, inherently or abnormally dangerous activities, and the hirer's own negligent selection or supervision (Restatement (Second) of Torts §§ 409–429). California limits "peculiar risk" liability to the contractor's own employees (*Privette v. Superior Court* (1993) 5 Cal.4th 689).

**Direct liability** (R-BUS-018): negligent hiring, training, supervision or retention; authorized or ratified torts; torts accomplished through apparent authority (fraud by a branch manager) (R3d §§ 7.03, 7.05, 7.08 [VERIFY V-701]).

**Agent's personal liability** (R-BUS-019): the employee-tortfeasor is always personally liable; respondeat superior adds a defendant.

**Partnership overlay.** For a **partner's** tort, use Corp. Code § 16305 (ordinary course of partnership business) rather than respondeat superior, then § 16306 for the partners' personal liability (R-BUS-027, R-BUS-028). For an **associate's** or employee's tort, use respondeat superior to reach the firm, then § 16306 to reach the partners (Jul 2023 Q1).

**How tested.** Jul 2023 Q1 asked which defendants (the attorney, the firm, the partners) were liable for a car accident; the firm required lawyers to carry and answer phones at all times [VERIFY V-071]. Jul 2025 Q3 part (d) asked who was liable for a customer's injuries. Answer defendant by defendant: tortfeasor → entity (respondeat superior or § 16305) → owners (§ 16306, LLP shield, corporate shield).

## II. General Partnerships (formation; partner liability; partner rights; partnership authority; dissolution)

California's Uniform Partnership Act of 1994, Corp. Code § 16100 et seq., follows RUPA (1997) and uses the same section numbers after "16" (RUPA § 202 = § 16202). General law and California are therefore essentially the same; cite the Corporations Code.

### II.A Formation (including partnership by estoppel)
- **Default entity.** "The association of two or more persons to carry on as coowners a business for profit forms a partnership, whether or not the persons intend to form a partnership" (Corp. Code § 16202(a); R-BUS-021). No filing; an oral agreement or conduct suffices (*Holmes v. Lerner* (1999) 74 Cal.App.4th 442).
- **Indicia.** Sharing profits (presumption, § 16202(c)(3); R-BUS-022); joint control or management; capital contributions; sharing losses; joint ownership of business property; holding out as partners; tax filings.
- **Not enough alone.** Co-ownership of property; sharing gross returns; receiving profits as debt repayment, wages, rent, annuity, loan interest or price of goodwill (§ 16202(c)).
- **Partnership by estoppel** (R-BUS-023; § 16308). Liability of a person who represents himself, or consents to being represented, as a partner, to a third party who relies by extending credit or entering a transaction. Consent requires more than silence about someone else's statement. If all partners consent, the purported partner's acts bind the partnership.
- **Entity status.** A partnership is an entity distinct from its partners (§ 16201); partnership property belongs to the partnership (§§ 16203–16204, 16501) (R-BUS-024).
- **How tested.** The first issue whenever owners "go into business together" without filing (Feb 2020 Q5; Feb 2022 Q5; Jul 2025 Q3). Also the period **before** a corporation is formed: co-owners who operate the business pre-incorporation are partners, so the partnership (and each partner) is liable for pre-incorporation debts, separately from promoter liability (Jul 2025 Q3 Delta loan) [VERIFY V-071].

### II.B Partner liability
- **Partnership's liability for partners' wrongs** (R-BUS-027): § 16305(a) — wrongful act or omission of a partner in the ordinary course of partnership business or with authority; § 16305(b) — misapplication of a third party's money received within apparent authority.
- **Partners' personal liability** (R-BUS-028): all partners jointly and severally liable for all partnership obligations (§ 16306(a)).
- **Exhaustion** (R-BUS-029): § 16307(d) — creditor must first obtain a judgment against the partnership and have execution returned unsatisfied, unless bankruptcy, agreement, court permission (assets clearly insufficient or exhaustion excessively burdensome), or liability imposed on the partner independently (own tort).
- **Incoming partner** (R-BUS-030): not personally liable for pre-admission obligations (§ 16306(b)); capital is at risk.
- **Dissociated partner**: liable for pre-dissociation obligations; may be liable for post-dissociation obligations to third parties who reasonably believed he was still a partner (RUPA two-year window; statement of dissociation) (§§ 16702–16704) [VERIFY V-723].
- **LLP shield** (§ 16306(c)) — see IV.
- **Contribution among partners**: a partner who pays more than his share is entitled to indemnity from the partnership and contribution through the settlement of accounts (§§ 16401(c), 16807).

### II.C Partner rights (management, profits, information, property)
| Default rule (absent agreement) | Corp. Code | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Equal management rights (per capita) | § 16401(f) | R-BUS-031 |
| Ordinary matters by majority; outside ordinary course and amendments unanimous | § 16401(j) | R-BUS-031 |
| Admission of new partner unanimous | § 16401(i) | R-BUS-031 |
| Equal share of profits; losses follow profits | § 16401(b) | R-BUS-032 |
| No salary (except winding up) | § 16401(h) | R-BUS-032 |
| Reimbursement and indemnity for ordinary-course payments; interest on advances | § 16401(c)–(e) | R-BUS-032 |
| Access to books; information | § 16403 | R-BUS-033 |
| Action for accounting and other relief | § 16405 | R-BUS-033 |
| Transferable interest = right to distributions only; charging order | §§ 16502–16504 | R-BUS-033 |

- **Capital-only vs. services-only partner.** Statutory default: losses shared like profits (§ 16401(b)). *Kovacik v. Reed* (1957) 49 Cal.2d 166 [S-818]: where one partner contributed money and the other only services and nothing was said about losses, the services partner does not contribute toward the capital loss — survival under the 1994 Act is unsettled [VERIFY V-706]. Argue both.

### II.D Partnership authority
- **Statutory apparent authority** (R-BUS-025): every partner is an agent of the partnership; an act "for apparently carrying on in the ordinary course the partnership business or business of the kind carried on by the partnership" binds it unless the partner lacked authority **and** the third party knew or had received notification of that (§ 16301(1)).
- **Extraordinary acts** (R-BUS-026): bind only with actual authority — i.e., all partners' consent (§§ 16301(2), 16401(j)).
- **Statement of partnership authority** (§ 16303): a filed grant of authority protects third parties who rely on it; a filed **limitation on authority to transfer real property** is constructive notice; other limitations bind only those who know of them.
- **After dissolution**: a partner binds the partnership for acts appropriate for winding up, and for other acts if the third party lacked notice of the dissolution (§ 16804).
- **How tested.** Owner A signs a large inventory contract over B's objection: within the ordinary course of a shoe store? Did the supplier know of the limit? (Jul 2025 Q3.) A law-firm partner signs an agreement with an outsider: is it the kind of business the firm carries on? (Feb 2020 Q5.)

### II.E Fiduciary duties of partners
- **Loyalty** (R-BUS-034; § 16404(b)) — exclusive list: (1) account for profits, property and **partnership opportunities**; (2) no dealing as or for an adverse party; (3) no competition before dissolution. *Meinhard v. Salmon*, 249 N.Y. 458, 164 N.E. 545 (1928): co-adventurers owe "the punctilio of an honor the most sensitive."
- **Care** (R-BUS-035; § 16404(c)) — refrain from **grossly negligent or reckless** conduct, intentional misconduct or knowing violation of law.
- **Good faith and fair dealing** (§ 16404(d)); pursuing one's own interest is not itself a breach (§ 16404(e)).
- **Limits on contracting out** (§ 16103(b)): the agreement may not eliminate loyalty (but may identify specific non-violating activities if not manifestly unreasonable, and partners may authorize after disclosure), may not unreasonably reduce care, may not eliminate good faith.
- **How tested.** Feb 2022 Q5 asked whether the inventor was liable to the investor; if they were partners before incorporating, partner duties (and, after incorporation, director/officer duties) frame the claim [VERIFY V-071]. Remedies: accounting (R-REM-054), constructive trust (R-REM-044), damages.

### II.F Dissociation, dissolution and winding up
- **Dissociation events** (R-BUS-036; § 16601): notice of express will to withdraw; agreed event; expulsion (agreement; unanimous vote in listed cases; judicial); bankruptcy; death; incapacity; entity partner's dissolution.
- **Wrongful dissociation** (§ 16602): breach of an express term; or, in a term/undertaking partnership, before expiration (by withdrawal, judicial expulsion, bankruptcy), except withdrawal within 90 days after another partner's death or wrongful dissociation. Wrongful dissociator is liable for damages.
- **Effect** (R-BUS-037; §§ 16603, 16701): either (a) dissolution and winding up (Article 8) **or** (b) buyout (Article 7) at the greater of liquidation value or going-concern value without the dissociated partner, less damages; wrongful dissociator from a term partnership may have to wait until term ends.
- **Dissolution events** (R-BUS-038; § 16801): at-will partnership — notice of a partner's express will to withdraw; term partnership — after death/wrongful dissociation if half the remaining partners vote within 90 days to wind up, express will of all partners, or expiration; agreed event; illegality; judicial decree.
- **Continuation** (§ 16802): after dissolution, all partners (except a wrongful dissociator) may waive winding up.
- **Winding up and settlement** (R-BUS-039; §§ 16803–16807): pay creditors (including partner-creditors); then settle capital accounts; partners with negative accounts contribute; shortfalls shared like losses.
- **UPA (1914) contrast.** Under the old UPA, any partner's departure dissolved the partnership; RUPA separates dissociation from dissolution.

## III. Limited Partnerships

California: Uniform Limited Partnership Act of 2008, Corp. Code § 15900 et seq. (based on ULPA 2001, with differences) [VERIFY V-703].

- **Formation** (R-BUS-040): filing a certificate of limited partnership with the Secretary of State; at least one general partner and one limited partner. No filing → general partnership.
- **General partner** (R-BUS-041): manages; agent of the LP; personally, jointly and severally liable for LP obligations; owes loyalty and care (same contours as II.E). Using a corporation or LLC as the general partner limits human exposure, subject to veil piercing.
- **Limited partner shield** (R-BUS-042):
  - ULPA (2001) § 303: no personal liability **even if** the limited partner participates in management and control (control rule abolished).
  - Revised ULPA (1976/1985) § 303: control rule — liability to persons who reasonably believed, based on the limited partner's conduct, that he was a general partner; safe-harbor list.
  - **California § 15903.03**: keeps a narrow control rule — a limited partner who participates in control may be liable as a general partner only to persons transacting with the LP with **actual knowledge** of that participation and a **reasonable belief**, based on the limited partner's conduct, that the limited partner is a general partner [S-808] [VERIFY V-703].
  - Other personal exposure: own torts; unpaid contribution obligations; name of limited partner used in LP name (under some statutes) [VERIFY V-703].
- **Limited partners' rights and duties** (R-BUS-043): information; voting on reserved matters; direct and derivative actions (demand or futility); no fiduciary duty solely as a limited partner.
- **Dissociation and dissolution** (R-BUS-043): limited partners generally cannot withdraw early; dissolution on agreed events, consent, loss of last GP without replacement, or judicial decree.
- **LLLP**: some states let an LP elect limited liability limited partnership status, shielding the general partner; whether California allows a domestic LLLP is not confirmed [VERIFY V-703].
- **How tested.** Rarely alone; appears in entity-comparison calls and as a trap ("the investor helped run the business — is she still shielded?").

## IV. Limited Liability Partnerships

- **Formation** (R-BUS-044): a general partnership files a registration (statement of qualification) after partner approval (RUPA §§ 1001–1003). California: registered LLPs only for law, public accountancy, architecture, engineering and land surveying, and the LLP must maintain security for claims (insurance or equivalent) (Corp. Code §§ 16101, 16953, 16956) [S-809] [VERIFY V-704] [VERIFY V-705].
- **Shield** (R-BUS-045): partners not personally liable for partnership obligations incurred while registered, whether in contract or tort, solely because they are partners (§ 16306(c); RUPA full shield) [S-809].
- **What the shield does not cover**: the partnership's own liability; each partner's own torts, malpractice and (depending on statute) supervision failures; personal guarantees; obligations incurred before registration [VERIFY V-705].
- **How tested.** Jul 2014 Q3 (a PR crossover) asked what claims a client had against the lawyer, the LLP and the other partner after the lawyer traded on the client's tender-offer plans [VERIFY V-071]: the trading lawyer is liable personally; the LLP is liable (§ 16305); the other partner is shielded unless he participated or (arguably) failed to supervise. Feb 2020 Q5 is the inverse: three lawyers who never registered are a general partnership with unlimited liability.
- **Law-firm entity choice in California** (R-BUS-108): law corporation (Moscone-Knox Act, Corp. Code § 13400 et seq.; Bus. & Prof. Code § 6160 et seq.) or registered LLP; a California LLC may not render professional services (Corp. Code § 17701.04(e)) [S-826].

## V. Corporations

**Sources of law.** General principles = MBCA plus Delaware case law (the leading body of corporate law) and the common law. California = General Corporation Law, Corp. Code § 100 et seq. Section V is the most-tested part of the subject: formation and promoters, veil piercing, director duties, shareholder suits and insider trading.

**Essay order for "who is liable / what claims" calls.** (1) Entity status (de jure, de facto, estoppel, or partnership) → (2) entity's liability (authority; respondeat superior) → (3) owners' and managers' personal liability (promoter, veil piercing, own torts, fiduciary breaches) → (4) procedure (direct vs. derivative) → (5) remedies. See [`issue-checklist.md`](issue-checklist.md).

### V.A Limited liability and its limits

#### V.A.1 The corporation as a separate entity; shareholder limited liability
- A corporation is a legal person; shareholders' risk is limited to their investment (MBCA § 6.22; Corp. Code § 200(c)) (R-BUS-046).
- Directors and officers are not liable for corporate obligations merely by holding office.
- **Personal-liability exceptions** to test in every owner-liability call: (1) promoter liability (V.C.3); (2) veil piercing (V.A.2); (3) own torts (V.A.3); (4) personal guarantee or contract in own name; (5) unlawful distributions received or approved (V.E.4); (6) fiduciary breach (V.D.3); (7) defective incorporation (V.C.2); (8) unpaid share subscriptions (watered stock).
- **Watered stock.** Shares issued for less than the consideration the board fixed: the recipient owes the shortfall to the corporation. Under the MBCA (§ 6.21) and California (§ 409), the board's good-faith valuation of non-cash consideration is conclusive, so watered-stock claims are now rare.

#### V.A.2 Piercing the corporate veil (alter ego)
**Rule** (R-BUS-047). Courts disregard the entity when (1) there is such unity of interest and ownership that the separate personalities of the corporation and owner no longer exist, and (2) respecting the entity would sanction fraud or promote injustice (*Associated Vendors, Inc. v. Oakland Meat Co.* (1962) 210 Cal.App.2d 825; *Mesler v. Bragg Management Co.* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 290; *Sonora Diamond Corp. v. Superior Court* (2000) 83 Cal.App.4th 523).

**Factors** (R-BUS-048):

| Factor | What to look for in the facts |
|---|---|
| **Undercapitalization** | Capital tiny relative to foreseeable risks and debts ($1,000 for a store; $50 a share); no insurance; owner funds by "loans" rather than equity (*Automotriz del Golfo de California v. Resnick* (1957) 47 Cal.2d 792; *Minton v. Cavaney* (1961) 56 Cal.2d 576) |
| **Commingling** | Personal bills paid from corporate account; one bank account; corporate assets used personally |
| **Domination / instrumentality** | Owner treats the corporation as an extension of self; no independent decision-making |
| **Disregard of formalities** | No meetings, minutes, elections, bylaws, issued shares, separate records |
| **Diversion of assets** | Moving money out to owners or affiliates as creditors go unpaid |
| **Holding out** | Owner tells creditors he personally stands behind the debts, or blurs which entity contracts |
| **Same owners/offices/employees** | Among related entities (enterprise theory) |

**Second prong — injustice.** Evading an existing obligation, defrauding creditors, siphoning, or misleading creditors. Mere inability to collect is not enough.

**Distinctions to argue.**
- **Tort vs. contract creditor**: easier for tort victims (involuntary creditors); a contract creditor who extended credit without a guarantee assumed the risk (*Walkovszky v. Carlton*, 18 N.Y.2d 414 (1966) — taxi fleet split into two-cab corporations; insufficient without showing personal use).
- **Active vs. passive shareholders**: only those who participated in the abuse are liable.
- **Close vs. public corporations**: virtually never pierced for public companies.
- **California statutory overlays**: statutory close corporation — failure to observe formalities under a § 300(b) agreement is not a factor (§ 300(e)); LLCs — failure to hold meetings is not a factor unless required (§ 17703.04) [S-805].
- **Parent-subsidiary and enterprise liability; reverse piercing** (R-BUS-049): *Sea-Land Services, Inc. v. Pepper Source*, 941 F.2d 519 (7th Cir. 1991); California rejected outside reverse piercing of corporations (*Postal Instant Press, Inc. v. Kaswa Corp.* (2008) 162 Cal.App.4th 1510) but allowed it for an LLC (*Curci Investments, LLC v. Baldwin* (2017) 14 Cal.App.5th 214) [VERIFY V-724].

**How tested.** Feb 2012 Q4 (owners paid $50 each for their shares); Feb 2017 Q4 (president of thinly capitalized Retail, Inc.); Jul 2026 Q4 ($1,000 of capital, no formalities). Selected answers open with limited liability, then go factor by factor with the facts, then the injustice prong, then a conclusion for each owner [VERIFY V-071].

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Company Law's 法人人格否认 (piercing for abuse of independent status to evade debts) is the closest analogue; US courts weigh many factors case by case rather than applying a statutory test (approx.).

#### V.A.3 Personal liability of directors, officers and shareholders for their own wrongs
- A person who personally commits, participates in, or directs a tort is liable regardless of the corporate shield (*Frances T. v. Village Green Owners Assn.* (1986) 42 Cal.3d 490) (R-BUS-050).
- Officers who sign in their own names, or without disclosing the corporate principal, are bound as agents (R-BUS-012).
- Feb 2015 Q5 (libel by newsletter owners): analyze the tort against the business and against each owner who wrote or published the story; then entity status for the contract claim [VERIFY V-071].

### V.B Corporate powers, ultra vires and officers' authority
- **Purposes and powers** (R-BUS-057): any lawful business unless the articles limit it (Corp. Code § 206; MBCA § 3.01); powers of a natural person (§ 207; MBCA § 3.02), including guarantees, borrowing, and **charitable donations** for the public welfare (§ 207(e)) (R-BUS-058; *A.P. Smith Mfg. Co. v. Barlow*, 98 A.2d 581 (N.J. 1953); *Theodora Holding Corp. v. Henderson*, 257 A.2d 398 (Del. Ch. 1969)).
- **Ultra vires** (Corp. Code § 208; MBCA § 3.04): an act beyond a purpose limitation is valid as to third parties; it may be challenged only by (1) a shareholder suit to enjoin the act, (2) the corporation (or shareholders derivatively) against directors and officers, (3) the state (dissolution or injunction). Under MBCA § 3.04(b), an injunction against performance of a contract may issue if equitable and the court may award the other party compensation for loss (not lost profits) [VERIFY V-728].
- **Officers' authority** (R-BUS-060): agency rules — actual (bylaws, resolutions, inherent in office), apparent (title, course of dealing), ratification. President/CEO: ordinary-course matters only. California § 313: an instrument signed by two designated officers (one from each group) binds the corporation as to a person without actual knowledge of a lack of authority [VERIFY V-728]. Officers: chair or president, secretary and CFO (Corp. Code § 312).
- **How tested.** Jul 2022 Q4: articles stated a TV-manufacturing purpose; the president moved the company into bicycles; a shareholder sought an injunction and damages [S-814]. Structure: ultra vires (purpose clause) → shareholder injunction (equitable factors; third-party contracts) → fiduciary claims against the president (care, BJR, loyalty) → direct vs. derivative.

### V.C Formation (articles; promoters; defective incorporation)

#### V.C.1 De jure incorporation
- Articles executed and filed with the Secretary of State; existence begins on filing (Corp. Code § 200(c); MBCA § 2.03) (R-BUS-051).
- **Mandatory contents** (Corp. Code § 202): name; general purpose statement; agent for service of process; number of authorized shares (and classes/series rights if more than one class). MBCA § 2.02(a): name, authorized shares, registered office and agent, incorporators.
- **Optional contents** (Corp. Code § 204): limits on purposes; cumulative voting changes (listed corporations); supermajority requirements; transfer restrictions; preemptive rights (only if the articles grant them, § 204(a)(2)); director exculpation (§ 204(a)(10)); expanded indemnification (§ 204(a)(11)).
- **Bylaws** (Corp. Code § 212): number of directors, meetings, officers; adopted/amended by shareholders, and by the board unless restricted.
- **Organization**: incorporators or initial directors adopt bylaws, elect officers, issue shares.

#### V.C.2 Defective incorporation: de facto corporation and corporation by estoppel
| Doctrine | Requirements | Effect | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **De facto corporation** (R-BUS-052) | (1) statute available; (2) good-faith, colorable attempt to incorporate; (3) actual exercise of corporate powers | Treated as a corporation against everyone but the state | MBCA § 2.04: those who act as a corporation **knowing** there is no incorporation are jointly and severally liable; California's current treatment unconfirmed [S-823] [VERIFY V-713] |
| **Corporation by estoppel** (R-BUS-053) | Third party dealt with the business as a corporation and relied on corporate credit | Third party estopped to deny corporate status (and the business estopped to deny it) | Contract claims only; not tort victims; weak where owners knew no articles were filed |

- **If neither applies**: owners who acted for the business are personally liable as **partners** (two or more co-owners → general partnership; R-BUS-021) or as agents/promoters (R-BUS-054).
- **How tested.** Owners who "thought they had incorporated" (articles lost or never filed); Feb 2015 Q5 and Feb 2022 Q5 (unconfirmed) [VERIFY V-071].

#### V.C.3 Promoters and pre-incorporation contracts
- **Promoter's personal liability** (R-BUS-054): a person who contracts for a corporation not yet formed is personally liable, even after the corporation forms and adopts the contract, unless the contract shows the other party agreed to look only to the corporation, or a novation occurs (R3d Agency § 6.04 [VERIFY V-714]; MBCA § 2.04).
- **Corporation's liability** (R-BUS-055): none until **adoption** — express (board resolution) or implied (knowing acceptance of benefits). Adoption operates from the date of adoption; it is not true ratification because the principal did not exist (*McArthur v. Times Printing Co.* (Minn. 1892) [VERIFY V-714]).
- **Release of the promoter**: only by **novation** — the third party, the corporation and the promoter agree to substitute the corporation (R-CTR-088). Adoption alone does not release.
- **After adoption**: both promoter and corporation liable; promoter may seek indemnification from the corporation.
- **Promoters' fiduciary duties** (R-BUS-056): to the future corporation and co-promoters; no secret profits on sales to the corporation; disclosure to an independent board or all shareholders (*Old Dominion Copper Mining & Smelting Co. v. Lewisohn*, 210 U.S. 206 (1908)).
- **Multiple promoters**: co-promoters working together pre-incorporation may be partners, jointly liable for each other's pre-incorporation contracts (R-BUS-021, R-BUS-028).
- **How tested.** Feb 2022 Q5: one owner signed a lease for an unformed corporation, which later voted to assume it; the call asked for each owner's personal liability to the landlord [VERIFY V-071]. Jul 2025 Q3: debts before and after incorporation. Answer in sequence: promoter liability of the signer → partnership liability of the co-owner → corporation's adoption → novation? → conclusion per owner.

### V.D Directors and officers

#### V.D.1 Board structure and action
- Business and affairs managed by or under the direction of the board (Corp. Code § 300(a); MBCA § 8.01; DGCL § 141(a)) (R-BUS-059).
- **Election**: annually at the shareholders' meeting (Corp. Code § 301), by plurality; cumulative voting in California (V.E.1).
- **Meetings**: regular and special; notice for special meetings per bylaws; quorum = majority of the authorized number unless bylaws set lower (not below one-third or two); act by majority of directors present at a meeting with quorum; unanimous written consent without meeting; telephone/electronic participation (Corp. Code § 307; MBCA §§ 8.20–8.24).
- **Committees** may exercise board powers except fundamental matters (e.g., approving actions requiring shareholder approval, filling board vacancies in some cases, amending bylaws, distributions except within limits) (Corp. Code § 311; MBCA § 8.25).
- **Removal and vacancies** (R-BUS-075; Corp. Code §§ 303–305; MBCA § 8.08; DGCL § 141(k)).
- **Individual directors** have no authority as agents by virtue of the office.

#### V.D.2 Officers
- Required: chair or president, secretary, chief financial officer (Corp. Code § 312); others as bylaws provide. Officers are agents (R-BUS-060). Officers owe the same fiduciary duties as directors (and in Delaware are subject to the same care/loyalty standards; California's exculpation does not cover officers acting as officers).

#### V.D.3 Fiduciary duties - care and loyalty (business judgment rule; interested-director transactions; corporate opportunity; controlling shareholders)
**Essay sequence for each director**: (1) duty of care → (2) BJR protection? → (3) duty of loyalty (conflict?) → (4) safe harbors / fairness → (5) defenses (exculpation, reliance) → (6) remedy and who sues.

**(a) Duty of care** (R-BUS-061)
- Standard: good faith; reasonable belief in the corporation's best interests; care, **including reasonable inquiry**, of an ordinarily prudent person in a like position (Corp. Code § 309(a); MBCA § 8.30).
- **Reliance** on officers, employees, counsel, experts and committees believed reliable and competent, without knowledge making reliance unwarranted (Corp. Code § 309(b); MBCA § 8.30(e)–(f); DGCL § 141(e)).
- Liability standard: MBCA § 8.31 (bad faith, not informed, lack of objectivity, sustained failure to oversee, improper benefit); Delaware: **gross negligence** for uninformed decisions (*Smith v. Van Gorkom*, 488 A.2d 858 (Del. 1985) — board approved a merger after a two-hour meeting without valuation work); passive director liable for total inattention (*Francis v. United Jersey Bank*, 432 A.2d 814 (N.J. 1981)).
- **Causation**: most courts require the breach to have caused the loss.
- **Oversight (Caremark)** (R-BUS-063): liability only for utter failure to implement reporting systems or conscious disregard of red flags (*In re Caremark*, 698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996); *Stone v. Ritter*, 911 A.2d 362 (Del. 2006); *Marchand v. Barnhill*, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019)). California: *Kanter v. Reed* (2023) treated a *Caremark*-type theory as consistent with § 204(a)(10)'s "reckless disregard" and "pattern of inattention" exclusions [S-817] [VERIFY V-721].

**(b) Business judgment rule** (R-BUS-062)
- Presumption that disinterested, independent directors acted on an informed basis, in good faith, in the honest belief the action was in the corporation's best interests (*Aronson v. Lewis*, 473 A.2d 805 (Del. 1984)).
- Rebutted by: fraud, illegality, conflict of interest, bad faith, failure to inform (gross negligence), or waste (no rational business purpose).
- Classic illustrations: *Shlensky v. Wrigley*, 237 N.E.2d 776 (Ill. App. 1968) (no night games); *Dodge v. Ford Motor Co.*, 170 N.W. 668 (Mich. 1919) (court ordered dividend where controller openly ran the company for non-shareholder aims); *Kamin v. American Express Co.*, 383 N.Y.S.2d 807 (Sup. Ct. 1976) (dividend-in-kind decision protected); *In re Walt Disney Co. Derivative Litigation*, 906 A.2d 27 (Del. 2006) (bad faith requires more than gross negligence).
- **California**: § 309 is a statutory BJR (a director who meets it has no liability, § 309(c)); California courts also apply a common-law BJR (*Lee v. Interinsurance Exchange* (1996) 50 Cal.App.4th 694; *Gaillard v. Natomas Co.* (1989) 208 Cal.App.3d 1250; *Oakland Raiders v. National Football League* (2001) 93 Cal.App.4th 572).
- **How tested.** Oct 2020 Q2 ($20 million dividend; $100,000 university donation); Jul 2024 Q1 (directors Alex, Baker and Cate); Jul 2025 Q3 (selected answer applied the BJR to the Echo loan) [VERIFY V-071]. Graders want: rule → whether the directors were informed (meeting length, documents, experts) → disinterest → conclusion per director.

**(c) Duty of loyalty — overview** (R-BUS-064)
- A fiduciary may not put personal interests ahead of the corporation's (*Guth v. Loft, Inc.*, 5 A.2d 503 (Del. 1939)). Good faith is a component of loyalty (*Stone v. Ritter*).
- Situations: self-dealing/interested transactions; corporate opportunities; competition; misuse of information; compensation and waste; controlling-shareholder transactions.

**(d) Interested-director (self-dealing) transactions**
| Approach | Safe harbor | Effect | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **California § 310(a)** [S-803] | (1) disclosure + approval by disinterested **shareholders** (interested shares not counted); or (2) disclosure + approval by disinterested **board** majority **and** transaction just and reasonable when approved; or (3) proponent proves just and reasonable | Not void or voidable because of the interest | R-BUS-065 |
| **MBCA §§ 8.60–8.63** | Disclosure + approval by qualified directors or qualified shares; or fair to the corporation | No damages/equitable relief on ground of the interest | R-BUS-066 |
| **Delaware § 144 (pre-2025)** and case law | Disclosure + disinterested director or stockholder approval → BJR; otherwise **entire fairness** (fair dealing + fair price) (*Benihana*, 906 A.2d 114 (Del. 2006)) | | R-BUS-066 |
| **Delaware § 144 (2025, SB 21)** | Statutory safe harbors: disinterested-director committee **or** disinterested-stockholder approval; both for controller going-private deals; "controlling stockholder" defined [S-811] [S-812] [VERIFY V-716] | Bars equitable relief and damages if conditions met | R-BUS-066 |

- **Material financial interest** is required; a common directorship alone is not (Corp. Code § 310(a), (b)).
- **Quorum**: interested directors may be counted for the quorum (Corp. Code § 310(c)) [VERIFY V-726].
- **Burden under § 310(a)(2)** (who must prove just and reasonable) is not confirmed [VERIFY V-726].

**(e) Corporate opportunity** (R-BUS-067)
- Tests: **line of business** and **interest or expectancy**, plus the corporation's financial ability (*Guth*; *Broz v. Cellular Information Systems, Inc.*, 673 A.2d 148 (Del. 1996) — no need to formally present an opportunity the corporation could not or would not take, though presentation creates a safe harbor); ALI § 5.05 (offered to the fiduciary in corporate capacity, or closely related to the business; must first offer with disclosure and obtain rejection); MBCA § 8.70 (safe harbor by disclosure and disinterested disclaimer).
- California: existence is primarily a question of fact; courts use the same factors (*Kelegian v. Mgrdichian* (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 982) [S-821] [VERIFY V-725]. LLC managers: § 17704.09(b) codifies the duty.
- **Remedy**: constructive trust over the opportunity and its profits (R-REM-044), or damages.

**(f) Competition, compensation, waste** (R-BUS-068): no competing while serving; no secret recruitment of employees for a competitor (*Bancroft-Whitney Co. v. Glen* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 327); compensation fixed by interested directors must be fair; **waste** = exchange so one-sided that no reasonable business person would approve (*Brehm v. Eisner*, 746 A.2d 244 (Del. 2000)).

**(g) Loans to directors and officers** (R-BUS-069): California § 315 requires shareholder approval (benefited director's shares excluded), or board approval under a bylaw in corporations with 100+ holders [VERIFY V-712]; public companies: federal ban (15 U.S.C. § 78m(k)).

**(h) Controlling shareholders' duties** (R-BUS-071, R-BUS-072)
- *Jones v. H.F. Ahmanson & Co.* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 93: majority shareholders may not use control to benefit themselves alone or to the detriment of the minority; use of control must benefit all shareholders proportionately.
- *Sinclair Oil Corp. v. Levien*, 280 A.2d 717 (Del. 1971): entire fairness only where the parent receives a benefit to the exclusion of the minority (self-dealing); pro-rata dividends get BJR.
- Freeze-outs: entire fairness (*Weinberger v. UOP, Inc.*, 457 A.2d 701 (Del. 1983)); BJR if conditioned ab initio on independent-committee and majority-of-minority approval (*Kahn v. M&F Worldwide Corp.*, 88 A.3d 635 (Del. 2014)); 2025 § 144 safe harbors (above).
- Sale of control at a premium: generally allowed; liability for selling to looters or for selling a corporate asset (*Perlman v. Feldmann*, 219 F.2d 173 (2d Cir. 1955)).

**(i) Exculpation and indemnification** (R-BUS-070)
- Exculpation: Corp. Code § 204(a)(10) (directors only; exclusions for intentional misconduct, bad faith, improper personal benefit, reckless disregard, unexcused pattern of inattention, § 310 transactions, § 316 distributions) [S-817]; DGCL § 102(b)(7) (directors; officers for direct claims since 2022); MBCA § 2.02(b)(4).
- Indemnification: Corp. Code § 317; DGCL § 145; MBCA §§ 8.50–8.59 — mandatory if successful on the merits; permissive on good faith and reasonable belief; derivative-suit judgments need court approval; advancement of expenses with an undertaking to repay.
- **D&O insurance** may cover what indemnification cannot.

**(j) Remedies for fiduciary breach**: damages; rescission of self-dealing contracts; disgorgement and constructive trust; injunction against the transaction (R-REM-055); removal of directors (court, Corp. Code § 304).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC directors owe 忠实义务 (loyalty) and 勤勉义务 (diligence) under the Company Law; the US business judgment rule has no exact PRC equivalent — it is a strong presumption that keeps courts out of informed, disinterested decisions (approx.).

#### V.D.4 Directors' duties to creditors
- Directors owe fiduciary duties to the corporation and shareholders, not to creditors; on insolvency, creditors gain standing to sue derivatively, but no direct duty arises in California beyond the narrow trust-fund doctrine prohibiting diversion of assets to insiders (*Berg & Berg Enterprises, LLC v. Boyle* (2009) 178 Cal.App.4th 1020).
- Creditors' tools instead: veil piercing, fraudulent transfer, unlawful distribution statutes (§§ 316, 506), equitable subordination in bankruptcy (V.J).

### V.E Shareholders

#### V.E.1 Meetings, voting and elections
- Annual meeting to elect directors; special meetings called by board, chair, president or 10% holders (Corp. Code § 600) (R-BUS-073).
- Notice 10–60 days (Corp. Code § 601); quorum majority of shares (articles may reduce to not less than one-third) (§ 602); one vote per share (§ 700); written consent by required votes (§ 603), but director elections only by unanimous written consent (except vacancies).
- **"Approved by the shareholders"** (§ 153: majority of shares represented and voting) vs. **"approved by the outstanding shares"** (§ 152: majority of all outstanding voting shares) — fundamental changes use § 152.
- **Cumulative voting** (R-BUS-074): mandatory in California on notice at the meeting (Corp. Code § 708), opt-out for listed corporations (§ 301.5); opt-in under the MBCA (§ 7.28) and Delaware (§ 214). Formula: shares needed to elect *n* directors = (S × n)/(D + 1) + 1.
- **Class voting** on amendments affecting a class (Corp. Code § 903; MBCA § 10.04).
- **Shareholder proposals and proxy rules** (federal, Exchange Act § 14(a), Rule 14a-8) apply to public companies only; rarely tested.

#### V.E.2 Voting agreements, voting trusts, proxies and transfer restrictions
- **Voting (pooling) agreements** (R-BUS-076): written, signed; specifically enforceable (MBCA § 7.31; DGCL § 218(c); Corp. Code § 706 [VERIFY V-719]).
- **Voting trusts**: title to shares transferred to trustee; written agreement filed with the corporation; limited duration (MBCA § 7.30; DGCL § 218(a); Corp. Code § 706(b) [VERIFY V-719]).
- **Proxies** (Corp. Code § 705; MBCA § 7.22; DGCL § 212): revocable unless "irrevocable" and coupled with an interest; valid 11 months by default (California, MBCA) or 3 years (Delaware).
- **Agreements binding directors' discretion**: traditionally invalid outside close corporations (*McQuade v. Stoneham*, 263 N.Y. 323 (1934)); valid in close corporations by statute (Corp. Code § 300(b); MBCA § 7.32).
- **Transfer restrictions** (R-BUS-077): valid if reasonable and noted conspicuously on the certificate, or the transferee has actual knowledge (Corp. Code §§ 204(b), 418; Cal. Com. Code § 8204; MBCA § 6.27; DGCL § 202).
- **How tested.** Jul 2022 Q4 asked whether a shareholder voting agreement was valid and whether a later buyer of shares was bound by it [S-814].

#### V.E.3 Inspection rights
- California (R-BUS-078): any shareholder, written demand, purpose reasonably related to interests as a shareholder → accounting books, records, minutes (Corp. Code § 1601); 5% holders (or 1% + SEC filing) → absolute right to shareholder list; others for proper purpose (§ 1600); **directors → absolute right** (§ 1602); court enforcement and costs (§§ 1603–1604).
- Delaware § 220: sworn written demand; proper purpose; scope narrowed by 2025 amendments (defined "books and records"; conditions) [S-811] [VERIFY V-716].
- MBCA § 16.02: basic records on 5 business days' notice; other records require good faith, proper purpose described with reasonable particularity, and records directly connected to the purpose.
- **Proper purposes**: valuing shares; investigating mismanagement or waste; communicating with shareholders about a proxy contest; evaluating a derivative claim. **Improper**: harassment, aiding a competitor, purely personal agendas.
- **How tested.** Oct 2020 Q2 (shareholder demanded corporate records after a disputed dividend and donation) [VERIFY V-071].

#### V.E.4 Distributions (dividends and repurchases)
- **Board discretion** (R-BUS-079): no right to a dividend until declared; courts compel only for bad faith or abuse (*Dodge v. Ford*). Declared cash dividend = corporate debt.
- **Legal limits** (R-BUS-080):

| Regime | Test |
|---|---|
| **California** (§§ 500–501, post-2012) [S-800] [S-801] | (a) retained earnings ≥ distribution + preferential arrears, **or** (b) after distribution, assets ≥ liabilities + senior preferential rights; **and** not likely unable to meet liabilities as they mature |
| **MBCA** § 6.40(c) | (1) able to pay debts as they come due (equity insolvency) **and** (2) assets ≥ liabilities + preferential rights (balance sheet) |
| **Delaware** §§ 170, 160 | Out of **surplus**, or if none, net profits of the current and/or preceding fiscal year ("nimble dividends"); repurchases may not impair capital |

- **Liability**: directors who approve unlawful distributions are jointly and severally liable to the corporation for the benefit of creditors/senior shareholders (Corp. Code § 316(a)(1); DGCL § 174), subject to good-faith reliance (§ 309(b)); shareholders who receive with knowledge of the impropriety must repay (Corp. Code § 506).
- **How tested.** Oct 2020 Q2 board split over a $20 million dividend: BJR on whether to pay; statutory limits on whether it may be paid [VERIFY V-071].

#### V.E.5 Derivative actions (direct vs. derivative; standing; demand; security for expenses)
**(a) Direct vs. derivative** (R-BUS-081): whose injury, and who gets the recovery? (*Tooley v. Donaldson, Lufkin & Jenrette, Inc.*, 845 A.2d 1031 (Del. 2004).)

| Typically **derivative** | Typically **direct** |
|---|---|
| Breach of care causing corporate loss; waste; self-dealing; usurped opportunity; excessive compensation; illegal distributions harming the corporation | Denial of voting or inspection rights; refusal to pay a declared dividend; dilution or freeze-out of the minority; disclosure violations in a vote; enforcing a shareholder agreement; *Jones v. Ahmanson*-type exclusion of the minority |

**(b) Standing** (R-BUS-082)
- **Contemporaneous ownership** at the time of the wrong (or by operation of law from such a holder) (MBCA § 7.41; DGCL § 327; Fed. R. Civ. P. 23.1).
- **California exception** (Corp. Code § 800(b)(1)): the court may permit a non-contemporaneous owner on finding a strong prima facie case, no similar action, acquisition before disclosure of the wrong, that the defendant would otherwise retain a gain from willful breach, and no unjust enrichment [VERIFY V-707].
- **Continuous ownership** through the litigation (*Grosset v. Wenaas* (2008) 42 Cal.4th 1100).
- **Adequate representation** of the corporation's interests.

**(c) Demand** (R-BUS-083)
- **MBCA § 7.42 (universal demand)**: written demand in every case; wait 90 days unless rejected earlier or irreparable injury.
- **Delaware**: demand required unless futile. Old tests: *Aronson* (board that approved the transaction: reasonable doubt that directors are disinterested/independent, or that the transaction was a valid exercise of business judgment) and *Rales* (all other cases). Since *United Food & Commercial Workers Union v. Zuckerberg* (Del. 2021), one director-by-director test: demand is futile if at least half the board (1) received a material personal benefit, (2) faces a substantial likelihood of liability (exculpated care claims do not count), or (3) lacks independence from someone who did [S-813] [VERIFY V-715].
- **California** (§ 800(b)(2)): plead with particularity the efforts to obtain board action, or the reasons for not trying, and that the plaintiff informed the corporation or board in writing of the ultimate facts or delivered a true copy of the complaint. Courts look to Delaware tests as persuasive (*Shields v. Singleton* (1993) 15 Cal.App.4th 1611; *Bader v. Anderson* (2009) 179 Cal.App.4th 775) [S-824]; *Tola v. Bryant* applied *Zuckerberg* (to a Delaware company) [S-825] [VERIFY V-715].
- **Demand refused**: review under BJR (wrongful refusal); in Delaware, making demand concedes independence.

**(d) Security for expenses (California)** (R-BUS-084)
- Within 30 days after service, the corporation or a defendant may move for a bond on the ground there is no reasonable possibility the action will benefit the corporation or its shareholders, or (for a defendant) that it did not participate in the transaction; bond for reasonable expenses including attorney's fees; **cap $50,000 total** (Corp. Code § 800(c)–(d); *Hale v. Southern California IPA Medical Group, Inc.* (2001) 86 Cal.App.4th 919) [S-802] [VERIFY V-707]. Failure to post → dismissal. The MBCA and Delaware have no such requirement.

**(e) Special litigation committees and dismissal**: independent committee's good-faith, informed recommendation to dismiss gets deference (*Auerbach v. Bennett*, 47 N.Y.2d 619 (1979)); in Delaware, where demand was excused, the court also applies its own business judgment (*Zapata Corp. v. Maldonado*, 430 A.2d 779 (Del. 1981)); MBCA § 7.44.

**(f) Recovery, settlement, fees**: recovery goes to the corporation; settlement or dismissal needs court approval and notice to shareholders (MBCA § 7.45; Fed. R. Civ. P. 23.1); successful plaintiff's counsel paid from the common fund or by the corporation under the substantial-benefit doctrine.

**How tested.** Oct 2020 Q2 (shareholder sued over dividend and donation); Jul 2024 Q1 ("on what theories can shareholders sue directors Alex and Baker, and director Cate?") [VERIFY V-071]. Write: derivative vs. direct → standing → demand/futility → bond (California) → merits (care/BJR/loyalty) → remedy.

#### V.E.6 Shareholder liability
- Shareholders are not liable for corporate debts except: veil piercing; unpaid subscriptions or watered stock; knowing receipt of unlawful distributions (Corp. Code § 506); controlling-shareholder fiduciary breaches (V.D.3(h)).

### V.F Securities (Rule 10b-5; insider trading; Section 16(b))

**Coverage.** Federal securities law applies on any essay, including to private closely held corporations (Rule 10b-5 reaches "any security" if interstate means are used). Section 16(b) applies only to public reporting companies.

#### V.F.1 Rule 10b-5 private action (R-BUS-088)
| Element | Rule | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Material misstatement or omission (or scheme) | Substantial likelihood a reasonable investor would consider it important; probability × magnitude for contingent events; half-truths actionable; pure silence actionable only with a duty to disclose | *TSC Industries*, 426 U.S. 438 (1976); *Basic*, 485 U.S. 224 (1988) |
| Scienter | Intent to deceive, manipulate or defraud; recklessness suffices in most circuits; not negligence | *Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder*, 425 U.S. 185 (1976); PSLRA strong-inference pleading, *Tellabs*, 551 U.S. 308 (2007) |
| In connection with purchase or sale | Plaintiff must be an actual purchaser or seller | *Blue Chip Stamps*, 421 U.S. 723 (1975) |
| Reliance | Presumed: fraud on the market (efficient market, public statement); omissions with a duty to disclose | *Basic*; *Affiliated Ute*, 406 U.S. 128 (1972); *Halliburton Co. v. Erica P. John Fund, Inc.*, 573 U.S. 258 (2014) |
| Economic loss and loss causation | Misstatement caused the loss (inflated price alone is not loss) | *Dura Pharmaceuticals*, 544 U.S. 336 (2005) |
| Jurisdictional means | Mail, phone, internet, exchange | 15 U.S.C. § 78j |

- **Who is liable**: the "maker" (ultimate authority over the statement) under 10b-5(b) (*Janus*, 564 U.S. 135 (2011)); no private aiding-and-abetting (*Central Bank of Denver*, 511 U.S. 164 (1994)); scheme liability for disseminating false statements (*Lorenzo v. SEC*, 587 U.S. 71 (2019)).
- **Limitations**: 2 years after discovery / 5 years after violation (28 U.S.C. § 1658(b)).
- **Remedies**: damages (out-of-pocket); rescission in privity cases.

#### V.F.2 Insider trading
| Theory | Duty owed to | Who | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Classical** | Shareholders of the issuer (counterparties) | Directors, officers, employees, controlling holders; **temporary insiders** (lawyers, accountants, bankers) | Disclose or abstain (*Chiarella*, 445 U.S. 222 (1980); *Dirks* fn. 14) — R-BUS-089 |
| **Tipper/tippee** | Derivative of the tipper's duty | Tippee who knew or should have known the tipper breached a duty for personal benefit | *Dirks*, 463 U.S. 646 (1983); *Salman*, 580 U.S. 39 (2016) (gift to relative/friend = benefit) — R-BUS-090 |
| **Misappropriation** | The **source** of the information | Outsiders entrusted with information (lawyer of the bidder; employee; family member under Rule 10b5-2) | *O'Hagan*, 521 U.S. 642 (1997) — R-BUS-091 |
| **Rule 14e-3** | None needed | Anyone trading on nonpublic tender-offer information from offeror/target | 17 C.F.R. § 240.14e-3 (upheld in *O'Hagan*) — R-BUS-091 |

- **"On the basis of"**: awareness of MNPI when trading (Rule 10b5-1(b)); affirmative defense for trades under a pre-arranged plan, with 2022 cooling-off and certification requirements [VERIFY V-718].
- **Rule 10b5-2** duties of trust and confidence: agreement to keep confidential; history, pattern or practice of sharing confidences; spouse, parent, child or sibling (rebuttable).
- **Sanctions**: SEC injunction and civil penalty up to 3× profit gained/loss avoided (15 U.S.C. § 78u-1); criminal (15 U.S.C. § 78ff); contemporaneous traders' private action capped at profit (15 U.S.C. § 78t-1); state law: corporation may recover insider's profits for breach of fiduciary duty (*Diamond v. Oreamuno*, 24 N.Y.2d 494 (1969)).
- **California** (R-BUS-093): Corp. Code § 25402 prohibition; § 25502 counterparty damages; § 25502.5 issuer recovery up to three times the profit [S-827] [VERIFY V-722].
- **How tested.** Jul 2014 Q3 (PR crossover): lawyer bought shares of a tender-offer target using a client's plans [VERIFY V-071]. Analyze: misappropriation (duty to client-source), Rule 14e-3, PR duties (R-PRF-026; confidentiality).

#### V.F.3 Section 16(b) short-swing profits (R-BUS-092)
- **Who**: directors and officers (status at purchase **or** sale); >10% beneficial owners (at **both** purchase and sale; *Foremost-McKesson*, 423 U.S. 232 (1976); *Reliance Electric*, 404 U.S. 418 (1972)).
- **What**: any purchase and sale (or sale and purchase) of the issuer's equity securities within **less than six months**; strict liability.
- **Profit**: lowest-in, highest-out matching; losses not netted (*Smolowe v. Delendo Corp.*, 136 F.2d 231 (2d Cir. 1943)).
- **Enforcement**: issuer; shareholder after 60-day demand or if issuer fails to prosecute diligently; plaintiff need only own a security at filing and keep a financial stake (*Gollust v. Mendell*, 501 U.S. 115 (1991)); 2-year limit.
- **Reporting**: § 16(a) Form 4 within two business days.

#### V.F.4 Registration and other securities topics (low yield)
- Securities Act of 1933 § 5 registration; exemptions (private placements, Regulation D); § 11 and § 12(a)(2) liability; Sarbanes-Oxley certifications. California qualification under Corp. Code § 25110 et seq. Rarely tested on essays; may appear in PTs.

### V.G Fundamental changes and dissenters' rights
- **Procedure** (R-BUS-085): board approval, then shareholders (outstanding shares, § 152), with class votes where applicable.
  - Amendment of articles: Corp. Code §§ 902–903; MBCA §§ 10.03–10.04; DGCL § 242.
  - Mergers and reorganizations: Corp. Code §§ 1101, 1200–1201; exception where the acquirer or its shareholders will hold more than five-sixths of the survivor's voting power [VERIFY V-711]; short-form merger at 90% (§ 1110); MBCA §§ 11.04–11.05; DGCL §§ 251, 253.
  - Sale of all or substantially all assets outside the regular course: Corp. Code § 1001; MBCA § 12.02; DGCL § 271. Selling shareholders vote; buyer's shareholders generally do not.
  - Dissolution: voluntary by 50% of voting power (Corp. Code § 1900); involuntary (V.H).
- **Dissenters' / appraisal rights** (R-BUS-086): Corp. Code §§ 1300–1312 [S-816]; MBCA ch. 13; DGCL § 262. Fair market value as of the day before announcement, excluding effects of the transaction (California). Market-out exception for listed shares [S-816]; the former California 5% carve-back was eliminated in 2012 [VERIFY V-711]. Appraisal is generally exclusive absent fraud, illegality or unfair dealing (Corp. Code § 1312).
- **Successor liability**: an asset buyer generally does not assume the seller's liabilities except by agreement, de facto merger, mere continuation, fraud, or (California, product liability) product-line continuation (*Ray v. Alad Corp.* (1977) 19 Cal.3d 22).
- **Takeover defenses** (R-BUS-087): *Unocal*, 493 A.2d 946 (Del. 1985); *Revlon*, 506 A.2d 173 (Del. 1986); *Corwin*, 125 A.3d 304 (Del. 2015). Low yield on California essays.
- **How tested.** Jul 2022 Q4 (switch from televisions to bicycles: ultra vires; sale of assets or amendment of articles needed?).

### V.H Closely held corporations; non-profit corporations

#### V.H.1 Closely held corporations
- **Features**: few shareholders; no market; owners are managers; reliance on agreements.
- **California statutory close corporation** (R-BUS-094): articles must state "This corporation is a close corporation" and limit holders of record to a specified number not exceeding 35 (Corp. Code § 158); certificates must bear a legend (§ 418); a written shareholders' agreement signed by all shareholders may govern any phase of the business, including management by shareholders without a board (§ 300(b)); shareholders who manage assume directors' liabilities; failure to observe formalities under the agreement is not an alter-ego factor (§ 300(e)); voting agreements (§ 706(a)) [VERIFY V-719].
- **General law**: MBCA § 7.32 shareholder agreements (unanimous; may eliminate the board, set distributions, etc.); DGCL §§ 341–356 statutory close corporations.
- **Fiduciary duties among shareholders** (R-BUS-095): *Donahue v. Rodd Electrotype Co.*, 328 N.E.2d 505 (Mass. 1975) (equal opportunity to sell shares to the corporation); *Wilkes v. Springside Nursing Home, Inc.*, 353 N.E.2d 657 (Mass. 1976) (legitimate business purpose; less harmful alternative); contra *Nixon v. Blackwell*, 626 A.2d 1366 (Del. 1993); California: *Jones v. Ahmanson*.
- **Involuntary dissolution and buyout** (R-BUS-096): Corp. Code § 1800 (standing: holders of one-third, excluding wrongdoers' shares, or any shareholder of a close corporation; grounds: deadlock, persistent fraud/mismanagement/abuse, persistent unfairness, waste, and — with 35 or fewer shareholders — liquidation reasonably necessary to protect the complaining shareholder); § 2000 (corporation or 50% holders may buy out the plaintiffs at fair value) [S-815] [VERIFY V-710]; MBCA §§ 14.30 (oppression), 14.34 (election to purchase).
- **Transfer restrictions and buy-sell agreements** (R-BUS-077) are the standard planning tools.
- **How tested.** Feb 2015 Q5 (close corporation and owners' liability); Jul 2022 Q4 (voting agreement; buyer bound?; enjoin a change of business) [S-814] [VERIFY V-071].

#### V.H.2 Non-profit corporations
- **California Nonprofit Corporation Law** (R-BUS-097): public benefit (Corp. Code § 5110 et seq.), mutual benefit (§ 7110 et seq.) and religious (§ 9110 et seq.) corporations. No shareholders; no distributions of profits; members optional.
- **Directors' duties**: care standard mirroring § 309 (§ 5231); self-dealing transactions regulated (§ 5233: Attorney General or court approval, or board approval meeting statutory conditions); no more than 49% of directors may be interested persons (§ 5227) [S-822] [VERIFY V-717].
- **Enforcement**: the Attorney General supervises charitable assets; standing for members, directors, officers and others listed in § 5142; *Turner v. Victoria* (2023) 15 Cal.5th 99 (former directors keep standing to pursue claims filed while serving) [VERIFY V-717].
- **General law**: Model Nonprofit Corporation Act; charitable-trust principles restrict donated assets.
- **How tested.** Rare on essays; PT Libraries have included nonprofit governance (see [`../../06-past-exams/frequency-analysis.md`](../../06-past-exams/frequency-analysis.md) finding 9).

### V.I Choice of law: internal affairs doctrine and pseudo-foreign corporations
- **Internal affairs doctrine** (R-BUS-098): the state of incorporation's law governs relations among the corporation, its directors, officers and shareholders (*Edgar v. MITE Corp.*, 457 U.S. 624 (1982); *CTS Corp. v. Dynamics Corp. of America*, 481 U.S. 69 (1987)).
- **California § 2115**: applies listed California provisions to a foreign corporation (unlisted) that does more than half its business in California (average of property, payroll, sales factors) with more than half of its voting securities held of record by California residents [S-804] [VERIFY V-708]. Enforced against a Utah corporation (*Wilson v. Louisiana-Pacific Resources, Inc.* (1982)) [S-828]; held inapplicable to a Delaware corporation's internal affairs (*VantagePoint Venture Partners 1996 v. Examen, Inc.*, 871 A.2d 1108 (Del. 2005)) [S-804].
- **Securities insider-trading claims** under Corp. Code § 25502.5 are not internal affairs (*Friese*) [S-827] [VERIFY V-722].
- **How tested.** Only if the facts specify an out-of-state corporation operating mostly in California; one paragraph.

### V.J Insider shareholder loans and equitable subordination in bankruptcy (deep-rock doctrine)
- **Question pattern.** "Can the president/controlling shareholder collect his $50,000 loan to the corporation in its bankruptcy?" (Feb 2017 Q4).
- **Step 1 — allowed claim?** An insider's loan is a claim if it is genuinely debt. **Recharacterization** (R-BUS-100): if in substance equity (no note, no interest, no maturity, repayment only from profits, inadequate capitalization, no outside lender would lend, identity between lender and shareholder), it is treated as a capital contribution and paid after all creditors (*Bayer Corp. v. MascoTech, Inc. (In re AutoStyle Plastics, Inc.)*, 269 F.3d 726 (6th Cir. 2001)); Ninth Circuit decides by reference to state law (*In re Fitness Holdings Int'l, Inc.*, 714 F.3d 1141 (9th Cir. 2013)) [S-820] [VERIFY V-724].
- **Step 2 — equitable subordination** (R-BUS-099): 11 U.S.C. § 510(c) lets the court subordinate an allowed claim to other claims. Test (*In re Mobile Steel Co.*, 563 F.2d 692 (5th Cir. 1977)) [S-820]: (1) inequitable conduct by the claimant; (2) injury to creditors or unfair advantage to the claimant; (3) consistent with the Code. Insiders and fiduciaries get rigorous scrutiny: the court may "sift the circumstances surrounding any claim" so that a fiduciary does not use his position to gain priority (*Pepper v. Litton*, 308 U.S. 295 (1939)). **Deep Rock doctrine**: a parent's claims against an undercapitalized subsidiary it mismanaged subordinated to the subsidiary's preferred shareholders and creditors (*Taylor v. Standard Gas & Electric Co.*, 306 U.S. 307 (1939)). No categorical subordination without case-specific inquiry (*United States v. Noland*, 517 U.S. 535 (1996)).
- **Step 3 — insider preference**: repayment to an insider within **one year** before the petition on an antecedent debt is avoidable (11 U.S.C. § 547(b)(4)(B)); "insider" includes directors, officers, persons in control and their relatives (§ 101(31)).
- **Relation to veil piercing.** The same facts (undercapitalization, commingling) support both; subordination is the bankruptcy-court counterpart that reorders priority rather than imposing personal liability.
- **Conclusion pattern.** If the insider caused or exploited undercapitalization, treated the corporation as his own, or preferred himself, his claim is likely subordinated or recharacterized; a properly documented arm's-length loan to an adequately capitalized company is paid pro rata with other unsecured claims.

## VI. Other Business Structures (limited liability companies; joint ventures; sole proprietorships)

### VI.A Limited liability companies
California: Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act, Corp. Code § 17701.01 et seq. (effective 2014), based on RULLCA (2006) with California changes [VERIFY V-729].

- **Formation** (R-BUS-101): articles of organization filed with the Secretary of State (§ 17702.01); operating agreement (oral, written or implied) governs, subject to nonwaivable rules (§ 17701.10); member-managed unless the articles state manager-managed (§ 17702.01(b)(5) per commentary) [S-806].
- **Professional services**: a California LLC may not render professional services as defined in §§ 13401 and 13401.3 (§ 17701.04(e)) [S-826] — lawyers, doctors, accountants use professional corporations or (lawyers, accountants and listed design professions) LLPs.
- **Liability shield** (R-BUS-102): members and managers not personally liable solely by reason of status (§ 17703.04(a)); alter-ego liability "to the same extent" as corporate shareholders, except failure to hold meetings or observe meeting formalities is not a factor unless the articles or operating agreement require meetings (§ 17703.04(b)) [S-805]. Reverse piercing of an LLC permitted in California (*Curci*) [VERIFY V-724].
- **Management** (R-BUS-103): member-managed — equal rights, majority for ordinary matters, unanimity for extraordinary matters and amending the operating agreement; manager-managed — managers decide, members' consent for listed matters (§ 17704.07) [S-807] [VERIFY V-709].
- **Agency**: California keeps statutory apparent authority — members of a member-managed LLC and managers of a manager-managed LLC bind the LLC for acts apparently carrying on its business in the usual way, unless the third party actually knows of the lack of authority (§ 17703.01) [S-807] [VERIFY V-709]; uniform RULLCA § 301: no agency by status alone (use agency law).
- **Fiduciary duties** (R-BUS-104): loyalty and care (gross negligence, recklessness, intentional misconduct, knowing violation of law) as for partners; in a manager-managed LLC the managers owe them and members do not solely as members (§ 17704.09) [S-806]; good faith and fair dealing; operating-agreement limits (§ 17701.10).
- **Information**: § 17704.10.
- **Distributions**: limits similar to corporations (insolvency/balance-sheet tests) and liability for wrongful distributions (§§ 17704.05–17704.06) [VERIFY V-729].
- **Transfers, dissociation, dissolution** (R-BUS-105): transferable interest = distributions only; charging order exclusive remedy for creditors (§ 17705.03); dissociation does not trigger a buyout under RULLCA; dissolution by agreement, consent, or judicial decree (§§ 17707.01, 17707.03) [VERIFY V-729].
- **Derivative suits**: by members after demand or futility (§ 17709.02) [VERIFY V-729].
- **Tax**: pass-through by default (partnership or disregarded entity) — the reason LLCs dominate small-business formation.
- **How tested.** Usually as part of entity choice or veil piercing; a member's authority or a manager's loyalty may also appear.

### VI.B Joint ventures
- **Definition** (R-BUS-106): an association of two or more persons who combine property, skill or knowledge to carry out a single business enterprise for profit, with an agreement (express or implied) to share profits and losses and a right of joint control (*Holtz v. United Plumbing & Heating Co.* (1957) 49 Cal.2d 501; CACI No. 3712) [S-819].
- **Treated as a partnership** for limited purposes: partnership law governs; each venturer is an agent of the venture within its scope; venturers are jointly and severally liable; partners' fiduciary duties apply (*Meinhard v. Salmon*).
- **Distinguish**: a joint venture is limited to one project; authority is narrower; dissolves on completion.
- **How tested.** Two parties combine resources for one deal (Feb 2022 Q5: investor's cash plus inventor's patent to launch a product) — characterize as JV or partnership, then apply liability and duties [VERIFY V-071].

### VI.C Sole proprietorships
- One owner, no separate entity, unlimited personal liability, owner taxed directly; fictitious business name statement if operating under another name (Bus. & Prof. Code § 17910 et seq.) (R-BUS-107).
- Adding a co-owner who shares profits and control creates a general partnership.

### VI.D Choosing among entity forms (comparison)

| | General partnership | Limited partnership | LLP | Corporation | LLC | Sole proprietorship |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Formation** | No filing; conduct | Certificate filed | GP + registration | Articles filed | Articles of organization filed | None |
| **Owner liability** | Unlimited, joint and several (exhaustion first) | GPs unlimited; LPs shielded (California control rule) | Shield for partnership obligations; own torts personal | Shield; veil piercing | Shield; veil piercing (formalities exception) | Unlimited |
| **Management** | All partners equally | General partners | All partners | Board → officers | Members or managers | Owner |
| **Agency authority** | Each partner (§ 16301) | General partners | Each partner | Officers by agency law | California: members (member-managed) or managers (§ 17703.01) | Owner and agents |
| **Fiduciary duties** | Loyalty, care (gross negligence), good faith (§ 16404) | GPs: same as partners; LPs: none as such | Same as GP | Directors/officers: care (§ 309), loyalty; controllers (*Jones*) | Members (member-managed) or managers (§ 17704.09) | — |
| **Transferability** | Economic interest only | Economic interest only | Economic interest only | Freely transferable shares (subject to restrictions) | Economic interest only | Sale of the business |
| **Exit / continuity** | Dissociation → buyout or dissolution | Limited partners cannot withdraw early | As GP | Perpetual; involuntary dissolution for close corps | Dissociation without buyout; judicial dissolution | Ends with owner |
| **California professional use** | Allowed | Allowed | Only law, accountancy, listed design professions | Professional corporations (law corporations) | **Not** for licensed professions | Allowed |
| **Tax default** | Pass-through | Pass-through | Pass-through | Entity-level (C corp) unless S election | Pass-through | Owner |

**Essay template for "what type of entity is this?"** (Feb 2020 Q5): (1) Was anything filed? (2) If not, two or more co-owners carrying on a business for profit → general partnership (R-BUS-021). (3) Who are the partners (profit-sharing presumption and exceptions; associates are employees; "senior attorneys" analyzed on profit share, control, capital) (R-BUS-022). (4) Partnership by estoppel for anyone held out as a partner (R-BUS-023). (5) Consequences: authority (R-BUS-025), liability (R-BUS-027–029). (6) Note what filing as an LLP or law corporation would have changed (R-BUS-044, R-BUS-108).
