## Scope

The NCBE outline has five categories [S-040]:
1. **I. Homicide** — intended killings (premeditation, provocation) and unintended killings (intent to injure, reckless and negligent killings, felony murder, misdemeanor manslaughter).
2. **II. Other crimes** — theft and receiving, robbery, burglary, assault and battery, rape and statutory rape, kidnapping, arson, possession offenses.
3. **III. Inchoate crimes and parties** — attempt, conspiracy, solicitation, accomplice liability.
4. **IV. General principles** — acts and omissions, mental states (including strict liability and mistake), responsibility (insanity, intoxication), causation, justification and excuse, jurisdiction.
5. **V. Constitutional protection of accused persons** — search and seizure, confessions, identification, counsel, fair trial and pleas, double jeopardy, cruel and unusual punishment, burdens of proof, appeal and error.

**What the MBE stresses.** About half of the scored questions come from category V and half from I–IV together [S-040]. See [mbe-rules.md](mbe-rules.md).

**What California essays stress.** Procedure more than substance, and the Fourth Amendment above all.

## Study order (no prior coursework)

1. **[primer.md](primer.md)** — the map and vocabulary. Read it twice.
2. **General principles** (outline IV): act, mental state, causation, defenses. Everything else builds on them.
3. **Fourth Amendment** (outline V.A) — the most-tested essay topic and about a fifth of the MBE questions. Learn the analysis order in [flowcharts.md](flowcharts.md) chart 6.
4. **Confessions** (V.B) and **counsel/identification** (V.C–V.D).
5. **Homicide** (I), then **inchoate crimes and parties** (III), then **other crimes** (II).
6. **Remaining procedure** (V.E–V.I): fair trial, pleas, double jeopardy, Eighth Amendment, burdens, appeal — MBE-heavy, essay-light.
7. Drill [worked-examples.md](worked-examples.md) and [traps.md](traps.md); start MBE practice by category as soon as each block is read.
