# Criminal Law and Procedure — essay templates

California Criminal essays have taken a few recurring shapes since 2012 (`06-past-exams/index.csv`) [VERIFY V-071]: (1) **crimes and defenses** for one or more defendants; (2) **suppression** of physical evidence under the Fourth Amendment; (3) **suppression of statements** (Miranda, voluntariness, Sixth Amendment); (4) **identification**; (5) **trial-rights and plea** questions; and (6) **Evidence crossovers** at trial. Many questions combine two of these. Structure follows the call (grader-expectations §5).

**Paragraph structure for every issue (IRAC):**
1. **Issue** as a heading: the crime, defense or item of evidence.
2. **Rule**: the "write this" sentence from [rule-statements.md](rule-statements.md) (1–3 sentences).
3. **Analysis**: element by element, quoting specific facts; argue both sides where the facts cut both ways; name the majority and MPC (or other) rule where they would change the result.
4. **Conclusion**: one sentence, hedged ("likely," "probably").

**Timing (one hour):** 10–15 minutes reading and outlining; 45–50 minutes writing. Spend the most time on the issue the facts develop most.

## Template 1 — "What crimes can D be charged with, and what defenses?"

Used: Feb 2013 Q1 (four defendants; receiving and conspiracy); Oct 2020 Q4 (burglary and theft, defenses); Feb 2022 Q1 (robbery, conspiracy, felony murder, attempted murder).

```
I. [Defendant A]
   A. Conspiracy (if two or more acted together)         R-CRM-065 to R-CRM-068
      - Agreement / intent / overt act / bilateral vs. unilateral
   B. [Target crime 1, e.g., Robbery]                      element headings
      - Element 1 ... Element n (facts for each)
      - Accomplice / Pinkerton liability if A did not personally act
   C. [Target crime 2]
   D. Homicide (for each death) -> use Template 2
   E. Attempt (for each incomplete crime)                  R-CRM-060 to R-CRM-063
   F. Defenses
      - [Defense]: rule, facts, conclusion
   G. Conclusion: list the likely convictions
II. [Defendant B]  (same order; cross-refer to I where identical: "See above")
```

**Tips.**
- Start with **conspiracy** when the facts show planning: it sets up Pinkerton liability for everything else.
- For each crime, write **every element**, even the obvious ones, in one short sentence each; spend your words on the disputed element (intent at entry; force vs. stealth; knowledge of stolen character).
- **Defenses** go after the crimes they answer, or in one "Defenses" section if they apply to all charges.
- When a modern statute or the MPC changes the outcome, add one sentence: "Under the MPC/modern statutes, however, …"
- **Merger:** finish by noting which convictions merge (attempt and solicitation into the completed crime; robbery's included larceny) and which do not (conspiracy).

## Template 2 — Homicide

Used: Feb 2022 Q1 (customer and co-felon deaths during robbery; attempted murder of a witness); Jul 2026 Q5 (second-degree murder trial, with evidence issues).

```
[Death of V1]
1. Causation (actual and proximate)                         R-CRM-012, R-CRM-013
2. Murder — malice aforethought                             R-CRM-031
   a. Intent to kill (deadly-weapon inference)               R-CRM-032
   b. Intent to inflict serious bodily injury                R-CRM-035
   c. Depraved heart                                         R-CRM-036
   d. Felony murder                                          R-CRM-039 to R-CRM-041
      - qualifying felony; independent (merger); during the felony; who killed
        (agency vs. proximate cause); co-felon victim
3. Degree — first degree? premeditation/deliberation; means; enumerated felony   R-CRM-033
4. Mitigation — voluntary manslaughter (provocation; imperfect self-defense)      R-CRM-034, R-CRM-023
5. Involuntary manslaughter (criminal negligence; misdemeanor manslaughter)       R-CRM-037, R-CRM-042
6. Defenses (self-defense, insanity, intoxication)
7. Conclusion
[Death of V2] ... repeat; "Attempted murder of W": intent to kill + act   R-CRM-060
```

**Tips.**
- Do **all four malice theories** only where facts support them; one line rejecting a theory is fine.
- In felony murder, the **agency vs. proximate-cause** split is usually the point; present both and conclude under each.
- For California, add one sentence on Pen. Code § 189(e) only if the call asks for California law (R-CRM-043).

## Template 3 — Fourth Amendment suppression (item by item)

Used: Jul 2012 Q6; Jul 2015 Q3 (box; overheard conversation); Feb 2017 Q6 (cocaine); Feb 2018 Q4 (bomb, cocaine, map); Jul 2019 Q3 (overheard calls; coins); Jul 2021 Q4 (heroin in trunk); Feb 2022 Q1 (gun from a stop); Feb 2025 Q1 (gun and money).

```
Motion to suppress [Item 1]
  1. Government action                                       R-CRM-072
  2. Standing (D's own REP / property interest)              R-CRM-075
  3. Was there a search or seizure?                          R-CRM-073, R-CRM-074, R-CRM-076
  4. Was it reasonable?
     a. Warrant? (probable cause, particularity, Franks)      R-CRM-082
     b. If no warrant — each plausible exception:
        - Terry stop/frisk; traffic stop                      R-CRM-077 to R-CRM-079
        - Search incident to arrest (arrest valid? Gant/Riley) R-CRM-080, R-CRM-083
        - Automobile exception                                R-CRM-084
        - Plain view                                          R-CRM-085
        - Consent                                             R-CRM-086
        - Exigency / emergency aid                            R-CRM-087
        - Inventory / special needs                           R-CRM-088
  5. Remedy: exclusionary rule and limits
     - Fruit of the poisonous tree; independent source; inevitable discovery;
       attenuation; good faith; impeachment                   R-CRM-090 to R-CRM-092
  6. Conclusion: suppress / admit
Motion to suppress [Item 2] ... (refer back: "Standing — see above")
```

**Tips.**
- **One heading per item** named in the call, in the call's order.
- When the initial stop or arrest is illegal, later items become **fruits**: analyze the primary illegality once, then the fruit doctrine for each derivative item.
- For **electronic** evidence (overheard calls, phones, location data), start with Katz, then Kyllo/Carpenter/Chatrie, and Riley for phone contents (R-CRM-089).
- For a search **followed** by a warrant (Feb 2018 type), discuss exigency for the first entry and **independent source** for items seized under the warrant.
- California Truth-in-Evidence: one sentence at most, and only if the facts are set in a California court ("California applies the federal rule") (R-CRM-090).

## Template 4 — Suppression of statements

Used: Feb 2017 Q6 (post-arrest statement); Jul 2021 Q4 (text message after interrogation); Jul 2023 Q4 (statement); Feb 2025 Q1 ("Yes, it was me" and a statement about the money).

```
Motion to suppress [Statement 1]
  1. Due-process voluntariness                                R-CRM-093
  2. Miranda
     a. Custody                                               R-CRM-094
     b. Interrogation (or volunteered)                        R-CRM-095
     c. Public-safety exception?                              R-CRM-099
     d. Warnings adequate? Waiver knowing/voluntary?          R-CRM-096
     e. Invocation — silence (Mosley) / counsel (Edwards)     R-CRM-097, R-CRM-098
  3. Sixth Amendment (only if formal charges filed)           R-CRM-102
  4. Fruit of an illegal arrest or search?                    R-CRM-091
  5. Use for impeachment                                      R-CRM-100
  6. Conclusion
Motion to suppress [Statement 2] — add the two-step analysis (Elstad / Seibert) if the
second statement followed an unwarned first one              R-CRM-100
Physical evidence derived from the statement — Patane         R-CRM-100
```

**Tips.**
- Two selected answers to the same question can reach opposite conclusions on custody or waiver (Feb 2025 Q1); the points are in the **two-sided analysis**.
- Do not discuss the Sixth Amendment unless charges had been filed; say so in one sentence if the facts tempt you.

## Template 5 — Identification

Used: Oct 2020 Q4 (pre-arraignment lineup under the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments).

```
Motion to suppress the identification
  1. Right to counsel (only if post-charge live lineup)        R-CRM-106
     — if the call limits to due process, say so and skip
  2. Due process
     a. Police-arranged and unnecessarily suggestive?          R-CRM-107
        (lineup composition; single photo; show-up; comments)
     b. Reliability — Biggers factors one by one:
        opportunity to view; attention; accuracy of description;
        certainty; time elapsed
  3. In-court identification — independent source
  4. Conclusion
```

## Template 6 — Pretrial motions, trial rights and pleas

Used: Jul 2014 Q4 (motion to suppress; request to self-represent a week before trial; client's planned false testimony); Jul 2025 Q5 (judge's acceptance of a guilty plea; sentence; with PR).

```
1. [Motion / ruling 1, e.g., Request for self-representation]   R-CRM-104
   - timely and unequivocal? knowing and intelligent waiver? competence? standby counsel
2. [Counsel issues] — appointment; conflict; ineffective assistance          R-CRM-103, R-CRM-105
3. [Guilty plea] — Boykin/Rule 11 colloquy: charge, maximum, rights waived;
   voluntariness; factual basis; plea bargain and the judge's role            R-CRM-115
4. [Sentence] — Eighth Amendment proportionality; Apprendi/upper-term facts   R-CRM-118, R-CRM-111
5. [PR crossover] — handle under the PR template (California and ABA rules)
```

**Tip.** Where the call asks "Did the court properly …?", answer each ruling with "The court [properly / improperly] …" as the conclusion.

## Template 7 — Evidence + Criminal crossover at trial

Used: Feb 2024 Q4 (suppressing a photograph; admitting ATM records — California Evidence Code); Jul 2026 Q5 (objections at six points of a murder trial — California Evidence Code and U.S. Constitution).

```
[Point 1: the item or testimony]
  1. Evidence objection(s): relevance (CEC 350, 351), 352 balancing,
     hearsay and exception, character, privilege — per the Evidence template
  2. Constitutional objection(s):
     - Confrontation (testimonial?)                           R-CRM-112
     - Miranda / voluntariness (defendant's statements)        R-CRM-093 to R-CRM-100
     - Fourth Amendment suppression (illegally obtained items)  R-CRM-090
     - Brady / due process (late disclosure)                   R-CRM-113
  3. Ruling: sustain / overrule; harmless error if on appeal  R-CRM-120
[Point 2] ...
```

**Tip.** In a CEC call, Truth-in-Evidence (Cal. Const. art. I, § 28(f)(2)) explains why California exclusion follows the federal Constitution; one sentence earns the point (R-CRM-090; [ca-distinctions.md](ca-distinctions.md)).

## Template 8 — Inchoate crimes with a sting or informant

Used: Feb 2013 Q1 (copy of a stolen painting; sons working with police; conspiracy, receiving, attempt); Feb 2017 Q6 (offer to an informant to find a hit man; entrapment).

```
1. Solicitation (each request)                                R-CRM-064
2. Conspiracy — agreement with whom?
   - Feigned agreement by an informant: bilateral vs. unilateral   R-CRM-065
   - Overt act; scope; Wharton/protected class                     R-CRM-068
3. Completed crime (e.g., receiving) — element missing because of police control?   R-CRM-048
4. Attempt — specific intent; act; impossibility (factual / hybrid legal / pure legal) R-CRM-060 to R-CRM-062
5. Entrapment — inducement by a government agent; predisposition vs. objective test   R-CRM-029
6. Conclusions per defendant
```
