# Criminal Law and Procedure — MBE rules

## 1. What the MBE assumes

- **Weight.** 25 scored Criminal Law and Procedure questions per exam [S-007]. About **half** come from category V (constitutional protection of accused persons) and about half from categories I–IV together [S-040].
- **Governing law.** Substantive questions are answered under **generally accepted principles** [S-040]: the common law as modified by the modern majority. When a question turns on a split, it usually tells you the rule ("the jurisdiction follows the Model Penal Code," "a statute provides…," "the common-law rule applies"). If it does not, choose the majority view [VERIFY V-256].
- **Statutes in the question control.** When the question quotes a statute, apply its words, using the MPC rules of construction for mental states (R-CRM-005): a stated mental state usually applies to every element; a silent statute usually requires at least recklessness unless it is a public-welfare offense.
- **Procedure** is the U.S. Constitution as the Supreme Court reads it. Never apply California's distinctions on the MBE.
- **"Most likely" and "strongest defense" questions.** Choose the answer that is legally correct and best supported by the facts given; do not assume facts. A defense that negates an element beats a policy argument.

## 2. Black-letter rules the MBE favours

### Category I — Homicide
- Four malice states for murder; **depraved heart** requires awareness of a very high risk (R-CRM-036); intent to inflict serious bodily injury is malice (R-CRM-035).
- **Premeditation** needs some reflection; look for planning, lying in wait, weapon acquisition (R-CRM-033).
- **Provocation:** objective (reasonable person) and subjective (actually provoked); **words alone are not enough**; cooling time judged objectively (R-CRM-034).
- **Felony murder:** inherently dangerous felony; **merger** (assault is not a predicate); killing must be **in furtherance** and before reaching a place of temporary safety; the **agency** rule (no liability for a killing by a victim or police) is the default (R-CRM-039–R-CRM-041).
- **Misdemeanor manslaughter** for deaths during malum in se misdemeanors (R-CRM-042).
- **Causation:** ordinary medical negligence does not break the chain (R-CRM-013).

### Category II — Other crimes
- **Larceny:** intent to permanently deprive **at the time of taking**; continuing trespass; custody (employee) vs. possession (embezzlement) (R-CRM-044, R-CRM-045).
- **False pretenses** (title) vs. **larceny by trick** (possession) (R-CRM-046).
- **Robbery:** force or threat of **immediate** harm, from person or presence; pickpocket without awareness is larceny (R-CRM-049).
- **Burglary:** at common law — breaking, entering, dwelling of another, at night, intent to commit a felony **at the time of entry** (R-CRM-050).
- **Receiving stolen property:** knowledge; goods recovered by police are no longer stolen → attempt (R-CRM-048).
- **Assault:** attempted battery (victim need not be aware) or apprehension (victim must be aware) (R-CRM-052).
- **Statutory rape:** strict liability as to age (R-CRM-055).
- **Arson:** charring of a structure; malice includes recklessness (R-CRM-058).

### Category III — Inchoate crimes and parties
- **Attempt:** specific intent to commit the crime; **no attempted involuntary manslaughter**, no attempted felony murder; substantial step (MPC) vs. dangerous proximity; **factual impossibility no defense**; abandonment no defense at common law (R-CRM-060–R-CRM-063).
- **Conspiracy:** agreement + intent + overt act (most jurisdictions); **bilateral vs. unilateral** is heavily tested (undercover agent, acquitted co-conspirator); **no merger**; **Pinkerton** liability; withdrawal cuts off later crimes only if communicated to all; **Wharton's rule** and the **Gebardi** protected-class rule (R-CRM-065–R-CRM-068).
- **Solicitation:** complete on asking; merges (R-CRM-064).
- **Accomplice:** purpose to aid + aid; mere presence insufficient; liable for foreseeable crimes (natural and probable consequences) unless the MPC applies; withdrawal requires neutralizing aid (R-CRM-069–R-CRM-071).

### Category IV — General principles
- **Voluntary act**; **omission** only with a legal duty (R-CRM-002, R-CRM-003).
- **Specific vs. general intent** for intoxication and mistake (R-CRM-004, R-CRM-010, R-CRM-018).
- **MPC culpability**: purpose, knowledge, recklessness, negligence; default recklessness (R-CRM-005).
- **Strict liability** for public-welfare offenses; presumption of mens rea otherwise (R-CRM-006).
- **Mistake of law** is no defense except reliance on an official statement or negating a mental element (R-CRM-011).
- **Insanity:** M'Naghten (knowledge of nature or wrongfulness) unless another test is named (R-CRM-015).
- **Self-defense:** deadly force only against death or serious bodily harm; no duty to retreat (majority); initial aggressor rules (R-CRM-020–R-CRM-022).
- **Duress** no defense to murder; **necessity** requires greater harm avoided; **entrapment** = subjective predisposition test (R-CRM-027–R-CRM-029).

### Category V — Constitutional protection
- **Fourth Amendment:** standing (Rakas, Olson, Carter, Byrd); search tests (Katz, Jones, Kyllo, Carpenter); no REP in open fields, trash, bank records; warrant requirements (Gates, Franks, particularity); exceptions — **search incident to arrest (Chimel, Robinson, Gant, Riley)**, **automobile (Carroll, Acevedo)**, plain view (Horton, Hicks), consent (Schneckloth, Rodriguez, Randolph), exigency (Brigham City, King, McNeely), **Terry** stops and frisks (J.L., Wardlow, Dickerson), traffic stops (Whren, Rodriguez), inventory and checkpoints; exclusionary rule limits (**Leon**, **Herring**, **Hudson**, impeachment, grand jury) and fruits exceptions (**Murray**, **Nix**, **Brown/Strieff**).
- **Confessions:** voluntariness (Connelly); **Miranda custody and interrogation** (Berkemer, Innis, Perkins); waiver (Thompkins); invocation (**Edwards**, Davis, Mosley, Shatzer, Roberson, Minnick); Quarles; **Elstad vs. Seibert**; Patane; Harris; Doyle.
- **Identification:** Wade/Kirby/Ash for counsel; Biggers/Brathwaite for due process.
- **Counsel:** attachment; Massiah/Henry vs. Kuhlmann; offense-specific (McNeil, Cobb); Gideon/Argersinger/Scott; Faretta; **Strickland**; Cuyler conflicts.
- **Trial:** jury for > 6 months; unanimity; Batson; Apprendi; Crawford; Brady.
- **Pleas:** Boykin record; Rule 11 elements; Santobello.
- **Double jeopardy:** attachment (jury sworn); **Blockburger**; lesser-included (Brown v. Ohio); separate sovereigns; mistrial rules; Ashe.
- **Eighth Amendment:** proportionality; death-penalty categories; Robinson status crimes.
- **Burdens:** Winship; Mullaney vs. Patterson; Sandstrom presumptions.

## 3. How the MBE tests each category

| Category | Weight [S-040] | What questions look like |
|---|---|---|
| I Homicide | Part of I–IV (~½ together) | "Of which of the following crimes is D most likely guilty?" — the answers are murder, voluntary manslaughter, involuntary manslaughter, no crime. Decide the mental state, then mitigation |
| II Other crimes | Part of I–IV | A single element missing (no intent at time of entry; force after taking; goods no longer stolen). Pick the crime that fits **all** elements |
| III Inchoate; parties | Part of I–IV | Bilateral/unilateral conspiracy; Pinkerton; withdrawal; attempt mental state; impossibility; accomplice purpose vs. knowledge |
| IV General principles | Part of I–IV | Statute-reading questions on mental state; "best defense" questions (mistake, intoxication, insanity, entrapment, self-defense) |
| V Constitutional protection | ~½ (about 12–13 scored) | "Should the motion to suppress be granted?" — the answer turns on standing, an exception, or an exclusionary-rule limit. Miranda custody/interrogation/invocation. Double jeopardy same-offense questions |

## 4. Recurring fact patterns

1. **Drunk defendant takes property believing it his own** → specific intent negated (no larceny).
2. **Defendant enters a building, then decides to steal** → no burglary (intent at entry), but larceny.
3. **Robber's victim/police kill a bystander or co-felon** → agency rule: no felony murder.
4. **Undercover officer "agrees" to a crime** → no conspiracy under the bilateral rule; conspiracy under the unilateral (MPC) rule; solicitation in either case.
5. **Conspirator who tells only one partner he quits** → still liable for conspiracy and later foreseeable crimes.
6. **Shooting to scare, no one hurt** → not attempted murder (no intent to kill).
7. **Thief who sells goods to a "fence" after police recovered them** → attempted receiving.
8. **Police stop based on an anonymous tip about a gun** → no reasonable suspicion (J.L.) unless corroborated or predictive.
9. **Search of a car after the driver is handcuffed in the patrol car** → invalid incident to arrest (Gant), but check automobile exception (probable cause?) and inventory.
10. **Overnight guest vs. short-term business visitor** → standing vs. no standing.
11. **Suspect says "Maybe I should get a lawyer"** → not an unambiguous invocation (Davis).
12. **Suspect invokes counsel; police later question about a different crime** → still barred (Roberson), unless 14-day break (Shatzer).
13. **Unwarned statement leads to a gun** → gun admissible (Patane, plurality); statement inadmissible in case-in-chief but usable to impeach (Harris).
14. **Informant in a cell after indictment** → Sixth Amendment violation if he deliberately elicits (Henry), not if a passive listener (Kuhlmann).
15. **Pre-charge lineup without counsel** → no Sixth Amendment violation (Kirby, plurality); test due process only.
16. **Mistrial on defense motion** → retrial allowed unless prosecution goaded it (Oregon v. Kennedy).
17. **State then federal prosecution for the same act** → allowed (separate sovereigns).
18. **Judge instructs that the law presumes intent from the act** → Sandstrom violation.

## 5. Answer-elimination tips

- Eliminate any answer that relies on a fact the question does not give.
- In "most serious crime" questions, check the **mental state** before the act.
- In suppression questions, ask first whether the defendant has **standing** and whether there was a **search** at all — many correct answers say "No, because the defendant had no reasonable expectation of privacy."
- "Because the officer's motive was pretextual" is almost always wrong (Whren).
- "Because the defendant was not told he could refuse" is almost always wrong for consent (Schneckloth).
- "Because the statement was obtained without Miranda warnings" is wrong if there was no custody or no interrogation.
- For double jeopardy, apply Blockburger mechanically: list the elements of each offense.
