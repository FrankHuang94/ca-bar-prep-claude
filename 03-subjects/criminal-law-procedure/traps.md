# Criminal Law and Procedure — traps

Each item: the trap, the correct rule, and the rule ID in [rule-statements.md](rule-statements.md).

## Substantive criminal law

1. **"Intent" to injure ≠ intent to kill for attempt.** Attempted murder requires intent to kill; a defendant who meant only serious injury (or acted recklessly) and missed is guilty of assault, not attempted murder — even though the same mental state would be murder if death resulted (R-CRM-060, R-CRM-035). Likewise, there is no attempted involuntary manslaughter and no attempted felony murder: you cannot intend an unintended crime (R-CRM-060).
2. **Conspiracy does not merge; attempt and solicitation do.** A distractor will say the conspiracy conviction "merges into the robbery conviction." Wrong under the common law and federal law; right only under MPC § 1.07 (R-CRM-068).
3. **Bilateral vs. unilateral.** If the only other "conspirator" is an undercover officer, the common-law answer is "no conspiracy"; the MPC answer is "conspiracy." Read the call for which rule applies (R-CRM-065).
4. **Withdrawal from conspiracy.** Withdrawal never undoes the conspiracy itself at common law (once agreement plus overt act exist); it only cuts off liability for later crimes, and only if communicated to all (R-CRM-068).
5. **Pinkerton is not the MPC rule.** Under the MPC, a conspirator is liable for a co-conspirator's crime only as an accomplice (R-CRM-067).
6. **Mere presence is not accomplice liability**, even with knowledge and approval (R-CRM-069).
7. **Knowledge vs. purpose for accomplices and suppliers.** Selling ordinary goods knowing of the buyer's crime is usually not enough; look for a stake in the venture (R-CRM-066, R-CRM-069).
8. **Burglary intent must exist at entry.** "D entered to get warm, then stole" → no burglary (R-CRM-050).
9. **Robbery timing.** At common law, force used after a stealthy taking makes it larceny (plus battery), not robbery; the modern and California rule differs (R-CRM-049).
10. **Larceny by trick vs. false pretenses** — the victim passed possession (trick) or title (false pretenses). A check or a sale = title (R-CRM-046).
11. **Custody vs. possession** — a cashier who pockets till money has custody: larceny, not embezzlement (R-CRM-044, R-CRM-045). Intent to repay money is no defense to larceny or embezzlement; intent to return the *same* item can be (R-CRM-044, R-CRM-045).
12. **Receiving goods recovered by police.** The completed crime fails; attempt survives under the modern rule (R-CRM-048, R-CRM-062).
13. **Factual impossibility is never a defense.** Empty pocket, unloaded gun, dead victim → attempt (R-CRM-062).
14. **Voluntary intoxication only helps for specific-intent crimes.** A drunk defendant charged with battery, rape or second-degree murder (malice) gets nothing (R-CRM-018). Similarly, an unreasonable mistake of fact helps only for specific intent; for general intent (rape, battery) the mistake must be reasonable, and for strict liability it is irrelevant (R-CRM-010).
15. **Advice of a private lawyer is not a mistake-of-law defense** (R-CRM-011).
16. **Words alone are not adequate provocation** (majority) — but the MPC's EMED test is broader (R-CRM-034).
17. **Felony murder: agency vs. proximate cause.** A killing by the victim or police is not felony murder under the majority agency rule (R-CRM-041).
18. **Merger.** An assault cannot be the predicate felony for felony murder (R-CRM-040).
19. **Duress is no defense to murder** (majority), but necessity and duress are different: necessity = natural forces and a balance of harms; duress = human threat (R-CRM-027, R-CRM-028).
20. **Deadly force for property is never justified**, including spring guns (R-CRM-025).
21. **Entrapment needs government inducement.** A private person's pressure is not entrapment; offering an ordinary opportunity is not inducement (R-CRM-029).
22. **Omission liability needs a legal duty.** The "Olympic swimmer watching a stranger drown" is not guilty (R-CRM-003).

## Criminal procedure

23. **Standing first.** A distractor will argue the search was illegal; the right answer may be that this defendant had no reasonable expectation of privacy (passenger in someone else's car; short-term business visitor) (R-CRM-075).
24. **Pretext is irrelevant** for traffic stops (Whren), but **prolonging** a stop for a dog sniff is not (Rodriguez) (R-CRM-079).
25. **Consent need not be informed.** Knowledge of the right to refuse is not required (Schneckloth) (R-CRM-086).
26. **Gant limits car searches incident to arrest**, but the **automobile exception** (probable cause) may still justify the search — check both (R-CRM-083, R-CRM-084).
27. **Riley: no warrantless phone searches incident to arrest** — the phone may be seized, not searched (R-CRM-083).
28. **Anonymous tip without predictive detail** does not create reasonable suspicion (J.L.) (R-CRM-077).
29. **Plain feel requires immediately apparent identity**; manipulating the object is an unlawful search (R-CRM-078).
30. **Knock-and-announce violations do not lead to exclusion** (Hudson) (R-CRM-092).
31. **Miranda requires both custody and interrogation.** Traffic stops are not custody; volunteered statements are not interrogation (R-CRM-094, R-CRM-095).
32. **Silence is not invocation** (Thompkins); "maybe I should get a lawyer" is not invocation (Davis) (R-CRM-097, R-CRM-098).
33. **Edwards is not offense-specific; the Sixth Amendment is.** After a counsel request under Miranda, police cannot ask about any crime (Roberson); after Sixth Amendment attachment, they may question about uncharged crimes (McNeil, Cobb) (R-CRM-098, R-CRM-102).
34. **Undercover cellmate:** no Miranda problem (Perkins); a Sixth Amendment problem only after charges and with deliberate elicitation (Henry vs. Kuhlmann) (R-CRM-095, R-CRM-102).
35. **Unwarned statements can impeach; involuntary ones cannot** (Harris vs. Mincey) (R-CRM-100, R-CRM-093).
36. **No right to counsel at photo arrays or pre-charge lineups** — only due process applies (Ash; Kirby, plurality) (R-CRM-106).
37. **Jeopardy attaches when the jury is sworn**, not at indictment or arraignment; a mistrial requested by the defense permits retrial unless the prosecutor goaded it (R-CRM-116, R-CRM-117).
38. **Separate sovereigns** may both prosecute; a city and its state may not (R-CRM-117).
39. **Apprendi excludes prior convictions**: a judge may find the fact of a prior conviction (R-CRM-111).
40. **Gideon covers felonies; misdemeanors only if jail is actually imposed** (Scott) (R-CRM-103).
