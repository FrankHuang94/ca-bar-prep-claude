# Criminal Law and Procedure — California distinctions

## California-specific criminal law distinctions (if any) on essays

**The headline: California Criminal essays are answered under general principles.**
- The essay instructions direct applicants to answer under "legal theories and principles of general application" unless the question asks for California law [S-077].
- Every Criminal Law and Procedure call whose wording was captured in the index (Feb 2012 to Jul 2026) asked either about crimes and defenses in general terms ("What crimes can D be charged with?") or about the **U.S. Constitution** (Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Fourteenth Amendments), sometimes alongside the California Evidence Code (`06-past-exams/index.csv`) [VERIFY V-071]. None asked for the California Penal Code; two calls (Jul 2012 Q6 and the first call of Jul 2023 Q4) were not captured.
- **What to do:** for crimes, write the **common-law** rule and note the **MPC** (and the modern majority) where it differs. For procedure, write the **federal constitutional** rule. Add a California point only if the call says "under California law," or in a California Evidence Code crossover where the call also asks about the Constitution (Feb 2024 Q4; Jul 2026 Q5) — then the Truth-in-Evidence point (row 1 below) is worth one sentence.
- **On the MBE:** never apply a California rule.

The table lists **real** differences, ordered by how likely they are to matter on an essay. Rule IDs point to [rule-statements.md](rule-statements.md).

## Notes for essay writing

- **Default structure** stays the same: crime → elements → defenses for each defendant; for procedure, standing → search/seizure → justification → exclusion, and custody → interrogation → warnings → waiver → invocation for statements.
- **When the call says "California law"** (not seen in the indexed Criminal calls), add the California rule after the general rule for felony murder, insanity, diminished capacity, intoxication, entrapment and the specific crimes in rows 3–26.
- **When the call is an Evidence Code crossover** (Feb 2024 Q4; Jul 2026 Q5), the constitutional objections (confrontation, Miranda, suppression) follow federal law, and Truth-in-Evidence explains why: rows 1–2 and 31.
- **Do not cite California statute numbers** you are unsure of on the exam; "California, by statute, …" earns the point.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: Like a PRC judicial interpretation that refines a national code article, a California statute can narrow a common-law doctrine (felony murder) without changing the general rule examiners expect you to state first (approx.).
