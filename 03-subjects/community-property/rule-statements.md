# Community Property — rule statements

**How to use this file.** Each block gives a rule you can write verbatim on an essay, then its elements, exceptions, authority and a short example. `build_anki.py` turns the **Rule (write this)** paragraphs into cards. The order follows [`outline.md`](outline.md).

**Governing law.** Community property is **not on the MBE**. Every California CP essay is answered under California law (the call says so). So the "MBE vs. CA essay" line below states only how the rule is used on the essay.

**Abbreviations.** CP = community property; SP = separate property; QCP = quasi-community property; DOS = date of separation; Fam. Code / Prob. Code / Evid. Code / Civ. Code = California codes.

## I Community property law

### R-CMP-001 — Community property defined
**Rule (write this):** California is a community property state. All property, real or personal, wherever situated, acquired by a married person during the marriage while domiciled in California is community property, unless a statute provides otherwise. During the marriage each spouse owns a present, existing and equal one-half interest in it.

**Elements:** 1. Acquired by a married person. 2. During marriage (wedding to date of separation). 3. While domiciled in California. 4. Not within a statutory SP category (§§ 770, 771, 781) or changed by agreement.

**Exceptions / defenses:** SP categories (R-CMP-002); valid premarital agreement or transmutation (R-CMP-018, R-CMP-022); property acquired while domiciled elsewhere is QCP or SP (R-CMP-003).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 760, 751, 63.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; open every CP answer with this rule.

**Example:** W earns a $90,000 salary during marriage while living in San Diego and buys a car with it. The salary and the car are CP, even though the car is registered only in W's name.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: close to 夫妻共同财产 for wages and business income earned during marriage (approx.).

### R-CMP-002 — Separate property defined
**Rule (write this):** Separate property includes all property owned by a spouse before marriage; all property acquired during marriage by gift, bequest, devise or descent; the rents, issues and profits of separate property; and the earnings and accumulations of a spouse after the date of separation. A spouse may manage and convey separate property without the other spouse's consent.

**Elements:** 1. Owned before marriage; or 2. acquired by gift, bequest, devise or descent; or 3. rents, issues and profits of SP; or 4. earnings and accumulations after DOS (or after a legal separation judgment); or 5. made SP by valid agreement, transmutation or statute (e.g., § 781).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Community labor that increases SP creates a community interest (R-CMP-033); community payments on an SP purchase loan create a pro tanto community share (R-CMP-037); SP loses its identity if commingled and untraceable (R-CMP-028).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 770, 771, 772.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; state with R-CMP-001 at the start.

**Example:** H inherits 1,000 shares from his father during marriage. The shares and their dividends are H's SP; he may sell them without W's consent.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: unlike the PRC default, an inheritance received during marriage is the heir's SP in California even if the will is silent (approx.).

### R-CMP-003 — Quasi-community property
**Rule (write this):** Quasi-community property is property, wherever situated, acquired by either spouse while domiciled outside California that would have been community property had the acquiring spouse been domiciled in California at the time, and property acquired in exchange for it. At dissolution it is treated like community property and divided equally, provided both spouses are then domiciled in California; at death, one-half of the decedent's quasi-community property belongs to the surviving spouse.

**Elements:** 1. Acquired while domiciled elsewhere. 2. Would have been CP under California law (e.g., marital earnings, not gifts). 3. Event that triggers QCP treatment: dissolution with both spouses domiciled in California, or death of a spouse domiciled in California.

**Exceptions / defenses:** At death, out-of-state real property is not QCP (Prob. Code § 66) and only the decedent's QCP is split; during marriage the acquiring spouse manages it as if SP [VERIFY V-327].

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 125, 63, 912, 2550, 2660; Prob. Code §§ 66, 101, 102; *Addison v. Addison* (1965) 62 Cal.2d 558; *In re Marriage of Roesch* (1978) 83 Cal.App.3d 96 [S-408]; *Fredericks v. Fredericks* (1991) 226 Cal.App.3d 875 (applying Roesch) [S-1014].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; a "State X, a non-community-property state" fact pattern always calls for it.

**Example:** H and W lived in New York for 10 years, where H's salary bought stock titled in H's name, then moved to California. At a California dissolution the stock is QCP and is divided equally.

### R-CMP-004 — Marital economic community and community estate
**Rule (write this):** The marital economic community begins at marriage and ends at the date of separation or the death of a spouse. The community estate subject to equal division includes both community property and quasi-community property.

**Elements:** 1. Valid marriage (or registered domestic partnership, or putative marriage). 2. Start date: marriage. 3. End date: DOS (§ 70) or death. 4. Domicile in California during acquisition (else QCP/SP).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Premarital cohabitation creates no CP (Marvin contract claims only); property that was CP at DOS stays CP until divided.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 63, 70, 760, 771; *Marvin v. Marvin* (1976) 18 Cal.3d 660.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; frame the time period before characterizing assets.

**Example:** H and W lived together from 2015, married in 2018 and separated in 2025. H's 2016 savings are SP; his 2019 bonus is CP; his 2026 salary is SP.

### R-CMP-005 — Choice of law: character fixed by domicile at acquisition
**Rule (write this):** The character of personal property is determined by the law of the acquiring spouse's domicile at the time of acquisition, and the character of land by the law of its situs. Property that was community property when acquired keeps that character if the couple later moves to a common-law state, and property acquired elsewhere becomes subject to California's quasi-community property rules only at dissolution or death.

**Elements:** 1. Identify domicile at acquisition. 2. Apply that state's characterization (or ask "what would California law have made it?" for QCP). 3. For land, situs law controls title.

**Exceptions / defenses:** California QCP statutes re-characterize out-of-state acquisitions for division at dissolution or death; out-of-state land is outside the Probate Code definition of QCP at death.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 125, 760, 2660; Prob. Code § 66; general conflict-of-laws principles.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; use as a one-sentence lead-in to QCP.

**Example:** H and W, California domiciliaries, buy shares with salary, then move to Texas and later to Oregon. The shares stay CP.

### R-CMP-006 — Putative spouses and quasi-marital property
**Rule (write this):** If a marriage is void or voidable but one or both parties believed in good faith that it was valid, the court declares the believing party a putative spouse and divides equally, as quasi-marital property, the property acquired during the union that would have been community or quasi-community property if the union had been valid. Good faith is the party's actual, subjective belief; its reasonableness is only evidence.

**Elements:** 1. Void or voidable marriage. 2. Good-faith belief in validity by at least one party. 3. Property acquired during the union that would have been CP or QCP. 4. Equal division under § 2550.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A party who knew the marriage was invalid is not a putative spouse; competing lawful and putative spouses may share equitably (*Estate of Vargas* (1974) 36 Cal.App.3d 714) [VERIFY V-312].

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 2251; *Ceja v. Rudolph & Sletten, Inc.* (2013) 56 Cal.4th 1113 [S-424].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; raise as a threshold issue when the marriage is defective.

**Example:** W marries H, not knowing H's earlier divorce was never finalized. W is a putative spouse; the house bought with both salaries is quasi-marital property divided equally.

### R-CMP-007 — Domestic partners and nonmarital cohabitants
**Rule (write this):** Registered domestic partners have the same property rights and obligations as spouses, so community property law applies to them in full. Unmarried cohabitants acquire no community property; their claims to each other's property rest on express or implied contract or equitable theories.

**Elements:** Registered partnership → treat as marriage. Cohabitation only → Marvin contract analysis (express agreement, implied-in-fact agreement from conduct, quantum meruit, constructive or resulting trust).

**Exceptions / defenses:** A Marvin agreement resting on sexual services as consideration is unenforceable to that extent.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 297.5; *Marvin v. Marvin* (1976) 18 Cal.3d 660.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; one line when facts involve partners or cohabitants.

**Example:** A and B, registered domestic partners, buy a condo with their salaries. It is CP and is divided equally on dissolution of the partnership.

### R-CMP-008 — Characterization method: source, time, actions of the parties
**Rule (write this):** Property is characterized at the time of acquisition by tracing its source and the time it was acquired, then applying the statutory presumptions, and finally asking whether the spouses changed its character by a valid agreement or transmutation or whether later contributions give the other estate an interest or a right of reimbursement.

**Elements:** 1. Source (earnings, gift, inheritance, loan, SP proceeds). 2. Time (before marriage / during / after DOS). 3. Presumptions (general; title; undue influence). 4. Actions of the parties (premarital agreement, transmutation, commingling). 5. Contributions (apportionment; reimbursement). 6. Division or disposition.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The form of title does not by itself fix character between spouses (R-CMP-012).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 760, 770, 852, 2581, 2640; *In re Marriage of Valli* (2014) 58 Cal.4th 1396.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; this is the asset-by-asset structure State Bar selected answers follow.

**Example:** For a condo: source (H's premarital savings plus marital salary payments), time (bought before marriage), presumptions (title in H), actions (no writing), contributions (Moore/Marsden).

### R-CMP-009 — Rents, issues and profits follow the source
**Rule (write this):** The rents, issues and profits of property take the character of the property that produced them: income from separate property is separate property and income from community property is community property, except to the extent community labor produced the income.

**Elements:** 1. Identify the producing asset's character. 2. Ask whether spousal labor (not passive ownership) generated the income. 3. Apportion if labor contributed.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Business profits from an SP business are apportioned when community effort contributed (Pereira/Van Camp).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 770(a)(3), 760; *Pereira v. Pereira* (1909) 156 Cal. 1.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W's premarital rental house earns $2,000 a month managed by an agent. The rent is W's SP.

### R-CMP-010 — Earnings and labor during marriage are community
**Rule (write this):** The skill, effort and labor of either spouse during marriage, and the earnings they produce, belong to the community, wherever and however they are paid. Property bought with earnings during marriage is community property.

**Elements:** 1. Labor or earnings. 2. Performed or earned during marriage before DOS. 3. Deferred compensation earned during marriage (pensions, options, bonuses) is CP in proportion to marital service.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Earnings after DOS (§ 771) and after legal separation (§ 772) are SP.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 760, 771; *In re Marriage of Brown* (1976) 15 Cal.3d 838.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H receives a $30,000 bonus in March 2026 for work done in 2025, while married. The bonus is CP even if paid after DOS, because it was earned during marriage.

## II.A Presumptions

### R-CMP-011 — General community property presumption
**Rule (write this):** Property acquired by a spouse during marriage while domiciled in California is presumed to be community property. The spouse claiming that it is separate property bears the burden of rebutting the presumption, generally by a preponderance of the evidence, by tracing it to a separate source or proving it was acquired before marriage, by gift or inheritance, or after separation.

**Elements:** 1. Acquisition during marriage (or possession during marriage). 2. California domicile. 3. Burden on SP proponent. 4. Rebuttal by tracing or proof of SP acquisition.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Special presumptions may displace it (§ 803 pre-1975 married woman; joint title § 2581 strengthens it).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 760; *See v. See* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 778; *In re Marriage of Mix* (1975) 14 Cal.3d 604.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; apply to every asset.

**Example:** W claims a $20,000 painting bought during marriage was paid for with her inheritance but has no records. The painting is CP.

### R-CMP-012 — Title in one spouse's name does not control between spouses
**Rule (write this):** Taking title to property in one spouse's name does not by itself make it that spouse's separate property. When property is acquired during marriage with community funds, it remains community property unless the spouses made a valid transmutation; the Evidence Code § 662 form-of-title presumption does not apply between spouses where it would conflict with the transmutation statutes.

**Elements:** 1. Acquired during marriage. 2. With CP funds. 3. Title in one spouse alone. 4. No § 852 express written declaration.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Evid. Code § 662 can still matter where no change of character between the spouses is in issue; a valid § 852 writing changes character (subject to the undue-influence presumption).

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Valli* (2014) 58 Cal.4th 1396; Fam. Code § 852; Evid. Code § 662; *In re Brace* (2020) 9 Cal.5th 903 [S-422].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H uses community savings to buy a life insurance policy owned by W. Without an express writing, the policy is CP, despite W's ownership designation (Valli).

### R-CMP-013 — Joint-title presumption at dissolution (§ 2581)
**Rule (write this):** For division at dissolution or legal separation, property acquired by the spouses during marriage in joint form, including joint tenancy, tenancy in common or community property, is presumed to be community property. The presumption can be rebutted only by a clear statement in the deed or other title document that the property is separate property, or by proof of a written agreement that it is separate property.

**Elements:** 1. Dissolution or legal separation proceeding. 2. Acquired during marriage. 3. Joint form of title. 4. No clear SP statement in title document and no written SP agreement.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The contributing spouse's remedy is § 2640 reimbursement of traced SP contributions (R-CMP-042). Does not apply at death or against creditors (see R-CMP-014). Limited retroactivity for pre-1984/1985 acquisitions [VERIFY V-329].

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 2581, 2640 [S-425]; *In re Marriage of Lucas* (1980) 27 Cal.3d 808 (superseded by statute).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; one of the most-tested presumptions.

**Example:** W pays the $100,000 down payment from her inheritance; the deed reads "H and W, as joint tenants." At divorce the house is CP; W gets $100,000 back under § 2640 and the rest is split.

### R-CMP-014 — Joint tenancy title at death and against creditors
**Rule (write this):** Outside dissolution, § 2581 does not apply. Property bought during marriage with community funds is presumptively community property even though title was taken in joint tenancy, and after 1984 its character changes only by an express written transmutation (Brace). But Brace expressly left undisturbed the right of survivorship that accompanies joint tenancy title at death, so the surviving joint tenant takes the whole asset and the decedent's will cannot reach it.

**Elements:** 1. Death or creditor context. 2. Joint tenancy title. 3. Source of funds (CP → presumptively CP for characterization). 4. Any § 852 writing? 5. At death: survivorship still operates.

**Exceptions / defenses:** If the spouses validly transmuted the asset in writing (§ 852) into CP held without survivorship, survivorship no longer applies and each spouse may will half. In creditor and bankruptcy disputes (Brace's own setting), the CP character controls what is reachable. QCP put into joint tenancy with a non-spouse can be recaptured under Prob. Code § 102 (R-CMP-093). CP with right of survivorship (Civ. Code § 682.1) also passes to the survivor.

**Authority:** *In re Brace* (2020) 9 Cal.5th 903 [S-422] [S-1016]; Fam. Code § 852; Civ. Code § 682.1.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; use on Wills/CP crossovers.

**Example:** H and W bought a cabin with salaries as joint tenants; H's will leaves "my half of the cabin" to his son. Brace makes the cabin presumptively CP, but it did not disturb survivorship at death: W takes the whole cabin and the gift to the son fails.

### R-CMP-015 — Married woman's pre-1975 presumption
**Rule (write this):** Property acquired by a married woman by a written instrument before January 1, 1975 is presumed to be her separate property; if she acquired it with another person, she is presumed to take her share as a tenant in common; and if husband and wife acquired it by an instrument describing them as husband and wife, it is presumed to be community property, in each case unless the instrument expresses a different intention.

**Elements:** 1. Acquired before Jan. 1, 1975. 2. By written instrument. 3. Who took: wife alone (SP); wife with another person (tenant in common); "husband and wife" (CP).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Rebuttable between the spouses, but conclusive in favor of a person dealing with the wife in good faith and for value; does not apply to acquisitions on or after Jan. 1, 1975.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 803 [S-419] [S-1018].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; rarely tested; one sentence when a pre-1975 deed to the wife appears.

**Example:** In 1972 W took a deed to a lot in her own name. It is presumed her SP.

### R-CMP-016 — Presumption of undue influence in interspousal transactions
**Rule (write this):** Spouses are fiduciaries who owe each other the highest good faith and fair dealing. When a transaction between spouses gives one of them an advantage, it is presumed to have been induced by undue influence, and the advantaged spouse must prove that the other spouse acted freely and voluntarily, with full knowledge of the facts and a complete understanding of the transaction's effect.

**Elements:** 1. Transaction between spouses. 2. Advantage to one spouse. 3. Presumption shifts the burden. 4. Rebuttal: free, voluntary, informed act.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Does not apply to premarital agreements (no fiduciary relation before marriage: Bonds). Independent advice and full disclosure help rebut it.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 721(b); *In re Marriage of Haines* (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 277; *In re Marriage of Bonds* (2000) 24 Cal.4th 1.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; raise for every interspousal deed or transfer.

**Example:** W signs a quitclaim of her interest in the CP house to H while ill, without advice. H gained an advantage; the transfer is presumed the product of undue influence.

### R-CMP-017 — Gifts and inheritances during marriage
**Rule (write this):** Property a spouse receives during marriage by gift, bequest, devise or descent is that spouse's separate property, together with its rents, issues and profits. A gift made to both spouses together gives each a share; if title is taken in joint form during marriage, the joint-title presumption makes it community property at dissolution.

**Elements:** 1. Donative transfer from a third party (or a valid interspousal gift). 2. To one spouse (SP) or to both (co-owned). 3. Joint title → § 2581 at dissolution.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A "gift" that is really compensation for services (e.g., an employer's bonus labelled a gift) is CP earnings. Gifts between spouses need a § 852 writing unless § 852(c) applies.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 770(a)(2), 852, 2581 [VERIFY V-308].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W's mother deeds a house "to H and W as joint tenants." At dissolution it is presumed CP under § 2581 (no SP statement in the deed); each spouse takes half in any event.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: a PRC court would ask whether the parent's gift named one spouse; California asks how title was taken and applies § 2581 (approx.).

## II.B Agreements that alter characterization

### R-CMP-018 — Premarital agreements: formation and scope
**Rule (write this):** A premarital agreement must be in writing and signed by both parties; it is enforceable without consideration and takes effect on marriage. The parties may contract about their property rights, management, disposition on separation, dissolution or death, wills, life insurance and choice of law, but may not adversely affect a child's right to support.

**Elements:** 1. Writing signed by both. 2. Made in contemplation of marriage. 3. Effective on marriage. 4. Permissible subject matter. 5. Amendment or revocation after marriage only by signed writing.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Unenforceable if involuntary or unconscionable with inadequate disclosure (R-CMP-019, R-CMP-020); support provisions need counsel (R-CMP-021).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 1500, 1611, 1612, 1613, 1614.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Before marriage H and W sign an agreement that each spouse's earnings will be SP. If enforceable, W's salary during marriage is her SP.

### R-CMP-019 — Premarital agreements: voluntariness (§ 1615(c))
**Rule (write this):** A premarital agreement is unenforceable if the challenging party did not sign it voluntarily, and it is deemed involuntary unless the court finds that the party had independent counsel or waived counsel in a separate writing after being advised to seek it; had at least seven calendar days between first receiving the final agreement and signing; if unrepresented, was fully informed in writing, in a language the party understands, of the terms and the rights given up; and signed free of duress, fraud, undue influence and incapacity.

**Elements:** 1. Independent counsel or separate written waiver after advisement (advisement at least 7 days before signing). 2. Seven-day period (final agreement, for agreements signed on or after Jan. 1, 2020). 3. Written explanation for an unrepresented party, with a signed acknowledgment naming who provided it. 4. No duress, fraud, undue influence or incapacity. 5. Any other relevant factors.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Nonsubstantive amendments that do not change the terms do not restart the seven days; agreements signed from Jan. 1, 2002 to Jan. 1, 2020 use the earlier wording (seven days from first presentation of the agreement and advice to seek counsel) (§ 1615(c)(2)(A)–(B)).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1615(a)(1), (c) [S-401] [S-402] [S-1000]; *In re Marriage of Bonds* (2000) 24 Cal.4th 1 (decided under the earlier totality test; the Legislature responded by adding § 1615(c), effective 2002).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; walk through each factor on the facts.

**Example:** H hands W a prenup the day before the wedding; W has no lawyer and signs no waiver. The agreement is deemed involuntary under § 1615(c) and is unenforceable.

### R-CMP-020 — Premarital agreements: unconscionability and disclosure
**Rule (write this):** A premarital agreement is also unenforceable if it was unconscionable when executed and, before signing, the challenging party was not given fair, reasonable and full disclosure of the other party's property and financial obligations, did not voluntarily and expressly waive further disclosure in writing, and did not have, and reasonably could not have had, adequate knowledge of them. Unconscionability is decided by the court as a question of law.

**Elements:** 1. Unconscionable at execution. 2. No fair disclosure. 3. No written waiver of disclosure. 4. No actual or constructive knowledge. (All required.)

**Exceptions / defenses:** Disclosure or a written waiver defeats this ground even if the bargain is lopsided; involuntariness is a separate route.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1615(a)(2), (b).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H concealed a $5 million trust interest; W waived nothing in writing and had no way to know. If the terms were one-sided, the agreement fails under § 1615(a)(2).

### R-CMP-021 — Premarital waiver of spousal support
**Rule (write this):** A provision in a premarital agreement regarding spousal support, including a waiver, is not enforceable if the party against whom it is enforced was not represented by independent counsel when the agreement was signed, or if the provision is unconscionable at the time of enforcement. Being represented does not save a provision that is otherwise unenforceable.

**Elements:** 1. Spousal-support term. 2. Independent counsel at signing (required). 3. Not unconscionable at enforcement (measured at divorce).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Child support may never be adversely affected (§ 1612(b)).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1612(b), (c).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; raise whenever the agreement waives support.

**Example:** Lawyer H drafts a prenup in which W waives support; W declines to hire a lawyer. The support waiver is unenforceable under § 1612(c), even if the property terms survive.

### R-CMP-022 — Transmutation: definition and writing requirement
**Rule (write this):** Spouses may transmute community property into separate property, separate property into community property, or one spouse's separate property into the other's, by agreement or transfer, with or without consideration. A transmutation made on or after January 1, 1985 is valid only if made in writing by an express declaration that is made, joined in, consented to or accepted by the spouse whose interest is adversely affected.

**Elements:** 1. Agreement or transfer between spouses. 2. Writing. 3. Express declaration of a change in character or ownership. 4. Signed or accepted by the adversely affected spouse.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Personal-gift exception (R-CMP-024); pre-1985 transmutations (R-CMP-025); no part-performance or estoppel exception (Benson); undue-influence presumption (R-CMP-026).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 850, 852(a); *In re Marriage of Benson* (2005) 36 Cal.4th 1096.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; heavily tested.

**Example:** H tells W, "the boat is yours now," and W uses it alone for years. No writing, so the CP boat remains CP.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: like a written 夫妻财产约定, but California demands an express statement that ownership is changing (approx.).

### R-CMP-023 — Express declaration test
**Rule (write this):** A writing satisfies § 852 only if it contains language expressly stating that the characterization or ownership of the property is being changed; the intent to transmute must appear on the face of the writing, without resort to extrinsic evidence. A document that merely shows a transfer, a consent to a beneficiary designation or a change in title is not enough.

**Elements:** 1. Words showing a change of ownership or character. 2. Clear from the document itself. 3. Signed or accepted by the adversely affected spouse.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A deed using operative words of grant has been held sufficient (*Estate of Bibb* (2001) 87 Cal.App.4th 461 [S-415] [VERIFY V-326]); a broker transfer instruction was not (*In re Marriage of Barneson* (1999) 69 Cal.App.4th 583 [S-416]).

**Authority:** *Estate of MacDonald* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 262; Fam. Code § 852(a).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W signs her husband's IRA beneficiary form consenting to his designation of his children. That consent is not an express declaration transmuting her CP interest (MacDonald).

### R-CMP-024 — Gifts between spouses of personal items (§ 852(c))
**Rule (write this):** No writing is required for a gift between spouses of clothing, wearing apparel, jewelry or other tangible articles of a personal nature that are used solely or principally by the receiving spouse and are not substantial in value, taking into account the circumstances of the marriage. Such a gift becomes the receiving spouse's separate property.

**Elements:** 1. Interspousal gift. 2. Tangible personal article (clothing, jewelry, similar). 3. Used solely or principally by the donee. 4. Not substantial in value relative to the marriage's circumstances.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Valuable items (a $40,000 ring for a modest-income couple), investments, cars and household items fall outside the exception and need a writing.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 852(c).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; argue both sides on value and personal use.

**Example:** H gives W a $1,500 necklace from CP for her birthday; their combined income is $250,000. It is W's SP without any writing.

### R-CMP-025 — Pre-1985 transmutations
**Rule (write this):** The writing requirement of § 852 does not apply to transmutations made before January 1, 1985; those could be made orally or inferred from the spouses' conduct.

**Elements:** 1. Transmutation date before Jan. 1, 1985. 2. Proof of agreement (oral or conduct).

**Exceptions / defenses:** The spouse asserting it still bears the burden of proof.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 852(e).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; rarely tested.

**Example:** In 1980 H orally agreed that W's CP earnings deposited in her account were her SP. The oral transmutation can be proved.

### R-CMP-026 — Transmutation: undue influence, third parties and fraud on creditors
**Rule (write this):** Even a transmutation that meets § 852 is presumed to have been obtained by undue influence if it advantages one spouse, and the advantaged spouse must rebut that presumption. A transmutation of real property is not effective against third parties without notice unless recorded, and transmutations are subject to the law on fraudulent transfers.

**Elements:** 1. Valid writing. 2. Advantage → presumption of undue influence. 3. Recording for real property vs. third parties. 4. Fraudulent-transfer law protects creditors.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Rebuttal by showing a free, voluntary and fully informed act.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 721, 851, 852(b); *In re Marriage of Haines* (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 277.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W deeds her inherited house to "H and W as joint tenants." The deed may satisfy § 852, but H gained a one-half interest, so H must prove W acted freely and with full understanding.

### R-CMP-027 — Marital agreements during marriage
**Rule (write this):** Spouses may contract with each other about property during marriage, but such agreements are made between fiduciaries, so any advantage to one spouse raises a presumption of undue influence, and spouses cannot by contract alter their legal relations except as to property.

**Elements:** 1. Agreement during marriage. 2. Property subject matter. 3. Fiduciary scrutiny (§ 721). 4. Writing and § 852 if it changes character.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Terms penalizing personal conduct or promoting divorce contrary to public policy are unenforceable.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 721, 1500, 1620, 852.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H and W sign a postnup making H's business his SP; H's lawyer drafted it and W had no advice. The presumption of undue influence arises.

## II.C Commingled assets and tracing

### R-CMP-028 — Commingling and the burden to trace
**Rule (write this):** Mixing separate and community funds in one account does not change the character of either, but if the separate property cannot be traced, the commingled mass is treated as community property. The spouse claiming separate property bears the burden of tracing it, by direct tracing or the exhaustion method.

**Elements:** 1. Commingled account or asset. 2. Burden on SP proponent. 3. Accepted methods: direct tracing (Mix) or exhaustion (See). 4. Failure → CP.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Total recapitulation over the marriage is not enough (R-CMP-032).

**Authority:** *See v. See* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 778; *In re Marriage of Mix* (1975) 14 Cal.3d 604.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; frequent.

**Example:** H deposited a $50,000 inheritance into the joint account where both salaries went, and kept no records. The account and purchases from it are CP.

### R-CMP-029 — Direct tracing (Marriage of Mix)
**Rule (write this):** Under direct tracing, a spouse proves that an asset bought from a commingled account is separate property by showing, with adequate records, that separate funds were available in the account at the time of the purchase and that the spouse intended to use separate funds for it.

**Elements:** 1. SP funds on deposit when the purchase was made. 2. Intent to use SP funds for that purchase. 3. Adequate records or credible testimony.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Without records showing the timing and intent, the presumption prevails.

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Mix* (1975) 14 Cal.3d 604.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W deposits $40,000 of SP in a joint account on May 1 and on May 3 writes a $35,000 check for a car, noting "from inheritance." The car is SP by direct tracing.

### R-CMP-030 — Exhaustion method (See v. See)
**Rule (write this):** Under the exhaustion method, a spouse proves that an asset bought from a commingled account is separate property by showing that, at the time of the purchase, all community funds in the account had already been exhausted by family expenses, so the purchase must have been made with separate funds.

**Elements:** 1. Reconstruct the account at the purchase date. 2. Show CP was used up by family expenses. 3. Remaining funds were SP.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Requires contemporaneous records; total recapitulation fails.

**Authority:** *See v. See* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 778.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** By June 15 all $6,000 of salary in the account had been spent on rent and groceries; on June 20 H bought stock with the $30,000 left, all from his inheritance. The stock is SP.

### R-CMP-031 — Family expense presumption
**Rule (write this):** Family living expenses are presumed to be paid from community funds when community funds are available. A spouse who uses separate funds to pay family expenses is presumed to have made a gift to the community and is not reimbursed, absent an agreement.

**Elements:** 1. Family living expense (food, housing, utilities, ordinary costs). 2. Paid from SP. 3. No agreement for reimbursement → no reimbursement.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Contributions to the acquisition of a capital asset are not family expenses and are covered by § 2640.

**Authority:** *See v. See* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 778.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W paid $30,000 of family groceries and rent from her inheritance over three years. She has no claim for reimbursement.

### R-CMP-032 — Total recapitulation is insufficient
**Rule (write this):** A spouse cannot rebut the community presumption merely by showing that, over the whole marriage, more separate funds went into a commingled account than were spent on separate purposes; tracing must connect particular funds to a particular acquisition.

**Elements:** Overall-balance arithmetic alone is not tracing.

**Exceptions / defenses:** None; use Mix or See instead.

**Authority:** *See v. See* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 778.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H shows $500,000 of SP deposits and $300,000 of SP withdrawals over 20 years and claims the $200,000 balance. That alone fails to trace.

## II.D Community efforts and funds used for separate property

### R-CMP-033 — Community labor on separate property must be apportioned
**Rule (write this):** When a spouse's labor during marriage contributes to an increase in the value of separate property, usually a business, the increase must be apportioned between the separate estate, as a return on the separate capital, and the community, as a return on the spouse's labor. Growth caused only by the property itself or by market forces remains separate property.

**Elements:** 1. SP asset. 2. More-than-minimal community effort during marriage. 3. Increase in value. 4. Apportion by Pereira or Van Camp.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Passive investments managed with minimal effort stay SP.

**Authority:** *Pereira v. Pereira* (1909) 156 Cal. 1; *Van Camp v. Van Camp* (1921) 53 Cal.App. 17; Fam. Code § 770(a)(3).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W's premarital bakery triples in value during the marriage while she works 60 hours a week in it. The growth must be apportioned.

### R-CMP-034 — Pereira method
**Rule (write this):** Under Pereira, the separate property owner receives the value of the separate capital at the start of the marriage plus a fair rate of return on it for the period of the marriage, and the remaining value is community property. Courts use Pereira when the business's growth is mainly due to the personal efforts of the spouse.

**Elements:** 1. SP value at marriage. 2. Fair annual return (often the legal rate of interest, simple). 3. × years of marriage. 4. Remainder = CP.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No fixed rate; evidence may justify a higher return (*Beam v. Bank of America* (1971) 6 Cal.3d 12, where 7% was used absent other evidence [S-417]).

**Authority:** *Pereira v. Pereira* (1909) 156 Cal. 1; *Beam v. Bank of America* (1971) 6 Cal.3d 12.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's $200,000 consulting firm is worth $1 million at DOS after 8 years of H's personal work. At 10%, SP = $200,000 + $160,000 = $360,000; CP = $640,000.

### R-CMP-035 — Van Camp method
**Rule (write this):** Under Van Camp, the community receives the reasonable value of the spouse's services to the business during the marriage, less the salary or family expenses the community already received from the business, and the remaining value is separate property. Courts use Van Camp when the growth is mainly due to the nature of the separate capital or market conditions rather than the spouse's efforts.

**Elements:** 1. Reasonable annual compensation for the spouse's services. 2. × years of marriage. 3. Minus salary or family expenses already drawn. 4. Net = CP; remainder = SP.

**Exceptions / defenses:** If the community was fully compensated by salary, it receives nothing more.

**Authority:** *Van Camp v. Van Camp* (1921) 53 Cal.App. 17.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W's inherited orchard grows in value from $2 million to $4 million because land prices soar; her services were worth $80,000 a year and she drew $80,000 a year. The community gets nothing more; the growth is SP.

### R-CMP-036 — Choosing between Pereira and Van Camp
**Rule (write this):** The court may choose whichever apportionment method achieves substantial justice between the spouses: Pereira when growth is chiefly attributable to the spouse's personal efforts, and Van Camp when growth is chiefly attributable to the separate capital or outside factors. Neither formula is mandatory.

**Elements:** 1. Identify the main driver of growth. 2. Apply both formulas. 3. Argue which fits. 4. Conclude.

**Exceptions / defenses:** None mandatory. Whether a court may apply different methods to different periods of the same marriage is not settled in this repo [VERIFY V-900]; on an essay, argue for one method and mention the other.

**Authority:** *Beam v. Bank of America* (1971) 6 Cal.3d 12; *In re Marriage of Dekker* (1993) 17 Cal.App.4th 842 [S-410].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; always apply both and choose.

**Example:** A restaurant whose success depends on H's cooking and long hours → Pereira; a commercial building leased by an agent in a rising market → Van Camp.

### R-CMP-037 — Community payments on separately owned real property (Moore)
**Rule (write this):** When community funds reduce the principal of a purchase-money loan on a spouse's separately owned real property, the community acquires a pro tanto interest in the property: the community's share equals the principal it paid plus that principal's percentage of the purchase price multiplied by the appreciation during marriage. Payments of interest, taxes and insurance are not counted.

**Elements:** 1. SP real property (bought before marriage or with SP). 2. Community payments of principal on the purchase loan. 3. CP% = CP principal ÷ purchase price. 4. CP interest = CP principal + (CP% × marital appreciation). 5. SP = equity − CP interest.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Interest, taxes, insurance and maintenance excluded (treated as the cost of community use).

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Moore* (1980) 28 Cal.3d 366.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; show the formula.

**Example:** Purchase price $300,000; CP paid $60,000 principal; marital appreciation $200,000. CP% = 20%; CP interest = $60,000 + $40,000 = $100,000.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: similar to the PRC approach to a pre-marital home with a mortgage repaid jointly during marriage (approx.).

### R-CMP-038 — Premarital appreciation (Marsden)
**Rule (write this):** In a Moore apportionment, appreciation that occurred before the marriage belongs entirely to the separate property owner; only appreciation during the marriage is shared in proportion to the community's principal payments.

**Elements:** 1. Value at marriage. 2. Pre-marriage appreciation → SP. 3. Marital appreciation × CP% → CP.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Post-DOS appreciation is generally treated the same way as marital appreciation through trial (valuation at trial).

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Marsden* (1982) 130 Cal.App.3d 426.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H bought a condo for $200,000; it was worth $260,000 at marriage. The first $60,000 of appreciation is H's SP.

### R-CMP-039 — Community funds improving separate property
**Rule (write this):** When community funds are used during marriage to improve one spouse's separate property, the community is generally entitled to reimbursement, and the non-owner spouse's consent to the expenditure does not by itself show a gift. Courts measure the claim by the amount spent or by the increase in value the improvement produced.

**Elements:** 1. CP funds. 2. Improvement (not routine maintenance or property taxes) to SP. 3. No written gift or transmutation. 4. Measure: cost or value added (argue the measure that favors your client).

**Exceptions / defenses:** CP payments of property taxes on SP do not give a right of reimbursement (Wolfe). Some courts give the community a pro tanto interest instead, turning on whether the improvements enhanced the property's equity value (*Bono v. Clark* (2002) 103 Cal.App.4th 1409 [S-1010]). Allen rejected inferring a gift from the non-owner spouse's mere consent.

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Wolfe* (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 962 [S-1009]; *In re Marriage of Allen* (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 497 [S-409].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H and W spend $80,000 of CP to add a bedroom to W's premarital house. At divorce the community has a reimbursement claim measured by the $80,000 spent or the value the bedroom added; H argues for the larger figure.

### R-CMP-040 — Interest, taxes and insurance on separate property
**Rule (write this):** Community payments of interest, property taxes, insurance and maintenance on one spouse's separate property do not create a community ownership interest; they are generally treated as the cost of the community's use of the property and are not reimbursed.

**Elements:** 1. Payment type (not principal or improvement). 2. Community used the property (e.g., family home). 3. No ownership credit.

**Exceptions / defenses:** If the community received no use, argue reimbursement; courts vary.

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Moore* (1980) 28 Cal.3d 366.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** CP paid $50,000 of mortgage interest on H's premarital house where the family lived. No community interest arises from the interest payments.

### R-CMP-041 — Passive separate investments
**Rule (write this):** Separate property investments that grow through dividends, interest or market appreciation, with only minimal management by a spouse, remain separate property along with their earnings; substantial community effort spent managing them can create a community share that is apportioned.

**Elements:** 1. SP investment. 2. Level of spousal effort. 3. Minimal → SP; substantial → apportion.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Commingled income must still be traced.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 770(a)(3); *Pereira v. Pereira* (1909) 156 Cal. 1.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's inherited index fund doubles; he checks it twice a year. The growth is SP.

## II.E–F Separate property contributions

### R-CMP-042 — Reimbursement of SP contributions to community property (§ 2640(b))
**Rule (write this):** At dissolution, a spouse is reimbursed for separate property contributions to the acquisition of community property that are traced to a separate source, unless the spouse waived the right in writing. Contributions include down payments, improvements and principal payments, but not interest, maintenance, insurance or taxes; reimbursement is without interest or appreciation and may not exceed the property's net value at division.

**Elements:** 1. Contribution to acquisition (down payment, improvement, principal reduction). 2. Traced to SP. 3. To community (including § 2581 joint-title) property. 4. No written waiver. 5. Measure: dollar amount, capped at net value.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Written waiver; payments of interest, taxes, insurance or maintenance; untraceable contributions; SP used for family expenses (See).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 2640(a), (b); *In re Marriage of Walrath* (1998) 17 Cal.4th 907.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; heavily tested with § 2581.

**Example:** H used $60,000 of SP for the down payment on a joint-tenancy house now worth $40,000 net. H's reimbursement is capped at $40,000.

### R-CMP-043 — SP contributions to the other spouse's SP (§ 2640(c))
**Rule (write this):** A spouse is reimbursed for separate property contributions to the acquisition of the other spouse's separate property during marriage, unless there was a written transmutation or a written waiver; the reimbursement is without interest or appreciation and capped at the property's net value.

**Elements:** 1. SP of spouse A. 2. Contributed to acquisition of spouse B's SP. 3. During marriage. 4. No § 852 transmutation or written waiver.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A writing showing a gift defeats the claim.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 2640(c).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W pays $25,000 of her inheritance toward principal on H's premarital condo. At divorce, W recovers $25,000 from H.

### R-CMP-044 — Lucas and its statutory reversal
**Rule (write this):** Before 1984, a spouse who contributed separate funds to property taken in joint title was presumed to have made a gift and received nothing back unless the spouses agreed otherwise. The Legislature replaced that rule with the joint-title presumption and the right of reimbursement now found in §§ 2581 and 2640, though their retroactive reach to older acquisitions is limited.

**Elements:** 1. Acquisition date. 2. Post-1984 → §§ 2581/2640. 3. Pre-1984 → Lucas may govern [VERIFY V-329].

**Exceptions / defenses:** Due-process limits on retroactivity (*In re Marriage of Buol* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 751; *In re Marriage of Fabian* (1986) 41 Cal.3d 440; *In re Marriage of Heikes* (1995) 10 Cal.4th 1211).

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Lucas* (1980) 27 Cal.3d 808; Fam. Code §§ 2581, 2640.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; usually one sentence of history.

**Example:** W's SP down payment in 2015 on a joint-tenancy house is reimbursed under § 2640; Lucas no longer applies.

### R-CMP-045 — Transmutation and § 2640 reimbursement
**Rule (write this):** When a spouse transmutes separate property into community or joint form, the spouse may still seek § 2640 reimbursement of the separate value contributed unless the writing expressly waives reimbursement or has that effect.

**Elements:** 1. Valid transmutation of SP into CP/joint form. 2. Writing silent on reimbursement. 3. Traced SP contribution.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Only an express written waiver or "a writing that has the effect of a waiver" bars the claim; no magic words are needed, but the contributor's donative intent alone does not bar reimbursement (Carpenter). The opponent argues the transmutation writing itself has the effect of a waiver.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 852, 2640(b); *In re Marriage of Carpenter* (2002) 100 Cal.App.4th 424 [S-1013].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W deeds her inherited $400,000 house into joint tenancy with H. At divorce W argues for $400,000 off the top under § 2640; H argues the deed waived it.

## II.G and II.J.2 Education and training

### R-CMP-046 — Education is not property; § 2641 reimbursement
**Rule (write this):** A professional degree or enhanced earning capacity is not community property. Instead, the community is reimbursed, with interest at the legal rate, for community contributions to a spouse's education or training that substantially enhanced that spouse's earning capacity, and loans incurred during marriage for that education are assigned to the educated spouse. This reimbursement is the community's exclusive remedy.

**Elements:** 1. Community (or QCP) payments for education/training or its loans. 2. Substantial enhancement of earning capacity. 3. Reimbursement + legal-rate interest from the end of each calendar year of payment. 4. Loans assigned to educated spouse.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Reduction or modification under § 2641(c) (R-CMP-047). Support may still consider the education.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 2641(a), (b), (d); *In re Marriage of Sullivan* (1984) 37 Cal.3d 762.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** CP paid $70,000 of H's MBA tuition three years before W filed. The community is reimbursed $70,000 plus interest; H's MBA loans are his.

### R-CMP-047 — § 2641 reductions and the ten-year presumptions
**Rule (write this):** The education reimbursement and loan assignment are reduced or modified to the extent they would be unjust, including where the community substantially benefited from the education, where the other spouse received comparable community-funded education, or where the education reduces the educated spouse's need for support. The community is presumed not to have substantially benefited from contributions made less than ten years before the proceeding began, and presumed to have substantially benefited from contributions made more than ten years before.

**Elements:** 1. Substantial community benefit (ten-year presumptions). 2. Offsetting education of the other spouse. 3. Reduced need for support.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The presumptions affect the burden of proof and are rebuttable.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 2641(b)(2), (c).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** CP paid W's nursing school 12 years before the divorce and the family lived on her nurse's salary since. The community is presumed to have benefited; reimbursement is likely denied.

## II.H Retirement and disability

### R-CMP-048 — Pensions are community to the extent earned during marriage
**Rule (write this):** Retirement benefits are deferred compensation for services, so they are community property to the extent earned by employment during marriage and before separation, whether or not they are vested or matured at divorce.

**Elements:** 1. Pension or retirement right. 2. Earned through service during marriage before DOS. 3. Vesting irrelevant to character.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Federal benefits may be preempted (R-CMP-100 to R-CMP-103).

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Brown* (1976) 15 Cal.3d 838.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H worked 12 years for the city, 8 of them during marriage, and his pension is not yet vested. The marital portion is CP.

### R-CMP-049 — Time rule
**Rule (write this):** The community share of a defined-benefit pension is usually computed by the time rule: the number of months of service during marriage before separation divided by the total months of service credited when benefits are paid, multiplied by each benefit payment.

**Elements:** 1. Numerator: months of service from marriage to DOS. 2. Denominator: total months of service. 3. Fraction × benefit = CP; half to each spouse.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Defined-contribution plans are traced by contributions and growth; courts may depart from the time rule where it would be inequitable.

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Brown* (1976) 15 Cal.3d 838 (community owns the portion earned during marriage; the time rule is the standard measure).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** 10 of H's 25 years of service were during marriage. 40% of each payment is CP; W receives 20% of each payment.

### R-CMP-050 — Dividing retirement benefits
**Rule (write this):** The court must make orders ensuring each spouse receives his or her full community share of any retirement plan, either by cashing out the present value of the community share with other assets or by dividing each payment in kind when received, typically through a qualified domestic relations order. When the employee spouse is eligible to retire, the other spouse may elect to receive his or her share immediately.

**Elements:** 1. Identify community share. 2. Cash-out vs. in-kind (reserved jurisdiction). 3. QDRO for ERISA plans. 4. Gillmore election.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Federal preemption (VIII).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 2610; *In re Marriage of Gillmore* (1981) 29 Cal.3d 418; 29 U.S.C. § 1056(d)(3).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H, eligible to retire at 55, keeps working. W may elect to receive her community share now rather than wait (Gillmore).

### R-CMP-051 — Disability benefits
**Rule (write this):** Disability benefits are characterized by what they replace: to the extent they replace a spouse's earnings after separation they are that spouse's separate property, and to the extent they substitute for retirement benefits earned during marriage they are community property.

**Elements:** 1. Source of the right (employment during marriage; CP-paid premiums). 2. Timing of payments (before/after DOS). 3. Replacement of post-DOS earnings → SP; substitute for retirement → CP.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A policy renewed after DOS with SP yields SP benefits (*In re Marriage of Elfmont* (1995) 9 Cal.4th 1026) [VERIFY V-317].

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Stenquist* (1978) 21 Cal.3d 779; *In re Marriage of Saslow* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 848.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2022 Q2.

**Example:** W becomes disabled after DOS and receives $3,000 a month under a CP-funded policy; her retirement would have paid $1,800. $1,200 replaces post-DOS earnings (SP); $1,800 substitutes for retirement (CP to the extent of marital service).

### R-CMP-052 — Severance pay and earnings substitutes
**Rule (write this):** Severance pay, workers' compensation and similar payments are characterized by what they compensate: payments for services performed during marriage are community property, and payments replacing earnings lost after separation are separate property.

**Elements:** 1. Identify what the payment replaces. 2. Period covered (before/after DOS).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Courts disagree on severance packages that reward past service and compensate for lost future employment [VERIFY V-318].

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 760, 771; replacement principle of *In re Marriage of Stenquist* (1978) 21 Cal.3d 779.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H is laid off three months after DOS and gets one year's salary as severance tied to future lost wages. It is largely his SP.

## II.I and II.K Credit acquisitions and special assets

### R-CMP-053 — Employee stock options
**Rule (write this):** Employee stock options granted during marriage are community property to the extent they were earned by community effort, even if they vest after separation. The court allocates them with a time-rule fraction that fits their purpose: measured from the start of employment when the options mainly reward past services (Hug), or from the date of grant when they mainly reward future services (Nelson).

**Elements:** 1. Grant during marriage (or for marital services). 2. Purpose: past or future services. 3. Hug fraction: start of employment → DOS ÷ start of employment → exercisable date. 4. Nelson fraction: grant → DOS ÷ grant → exercisable date. 5. × shares vesting.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No formula is mandatory; the trial court has broad discretion and may fit the time rule to the employer's purpose in granting the options.

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Hug* (1984) 154 Cal.App.3d 780 [S-426]; *In re Marriage of Nelson* (1986) 177 Cal.App.3d 150 [S-427] [S-1012].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W got 4,000 options 2 years before DOS that vest 4 years after grant, to retain her for future work. Nelson: 2/4 = 50% of the vesting shares are CP.

### R-CMP-054 — Life insurance
**Rule (write this):** A whole-life policy is apportioned according to the community and separate premiums paid. A term policy's proceeds generally take the character of the premium paid for the final term, so a term policy renewed after separation with separate funds is generally separate property. A policy bought with community funds remains community property even if titled in one spouse's name, absent a valid transmutation.

**Elements:** 1. Type of policy (whole vs. term). 2. Source of premiums (all, or final term). 3. Title vs. transmutation. 4. Beneficiary designation and spousal consent.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Federal programs (NSLI, SGLI, FEGLI) follow federal designations (R-CMP-102). Earlier cases split on the theory for term policies (e.g., *Estate of Logan* (1987) 191 Cal.App.3d 319); Burwell adopted the final-term-premium rule. Spengler is cited for treating group term coverage as having no community value beyond the community-funded term [VERIFY V-901].

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Valli* (2014) 58 Cal.4th 1396; *Burwell v. Burwell* (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 1 [S-1011] [S-1021]; *In re Marriage of Spengler* (1992) 5 Cal.App.4th 288 [S-411]; Prob. Code § 5020.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's term policy was paid with CP for 10 years; after DOS he paid the last annual premium from his post-DOS salary and then died. The proceeds are likely SP.

### R-CMP-055 — Goodwill of a business or practice
**Rule (write this):** Goodwill of a business or professional practice developed during marriage is a community asset divisible at dissolution, valued as of the time of division without including the value of the spouse's post-separation efforts; a spouse's personal skill or enhanced earning capacity, by itself, is not goodwill.

**Elements:** 1. Business or practice that is CP (in whole or part). 2. Goodwill developed during marriage. 3. Valuation excludes post-DOS efforts. 4. Factors: age, health, skill, reputation, past profits [VERIFY V-328].

**Exceptions / defenses:** Goodwill attributable to an SP business before marriage is apportioned like other value.

**Authority:** *Golden v. Golden* (1969) 270 Cal.App.2d 401; *In re Marriage of Foster* (1974) 42 Cal.App.3d 577 [S-432]; *In re Marriage of Lopez* (1974) 38 Cal.App.3d 93.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W's dental practice, opened during marriage, has an established patient base worth $300,000 beyond its equipment. That goodwill is CP.

### R-CMP-056 — Personal injury damages: characterization
**Rule (write this):** Personal-injury damages are community property if the cause of action arose during marriage. They are the injured spouse's separate property if the cause of action arose after a judgment of dissolution or legal separation, while the injured spouse was living separate from the other spouse, or against the other spouse; damages for a cause of action that arose before marriage are separate property.

**Elements:** 1. When did the cause of action arise (injury date)? 2. Who is the tortfeasor (third party or spouse)? 3. Commingled? 4. Reimbursement of expenses paid by the other estate.

**Exceptions / defenses:** At dissolution, community PI damages go to the injured spouse unless justice requires otherwise (R-CMP-078). A cause of action that arose before marriage is SP as property owned before marriage [VERIFY V-331].

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 780, 781 [S-429] [S-1005], 770(a)(1).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H is rear-ended during the marriage and settles for $90,000. The settlement is CP when received.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC treats personal-injury compensation as the injured spouse's personal property from the start (approx.).

### R-CMP-057 — Lottery winnings, prizes and intellectual property
**Rule (write this):** A prize or lottery win obtained with a ticket bought with community funds during marriage is community property, and intellectual property created by a spouse's labor during marriage is community property to the extent created during marriage. Concealing such an asset breaches the fiduciary duty and can cost the concealing spouse the entire asset.

**Elements:** 1. Source (CP funds or marital labor). 2. Time (during marriage). 3. Disclosure duty.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A ticket bought with post-DOS earnings yields SP [VERIFY V-332].

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 760, 1101(h); *In re Marriage of Rossi* (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 34.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W wins $1.3 million with a ticket bought during marriage and hides it until after the divorce. The court awards all of it to H (Rossi).

### R-CMP-058 — Credit acquisitions: intent of the lender
**Rule (write this):** Property bought on credit during marriage is presumptively community property. Loan proceeds, and what they buy, are separate property only if the lender intended to rely, and did rely, primarily (historically solely) on the borrowing spouse's separate property; otherwise they are community property.

**Elements:** 1. Loan during marriage. 2. Lender's intent and reliance (SP collateral, SP income, application listing SP). 3. Signs of community reliance (community income, the other spouse's signature, community collateral).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Loans after DOS follow the post-DOS rules.

**Authority:** *Gudelj v. Gudelj* (1953) 41 Cal.2d 202; *In re Marriage of Grinius* (1985) 166 Cal.App.3d 1179 [S-428] (Grinius is summarized as requiring reliance solely on SP; whether current law says "primarily" or "solely" [VERIFY V-902]).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2022 Q2 and Feb 2024 Q1.

**Example:** H borrows $50,000 for a sailboat, pledging his inherited stock as sole collateral, and the bank looks only to the stock. The boat is SP.

### R-CMP-059 — Forged signatures and unauthorized borrowing
**Rule (write this):** A spouse who forges the other spouse's signature on a loan or encumbrance breaches the fiduciary duty; the forged spouse is not personally liable and that spouse's separate property is not reachable, but the community estate remains liable for a debt incurred during marriage, and an encumbrance of community real property without joinder is voidable.

**Elements:** 1. Forgery or unauthorized act. 2. § 910 community liability. 3. § 913 SP protection. 4. § 1102 joinder for real property. 5. § 1101 remedies against the forger.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Good-faith encumbrancer protection where title is in one spouse's name alone (§ 1102(c)) [S-1007].

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 721, 910, 913, 1100(e), 1101, 1102.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2024 Q1.

**Example:** H signs W's name to a loan secured by the CP house to buy a garage. W may void the deed of trust (no joinder) and sue H under § 1101; the bank can still reach the community estate on the note.

## II.J Reimbursement

### R-CMP-060 — Reimbursement map
**Rule (write this):** Contributions from one marital estate to another create rights of reimbursement only where a statute or case recognizes them: separate contributions to community or the other spouse's separate acquisitions (§ 2640); community contributions to education (§ 2641); community funds improving separate property; post-separation separate payments of community debts (Epstein); and statutory reimbursement for debts. Separate funds used for family expenses are not reimbursed.

**Elements:** 1. Identify the contributing and benefited estates. 2. Match to the recognized remedy. 3. Check waiver/writing. 4. Apply measure.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Written waivers; gift presumption for family expenses (See).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 920, 2626, 2640, 2641; *In re Marriage of Epstein* (1979) 24 Cal.3d 76; *See v. See* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 778.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W's SP down payment (§ 2640) and her post-DOS mortgage payments (Epstein) are both reimbursed; her SP grocery spending is not.

### R-CMP-061 — Reimbursement for payment of debts
**Rule (write this):** A statutory right of reimbursement for applying one estate's property to a debt arises whoever applied the property and whether the payment was voluntary or not; it is measured by the value of the property when the right arises, is subject to an express written waiver, and must be exercised no later than the earlier of three years after actual knowledge of the payment or the property-division or death proceeding.

**Elements:** 1. Statutory basis (e.g., §§ 914(b), 915(b), 916; § 2626 at dissolution). 2. Value at the time the right arises. 3. Timely assertion (earlier of the two deadlines). 4. No express written waiver.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Where community property paid a support obligation not arising from the marriage, the community recovers only to the extent the obligor's non-exempt separate income was available but not applied (§ 915(b)). Reimbursement under § 1000 (tort liabilities) may not be exercised more than seven years after actual knowledge.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 920 [S-1019], 914(b), 915 [S-1008], 1000.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** CP paid $20,000 of H's child support from his prior marriage while H's separate rental income was available. The community is reimbursed under § 915.

## III Management and control of community property

### R-CMP-062 — Equal management
**Rule (write this):** Either spouse has the management and control of community personal property, with the same absolute power of disposition, other than by will, that the spouse has over separate property, subject to the statutory limits on gifts, household goods, businesses, real property and the fiduciary duty.

**Elements:** 1. Community personal property. 2. Either spouse may act alone. 3. Limits: §§ 1100(b)–(e), 1102.

**Exceptions / defenses:** See R-CMP-064 to R-CMP-067.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1100(a).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W sells the couple's CP car for its market value without asking H. The sale is valid.

### R-CMP-063 — Fiduciary duty between spouses
**Rule (write this):** In managing community property, each spouse must act toward the other under the rules governing fiduciary relationships, with the highest good faith and fair dealing and without taking unfair advantage, until the property is divided. The duty includes full disclosure of all material facts about the existence, character and value of community assets and debts, equal access to information on request, and accounting for any benefit derived from community property without consent.

**Elements:** 1. Fiduciary relationship (marriage until division). 2. Duties: good faith, disclosure, access, accounting. 3. Breach impairing the other's one-half interest. 4. Remedies under § 1101.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Good-faith business judgment losses are not breaches.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 721(b), 1100(e).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; tested in 4 tagged essays.

**Example:** H moves $100,000 of CP into an account in his mother's name before filing. He breached his fiduciary duty.

### R-CMP-064 — Gifts of community personal property
**Rule (write this):** A spouse may not make a gift of community personal property, or dispose of it for less than fair and reasonable value, without the other spouse's written consent. Such a gift is voidable by the non-consenting spouse: entirely while the donor spouse is alive, and as to one-half after the donor spouse's death.

**Elements:** 1. CP personal property. 2. Gift or transfer for inadequate value. 3. No written consent. 4. Remedy: set aside (whole during life; half after death) and § 1101.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Ratification by the non-consenting spouse; small customary gifts (argue reasonableness). § 1100(b) does not apply to gifts both spouses make together to third parties or to gifts between the spouses.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1100(b); *Spreckels v. Spreckels* (1916) 172 Cal. 775 [S-433]; *Fields v. Michael* (1949) 91 Cal.App.2d 443, 448 [S-412] [S-1015].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H gives his girlfriend a $30,000 CP watch. W can recover the whole watch while H is alive, or half its value if she sues after H's death.

### R-CMP-065 — Family dwelling furnishings and clothing
**Rule (write this):** A spouse may not sell, convey or encumber community personal property used as the family dwelling, the furniture, furnishings or fittings of the home, or the clothing of the other spouse or the minor children without the other spouse's written consent.

**Elements:** 1. Listed household or clothing items. 2. Sale, conveyance or encumbrance. 3. No written consent.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Consent in writing; ratification.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1100(c).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W pawns the CP dining set without H's written consent. H may set aside the transaction.

### R-CMP-066 — Community business management
**Rule (write this):** A spouse who operates or manages a business that is all or substantially all community property has primary management and control of it and may act alone, but must give the other spouse prior written notice of any sale, lease, exchange, encumbrance or other disposition of all or substantially all of the business's personal property.

**Elements:** 1. CP business managed by one spouse. 2. Ordinary transactions: sole authority. 3. Disposition of all or substantially all business personal property: prior written notice. 4. Breach → § 1101.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Business real property still needs joinder (§ 1102).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1100(d).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H sells all the restaurant's equipment and inventory to a competitor without telling W. He violated § 1100(d) and W has § 1101 remedies.

### R-CMP-067 — Community real property: joinder
**Rule (write this):** Both spouses must join in any instrument by which community real property is leased for more than one year, sold, conveyed or encumbered. A transfer by one spouse alone is voidable by the other, but where record title stands in the transferring spouse's name alone, a good-faith purchaser or encumbrancer without knowledge of the marriage is protected by a presumption of validity, and an action to avoid the transfer must be brought within one year of recording.

**Elements:** 1. CP real property. 2. Lease > 1 year, sale, conveyance or encumbrance. 3. One spouse acted alone. 4. BFP without knowledge of marriage + title in transferor's name → presumed valid. 5. One-year limit from recording.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Leases, mortgages, conveyances and transfers of real property between the spouses themselves are outside § 1102 (§ 1102(b)).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1102 [S-1007].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2012 Q2, Feb 2014 Q2.

**Example:** H, with title in his name alone, sells the CP office building to his friend who knows H is married. W sues within a year; the sale is voidable.

### R-CMP-068 — Remedies for breach of fiduciary duty (§ 1101)
**Rule (write this):** A spouse may sue the other for any breach of fiduciary duty that impairs the claimant's one-half interest in the community estate and obtain an accounting, an order adding the claimant's name to title, and an award of 50% of any asset undisclosed or transferred in breach plus attorney's fees and costs, or 100% of the asset when the breach involved oppression, fraud or malice under Civil Code § 3294.

**Elements:** 1. Breach of fiduciary duty. 2. Impairment of the claimant's interest. 3. Remedy: accounting; title; 50%; 100% if § 3294. 4. Timely: within three years of the claimant's actual knowledge of the transaction (§ 1101(d)(1)), or without that limit when brought upon a spouse's death or with a dissolution, legal separation or nullity action (§ 1101(d)(2)).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Limitations; no impairment. Title cannot be reformed for a general-partnership interest held by the other spouse and certain other business interests (§ 1101(c)).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1101 [S-405] [S-1003]; Civ. Code § 3294; *In re Marriage of Rossi* (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 34.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H secretly transfers a $200,000 CP brokerage account to his brother. W recovers $100,000 (50%) plus fees, or $200,000 if the transfer was fraudulent.

## IV Management and control of separate property

### R-CMP-069 — Management of separate property
**Rule (write this):** Each spouse has sole management and control of his or her separate property and may sell, encumber, give away or devise it without the other spouse's consent, subject to the fiduciary duty in transactions between the spouses and to any community interest created by community labor or funds.

**Elements:** 1. SP. 2. Sole power of disposition. 3. Limits: interspousal fiduciary duty; community interests; creditor liability.

**Exceptions / defenses:** QCP during marriage is generally treated the same way during life [VERIFY V-327], subject to recapture at death (R-CMP-093).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 770(b), 721.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W sells her inherited stock without H's knowledge. The sale is valid; the proceeds are her SP.

## V Separation

### R-CMP-070 — Date of separation (§ 70)
**Rule (write this):** The date of separation is the date a complete and final break in the marital relationship occurred, shown by both a spouse's expression to the other spouse of the intent to end the marriage and conduct consistent with that intent. The court considers all relevant evidence, and spouses may separate while still living under the same roof.

**Elements:** 1. Expressed intent to the other spouse. 2. Consistent conduct (moving out, separate finances, dating, filing separately, ending marital relations). 3. Totality of evidence.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Reconciliation attempts or continued joint finances cut against an earlier date. § 70 abrogated *In re Marriage of Davis* (2015) 61 Cal.4th 846 (separate residences required) and *In re Marriage of Norviel* (2002) 102 Cal.App.4th 1152 [S-435].

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 70 [S-400].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2017 Q1.

**Example:** In March W tells H "I'm done; I'm filing for divorce," moves to the guest room, opens her own account and stops joint holidays, though she stays in the house for the children. March is likely the DOS.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC joint property generally continues until the divorce itself; California ends community acquisitions at the date of separation (approx.).

### R-CMP-071 — Earnings and accumulations after separation
**Rule (write this):** The earnings and accumulations of a spouse, and of the minor children living with or in the custody of that spouse, after the date of separation are that spouse's separate property, as are earnings after a judgment of legal separation; property acquired with them is separate property.

**Elements:** 1. Earnings or accumulations. 2. After DOS (or legal separation judgment). 3. Traceable to post-DOS earnings.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Payments received after DOS for work done before DOS (bonuses, commissions) are CP.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 771, 772.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2024 Q1.

**Example:** After DOS H invests his paychecks in a brokerage account. The account is H's SP.

### R-CMP-072 — Post-separation growth of a community business
**Rule (write this):** When a community business increases in value after separation because of one spouse's post-separation efforts, the increase is apportioned between the community, as the return on the community's capital and passive growth, and the working spouse's separate property, as the return on post-separation labor.

**Elements:** 1. Community business. 2. Growth between DOS and trial. 3. Cause: spouse's post-DOS labor vs. capital/market. 4. Apportion (reverse Pereira/Van Camp).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Purely passive growth stays CP [VERIFY V-325].

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Imperato* (1975) 45 Cal.App.3d 432 [S-421]; Fam. Code §§ 771, 2552.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W's CP design firm was worth $400,000 at DOS and $700,000 at trial, entirely because W landed new clients after DOS. Most of the $300,000 increase is W's SP.

### R-CMP-073 — Epstein credits
**Rule (write this):** A spouse who uses separate funds, such as post-separation earnings, to pay a community obligation after separation and before trial is generally entitled to reimbursement from the community, unless the payment was a gift, was in lieu of support, was made under an agreement not to seek reimbursement, or is offset by the paying spouse's exclusive use of the asset.

**Elements:** 1. Post-DOS, pre-trial payment. 2. With SP. 3. Of a community debt (mortgage, taxes, insurance, community credit card). 4. No disqualifying circumstance [VERIFY V-321].

**Exceptions / defenses:** Payments that were really support; exclusive-use offset (Watts).

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Epstein* (1979) 24 Cal.3d 76; Fam. Code § 2626.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** After DOS W pays $2,500 a month on the CP house mortgage from her salary for 12 months. She may claim $30,000 in Epstein credits.

### R-CMP-074 — Watts charges
**Rule (write this):** A spouse who has exclusive use of a community asset, usually the family home, after separation may be charged for the reasonable value of that use, payable to the community, in the court's discretion.

**Elements:** 1. Post-DOS exclusive use. 2. Community asset. 3. Fair rental value. 4. Discretion (children, support, offsetting credits).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Use in lieu of support; offset against Epstein credits.

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Watts* (1985) 171 Cal.App.3d 366.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W lives alone in the CP house (rental value $3,000/month) for a year after DOS. H seeks a $36,000 Watts charge, offset against W's Epstein credits.

## VI Division at dissolution

### R-CMP-075 — Equal division and valuation
**Rule (write this):** Unless the spouses agree in writing or stipulate in open court, the court divides the community estate, community and quasi-community property, equally, valuing assets as near as practicable to the time of trial unless good cause supports an alternate date between separation and trial. The court may divide items in kind or award them to one spouse with offsetting assets, so long as the net community estate is split equally; separate property is confirmed to its owner.

**Elements:** 1. Identify community estate. 2. Value (trial date or alternate). 3. Divide net estate equally (aggregate). 4. Confirm SP. 5. Apply deviations.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Statutory deviations (R-CMP-076 to R-CMP-081); agreement.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 2550, 2552, 2601, 63.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** The CP house ($500,000 equity) goes to W; H gets the CP brokerage account ($300,000) and a $100,000 equalizing payment from W.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC courts divide joint property with discretion favoring children, the woman and the non-fault party; California's division is equal by statute (approx.).

### R-CMP-076 — Misappropriation (§ 2602)
**Rule (write this):** As an additional award or an offset, the court may award from a spouse's share any sum that spouse deliberately misappropriated to the exclusion of the other spouse's interest in the community estate.

**Elements:** 1. Deliberate misappropriation. 2. Of community funds/assets. 3. Exclusion of the other's interest. 4. Award or offset.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Good-faith expenditures for family or community purposes are not misappropriation.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 2602.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H gambled away $40,000 of CP savings in Las Vegas after DOS. W receives $20,000 more from the community (or H's share is reduced).

### R-CMP-077 — Failure to disclose
**Rule (write this):** Each spouse must fully disclose all assets and debts during dissolution; a spouse who conceals or fails to disclose a community asset may be ordered to pay the other 50% of its value, or 100% where the concealment involved fraud, oppression or malice, and may also face monetary sanctions, while omitted assets remain subject to later division.

**Elements:** 1. Disclosure duty (§§ 1100(e), 2100 et seq.). 2. Concealment or nondisclosure. 3. Remedy: § 1101(g)/(h); § 2107 sanctions; § 271 fees; § 2556 omitted assets; § 2122 set-aside.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Noncompliance that is justified.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 1101(g)–(h), 2100–2107, 2122, 2556, 271; *In re Marriage of Feldman* (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1470 [S-431]; *In re Marriage of Rossi* (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 34.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W omits a $150,000 CP retirement account from her disclosure declarations. The court may award H the account in full if the omission was fraudulent.

### R-CMP-078 — Personal-injury damages at dissolution: third-party tortfeasor (§ 2603)
**Rule (write this):** Community personal-injury damages are assigned to the injured spouse at dissolution unless the court, considering the spouses' economic condition and needs, the time since the recovery or the accrual of the claim, and all other facts, finds that justice requires another division; even then the injured spouse must receive at least one-half. Damages commingled with other community property lose this treatment and are divided equally.

**Elements:** 1. CP PI damages (claim arose during marriage; not SP under § 781). 2. Not commingled. 3. Default: all to injured spouse. 4. Justice exception: at least 50% to injured spouse.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Commingling; § 781 SP categories; reimbursement of expenses paid by the other estate (§ 781(b)).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 2603.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2015 Q4.

**Example:** W's $120,000 accident settlement sits untouched in an account in her name. At divorce it goes entirely to W unless H shows great need.

### R-CMP-079 — Personal-injury claims between spouses
**Rule (write this):** Damages one spouse recovers from the other for a personal injury during marriage are the injured spouse's separate property, and community property may not be used to pay the tortfeasor spouse's liability to the injured spouse until the tortfeasor's non-exempt separate property is exhausted, unless the injured spouse consents in writing after the injury.

**Elements:** 1. Spouse v. spouse injury claim. 2. Recovery = injured spouse's SP. 3. Satisfaction: tortfeasor's SP first (same rule for contribution or indemnity owed to a third-party tortfeasor). 4. Written consent after the injury; insurance unaffected.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Rights to indemnity under an insurance or other contract are preserved, even if CP paid the premiums (§ 782(c)).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 781(c), 782 [S-406] [S-1004].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's drunk driving injures W, his passenger. W's $100,000 judgment is her SP; H must pay it from his SP before CP is used.

### R-CMP-080 — Educational loans at division
**Rule (write this):** A loan incurred during marriage for a spouse's education or training is not divided as a community debt but is assigned to the spouse who received the education, subject to reduction where the community substantially benefited or other § 2641 factors apply.

**Elements:** 1. Educational loan. 2. Incurred during marriage. 3. Assigned to educated spouse. 4. § 2641(c) adjustments.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Assignment governs the spouses only; creditors may still reach the community estate (§ 910).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 2627, 2641(b)(2) [S-407].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's $90,000 law-school loans from year 2 of the marriage are assigned to H at the year-5 divorce.

### R-CMP-081 — Tort liabilities and debts exceeding assets at division
**Rule (write this):** At division, a liability for death or injury that was not incurred in an activity for the benefit of the community is assigned to the spouse whose act or omission caused it, without offset; and where community debts exceed community assets, the excess is assigned as the court finds just and equitable, considering the spouses' relative ability to pay.

**Elements:** 1. Tort liability: community-benefit activity? If not → assign to tortfeasor without offset. 2. Debts > assets: allocate excess by ability to pay.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Liability to creditors is unaffected (§§ 910, 1000). The State Bar outline's items VI.B.6–7 may label these differently [VERIFY V-025].

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 2627 [S-407] [S-1006], 2622(b), 1000(b).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's $80,000 judgment for assaulting a neighbor is assigned to H alone at the divorce, with no offset against W's share.

### R-CMP-082 — Retirement and pension benefits at division
**Rule (write this):** At division, the court must protect each spouse's full community share of retirement benefits, using the time rule to fix the community fraction and either a present-value cash-out or an in-kind division of future payments, through a qualified domestic relations order where the plan requires one.

**Elements:** 1. Community fraction (time rule). 2. Method (cash-out or in-kind). 3. QDRO. 4. Survivor benefits. 5. Federal limits.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Social Security and some federal benefits cannot be divided (R-CMP-100, R-CMP-103).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 2610; *In re Marriage of Brown* (1976) 15 Cal.3d 838.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** The court reserves jurisdiction over H's unvested pension and orders W paid her share of each future payment by QDRO.

### R-CMP-083 — Out-of-state real property at dissolution
**Rule (write this):** When the community estate includes real property in another state, the court should divide the estate so that title to the out-of-state land need not change, for example by awarding it to one spouse and offsetting with other assets; if that is impossible, the court may order the spouses to execute conveyances or award the money value of the interest.

**Elements:** 1. Out-of-state land that is CP/QCP. 2. Preferred: allocate without changing title. 3. Fallback: order conveyance or award money value.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The court acts in personam; it cannot directly transfer foreign title.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 2660 [S-418]; Fam. Code § 125.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** The QCP ranch in Montana (H's name) goes to H; W receives the California house of equal value.

## VI.C Debts

### R-CMP-084 — Community estate liable for either spouse's debts
**Rule (write this):** The community estate is liable for a debt incurred by either spouse before or during marriage, regardless of which spouse manages the property and whether one or both spouses are parties to the debt or judgment; for this purpose "during marriage" excludes the period after the date of separation and before a judgment of dissolution or legal separation.

**Elements:** 1. Debt incurred before marriage or during marriage before DOS. 2. Community estate (CP + QCP) reachable. 3. Exceptions by statute.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Earnings exception for premarital debts (§ 911); post-DOS debts excluded.

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 910 [S-414], 912.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W's pre-marriage credit-card debt of $15,000 can be collected from the CP brokerage account.

### R-CMP-085 — Earnings exception for a spouse's premarital debts
**Rule (write this):** A married person's earnings during marriage are not liable for the other spouse's premarital debts, and they stay protected after payment while held in a deposit account in which the debtor spouse has no right of withdrawal and which is not commingled with other community property, except insignificant amounts.

**Elements:** 1. Non-debtor's earnings. 2. Premarital debt of the other spouse. 3. Separate account; no withdrawal right; uncommingled.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Commingling or a joint account destroys the protection.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 911.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's premarital creditor tries to garnish W's paycheck account, which is in W's name alone and holds only her salary. The account is protected.

### R-CMP-086 — Liability of separate property and quasi-community property
**Rule (write this):** A spouse's separate property is liable for that spouse's own debts incurred before or during marriage but not for the other spouse's debts, except debts for necessaries, and joining in an encumbrance to secure the other's debt does not expose the joining spouse's separate property. Quasi-community property is liable to the same extent as community property.

**Elements:** 1. Whose debt? 2. Debtor's SP → liable. 3. Non-debtor's SP → not liable (necessaries exception). 4. QCP = CP for liability.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Necessaries (§ 914).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 912, 913.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's business-loan default cannot be collected from W's inherited condo.

### R-CMP-087 — Necessaries
**Rule (write this):** A married person is personally liable for debts incurred for the necessaries of life of his or her spouse before the date of separation, and for the common necessaries of life of the spouse while they live separately, and that person's separate property may be applied, with reimbursement if community property or the other spouse's separate property was available but not used.

**Elements:** 1. Necessaries (marital standard) before DOS; common necessaries while living separately. 2. Personal liability of non-incurring spouse. 3. SP reachable. 4. Reimbursement right.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No liability for support while living apart by agreement unless support is stipulated (§ 4302).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 914 [S-413].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W incurs $8,000 of hospital bills during the marriage. The hospital can reach H's SP.

### R-CMP-088 — Tort liability: order of satisfaction
**Rule (write this):** A spouse is not liable for the other spouse's torts merely because of the marriage. A tortfeasor spouse's liability is satisfied first from community property and then from that spouse's separate property if the tort occurred while performing an activity for the benefit of the community; otherwise it is satisfied first from the tortfeasor's separate property and then from community property.

**Elements:** 1. Tort by one spouse. 2. Activity for the benefit of the community? 3. Order: CP then SP (community benefit) or SP then CP (no benefit). 4. Non-tortfeasor's SP not reachable.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Liability insurance proceeds; spouse-v-spouse claims (§ 782).

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1000.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2012 Q2, Jul 2013 Q3.

**Example:** W, driving home from a personal errand to buy herself a gift, injures a cyclist. The judgment is paid first from W's SP, then CP; H's SP is safe.

### R-CMP-089 — Allocation of debts at dissolution
**Rule (write this):** At dissolution, premarital debts are confirmed to the spouse who incurred them; debts incurred during marriage before separation are divided equally with the community estate; debts incurred after separation and before judgment are confirmed to the incurring spouse unless they were for the common necessaries of either spouse or the children, which are allocated by need and ability to pay; and the court may order reimbursement for community debts paid after separation.

**Elements:** 1. Date incurred. 2. Purpose (necessaries; community benefit). 3. Allocation rule (§§ 2621–2625). 4. Reimbursement (§ 2626).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Debts not for community benefit may be confirmed to the incurring spouse (§ 2625); educational loans and certain tort liabilities (§ 2627).

**Authority:** Fam. Code §§ 2620–2627.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** After DOS H runs up $10,000 on a credit card for vacations. The debt is confirmed to H.

### R-CMP-090 — Liability after division
**Rule (write this):** After division, a spouse's separate property and the property received in the division remain liable for that spouse's own premarital and marital debts even if they were assigned to the other spouse, but are not liable for the other spouse's debts unless those debts were assigned to the first spouse; a spouse who pays a debt assigned to the other may seek reimbursement.

**Elements:** 1. Whose debt? 2. Was it assigned in the judgment? 3. Creditor reach. 4. Reimbursement.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Liens on property follow the property [VERIFY V-315].

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 916.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's premarital loan was assigned to H. After the divorce the lender cannot reach the house awarded to W.

## VII Death

### R-CMP-091 — Community property at death; testamentary power
**Rule (write this):** On a spouse's death, one-half of the community property belongs to the surviving spouse and the other half to the decedent, and the decedent may dispose by will only of his or her separate property, one-half of the community property and one-half of his or her quasi-community property. Each spouse owns one-half of each community asset, so a will can pass only the decedent's half of any particular item.

**Elements:** 1. Characterize each asset. 2. Survivor owns half of CP outright. 3. Decedent's power: SP + half CP + half own QCP. 4. Item-by-item.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Equitable election where the will purports to dispose of the survivor's half [VERIFY V-322].

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 100, 101, 6101.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Wills crossovers.

**Example:** H's will leaves "my house" (CP) to his son. The son takes H's half; W keeps her half.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: as in PRC succession practice, the survivor's half of joint property is set aside first and only the decedent's half is estate property (approx.).

### R-CMP-092 — Quasi-community property at death
**Rule (write this):** When a married person domiciled in California dies, one-half of the decedent's quasi-community property belongs to the surviving spouse and one-half to the decedent; the surviving spouse's own quasi-community property is not divided. At death, quasi-community property includes personal property wherever located and California real property, but not real property located in another state, which is governed by the situs state's law.

**Elements:** 1. Decedent domiciled in California at death. 2. Decedent's property acquired while domiciled elsewhere that would have been CP. 3. Personal property anywhere; California realty only. 4. Half to survivor.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Out-of-state land → situs law; survivor's own QCP stays with survivor.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 66 [S-404], 101.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2019, Jul 2022, Jul 2026.

**Example:** H and W earned money in Ohio, bought a California house in H's name and an Ohio farm, then H died domiciled in California. The house is QCP (half to W); the Ohio farm passes under Ohio law.

### R-CMP-093 — Recapture of quasi-community property (§ 102)
**Rule (write this):** The surviving spouse may require a transferee to restore to the decedent's estate one-half of quasi-community property (or of its proceeds or value) that the decedent transferred to someone other than the surviving spouse without substantial consideration and without the survivor's written consent or joinder, if the decedent died domiciled in California and at death retained possession, enjoyment or income, retained a power to revoke or to consume, invade or dispose of the principal, or held the property with the transferee as joint tenants with right of survivorship. Restored property belongs to the surviving spouse as though the transfer had not been made.

**Elements:** 1. Decedent domiciled in California. 2. QCP transferred to a non-spouse. 3. No substantial consideration. 4. No written consent or joinder. 5. Retained interest: possession/income, power to revoke or invade, or survivorship.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No restoration of life insurance, accident insurance, a joint annuity or a pension payable to someone other than the surviving spouse (§ 102(b)).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 102 [S-403] [S-1002].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2022 Q5.

**Example:** H deeds QCP land to "H and daughter D, joint tenants" without W's consent, then dies. W can compel D to restore one-half.

### R-CMP-094 — Intestate succession by the surviving spouse
**Rule (write this):** When a married person dies intestate, the surviving spouse takes the decedent's one-half of the community property and of the decedent's quasi-community property, and a share of the separate property: all of it if the decedent left no issue, parent, sibling or issue of a deceased sibling; one-half if the decedent left one child or the issue of one deceased child, or no issue but a parent or a parent's issue; and one-third if the decedent left more than one child, one child and the issue of a deceased child, or the issue of two or more deceased children.

**Elements:** 1. CP → all to survivor. 2. QCP → decedent's half to survivor. 3. SP share depends on surviving relatives (§ 6401(c)). 4. Remainder by § 6402.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Omitted-spouse rules where there is a will (R-CMP-097).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6401.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Wills crossovers (Jul 2026 Q2).

**Example:** W dies intestate leaving H and her sister. H takes all CP and W's QCP and half of W's SP; the sister takes the other half of W's SP.

### R-CMP-095 — Equitable election by the surviving spouse
**Rule (write this):** If a decedent's will purports to dispose of the surviving spouse's half of community property and also gives the survivor benefits, the survivor must elect either to take under the will, allowing the survivor's half to pass as the will directs, or to keep his or her half of the community property and forgo the will's benefits.

**Elements:** 1. Will disposes of more than the decedent's half. 2. Survivor receives benefits under the will. 3. Election.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No election if the will is read as disposing only of the decedent's half [VERIFY V-322].

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 100, 6101; equitable doctrine of election.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's will leaves "all our community property" to his son and a $50,000 legacy to W. W must choose between the legacy and her half of the CP.

### R-CMP-096 — Nonprobate transfers of community property
**Rule (write this):** A nonprobate transfer of community property, such as a beneficiary designation or pay-on-death account, made by one spouse without the other's written consent does not take effect against the non-consenting spouse's interest, and the court may set it aside to that extent; a spouse's written consent to such a transfer is not a transmutation unless it meets § 852.

**Elements:** 1. Nonprobate transfer of CP. 2. Written consent? 3. Without consent → ineffective as to the survivor's half. 4. Consent ≠ transmutation without § 852.

**Exceptions / defenses:** ERISA and federal programs follow federal law (R-CMP-101, R-CMP-102).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 5020, 5021, 5022 [S-420]; *Estate of MacDonald* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 262.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H names his brother beneficiary of a CP-funded life policy without W's consent. W can recover half the proceeds.

### R-CMP-097 — Omitted spouse and effect of dissolution
**Rule (write this):** A surviving spouse who married the decedent after all of the decedent's testamentary instruments were executed and was not provided for receives the decedent's one-half of the community and quasi-community property and an intestate share of the separate property capped at one-half, unless the omission was intentional and that intention appears from the testamentary instruments, the spouse was provided for outside the will in lieu of a testamentary gift, or the spouse validly waived the share. Dissolution of marriage revokes will provisions in favor of the former spouse.

**Elements:** 1. Marriage after all instruments. 2. No provision. 3. No § 21611 exception. 4. Share: CP/QCP half + SP intestate share ≤ 1/2.

**Exceptions / defenses:** § 21611 exceptions; failure of nonprobate transfers to a former spouse after dissolution (Prob. Code § 5040) [S-605].

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21610, 21611, 6122.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2018 Q5.

**Example:** H's 2010 will leaves everything to his children; he marries W in 2020 and dies. W takes H's half of the CP and up to half of his SP.

### R-CMP-098 — Passage to the surviving spouse; survivorship forms
**Rule (write this):** Community and quasi-community property that passes to or belongs to the surviving spouse can generally be confirmed without probate administration, and community property held with a right of survivorship passes to the survivor at death while being treated as community property for other purposes.

**Elements:** 1. Property passing to survivor. 2. Spousal property petition or no administration. 3. CP with right of survivorship (Civ. Code § 682.1).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Creditors' claims and elections may require administration [VERIFY V-323].

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 13500, 13650; Civ. Code § 682.1.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; nice-to-know.

**Example:** H and W hold their house "as community property with right of survivorship." When H dies W takes the whole house without probate.

### R-CMP-099 — Gifts of community property challenged after death
**Rule (write this):** After the donor spouse's death, the surviving spouse may set aside a lifetime gift of community personal property made without written consent only as to the survivor's one-half, because the decedent could have disposed of the other half.

**Elements:** 1. Lifetime gift of CP personal property. 2. No written consent. 3. Donor has died. 4. Recovery limited to one-half.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Ratification; laches. The survivor may instead recover one-half of the value from the decedent's estate.

**Authority:** Fam. Code § 1100(b); *Fields v. Michael* (1949) 91 Cal.App.2d 443, 448 [S-412] [S-1015].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H gave $100,000 of CP to a charity without W's consent and died a year later. W recovers $50,000 from the charity.

## VIII Federal preemption

### R-CMP-100 — Supremacy Clause and federal benefits
**Rule (write this):** Under the Supremacy Clause, California community property law yields when a federal statute designates who receives a federal benefit or bars its assignment; a state court may not divide such a benefit or award offsetting property to replace it. Military retired pay may be divided only as Congress allows, and retired pay waived to receive veterans' disability benefits cannot be divided or replaced by an indemnity order.

**Elements:** 1. Federal benefit. 2. Federal statute controlling beneficiary or barring assignment. 3. Conflict with state division. 4. Preemption.

**Exceptions / defenses:** USFSPA permits division of disposable military retired pay.

**Authority:** U.S. Const. art. VI, cl. 2; *Hisquierdo v. Hisquierdo*, 439 U.S. 572 (1979); *McCarty v. McCarty*, 453 U.S. 210 (1981); 10 U.S.C. § 1408; *Mansell v. Mansell*, 490 U.S. 581 (1989); *Howell v. Howell*, 581 U.S. 214 (2017) [S-423].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; one paragraph when a federal benefit appears.

**Example:** H waives part of his military retired pay to receive VA disability pay after the divorce. W cannot obtain an order requiring H to make up the reduction (Howell).

### R-CMP-101 — ERISA plans
**Rule (write this):** ERISA's anti-alienation rule bars assignment of plan benefits except under a qualified domestic relations order, and ERISA preempts state community property and probate rules that would let a nonparticipant spouse bequeath an interest in undistributed plan benefits or that would revoke a former spouse's plan beneficiary designation on divorce.

**Elements:** 1. ERISA plan. 2. Division only by QDRO. 3. Plan documents control beneficiaries.

**Exceptions / defenses:** QDROs and spousal survivor annuity rights under ERISA.

**Authority:** 29 U.S.C. § 1056(d)(1), (3); *Boggs v. Boggs*, 520 U.S. 833 (1997); *Egelhoff v. Egelhoff*, 532 U.S. 141 (2001).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** W, the nonparticipant spouse, dies first and leaves her CP interest in H's 401(k) to her children. Under Boggs the bequest is preempted.

### R-CMP-102 — Federal life insurance and savings bonds
**Rule (write this):** Beneficiary and survivorship designations under federal life insurance programs and United States savings bonds control over contrary community property claims, subject to a fraud exception where a spouse used community funds to buy bonds in breach of the other spouse's rights.

**Elements:** 1. Federal instrument (NSLI, SGLI, FEGLI, savings bonds). 2. Federal designation. 3. Fraud exception for bonds.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Yiatchos fraud exception.

**Authority:** *Wissner v. Wissner*, 338 U.S. 655 (1950); *Ridgway v. Ridgway*, 454 U.S. 46 (1981); *Hillman v. Maretta*, 569 U.S. 483 (2013); *Free v. Bland*, 369 U.S. 663 (1962); *Yiatchos v. Yiatchos*, 376 U.S. 306 (1964).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H, a soldier, names his mother beneficiary of his SGLI policy paid with CP. The mother takes the proceeds (Ridgway).

### R-CMP-103 — Social Security is not divisible
**Rule (write this):** Social Security benefits are not community property subject to division, and a court may not award offsetting property to compensate for the other spouse's expected benefits, because federal law bars their transfer or assignment.

**Elements:** 1. Social Security benefit. 2. Anti-assignment rule. 3. No division or offset.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Benefits once paid and deposited may be traced like other funds.

**Authority:** 42 U.S.C. § 407(a); *Hisquierdo v. Hisquierdo*, 439 U.S. 572 (1979) (by analogy).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H argues he should get more of the CP house because W will receive larger Social Security benefits. The court may not make that offset.
