# Community Property — comprehensive outline

**What this file is.** The complete reference for California community property. It follows the structure of the State Bar's Community Property essay outline (I–VII plus federal preemption) [S-086]. The outline's exact wording and numbering were seen only through search summaries [VERIFY V-024], so the numbering below is the repo's working version of it.
- Each topic gives the rule, its authority, the sub-rules and exceptions, and how the State Bar tests it.
- Essay-ready wording lives in [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md); rule IDs (`R-CMP-###`) point there.
- The California-versus-common-law comparison (including quasi-community property and choice of law) is in [`ca-distinctions.md`](ca-distinctions.md).

**Governing law.** Every Community Property essay is answered under **California law**; the calls say so ("Answer according to California law") [S-079]. Community property is not on the MBE.

**Abbreviations.** CP = community property; SP = separate property; QCP = quasi-community property; H/W = husband/wife (the State Bar's facts usually use spouses named H… and W…); Fam. Code / Prob. Code / Evid. Code / Civ. Code = California Family, Probate, Evidence and Civil Codes; DOS = date of separation.

**The one method that runs through everything (asset by asset).**
1. **Source:** what money, labor or right produced the asset?
2. **Time:** acquired before marriage, during marriage (before DOS), or after DOS?
3. **Presumptions:** general CP presumption; form-of-title presumptions; special presumptions.
4. **Actions of the parties:** transmutation (a § 852 writing), premarital agreement, commingling, tracing.
5. **Contributions:** apportionment (Pereira / Van Camp / Moore–Marsden) and reimbursement (§§ 2640, 2641, Epstein, improvements).
6. **Remedy:** equal division (§ 2550) or a statutory deviation; debts; at death, Probate Code rules.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the structure resembles the PRC split between 夫妻共同财产 and 个人财产, but California decides character asset by asset with formal presumptions and tracing (approx.).

## I Community property law

### I.A Principles

- **California is a community property state.** Each spouse owns a present, existing and equal one-half interest in all community property during the marriage (Fam. Code § 751). The interest exists from acquisition; it is not a mere expectancy that arises at divorce.
- **Characterization is fixed at acquisition** ("inception of title"). The character of property is determined when it is acquired, by its **source**, the **time** of acquisition and the spouses' **domicile** at that time. Later changes come only through a recognized mechanism: transmutation, agreement, apportionment of later contributions, or statute (R-CMP-008).
- **Character follows the source.** Property bought with SP is SP; property bought with CP is CP; rents, issues and profits keep the character of the property that produced them (Fam. Code § 770(a)(3); § 760). Exchanging an asset does not change its character (the proceeds are traced).
- **Labor is community.** The skill, effort and time of either spouse during marriage is a community resource. Earnings during marriage are CP, and community labor that increases SP creates a community interest that must be apportioned (II.D).
- **Statutory regime.** The Family Code governs characterization (Div. 4, §§ 750–1102), division at dissolution (Div. 7, §§ 2500–2660) and debts (§§ 900–1000, 2620–2627). The Probate Code governs death (§§ 100–104, 6101, 6401, 21610).
- **Equal division, not equitable distribution.** At dissolution the community estate is divided **equally** unless the parties agree otherwise or a statute directs a different allocation (Fam. Code § 2550). Fault is not a factor in property division (California is a no-fault state), though breach of fiduciary duty has its own remedies (§ 1101).
- **Spouses are fiduciaries.** Spouses owe each other the duty of highest good faith and fair dealing (Fam. Code § 721(b)); this shapes interspousal transactions (II.A.3) and management (III).
- **How tested.** Principles are rarely a separate call; they supply the opening paragraph of every CP answer: "California is a community property state. All property acquired during marriage by a spouse domiciled in California is presumed CP; property acquired before marriage, after separation, or by gift, bequest, devise or descent is SP" (R-CMP-001, R-CMP-002).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: like the PRC default joint-property regime, but California's equal division is mandatory and fault-blind, whereas PRC courts weigh the circumstances (approx.).

### I.B Definitions

#### I.B.1 Community property

- **Rule.** Except as otherwise provided by statute, all property, real or personal, wherever situated, acquired by a married person during the marriage while domiciled in California is CP (Fam. Code § 760). (R-CMP-001)
- **Elements.** (1) Acquired by a married person; (2) during marriage, meaning from the wedding until the date of separation (§§ 70, 771); (3) while domiciled in California; (4) not within a statutory SP category.
- **Typical CP.** Wages, salary, bonuses and commissions earned during marriage; property bought with those earnings; the community share of pensions, stock options and other deferred compensation earned during marriage (II.H, II.K); businesses started during marriage; personal-injury recoveries for causes of action arising during marriage (§ 780, subject to § 2603 at division).
- **Forms of holding.** Spouses may hold property as joint tenants, tenants in common, community property, or community property with right of survivorship (Fam. Code § 750; Civ. Code § 682.1). The label matters for presumptions at dissolution (§ 2581) and for passage at death.
- **Present equal interests.** § 751: each spouse's interest is present, existing and equal.

#### I.B.2 Separate property

- **Rule.** SP of a married person includes (1) all property owned before marriage; (2) all property acquired during marriage by **gift, bequest, devise or descent**; (3) the rents, issues and profits of SP (Fam. Code § 770(a)). Earnings and accumulations after the **date of separation** are SP (§ 771), as are earnings after a judgment of legal separation (§ 772). (R-CMP-002)
- **Other SP by statute or agreement.** Property designated SP by a valid premarital agreement (§ 1612) or transmutation (§ 852); personal-injury damages for a cause of action arising after separation or against the other spouse (§ 781); property acquired with traced SP funds.
- **Gifts to both spouses.** A gift made to both spouses together is not a gift to "the person" alone. Analysis: each spouse arguably receives a one-half SP interest by gift (§ 770(a)(2)), but if title is taken in joint form during marriage, the joint-title presumption of § 2581 makes it CP for division at dissolution. Either way each spouse usually ends up with half; argue both lines [VERIFY V-308].
- **Management.** A spouse may convey his or her SP without the other spouse's consent (§ 770(b)); see IV.
- **Mixed character.** A single asset can be part SP and part CP (pro tanto). Pensions, homes paid down during marriage, and businesses grown with community labor are the usual examples.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: in PRC law a gift or inheritance received during marriage is generally joint property unless the will or gift designates one spouse; in California it is always the recipient's SP (approx.). This is the most common transfer error for PRC-trained readers.

#### I.B.3 Quasi-community property

- **Definition (dissolution).** QCP is all real or personal property, **wherever situated**, acquired (a) by either spouse while domiciled elsewhere that would have been CP had the acquiring spouse been domiciled in California at acquisition, or (b) in exchange for such property (Fam. Code § 125). (R-CMP-003)
- **Why it exists.** Common-law states give title to the earning spouse. When a couple moves to California, QCP lets California treat the out-of-state earnings like CP at dissolution or death, protecting the non-titled spouse.
- **At dissolution.** QCP is part of the "community estate" (Fam. Code § 63) and is divided equally with CP (§ 2550). California courts have applied QCP at dissolution only when **both spouses are domiciled in California** when the dissolution is sought (*In re Marriage of Roesch* (1978) 83 Cal.App.3d 96) [S-408] [VERIFY V-305]. Out-of-state real property is included, but the court divides it in a way that avoids changing title to the foreign land where possible (§ 2660; VI.A.3). *Addison v. Addison* (1965) 62 Cal.2d 558 upheld applying QCP at divorce against due process and privileges-and-immunities challenges.
- **At death.** The Probate Code definition is narrower: QCP is **personal property wherever situated and real property situated in California** acquired while domiciled elsewhere that would have been CP (Prob. Code § 66) [S-404]. Out-of-state land is **not** QCP at death; the situs state's law governs it. Only the **decedent's** QCP is split: half belongs to the surviving spouse, half to the decedent (Prob. Code § 101). The survivor's own QCP is not divided (VII.B).
- **During marriage.** QCP is generally managed and controlled by the acquiring spouse as though it were that spouse's SP; its quasi-community character matters at dissolution, at death and for creditors [VERIFY V-327]. For debts, QCP is liable to the same extent as CP (Fam. Code § 912).
- **Recapture at death.** If the decedent gave QCP away while keeping control or enjoyment of it (for example, by deeding it into joint tenancy with a child without the spouse's written consent), the survivor may recover half from the transferee (Prob. Code § 102) [S-403] (VII.B).
- **How tested.** Very often: QCP was a tagged issue in 4 of 17 CP essays, including crossovers with Wills (Jul 2014, Feb 2019, Jul 2022, Jul 2026) [S-079]. The facts always include a move from "State X, a non-community-property state."

#### I.B.4 Marital economic community; community estate

- **Community estate.** The "community estate" includes both CP and QCP (Fam. Code § 63). Division, debt liability and support provisions speak of the community estate.
- **When the marital economic community begins.** On marriage. Property acquired before the marriage is SP even if the couple was already cohabiting (premarital cohabitation creates no CP; any claim rests on contract under *Marvin v. Marvin* (1976) 18 Cal.3d 660; see I.C).
- **When it ends.** At the **date of separation** (Fam. Code §§ 70, 771) or on the death of a spouse. After DOS, each spouse's earnings and accumulations are SP, but property that was CP at DOS remains CP until divided (V).
- **Domicile requirement.** CP arises only while the acquiring spouse is domiciled in California; property acquired while domiciled elsewhere is SP or QCP depending on what it would have been in California (§§ 760, 125).
- **Moving out of California.** Under general conflict-of-laws principles, property that was CP when acquired keeps that character after the couple moves to a common-law state (character fixed at acquisition) (R-CMP-005).

### I.C Putative spouses and quasi-marital property (and other relationships)

- **Putative spouse.** When a marriage is void or voidable (bigamy, incest, defective formalities) and either or both parties believed **in good faith** that it was valid, the court declares the believing party a putative spouse and divides the property acquired during the union that would have been CP or QCP had the union been valid. That property is **quasi-marital property**, divided equally under § 2550 (Fam. Code § 2251). (R-CMP-006)
- **Good faith is subjective.** The question is the claimant's actual, honest belief in a valid marriage; the reasonableness of the belief is evidence bearing on whether it was genuinely held, not a separate requirement (*Ceja v. Rudolph & Sletten, Inc.* (2013) 56 Cal.4th 1113) [S-424].
- **Bad-faith partner.** A party who knew the marriage was invalid is not a putative spouse, but the court may still divide quasi-marital property where the other party qualifies; a bad-faith partner's claims otherwise rest on contract or equity.
- **Competing spouses.** Where a decedent had both a lawful spouse and a putative spouse, courts have divided the property equitably between the two innocent claimants, including equal division (*Estate of Vargas* (1974) 36 Cal.App.3d 714) [S-430] [VERIFY V-312].
- **Registered domestic partners** have the same property rights and obligations as spouses (Fam. Code § 297.5). Treat a registered partnership exactly like a marriage.
- **Nonmarital cohabitants** acquire no CP. Their property rights rest on express or implied contract or equitable theories (*Marvin v. Marvin* (1976) 18 Cal.3d 660).
- **How tested.** Occasionally as a threshold issue (a defective or bigamous marriage, a couple who never married). Address it first: "Before characterizing, decide whether there is a marital community at all."

## II Characterization of property

**The three-step characterization routine** (R-CMP-008):
1. **Source** (what was used to acquire it) and **time** (before marriage / during / after DOS).
2. **Presumptions** (general; title; special).
3. **Actions of the parties** that changed character (agreements, transmutation, commingling), then contributions (apportionment, reimbursement).

### II.A Presumptions (general community property presumption; title; overcoming them)

#### II.A.1 General community property presumption

- **Rule.** Property acquired by a spouse during marriage while domiciled in California is presumed CP (Fam. Code § 760). Property possessed during marriage is also presumed to have been acquired during marriage, so the party claiming it was owned before marriage must prove it. (R-CMP-011)
- **Burden.** The spouse claiming SP must rebut the presumption, generally by a **preponderance of the evidence**, by tracing the asset to an SP source or proving acquisition before marriage, by gift or inheritance, or after DOS.
- **Earnings presumption.** Earnings during marriage are CP; anything bought with them is CP.
- **Family expense presumption** (See): family living expenses are presumed paid from CP when CP was available (II.C).
- **How tested.** Every CP answer. Apply it asset by asset: "The condo was acquired during marriage while H and W were domiciled in California, so it is presumptively CP. H can rebut only by tracing…"

#### II.A.2 Presumptions based on form of title

1. **Title in one spouse's name, acquired during marriage with CP.** Title alone does not make it SP. Between spouses, the form-of-title presumption of Evid. Code § 662 (owner of legal title presumed owner of full beneficial title, rebuttable only by clear and convincing proof) **does not apply** where it conflicts with the transmutation statutes; a spouse claiming the asset became his or her SP must show a valid § 852 transmutation (*In re Marriage of Valli* (2014) 58 Cal.4th 1396: life insurance policy bought with CP and titled in W's name remained CP). (R-CMP-012)
2. **Joint title at dissolution (§ 2581).** For division at dissolution or legal separation, property acquired **during marriage in joint form** (joint tenancy, tenancy in common, tenancy by the entirety, or as CP) is presumed CP. The presumption affects the burden of proof and can be rebutted **only** by (a) a clear statement in the deed or other documentary evidence of title that the property is SP and not CP, or (b) proof of a written agreement that the property is SP (Fam. Code § 2581) [S-425]. A spouse who contributed SP is protected by **§ 2640 reimbursement**, not by rebutting the presumption. (R-CMP-013)
   - **History.** *In re Marriage of Lucas* (1980) 27 Cal.3d 808 held that a spouse who used SP toward an asset taken in joint title got nothing back unless the spouses had agreed otherwise; the SP contribution was treated as a gift to the community. The Legislature replaced Lucas with the predecessors of §§ 2581 and 2640 (effective 1984). Retroactive application of the new statutes to earlier acquisitions was limited on due-process grounds (*In re Marriage of Buol* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 751; *In re Marriage of Fabian* (1986) 41 Cal.3d 440; *In re Marriage of Heikes* (1995) 10 Cal.4th 1211) [VERIFY V-329].
3. **Joint tenancy outside dissolution (death, creditors).** § 2581 applies only to division at dissolution or legal separation. At death, the traditional answer was that joint tenancy title governs and the survivor takes by survivorship. In *In re Brace* (2020) 9 Cal.5th 903, the Supreme Court held (in a bankruptcy dispute) that property bought with community funds during marriage is presumptively CP even though title was taken as joint tenants; for acquisitions on or after January 1, 1985 a change of character requires an express § 852 writing, and a joint-tenancy deed alone is not enough [S-422]. On a death essay, argue both (survivorship from title vs. CP character after Brace) when the decedent tried to will half of a jointly titled asset [VERIFY V-319]. (R-CMP-014)
4. **Community property with right of survivorship** (Civ. Code § 682.1): CP for all purposes during life and at dissolution; passes to the survivor at death without administration.
5. **Married woman's pre-1975 presumption.** Property acquired by a married woman by written instrument **before January 1, 1975** is presumed her SP; if she took with her husband or another person, she is presumed to take her part as a tenant in common, unless a different intention appears (Fam. Code § 803) [S-419]. (R-CMP-015) Rarely tested; mention it only if the facts give a pre-1975 deed to the wife.
6. **Interspousal transfers.** A deed or other transfer between spouses is analyzed as a **transmutation** (II.B.2) and is subject to the presumption of undue influence (II.A.3), not to a simple title presumption.

#### II.A.3 Presumption of undue influence in interspousal transactions

- **Rule.** Spouses are fiduciaries (Fam. Code § 721(b)). When an interspousal transaction gives one spouse an **advantage** over the other, a presumption arises that the advantaged spouse obtained it by **undue influence**. The advantaged spouse must rebut the presumption by showing that the disadvantaged spouse acted freely and voluntarily, with full knowledge of the facts and a complete understanding of the effect of the transaction. (R-CMP-016)
- **Authority.** Fam. Code § 721; *In re Marriage of Haines* (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 277 (the undue-influence presumption prevails over the Evid. Code § 662 title presumption when one spouse obtains an advantage through a transfer).
- **Not for premarital agreements.** Parties who are not yet married are not fiduciaries in this sense; premarital agreements are governed by § 1615 (*In re Marriage of Bonds* (2000) 24 Cal.4th 1).
- **How tested.** Every interspousal deed or transfer: "Even if the deed satisfies § 852, H gained an advantage, so the transmutation is presumed to be the product of undue influence. H must prove W acted freely with full knowledge." (Feb 2024 Q1: house received from an aunt and later deeded into joint names.)

#### II.A.4 Overcoming presumptions

| Presumption | Who has the burden | How to rebut | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| General CP (acquired during marriage) | SP claimant | Trace to SP source; prove pre-marriage, gift/inheritance or post-DOS acquisition (preponderance) | § 760; See; Mix |
| Joint title at dissolution | Spouse claiming SP | Only a clear SP statement in the title document or a written SP agreement; otherwise § 2640 reimbursement | § 2581 |
| Title in one name (CP funds) | Titled spouse claiming SP | Valid § 852 transmutation; Evid. Code § 662 does not override between spouses | Valli; Brace |
| Undue influence (advantage to one spouse) | Advantaged spouse | Free, voluntary act with full knowledge and understanding | § 721; Haines |
| Family expenses paid with CP | Spouse claiming SP purchase | Exhaustion of CP at time of purchase, or direct tracing | See v. See |
| Married woman pre-1975 | Party contesting | Contrary intent in instrument; evidence of CP source | § 803 |

### II.B Agreements that alter characterization (premarital agreements; transmutation)

#### II.B.1 Premarital agreements

- **Scope.** Spouses (and prospective spouses) may alter their property rights by a premarital agreement or other marital property agreement (Fam. Code § 1500). California's Uniform Premarital Agreement Act is at §§ 1600–1617.
- **Formation.** A premarital agreement must be **in writing and signed by both parties**; it is enforceable **without consideration** (§ 1611) and takes effect on marriage (§ 1613). After marriage it may be amended or revoked only by a written agreement signed by both (§ 1614). (R-CMP-018)
- **Permitted subjects** (§ 1612(a)): rights in property of either or both parties wherever located; the right to buy, sell, use, manage and control property; disposition on separation, dissolution, death or any other event; making wills or trusts; life insurance death benefits; choice of law; and any other matter, including personal rights, not in violation of public policy or a criminal statute.
- **Child support** may not be adversely affected (§ 1612(b)).
- **Spousal support** provisions (including waivers) are **not enforceable** if the party against whom enforcement is sought was **not represented by independent counsel** when the agreement was signed, or if the provision is **unconscionable at the time of enforcement**; counsel alone does not make an otherwise unenforceable provision enforceable (§ 1612(c)). (R-CMP-021) (The Mar 2025 retest essay turned on a support waiver in an agreement drafted by the lawyer-spouse.)
- **Enforcement defenses** (§ 1615(a)). The agreement is unenforceable if the challenger proves either: (R-CMP-019, R-CMP-020)
  1. **Involuntary execution**; or
  2. **Unconscionability when executed**, **and** before execution the challenger (A) was not given fair, reasonable and full disclosure of the other's property and financial obligations, (B) did not voluntarily and expressly waive in writing any further disclosure, and (C) did not have, and reasonably could not have had, adequate knowledge of that property and those obligations. Unconscionability is a question of law (§ 1615(b)).
- **Statutory voluntariness checklist** (§ 1615(c)). The agreement is deemed **not** voluntary unless the court finds, in writing or on the record, **all** of the following [S-401] [S-402] [VERIFY V-300]:
  1. The challenger had **independent counsel** at signing, or, after being advised to seek counsel, **expressly waived counsel in a separate writing**; for current agreements the advisement must come at least seven calendar days before the final agreement is signed.
  2. **Seven-day rule.** For agreements signed on or after January 1, 2020: at least **seven calendar days** between first presentation of the **final** agreement and signing, whether or not the party had counsel (for 2002–2019 agreements, seven days from first presentation and advice to seek counsel). Nonsubstantive amendments do not restart the clock.
  3. If **unrepresented**: fully informed in writing, in a language in which the party is proficient, of the terms and basic effect of the agreement and the rights and obligations given up, and the party signed a document declaring receipt and naming who provided it.
  4. No **duress, fraud or undue influence**, and no lack of **capacity**.
  5. Any other factors the court deems relevant.
- **Background.** *In re Marriage of Bonds* (2000) 24 Cal.4th 1 held a premarital agreement signed without counsel by the less sophisticated party could still be voluntary under the then-existing totality test, and that the § 721 fiduciary presumption does not apply between prospective spouses. The Legislature responded by adding § 1615(c) and the counsel rule for support waivers in § 1612(c).
- **Limitations.** Any statute of limitations on an action asserting a claim for relief under a premarital agreement is tolled during the marriage (§ 1617).
- **How tested.** Mar 2025 (enforceability, independent counsel, support waiver); the agreement also appears in crossover facts where the drafting lawyer is the other spouse (PR conflict). Analyze: formation → § 1615(c) voluntariness factors one by one → unconscionability + disclosure → § 1612(c) support waiver → then characterize assets under the agreement (if enforceable) or under default law (if not).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC spouses can agree in writing on property ownership (夫妻财产约定), but PRC law has no seven-day or independent-counsel rules like § 1615(c) (approx.).

#### II.B.2 Transmutation

- **Definition.** Spouses may by agreement or transfer, with or without consideration, transmute (a) CP to SP of either spouse, (b) SP of either spouse to CP, or (c) SP of one spouse to SP of the other (Fam. Code § 850). (R-CMP-022)
- **Formalities (§ 852(a)).** A transmutation is **not valid unless made in writing by an express declaration** that is made, joined in, consented to or accepted by the spouse whose interest is adversely affected.
- **Express declaration test.** The writing must contain language that **expressly states that the characterization or ownership of the property is being changed**; extrinsic evidence may not be used to supply the intent (*Estate of MacDonald* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 262: wife's signed consent to husband's IRA beneficiary designation was not an express transmutation of her CP interest). The word "transmutation" or "community property" is not required, but a writing that merely shows a transfer or a title change, with no statement of a change in ownership, fails. (R-CMP-023)
  - *In re Marriage of Barneson* (1999) 69 Cal.App.4th 583: an instruction to a broker to "transfer" stock into the wife's name did not expressly state a change in ownership; no transmutation [S-416].
  - *Estate of Bibb* (2001) 87 Cal.App.4th 461: a grant deed using the operative word "grant" satisfied § 852(a), but a DMV registration change did not [S-415] [VERIFY V-326].
  - *In re Marriage of Benson* (2005) 36 Cal.4th 1096: the **part-performance** exception to the statute of frauds does **not** apply to § 852; an oral transmutation partly performed is still invalid.
  - *In re Marriage of Valli* (2014) 58 Cal.4th 1396: buying an asset with CP and putting title in one spouse's name is a transmutation question; no express writing, no transmutation.
- **Personal-gift exception (§ 852(c)).** No writing is needed for a **gift between spouses** of clothing, wearing apparel, jewelry or other **tangible articles of a personal nature** that are **used solely or principally by the donee spouse** and **not substantial in value** taking into account the circumstances of the marriage. Such a gift becomes the donee's SP. (R-CMP-024) Typical facts: a necklace (Jul 2015), a framed signed jersey or watch (Feb 2026; Mar 2025). Argue both sides: value relative to the couple's wealth, and whether the item is used personally by the donee or displayed for the household.
- **Pre-1985 transmutations.** § 852 does not apply to transmutations made before **January 1, 1985**; those could be oral or inferred from conduct (§ 852(e)). (R-CMP-025)
- **Wills.** A statement in a will about the character of property is not admissible as evidence of a transmutation in proceedings commenced before the death of the person who made the will (§ 852(d)).
- **Third parties.** A transmutation of real property is not effective against third parties without notice unless recorded (§ 852(b)). Transmutations are subject to the laws on fraudulent transfers (§ 851).
- **Undue influence overlay.** A valid § 852 writing that advantages one spouse is still **presumed** to have been obtained by undue influence (§ 721; Haines; II.A.3). (R-CMP-026)
- **Effect on reimbursement.** When SP is transmuted into CP or joint form, the contributing spouse may still claim § 2640 reimbursement unless the writing waives it; courts have treated the SP value contributed as a "contribution to the acquisition" of the community asset [VERIFY V-313].
- **How tested.** Very often (3 tagged essays; implicit in most title-change facts). Write: rule → is there a writing? → does it expressly state a change of ownership? → § 852(c) gift exception? → undue-influence presumption → § 2640 reimbursement.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: both systems require writing for spousal property agreements, but California's "express declaration" test is stricter than reading the parties' intent from context (approx.).

#### II.B.3 Other marital agreements

- **Postnuptial (marital) property agreements** are permitted (§ 1500) but, unlike premarital agreements, are made between fiduciaries; any advantage to one spouse triggers the undue-influence presumption (§ 721).
- **Limits.** Spouses cannot, by contract with each other, alter their legal relations **except as to property** (Fam. Code § 1620). Agreements that penalize conduct or promote dissolution contrary to public policy are unenforceable.
- **Marital settlement agreements** at dissolution divide property by agreement or oral stipulation in open court instead of the equal-division rule (§ 2550).

### II.C Commingled assets

- **Rule.** Mixing SP and CP in one account or asset does not by itself change the character of either. But if the mixed funds cannot be traced, the whole is treated as CP, because the general presumption controls (*See v. See* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 778). The SP claimant carries the burden of tracing. (R-CMP-028)
- **Commingled income from an SP asset.** Where an SP business or investment generates income with community labor, apportion (II.D); where SP rents are deposited into a joint account, trace.
- **How tested.** Bank accounts that held both an inheritance and salary; stock bought from a mixed account (Feb 2022 Q2); house down payments from mixed accounts.

#### II.C.1 Tracing Methods

Two accepted methods; choose the one the facts support and address the other.

##### II.C.1.a Exhaustion method, See v. See

- **Rule.** Under the exhaustion (recapitulation) method, the SP claimant shows that, **at the time of the purchase**, all community funds in the account had already been **exhausted by family expenses**, so the purchase must have been made with SP funds (*See v. See* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 778). (R-CMP-030)
- **Family expense presumption.** Family living expenses are presumed paid from community funds to the extent CP is available. Therefore: (R-CMP-031)
  - CP is presumed spent first on family expenses; only when CP is gone can SP be shown to have funded an acquisition.
  - A spouse who **uses SP for family expenses** while CP is available, or chooses to, is presumed to have made a **gift** to the community and gets **no reimbursement** absent an agreement (See).
- **Records needed.** The claimant must reconstruct deposits and expenditures at the relevant time. A mere **total recapitulation** (showing that, over the whole marriage, SP deposits exceeded SP withdrawals) is **not** sufficient (See). (R-CMP-032)
- **Example.** On June 1, the joint account holds $5,000 CP and $40,000 from W's inheritance; family expenses that month consume all $5,000 CP; on June 20 W buys $30,000 of stock. The stock is traceable to SP by exhaustion.

##### II.C.1.b Direct tracing, Marriage of Mix

- **Rule.** Under direct tracing, the claimant shows with adequate records that (1) **sufficient SP funds were available** in the account when the purchase was made and (2) the spouse **intended to use SP funds** for that purchase (*In re Marriage of Mix* (1975) 14 Cal.3d 604). (R-CMP-029)
- **Features.** It does not require CP to be exhausted. It requires contemporaneous records or credible testimony linking the SP deposit to the purchase. Mix accepted the wife's detailed testimony and records of her SP deposits and withdrawals.
- **Exam use.** Direct tracing usually succeeds where the SP spouse moved the inheritance into a separate account and wrote the purchase check from it; it fails where the account was a family operating account with no records of intent.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC practice also lets a spouse prove pre-marital or personal funds by records, but California's See/Mix framework gives fixed methods and a family-expense presumption (approx.).

### II.D Community efforts (or funds) used for separate property purposes

The community can acquire an interest in SP in two ways: **labor** (community effort during marriage improving SP) and **funds** (CP used to pay for or improve SP). Each has its own remedy.

#### II.D.1 Community labor on separate property

- **Rule.** Each spouse's labor during marriage belongs to the community. When a spouse's more-than-minimal efforts during marriage increase the value of SP (usually a business), the increase must be **apportioned** between SP and CP; when growth is due only to the SP itself or passive market forces, it stays SP (§ 770(a)(3)). (R-CMP-033)
- **Passive investment.** Rents, dividends and appreciation of SP investments managed with minimal effort stay SP. Where a spouse spends substantial working time managing an SP portfolio or property, apportionment may be required (the same Pereira/Van Camp approach).
- **Post-separation labor on a community business** is the reverse case (V.B; *Imperato*).

#### II.D.2 Community funds improving separate property

- **Rule.** When community funds are used to **improve** (build on, renovate) one spouse's SP during marriage, the community is generally entitled to **reimbursement**. Consent of the non-owner spouse to the use of community funds does not, by itself, prove a gift to the SP owner (*In re Marriage of Allen* (2002) 96 Cal.App.4th 497) [S-409]. (R-CMP-039)
- **Measure.** Courts have measured the community's claim by the amount spent or by the increase in value attributable to the improvement; some decisions allow a pro rata ownership interest instead of reimbursement. On an essay, state the reimbursement claim and argue for the larger measure on the community's side [VERIFY V-306].
- **Old gift presumption.** Older cases presumed a gift when the non-owner spouse used community funds to improve the other spouse's SP; after § 852 (gifts between spouses need an express writing unless § 852(c) applies) that presumption is doubtful [VERIFY V-306].
- **Taxes, interest, insurance and maintenance** paid with CP on SP do not enlarge the community's ownership share (Moore) and are often offset by the community's use of the property.

#### II.D.3 Apportionment of separate property business value

When a spouse owned a business before marriage (or inherited it) and worked in it during marriage, the growth in value during marriage must be split between SP (return on capital) and CP (return on labor). California uses two judicially created formulas.

##### II.D.3.a Pereira method

- **Rule.** Under *Pereira v. Pereira* (1909) 156 Cal. 1, the SP owner receives the **original SP capital plus a fair rate of return** on it (simple interest) for the period of the marriage; **all remaining growth is CP** as the product of community labor. (R-CMP-034)
- **When used.** When the business's growth is due chiefly to the **personal efforts, skill and management** of the spouse (labor-intensive businesses, professional practices, a restaurant the spouse runs daily).
- **Rate.** No fixed rate is required; courts have used the legal rate of interest where no better evidence was offered (7% in *Beam v. Bank of America* (1971) 6 Cal.3d 12) and may allow a higher return where the facts justify it [S-417].
- **Formula.** SP = SP value at marriage + (SP value at marriage × rate × years of marriage). CP = value at DOS − SP.
- **Example.** H's shop is worth $100,000 at marriage; at DOS 10 years later it is worth $600,000, due to H's 70-hour weeks. At 10% simple: SP = $100,000 + $100,000 = $200,000; CP = $400,000.

##### II.D.3.b Van Camp method

- **Rule.** Under *Van Camp v. Van Camp* (1921) 53 Cal.App. 17, the community receives the **reasonable value of the spouse's services** during marriage, **less the family expenses (or salary) the community already received** from the business; **the rest of the business value is SP**. (R-CMP-035)
- **When used.** When the growth is due chiefly to the **character of the SP capital**, market conditions or other employees' work, and the spouse's efforts were ordinary (a business that would have grown anyway).
- **Formula.** CP = (reasonable annual salary × years of marriage) − (salary or family expenses actually drawn from the business during marriage). SP = value at DOS − CP. If the community was already fully paid through salary, it gets nothing more.
- **Example.** W's inherited apartment complex grows from $1 million to $3 million in a booming market; W's management was worth $60,000/yr and she drew $60,000/yr for family expenses. Van Camp: community already compensated; growth stays SP.

##### II.D.3.c Choosing the method

- **Discretion.** The court may use whichever method achieves **substantial justice** on the facts; neither is mandatory (*Beam v. Bank of America* (1971) 6 Cal.3d 12; *In re Marriage of Dekker* (1993) 17 Cal.App.4th 842 [S-410]). Some courts combine the methods over different periods. (R-CMP-036)
- **Essay technique.** Apply **both** methods, show the numbers if given, then argue which fits: personal effort → Pereira (favors the community); capital or market → Van Camp (favors the SP owner). Conclude with the more likely method.
- **Feb 2021 Q3** (inherited business grown during marriage) and **Jul 2013 Q3** (restaurant) called for exactly this.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC law similarly treats the appreciation of pre-marital property from joint effort (as opposed to natural appreciation) as joint property, but without fixed formulas like Pereira and Van Camp (approx.).

#### II.D.4 Community payments on a spouse's separately owned real property (Moore/Marsden pro tanto apportionment)

- **Rule.** When one spouse buys real property **before marriage** (or with SP during marriage) and **community funds** later reduce the **principal** of the purchase-money loan, the community acquires a **pro tanto (proportional) interest** in the property: the community's share of equity equals the principal it paid plus its proportionate share of the appreciation during marriage (*In re Marriage of Moore* (1980) 28 Cal.3d 366; *In re Marriage of Marsden* (1982) 130 Cal.App.3d 426). (R-CMP-037, R-CMP-038)
- **What counts.** Only payments that **reduce principal** count. Payments of **interest, taxes and insurance** are excluded; they are treated as the cost of the community's use of the property (Moore).
- **Moore formula (property bought before marriage, paid down during marriage):**
  1. **CP percentage** = community principal reduction ÷ purchase price.
  2. **SP percentage** = 100% − CP percentage (equivalently, (down payment + loan − community principal reduction) ÷ purchase price).
  3. **CP interest** = community principal reduction + (CP percentage × appreciation **during marriage**).
  4. **SP interest** = equity at division − CP interest (equivalently, down payment + SP principal reduction + pre-marriage appreciation + SP percentage × marital appreciation).
- **Marsden refinement.** Appreciation **before marriage** belongs entirely to the SP owner; only appreciation during marriage is shared pro rata (Marsden).
- **Worked numbers.** H buys a house before marriage for $200,000 ($40,000 down, $160,000 loan); pays $10,000 principal before marriage; value at marriage $260,000. CP pays $30,000 principal during marriage. Value at DOS $400,000; loan balance $120,000.
  - CP % = $30,000 ÷ $200,000 = 15%. Marital appreciation = $400,000 − $260,000 = $140,000.
  - CP interest = $30,000 + 15% × $140,000 = **$51,000**. Equity = $280,000; SP interest = **$229,000** (check: $40,000 + $10,000 + $60,000 + 85% × $140,000 = $229,000).
  - Each spouse receives half of the CP interest ($25,500); H also keeps his $229,000 SP interest.
- **Refinancing.** If the purchase-money loan is refinanced during marriage on the community's credit, the new loan proceeds used to pay off SP debt can be community contributions; trace the character of the new loan (lender intent; II.I).
- **Distinguish.** Community payments on a loan used to **improve** (not purchase) SP fall under II.D.2; SP payments on a **community** asset fall under § 2640 (II.E-F).
- **How tested.** Frequent with houses bought before marriage (Jul 2013 rental property; Feb 2026 house). Always show the formula even when numbers are missing: "The community acquired a pro tanto interest equal to…"

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: a PRC judicial interpretation reaches a similar result for a home bought before marriage with a mortgage repaid jointly during marriage: the titled spouse keeps the home and compensates the other for the joint repayments and the related appreciation (approx.).

### II.E-F Separate property enhancing community or other separate property

#### II.E SP contributions to community property (§ 2640(b))

- **Rule.** At division of the community estate, a party who made **contributions to the acquisition of community property** that are **traced to an SP source** is **reimbursed** for them, unless that party made a **written waiver** of the right or signed a writing that has the effect of a waiver (Fam. Code § 2640(b)). (R-CMP-042)
- **"Contributions to the acquisition"** include **down payments**, payments for **improvements**, and payments that **reduce principal** on a loan used to purchase or improve the property. They **exclude** payments of **interest**, **maintenance**, **insurance** and **taxes** (§ 2640(a)).
- **Measure.** Reimbursement is **without interest** and **without adjustment for changes in monetary value** (no share of appreciation), and **may not exceed the net value of the property** at division (§ 2640(b)).
- **Tracing.** The claimant must trace the contribution to SP. SP contributions can be traced through sales and refinances into replacement property (*In re Marriage of Walrath* (1998) 17 Cal.4th 907).
- **Relation to § 2581.** The typical pattern: W uses her inheritance as the down payment on a house titled "H and W, joint tenants." § 2581 makes the house CP; § 2640 gives W her down payment back off the top, then the remaining equity is split equally.
- **Lucas replaced.** Under Lucas, W would have been presumed to make a gift; § 2640 reverses that unless W waived reimbursement in writing.

#### II.F SP contributions to the other spouse's separate property (§ 2640(c))

- **Rule.** A party is reimbursed for SP contributions to the acquisition of property of the **other spouse's SP estate** during marriage, unless there has been a **transmutation in writing** under § 852 or a **written waiver**. The same measure applies: no interest, no appreciation, capped at the property's net value (§ 2640(c)). (R-CMP-043)
- **Example.** H uses $20,000 of his SP to pay principal on W's premarital condo loan. At dissolution H gets $20,000 back from W's SP condo (absent a writing making it a gift).
- **Contrast.** CP used for the other spouse's SP → Moore/Marsden pro tanto interest or improvement reimbursement (II.D); SP used for CP → § 2640(b); SP used for the other spouse's SP → § 2640(c); SP used for family expenses → no reimbursement (See).

### II.G Educational and training expenses (characterization)

- **Education is not property.** A professional degree, license or enhanced earning capacity is not CP and has no divisible value (*In re Marriage of Sullivan* (1984) 37 Cal.3d 762). The community's remedy is **reimbursement** under § 2641, which is the **exclusive remedy** for community contributions to education or training and any resulting enhancement of earning capacity (§ 2641(d)). (R-CMP-046)
- **What is a community contribution.** Payments made with CP or QCP for education or training of a party, or for repayment of a loan incurred for education or training, whether made in California or elsewhere (§ 2641(a)).
- **Support.** The education and the reimbursement may still be considered in setting spousal support (§ 2641(d); § 4320).
- **Details of the reimbursement claim** are at II.J.2; assignment of educational **loans** at VI.B.5.

### II.H Retirement benefits and disability payments

- **Pensions are deferred compensation.** Retirement benefits earned by employment during marriage are CP to the extent earned during marriage, **whether vested or not** (*In re Marriage of Brown* (1976) 15 Cal.3d 838, overruling the rule that nonvested pensions were a mere expectancy). (R-CMP-048)
- **Time rule.** The community share of a defined-benefit pension is usually fixed by the **time rule**: community fraction = **months of service during marriage before DOS ÷ total months of service** credited when benefits are paid; the community fraction × each payment is CP. Brown holds that the community owns the portion earned during marriage; the time rule is the standard way courts measure that portion. (R-CMP-049)
- **Defined-contribution plans (401(k), IRA).** Trace contributions and earnings: contributions from marital earnings (and their growth) are CP; premarital balances and their growth are SP.
- **Gillmore election.** When the employee spouse is eligible to retire but keeps working, the non-employee spouse may elect to receive his or her share immediately; the employee cannot defeat the other's interest by postponing retirement (*In re Marriage of Gillmore* (1981) 29 Cal.3d 418).
- **Division methods.** Cash-out (present value with an offsetting award of other assets) or in-kind division with reserved jurisdiction (each payment split when received), typically through a **qualified domestic relations order (QDRO)** for ERISA plans (29 U.S.C. § 1056(d)(3)); the court must make orders ensuring each party receives his or her full community share (Fam. Code § 2610) (VI.B.8).
- **Disability benefits.** Characterize by **what the payment replaces**: (R-CMP-051)
  - To the extent a disability pension **replaces post-separation earnings**, it is the disabled spouse's SP; to the extent it **substitutes for retirement benefits** the employee would have received, it is CP under the time rule (*In re Marriage of Stenquist* (1978) 21 Cal.3d 779).
  - Private disability insurance bought with CP: benefits received after separation are CP to the extent they substitute for retirement pay and SP to the extent they replace post-DOS earnings (*In re Marriage of Saslow* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 848).
  - A term disability policy **renewed after separation** with SP earnings yields SP benefits (*In re Marriage of Elfmont* (1995) 9 Cal.4th 1026) [VERIFY V-317].
- **Severance pay and similar substitutes.** Characterize by what the payment compensates: past services during marriage → CP; replacement of post-separation earnings → SP [VERIFY V-318].
- **Workers' compensation and wage replacements** follow the same "replacement" logic: payments for earnings lost after DOS are SP.
- **Federal limits.** Social Security is not divisible; military retired pay and ERISA plans are subject to federal rules (VIII).
- **How tested.** Feb 2022 Q2 (post-separation disability payments). Write: rule (replacement analysis) → when the right was earned vs. what period the payments cover → split.

### II.I Credit acquisitions (purchases and loans)

- **Rule.** Property bought **on credit** during marriage is presumptively CP, because loan proceeds acquired during marriage are presumed CP. The character of loan proceeds depends on the **intent of the lender**: if the lender relied **primarily (traditionally, solely) on the borrowing spouse's SP** (for example, SP collateral or SP income from before marriage), the proceeds and what they buy are SP; otherwise they are CP (*Gudelj v. Gudelj* (1953) 41 Cal.2d 202; *In re Marriage of Grinius* (1985) 166 Cal.App.3d 1179) [S-428]. (R-CMP-058)
- **Evidence of lender intent.** Hypothecation of SP; the loan application listing only SP assets; the lender's reliance on community income or the other spouse's signature (which points to CP). In Grinius, the community presumption prevailed because the lender relied on the community business and the wife signed the note.
- **Purchase-money loans** on property bought during marriage are community obligations unless the lender looked solely to SP.
- **Debts versus assets.** The loan's character tells you the character of what it buys; separately, the community estate is liable to the creditor for debts incurred during marriage regardless of character (§ 910; VI.C).
- **Forged signatures.** A spouse who forges the other spouse's signature on a loan breaches fiduciary duties (§ 721; § 1100(e)); the forged spouse is not personally liable, but the community estate remains liable for the debt incurred during marriage (§ 910) and the non-consenting spouse's SP is not (§ 913). Encumbering CP real property requires both spouses' joinder (§ 1102). (Feb 2024 Q1: garage bought with a bank loan on a forged signature.)
- **How tested.** Feb 2022 Q2 (sailboat loan); Feb 2024 Q1 (bank loan for garage).

### II.J Rights of reimbursement

The community or a spouse can be reimbursed, without changing title, in these situations (R-CMP-060):

| Contributor → beneficiary | Remedy | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| SP → acquisition of CP | Reimbursement of traced SP contributions (no interest/appreciation; capped at net value) unless written waiver | § 2640(b) |
| SP → acquisition of other spouse's SP | Same, unless written transmutation or waiver | § 2640(c) |
| CP → education/training of one spouse | Reimbursement with interest at legal rate; subject to reduction | § 2641 |
| CP → principal of SP purchase loan | Pro tanto ownership interest (not mere reimbursement) | Moore; Marsden |
| CP → improvements to SP | Reimbursement (cost or value added) | Allen [VERIFY V-306] |
| SP → family expenses | None (presumed gift) | See |
| Property of one estate → debt properly chargeable to another | Reimbursement as provided by statute | §§ 914, 915, 920, 2626 |
| Post-DOS SP → community debt | Epstein credit | Epstein (V.C) |
| Post-DOS exclusive use of community asset | Watts charge | Watts (V.C) |

#### II.J.1 Contributions to acquisition of property

- § 2640 is the general rule (II.E-F). Key points to write: (1) contributions defined (down payment, improvements, principal reduction; not interest/taxes/insurance/maintenance); (2) traced to SP; (3) no written waiver; (4) no interest or appreciation; (5) capped at net value at division. (R-CMP-042)
- **Waiver.** Only a **written** waiver, or a writing that has the effect of a waiver, defeats reimbursement (§ 2640(b)); an oral statement that "it's ours now" does not.
- **Interaction with transmutation.** If the SP spouse made a valid § 852 transmutation of the funds into CP without reserving reimbursement, the opponent argues the writing "has the effect of a waiver"; the contributor argues § 2640 still applies absent an express waiver [VERIFY V-313].

#### II.J.2 Educational and training expenses (§ 2641 reimbursement)

- **Rule.** At dissolution, the community is reimbursed for **community contributions to education or training** of a party that **substantially enhances** that party's **earning capacity**, **with interest at the legal rate** accruing from the end of the calendar year in which each contribution was made (Fam. Code § 2641(b)(1)). (R-CMP-046, R-CMP-047)
- **Loans.** A loan incurred during marriage for a party's education or training is **not** a community liability for division; it is **assigned to that party** (§ 2641(b)(2); § 2627).
- **Reduction or modification** (§ 2641(c)) to the extent circumstances make the reimbursement or assignment unjust, including:
  1. The community **substantially benefited** from the education, training or loan. **Rebuttable presumptions** (affecting the burden of proof): the community has **not** substantially benefited from contributions made **less than 10 years** before the proceeding began, and **has** substantially benefited from contributions made **more than 10 years** before.
  2. The education or training received by the party is **offset** by education or training received by the other party with community contributions.
  3. The education or training enables the party to engage in gainful employment that **substantially reduces the party's need for support** that would otherwise be required.
- **Exclusive remedy** (§ 2641(d)): no share of the degree's value or enhanced earnings; support may still consider it.
- **Example.** CP paid $80,000 of W's medical school tuition three years before H filed. The community is reimbursed $80,000 plus legal-rate interest; W's student loans are assigned to W; no reduction because fewer than 10 years passed and nothing shows a substantial benefit.

#### II.J.3 Reimbursement for payment of debts and support obligations

- **General rule.** A right of reimbursement for applying property of one estate to a debt arises **only as provided by statute** (Fam. Code § 920); it must be asserted within the statutory time (generally three years from actual knowledge of the application, or in the dissolution or probate proceeding, whichever is earlier), and can be waived in writing [VERIFY V-314].
- **Premarital child or spousal support** of a prior relationship is treated as a **premarital debt**; if CP pays it when the obligor's separate income was available, the community is reimbursed (Fam. Code § 915) [VERIFY V-330]. (Jul 2015 Q4 involved reimbursement relating to child support.)
- **Necessaries.** Where a spouse's SP pays a debt for the other spouse's necessaries while CP or the other's SP was available, reimbursement may be allowed (§ 914(b)) [S-413].
- **Post-separation payments** of community debts: Epstein credits and § 2626 (V.C; VI.C.5).

### II.K Life insurance, stock options and other special assets

- **Life insurance.** (R-CMP-054)
  - **Whole life (cash value):** apportion by the source of premiums (pro rata CP/SP), like any asset bought partly with each.
  - **Term life:** the policy's character usually follows the **premium paid for the final term** in which the insured died; a term policy that lapses or is renewed with post-DOS SP is generally SP (*Burwell v. Burwell* (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 1; *In re Marriage of Spengler* (1992) 5 Cal.App.4th 288) [S-411] [VERIFY V-307].
  - **Title does not control.** A policy bought with CP and titled in one spouse's name remains CP absent a § 852 transmutation (Valli).
  - **Beneficiary designations** of CP policies need the other spouse's written consent to defeat that spouse's half at death (Prob. Code § 5020; VII.D). Federal policies (NSLI, SGLI, FEGLI) follow federal law (VIII).
- **Employee stock options.** Options granted during marriage, even if unvested at DOS, are CP to the extent earned by community effort. Use a time-rule fraction that matches the **purpose** of the grant: (R-CMP-053)
  - ***In re Marriage of Hug*** (1984) 154 Cal.App.3d 780 (options mainly reward **past** services and attract the employee): community fraction = time from **start of employment** to DOS ÷ time from start of employment to the date the options are exercisable [S-426].
  - ***In re Marriage of Nelson*** (1986) 177 Cal.App.3d 150 (options mainly incentivize **future** services): community fraction = time from **grant** to DOS ÷ time from grant to exercisability [S-427] [VERIFY V-310].
  - Hug stresses that no single formula fits every case; the trial court has broad discretion.
- **Goodwill.** Goodwill of a business or professional practice developed during marriage is a CP asset (*Golden v. Golden* (1969) 270 Cal.App.2d 401; *In re Marriage of Foster* (1974) 42 Cal.App.3d 577 [S-432]); valuation may not include the spouse's post-separation efforts, and courts consider factors such as the practitioner's age, health, skill, reputation and past profits (*In re Marriage of Lopez* (1974) 38 Cal.App.3d 93) [VERIFY V-328]. Personal enhanced earning capacity is not goodwill.
- **Lottery winnings and prizes** won with a ticket bought with CP during marriage are CP; concealing them is a § 1101 breach (*In re Marriage of Rossi* (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 34: entire $1.3 million award to the wife under § 1101(h)).
- **Intellectual property** created by a spouse's labor during marriage is CP to the extent created during marriage [VERIFY V-332].
- **Businesses started during marriage** are CP; post-DOS growth from one spouse's efforts is apportioned (*Imperato*; V.B).
- **Gifts from third parties** to one spouse are SP (§ 770); wedding gifts to both are co-owned (see I.B.2).
- **How tested.** As one asset among four (Feb 2026: framed signed football jersey; Mar 2025: stock and watch). Characterize like any asset, then apply the special rule.

### II.L Personal injury damages (characterization)

- **CP rule.** Money or property received for personal injuries is **CP if the cause of action arose during marriage** (Fam. Code § 780), subject to special allocation at dissolution (§ 2603; VI.B.3).
- **SP exceptions** (§ 781): damages are the injured spouse's SP if the cause of action arose (1) after entry of a judgment of dissolution or legal separation, or (2) while the injured spouse was **living separate** from the other spouse [S-429] [VERIFY V-311]; and (3) damages paid **by one spouse to the other** for a cause of action arising during marriage are the injured spouse's SP (§ 781(c)).
- **Before marriage.** A cause of action that arose before marriage is SP as property owned before marriage (§ 770(a)(1)) [VERIFY V-331].
- **Reimbursement.** If the other spouse's SP or the CP paid expenses caused by the injury, that estate is reimbursed from the SP recovery (§ 781(b)).
- **What part of the award.** Lost earnings for the period of the marriage replace CP earnings; pain and suffering is personal. The statutes classify the whole recovery by when the cause of action arose; § 2603 then decides who gets it at division.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC treats personal-injury compensation as the injured spouse's personal property, while California calls it CP during marriage but generally awards it to the injured spouse at dissolution (approx.).

## III Management and control of community property

### III.A General rules

#### III.A.1 Equal management

- **Rule.** Except as otherwise provided, **either spouse** has the management and control of community **personal** property, whether acquired before or after 1975, with like absolute power of disposition, other than testamentary, as the spouse has of SP (Fam. Code § 1100(a)). (R-CMP-062)
- **Consequences.** Either spouse may sell community personal property for fair value, open accounts and incur debts that bind the community estate (§ 910). Third parties may deal with either spouse.
- **Limits** follow: gifts (§ 1100(b)), the home's furnishings and clothing (§ 1100(c)), businesses (§ 1100(d)), real property (§ 1102), and the fiduciary duty (§ 1100(e)).

#### III.A.2 Fiduciary duties

- **Rule.** In managing CP, each spouse must act toward the other under the general rules governing fiduciary relationships, which control the actions of persons having relationships of personal confidence, until the assets and liabilities have been divided by the parties or a court (Fam. Code § 1100(e)). The duty of highest good faith and fair dealing forbids taking unfair advantage of the other spouse (§ 721(b)). (R-CMP-063)
- **Content.** Includes (1) full disclosure of all material facts and information about the existence, characterization and valuation of all assets in which the community has or may have an interest and of debts for which the community is or may be liable, and providing equal access to related information on request (§ 1100(e)); and (2) the partnership-like duties incorporated by § 721(b): access to books, true and full information on request, and accounting for any benefit or profit derived without consent from transactions concerning CP.
- **Standard of care.** The duty is breached by self-dealing, concealment or transactions that impair the other's one-half interest; ordinary business losses from good-faith management are not breaches.
- **Duration.** The fiduciary duty continues after separation until the property is divided (§ 1100(e)), and disclosure duties continue during the dissolution (§ 2100 et seq.).
- **How tested.** Very often (4 tagged essays): secret gifts, forged signatures, concealed lottery winnings, sales of CP to friends below value.

#### III.A.3 Gifts of community personal property

- **Rule.** A spouse may not make a **gift** of community personal property, or dispose of it **without a valuable consideration**, without the **written consent** of the other spouse (Fam. Code § 1100(b)). (R-CMP-064)
- **Remedy.** A gift without consent is **voidable**, not void, at the option of the non-consenting spouse (*Spreckels v. Spreckels* (1916) 172 Cal. 775) [S-433]. During the donor's lifetime the non-consenting spouse may set aside the **entire** gift; after the donor spouse's death, only **one-half** (the survivor's share) (*Fields v. Michael* (1949) 91 Cal.App.2d 443, 448) [S-412] [VERIFY V-309]. The § 1101 remedies are also available.
- **Exceptions.** Gifts of modest value consistent with the marital standard of living (birthday presents, ordinary charitable gifts) are commonly treated as within normal management; the statute itself contains no dollar threshold, so argue both sides.
- **Example.** H gives his friend a $15,000 antique mirror bought with CP without W's written consent (Feb 2014 Q2 facts were similar). W may void the gift and recover the mirror.

#### III.A.4 Household goods and clothing

- **Rule.** A spouse may not sell, convey or encumber community personal property used as the **family dwelling**, or the **furniture, furnishings or fittings** of the home, or the **clothing or wearing apparel** of the other spouse or minor children, without the other spouse's **written consent** (Fam. Code § 1100(c)). (R-CMP-065)

#### III.A.5 Community business

- **Rule.** A spouse who is **operating or managing a business** (or an interest in one) that is all or substantially all CP has **primary management and control** of it and may act alone in all transactions, but must give **prior written notice** to the other spouse of any sale, lease, exchange, encumbrance or other disposition of **all or substantially all of the personal property used in the business** (Fam. Code § 1100(d)). Failure to give notice is remediable under § 1101. (R-CMP-066)
- **Real property of the business** remains subject to the joinder rule of § 1102.

#### III.A.6 Community real property: joinder and written consent

- **Rule.** Either spouse has management and control of community real property, but **both spouses must join** in executing any instrument by which community real property (or an interest in it) is **leased for more than one year**, or is **sold, conveyed or encumbered** (Fam. Code § 1102(a)). (R-CMP-067)
- **Effect of a sole transfer.** A transfer by one spouse alone is **voidable** by the non-consenting spouse. Protection of purchasers: where record title is in **one spouse's name alone**, that spouse's sole lease, contract, mortgage or deed to a **good-faith purchaser or encumbrancer without knowledge of the marriage** is presumed valid; and an action to avoid such an instrument must be commenced within **one year** after it is recorded (§ 1102(c)) [VERIFY V-316].
- **Remedy against the spouse.** The non-joining spouse may also seek § 1101 remedies for breach of fiduciary duty.
- **How tested.** Jul 2012 Q2 (lien on the family home given to a lawyer without the wife's consent); Feb 2014 Q2 (office building titled in one spouse's name and sold to a friend). Transfers of **QCP** land are not governed by § 1102 during marriage; they are attacked at death under Prob. Code § 102 (Jul 2022 Q5; VII.B). Ask: is the land CP? Was the buyer a good-faith purchaser without knowledge of the marriage? Was the suit within one year of recording?

#### III.A.7 Remedies for breach of fiduciary duty

- **Claim.** A spouse has a claim against the other spouse for any breach of the fiduciary duty that results in **impairment** to the claimant's present undivided one-half interest in the community estate (Fam. Code § 1101(a)). (R-CMP-068)
- **Remedies.** An **accounting** (§ 1101(b)); an order adding the claimant's name to title of CP (with exceptions, such as partnership interests held by the other spouse as a general partner) (§ 1101(c)); a court-ordered transaction where the other spouse's consent is required but unreasonably withheld; and money remedies:
  - **50%** of any asset undisclosed or transferred in breach (or an equal amount), plus attorney's fees and costs (§ 1101(g));
  - **100%** of the asset (or an equal amount) when the breach falls within **Civ. Code § 3294** (oppression, fraud or malice) (§ 1101(h)) [S-405]. *In re Marriage of Rossi* (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 34 awarded the wife all of a concealed lottery prize.
- **Limitations.** Generally within **three years** of the date the petitioning spouse had **actual knowledge** of the transaction; the claim may also be raised in a dissolution, legal separation or nullity proceeding, or after a spouse's death, as the statute provides (§ 1101(d)) [VERIFY V-301].
- **Disclosure sanctions** in dissolution proceedings (Fam. Code §§ 2100–2107, 271) are separate tools (VI.B.2).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Civil Code also lets a court give a spouse who concealed or transferred joint property a smaller share or none at division; California fixes the sanction at 50% or 100% of the asset under § 1101 (approx.).

## IV Management and control of separate property

- **Rule.** Each spouse has **sole management and control** of his or her SP and may convey, encumber, give away or devise it without the other spouse's consent (Fam. Code § 770(b)). (R-CMP-069)
- **Limits.** (1) The fiduciary duty of § 721 still governs **transactions between spouses** involving SP (an SP transfer that advantages one spouse raises the undue-influence presumption). (2) Community **labor** devoted to SP gives the community an interest (II.D), which the managing spouse may not destroy. (3) SP is liable for the owner's own debts (§ 913) and for the other spouse's necessaries (§ 914). (4) A spouse who uses CP to acquire or improve SP triggers community claims (II.D).
- **QCP during marriage** is generally managed like the acquiring spouse's SP [VERIFY V-327], but transfers of QCP without consideration may be recaptured at death (Prob. Code § 102; VII.B).
- **How tested.** Rarely alone; usually as the answer to "could H sell the inherited stock without W's consent?" (yes, it is his SP) or as the contrast to § 1100/1102 limits.

## V Separation

### V.A Determining date of separation

- **Rule.** The **date of separation** is the date a **complete and final break** in the marital relationship occurred, as evidenced by **both**: (1) the spouse has **expressed to the other spouse** the intent to end the marriage, and (2) the spouse's **conduct is consistent** with that intent. The court considers **all relevant evidence** (Fam. Code § 70, effective January 1, 2017) [S-400]. (R-CMP-070)
- **Living under one roof is possible.** § 70 abrogated *In re Marriage of Davis* (2015) 61 Cal.4th 846, which had required the spouses to live in separate residences, and *In re Marriage of Norviel* (2002) 102 Cal.App.4th 1152 (§ 70(c)) [S-435]. Spouses can therefore separate while still living in the same house if the expressed intent and consistent conduct exist.
- **Evidence courts weigh.** Statements to the other spouse and to third parties; moving out; separate bank accounts; filing separately; ending sexual relations; dating others; continued joint vacations or holidays; continued joint finances. Earlier cases emphasized conduct as the objective evidence of the break (*In re Marriage of Baragry* (1977) 73 Cal.App.3d 444 [S-434]).
- **Why it matters.** DOS ends CP earnings (§ 771), fixes the denominator of the time rule, ends § 910 liability for new debts, starts Epstein/Watts accounting, and may mark when § 781 SP treatment begins.
- **How tested.** Jul 2017 Q1 and Feb 2014 Q2 (when did the marital economic community end?). Write two candidate dates, apply both prongs of § 70 to each, and pick one.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC joint property generally continues until the divorce itself; California cuts off community acquisitions at the date of separation (approx.).

### V.B Earnings and accumulations while living separate and apart

- **Rule.** The **earnings and accumulations** of a spouse, and of the minor children living with or in the custody of that spouse, **after the date of separation** are that spouse's SP (Fam. Code § 771(a)). After a judgment of legal separation, earnings and accumulations are SP (§ 772). (R-CMP-071)
- **Post-DOS acquisitions** with post-DOS earnings are SP; post-DOS acquisitions with community funds are CP (trace).
- **Community business after DOS.** If a community business grows after separation because of one spouse's post-separation efforts, the increase is apportioned between CP (return on the community capital, passive growth) and that spouse's SP (return on post-DOS labor), using Pereira/Van Camp in reverse (*In re Marriage of Imperato* (1975) 45 Cal.App.3d 432) [S-421] [VERIFY V-325]. (R-CMP-072)
- **Pensions.** Service after DOS is SP; that is why the time-rule numerator stops at DOS.
- **Disability and severance** replacing post-DOS earnings are SP (II.H).
- **Debts after DOS** are generally the incurring spouse's, except necessaries for the spouse or children (VI.C.3).
- **How tested.** Feb 2022 Q2 (post-separation disability); Feb 2024 Q1 (investments made after separation).

### V.C Post-separation payments and use of assets (Epstein credits and Watts charges)

- **Epstein credits.** A spouse who, **after separation and before trial**, uses **SP (post-DOS earnings)** to pay a **community obligation** (mortgage, taxes, insurance on the family home; community credit cards) is generally entitled to **reimbursement** from the community (*In re Marriage of Epstein* (1979) 24 Cal.3d 76; see also Fam. Code § 2626). (R-CMP-073)
  - **No credit** where the payment was in effect a gift, was made in lieu of or as spousal or child support, was made under an agreement that no reimbursement would be sought, or where the paying spouse had exclusive use of the asset worth about as much as the payments (offset) [VERIFY V-321].
- **Watts charges.** A spouse who has **exclusive use of a community asset** (usually the family home) after separation may be **charged** for the reasonable value of that use, payable to the community (*In re Marriage of Watts* (1985) 171 Cal.App.3d 366). (R-CMP-074)
  - The court has discretion; it weighs whether the use was in lieu of support, whether children lived there, and offsetting Epstein credits.
- **Typical pattern.** H moves out; W lives in the CP house and pays the mortgage from her post-DOS salary. W claims Epstein credits for principal (and, often, interest, taxes and insurance) paid; H claims Watts charges for W's exclusive use. The court nets them.
- **Post-DOS principal payments on community property** are SP contributions to the community; they are handled under Epstein, not § 2640 (which addresses contributions to acquisition).
- **Post-DOS growth of a community business** from one spouse's labor (V.B; Imperato).

## VI Division of property at dissolution

### VI.A General rules

#### VI.A.1 Equal division

- **Rule.** Except upon the written agreement of the parties or their oral stipulation in open court, or as otherwise provided, the court divides the **community estate equally** in its judgment of dissolution or legal separation, or later if it expressly reserves jurisdiction (Fam. Code § 2550). (R-CMP-075)
- **Community estate** = CP + QCP (§ 63); quasi-marital property of a putative spouse is divided the same way (§ 2251).
- **SP is confirmed** to its owner, not divided. Debts are confirmed or divided under §§ 2620–2627.
- **Aggregate equality.** The court may divide **in kind** or **award assets to one party with offsetting assets** (or an equalizing payment) so that each party receives one-half of the **net** community estate; each asset need not be split.
- **Item theory vs. aggregate theory.** At **dissolution** the court divides the net community estate as a whole (aggregate). At **death**, each spouse owns one-half of **each** CP item, and a will can dispose only of the decedent's half of each item (VII.A).
- **Fault irrelevant.** Misconduct affects division only through specific statutes (for example §§ 1101 and 2602).
- **Joint petition.** Since January 1, 2026, spouses may file a joint petition for dissolution (SB 1427); this is procedural and does not change the property rules (recent-changes § 7).

#### VI.A.2 Valuation and method of division

- **Valuation date.** Assets and liabilities are valued **as near as practicable to the time of trial**, but on a showing of good cause the court may value particular assets or liabilities at a date after separation and before trial to accomplish an equal division in a just and equitable manner (Fam. Code § 2552). A business valued at trial date may require Imperato apportionment of post-DOS growth.
- **Omitted assets.** The court retains continuing jurisdiction to divide community assets or liabilities not divided in the judgment (§ 2556).
- **Deferred sale of family home** for the benefit of minor children is available in limited circumstances (§ 3800 et seq.; not commonly tested).

#### VI.A.3 Out-of-state real property

- **Rule.** If the community estate includes **real property in another state**, the court shall, if possible, divide the CP and QCP so that it is **not necessary to change the nature of the interests** held in the out-of-state land (for example, by awarding it to the titled spouse and offsetting with California assets). If that is not possible, the court may order the parties to **execute conveyances** of the out-of-state land, or award the party who would have benefited from the conveyance the **money value** of the interest (Fam. Code § 2660) [S-418]. (R-CMP-083)
- **Why.** A California court cannot directly transfer title to land in another state, but it can act in personam on spouses over whom it has jurisdiction.

### VI.B Deviation from equal distribution

The equal-division rule yields to specific statutes. Each deviation is a separate heading in an essay.

#### VI.B.1 Misappropriation

- **Rule.** As an additional award or offset against existing property, the court may award from a party's share **any sum it determines to have been deliberately misappropriated** by that party to the exclusion of the other party's interest in the community estate (Fam. Code § 2602). (R-CMP-076)
- **Overlap with § 1101.** A deliberate diversion (gambling away savings, transferring funds to a lover) supports both a § 2602 offset and § 1101 remedies.
- **Example.** H withdrew $60,000 of CP savings after separation and spent it on a new partner. The court awards W $30,000 from H's share (or offsets it).

#### VI.B.2 Failure to disclose property

- **Rule.** Spouses must disclose all assets and liabilities fully in dissolution proceedings (Fam. Code § 2100 et seq.; preliminary and final declarations of disclosure, §§ 2104–2105). A spouse who conceals or fails to disclose a community asset in breach of fiduciary duty may be ordered to pay the other spouse **50%** of the asset's value, or **100%** if the concealment involved fraud, oppression or malice (§ 1101(g), (h)). (R-CMP-077)
- **Additional tools.** Monetary sanctions for noncompliance with the disclosure statutes, imposed unless the noncompliance was justified (§ 2107(c)), and attorney's-fee sanctions for conduct frustrating settlement (§ 271) (*In re Marriage of Feldman* (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1470: large sanctions for nondisclosure without proof of harm) [S-431]; continuing jurisdiction to divide omitted assets (§ 2556); and set-aside of a judgment for fraud or nondisclosure (§ 2122).
- **Example.** H hid a $1 million lottery prize won during marriage (Rossi). The court awards W the entire prize under § 1101(h) because concealment was fraudulent.

#### VI.B.3 Personal injury recovery against a third party

- **Rule.** "Community estate personal injury damages" (a recovery for a cause of action arising during marriage that is not SP under § 781 and **has not been commingled** with other community assets) are **assigned to the injured spouse** at dissolution, unless the court, considering the parties' economic condition and needs, the time elapsed since the recovery or the accrual of the claim, and all other facts, determines that the **interests of justice require another disposition**. Even then, **at least one-half** must go to the injured spouse (Fam. Code § 2603). (R-CMP-078)
- **Commingled recoveries** lose the special treatment and are divided equally like other CP.
- **At death,** § 2603 does not apply; PI damages that are CP are CP (half each).
- **How tested.** Jul 2015 Q4 (car-accident settlement). Write: characterize under §§ 780–781 (when did the claim arise?), then § 2603 at division, then commingling.

#### VI.B.4 Personal injury recovery against a spouse

- **Rule.** Damages one spouse recovers from the **other spouse** for a personal injury during marriage are the injured spouse's **SP** (Fam. Code § 781(c)). (R-CMP-079)
- **Paying the judgment.** When the injury was caused in whole or part by the negligent or wrongful act of the spouse, **CP may not be used to satisfy the tortfeasor spouse's liability** to the injured spouse until the tortfeasor's **SP** (not exempt from enforcement) is **exhausted**; the injured spouse may consent in writing to the use of CP; and the rule does not affect rights under insurance policies (Fam. Code § 782) [S-406] [VERIFY V-302].
- **Example.** H negligently injures W in a boating accident; W recovers $200,000 from H. The recovery is W's SP, and H must pay it from his SP first.

#### VI.B.5 Educational debts

- **Rule.** Educational loans incurred during marriage are **assigned to the spouse who received the education** and are not community liabilities for division (Fam. Code §§ 2627, 2641(b)(2)) [S-407], subject to the § 2641(c) reduction factors (for example, the community substantially benefited). (R-CMP-080)
- **Contrast.** Liability to the lender is a separate question: the community estate is liable to the creditor for a debt incurred during marriage (§ 910); the § 2627 assignment governs only who bears the debt between the spouses.

#### VI.B.6-7 Other deviations (tort liabilities; debts exceeding assets; small estates)

The State Bar outline's items VI.B.6 and VI.B.7 were not captured in Phase 0 research; the statutory deviations below are the most likely candidates and are testable in any event [VERIFY V-025].
- **Tort liabilities.** At division, a liability for death or injury that was **not** incurred while performing an activity for the benefit of the community is **assigned to the spouse whose act or omission gave rise to it, without offset** (Fam. Code § 2627, cross-referencing § 1000(b)(2)) [S-407] [VERIFY V-303]. (R-CMP-081)
- **Debts exceeding assets.** To the extent community debts exceed total community and QCP assets, the **excess** is assigned **as the court deems just and equitable**, considering factors such as the parties' relative ability to pay (Fam. Code § 2622(b)).
- **Small or unlocatable estates.** If the net value of the community estate is small and one party cannot be located, the court may award the entire estate to the other party [VERIFY V-320].
- **Agreement or stipulation.** The parties may agree in writing, or stipulate orally in open court, to an unequal division (§ 2550).

#### VI.B.8 Retirement and pension benefits

- **Rule.** The court must make whatever orders are necessary to ensure that each party receives his or her full community share of any retirement plan, including orders for division of benefits and survivor benefits and for the use of qualified domestic relations orders (Fam. Code § 2610). (R-CMP-050)
- **Methods.** (1) **Cash-out:** award the whole pension to the employee and give the other spouse assets equal to the present value of the community share (common when the pension is small or easily valued); (2) **in-kind / reserved jurisdiction:** divide each payment by the time-rule fraction as it is paid (common for unvested or uncertain pensions; Brown).
- **Gillmore election** allows the non-employee spouse to take his or her share once the employee is eligible to retire (R-CMP-082).
- **Federal pensions and Social Security:** see VIII.

### VI.C Property liable for debts (premarital, marital and post-separation debts)

Two separate questions: (1) **liability to creditors** (what property can the creditor reach?) and (2) **allocation between spouses** at dissolution (who ultimately bears it?).

#### VI.C.1 Premarital debts

- **Liability.** The **community estate** is liable for a debt incurred by **either spouse before or during marriage**, regardless of which spouse manages the property and regardless of whether one or both spouses are parties to the debt or judgment (Fam. Code § 910(a)) [S-414]. (R-CMP-084)
- **Earnings exception.** The **earnings** of a married person during marriage are **not liable** for a debt incurred by the person's **spouse before marriage**. After payment, the earnings stay exempt so long as they are held in a **deposit account** in which the debtor spouse has **no right of withdrawal** and are **not commingled** with other community property (except insignificant amounts) (§ 911). (R-CMP-085)
- **Non-debtor's SP** is not liable for the other spouse's premarital debts (§ 913(b)(1)).
- **At division,** premarital debts are **confirmed to the spouse who incurred them** (§ 2621).

#### VI.C.2 Debts during marriage

- **Liability.** The whole **community estate** is liable for any debt incurred by either spouse during marriage (before DOS) (§ 910). The **debtor spouse's SP** is liable (§ 913(a)). The **non-debtor spouse's SP** is **not** liable (§ 913(b)(1)), except for **necessaries** (§ 914). Joining in or consenting to an encumbrance of property to secure the other spouse's debt does not subject the consenting spouse's SP to liability (§ 913(b)(2)). (R-CMP-086)
- **Tort liability: order of satisfaction.** A married person is **not liable** for injury caused by the other spouse except where he or she would be liable if not married (§ 1000(a)). The tortfeasor spouse's liability is satisfied: (1) if the act occurred while performing an activity **for the benefit of the community**, **first from CP, then from the tortfeasor's SP**; (2) otherwise, **first from the tortfeasor's SP, then from CP** (§ 1000(b)). (R-CMP-088)
- **Example.** H, driving to a client meeting for his job, injures a pedestrian. The activity benefited the community (earning CP), so the judgment is satisfied first from CP (including W's half of the CP house) and then from H's SP; W's SP is not reachable (Jul 2012 Q2 pattern).
- **Contract debts** incurred by either spouse during marriage bind the community estate even if the other spouse did not sign (§ 910).

#### VI.C.3 Post-separation debts

- **Liability.** For § 910, "during marriage" **excludes** the period after DOS and before a judgment of dissolution or legal separation (§ 910(b)) [S-414], so post-DOS debts are not automatically community-estate liabilities.
- **Allocation.** Debts incurred **after DOS but before judgment**: those for the **common necessaries of life** of either spouse or the children are confirmed to either spouse according to the parties' respective needs and abilities to pay at the time incurred; non-necessaries are confirmed **to the spouse who incurred them** (§ 2623). Debts incurred after entry of a judgment of dissolution but before termination of marital status, or after a legal separation judgment, are confirmed to the incurring spouse (§ 2624).

#### VI.C.4 Liability of earnings, quasi-community property and separate property; necessaries

- **Earnings.** A spouse's earnings are not liable for the other spouse's **premarital** debts if kept in an uncommingled account the debtor spouse cannot draw on (§ 911) (VI.C.1).
- **QCP** is liable for debts to the same extent and in the same manner as CP (§ 912).
- **SP.** Liable for the owner's own debts; not liable for the other spouse's debts (§ 913), except necessaries.
- **Necessaries.** A married person is **personally liable** for (1) debts incurred for the **necessaries of life** of the person's spouse **before the date of separation**, and (2) debts incurred for the **common necessaries of life** of the spouse **while living separately** (except where support is excused by agreement under § 4302). The person's SP may be applied; if it is applied while nonexempt CP or the spouse's SP was available but not applied, the person is entitled to reimbursement (§ 914) [S-413]. (R-CMP-087)
- **Necessaries** are measured by the marital standard of living (food, shelter, clothing, medical care); "common necessaries" are the basics needed to sustain life.
- **Support obligations of a prior relationship** are treated as premarital debts (§ 915) [VERIFY V-330].

#### VI.C.5 Allocation of debts at dissolution

| Debt | Allocation between spouses | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Incurred before marriage | Confirmed to the spouse who incurred it | § 2621 |
| Incurred during marriage, before DOS | Divided equally as part of the community estate; excess over assets assigned as just and equitable | § 2622 |
| Incurred during marriage but not for the benefit of the community | May be confirmed to the incurring spouse | § 2625 |
| After DOS, before judgment, for common necessaries | Confirmed according to needs and abilities to pay | § 2623(a) |
| After DOS, before judgment, not necessaries | Confirmed to the incurring spouse | § 2623(b) |
| After judgment / legal separation | Confirmed to the incurring spouse | § 2624 |
| Educational loans | Assigned to the educated spouse | §§ 2627, 2641 |
| Tort liabilities not for community benefit | Assigned to the tortfeasor, without offset | §§ 2627, 1000 |

- **Reimbursement** for debts paid after DOS but before trial: the court may order reimbursement in appropriate circumstances (§ 2626); see Epstein (V.C). (R-CMP-089)

#### VI.C.6 Liability after division and reimbursement

- **After division** (§ 916) [VERIFY V-315]: (R-CMP-090)
  1. Property a spouse **received** in the division, and that spouse's SP, remain liable for debts **that spouse** incurred before or during marriage, whether or not the debt was assigned to the other spouse.
  2. A spouse's SP and the property received in the division are **not** liable for a debt **the other spouse** incurred, unless the debt was **assigned** to the first spouse in the division.
  3. If property is applied to a debt assigned to the other spouse, the paying spouse has a right of **reimbursement** from that other spouse.
- **Creditor's view.** Allocation between spouses does not bind a creditor of the incurring spouse (who can still reach that spouse's property); it binds the spouses inter se and protects the non-incurring spouse once assets are divided.
- **Reimbursement claims** generally (§ 920): only as statute provides; timing limits [VERIFY V-314].

## VII Division of property at death

### VII.A Effect of death of married person on property

- **CP at death.** On the death of a married person, **one-half of the CP belongs to the surviving spouse** and the **other one-half belongs to the decedent** (Prob. Code § 100). The decedent may dispose of only his or her half by will (Prob. Code § 6101). (R-CMP-091)
- **Item theory.** At death each spouse owns one-half of **each** CP asset. A will that purports to give away an entire CP asset reaches only the decedent's half; the survivor keeps the other half of that item.
- **Spousal election.** If the decedent's will purports to dispose of the survivor's half too while giving the survivor benefits under the will, the survivor must **elect** either to take under the will (letting the survivor's half pass as the will directs) or to keep his or her half and forgo the will's gifts [VERIFY V-322].
- **Characterize first.** Every death essay begins with the same characterization routine (II) for each asset: the CP/SP/QCP label decides what the decedent could leave and what the survivor already owns.
- **Passing without administration.** CP and QCP that pass or belong to the surviving spouse can generally be confirmed without probate administration (Prob. Code § 13500; spousal property petition, § 13650) [VERIFY V-323].
- **Survivorship forms.** CP with right of survivorship (Civ. Code § 682.1) passes to the survivor; joint tenancy passes by survivorship (but see Brace, II.A.2).
- **SP** of the decedent passes by will or intestacy (VII.C).
- **Gifts of CP made without consent.** After the donor's death, the survivor may recover **one-half** of a gift of CP made without written consent (Fields; III.A.3).

### VII.B Quasi-community property at death and recapture

- **Decedent's QCP.** On the death of a married person **domiciled in California**, **one-half of the decedent's QCP belongs to the surviving spouse** and the other half belongs to the decedent (Prob. Code § 101). The survivor's own QCP is **not** affected: the survivor keeps all of it. (R-CMP-092)
- **Definition at death** (Prob. Code § 66): personal property wherever situated and **California** real property, acquired while domiciled elsewhere, that would have been CP if the decedent had been domiciled in California (and property acquired in exchange) [S-404]. **Out-of-state land is not QCP at death**; its succession is governed by the situs state's law.
- **Recapture of transferred QCP** (Prob. Code § 102) [S-403] [VERIFY V-304]: the surviving spouse may require the transferee to **restore one-half** of the property (or of its proceeds or value) to the decedent's estate if: (R-CMP-093)
  1. the decedent died domiciled in California;
  2. the decedent transferred QCP to someone other than the surviving spouse **without substantial consideration** and **without the survivor's written consent or joinder**; and
  3. the transfer is one in which the decedent **retained** at death (a) possession or enjoyment of, or the right to income from, the property; (b) a power to revoke or to consume, invade or dispose of the principal for the decedent's own benefit; or (c) the property was held by the decedent and another **with right of survivorship**.
  - Certain transfers (such as life insurance, accident insurance, joint annuities and pension payments to a non-spouse) are treated separately [VERIFY V-304].
- **Exam pattern.** Jul 2022 Q5: a couple domiciled elsewhere for 20 years bought California land with out-of-state earnings; H later deeded it to himself and his daughter as joint tenants without W's written consent; H died. The land is QCP (California realty, out-of-state marital earnings); the joint-tenancy deed is a survivorship transfer without consideration or consent; W may recapture one-half.
- **Jul 2026 Q2 pattern:** California house (QCP or CP), State X farm (not QCP at death; State X law), savings account (personal property; QCP if funded with marital earnings).

### VII.C Intestate succession and testamentary power

- **Testamentary power** (Prob. Code § 6101): a decedent may dispose by will of (1) his or her SP, (2) one-half of the CP, and (3) one-half of the decedent's QCP. (R-CMP-091)
- **Intestate share of the surviving spouse** (Prob. Code § 6401): (R-CMP-094)
  - **CP:** the decedent's one-half of the CP (so the survivor takes all CP) (§ 6401(a));
  - **QCP:** the decedent's one-half of the decedent's QCP (§ 6401(b));
  - **SP:** (1) **all** of it if the decedent left no issue, parent, sibling or issue of a deceased sibling; (2) **one-half** if the decedent left **one child** (or the issue of one deceased child), or left **no issue but a parent or parents or their issue** (siblings, nieces, nephews); (3) **one-third** if the decedent left **more than one child**, one child and the issue of one or more deceased children, or the issue of two or more deceased children (§ 6401(c)).
  - The rest of the intestate SP passes to the other heirs under Prob. Code § 6402 (Wills outline).
- **Example.** W dies intestate leaving H and her sister S, no issue or parents. H takes all CP, all of W's QCP, and half of W's SP; S takes the other half of W's SP.

### VII.D Nonprobate transfers, omitted spouse and other crossover rules

- **Nonprobate transfers of CP** (life insurance beneficiary designations, pay-on-death accounts, revocable trusts) made by one spouse **without the other's written consent** do not take effect against the non-consenting spouse's interest in that CP; the court may set the transfer aside as to that interest (Prob. Code §§ 5020, 5021) [S-420]. A spouse's written consent to a nonprobate transfer is **not** a transmutation unless it satisfies § 852 (Prob. Code § 5022; *Estate of MacDonald* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 262). (R-CMP-096)
- **Omitted (pretermitted) spouse.** A spouse who married the decedent after the decedent executed all testamentary instruments, and who is not provided for, receives the decedent's one-half of the CP, one-half of the decedent's QCP, and a share of the SP equal to the intestate share but **not more than one-half** of the SP (Prob. Code § 21610), unless an exception in § 21611 applies (intentional omission shown in the instrument; provision outside the will in lieu; valid waiver). (R-CMP-097) (Feb 2018 Q5 crossover.)
- **Effect of dissolution on wills and nonprobate transfers.** Dissolution or annulment revokes will provisions in favor of the former spouse (Prob. Code § 6122) and, generally, nonprobate transfers to the former spouse (Prob. Code § 5600) [VERIFY V-324]; ERISA plan designations are governed by federal law (Egelhoff; VIII).
- **Trusts crossover.** CP transferred by the spouses into a revocable trust generally keeps its community character unless the trust instrument states otherwise [VERIFY V-333]; creditors of a settlor can reach revocable-trust property (Trusts outline).

## VIII Federal preemption (Supremacy Clause)

- **Rule.** Under the **Supremacy Clause** (U.S. Const. art. VI, cl. 2), state community property law yields when it conflicts with a federal statute that **designates who receives** a federal benefit or that **forbids assignment** of it; the state court may not divide or reassign such a benefit, or circumvent the federal rule with an offsetting award designed to replace it. (R-CMP-100)
- **Railroad Retirement benefits** are not divisible as CP (*Hisquierdo v. Hisquierdo*, 439 U.S. 572 (1979)).
- **Social Security** benefits are not divisible and may not be offset by awarding other assets for them (anti-assignment clause, 42 U.S.C. § 407(a)). (R-CMP-103)
- **Military retired pay.** *McCarty v. McCarty*, 453 U.S. 210 (1981), held it could not be divided as CP; Congress responded with the **Uniformed Services Former Spouses' Protection Act**, 10 U.S.C. § 1408, which lets states treat **disposable retired pay** as CP. Retired pay **waived to receive VA disability benefits** is not divisible (*Mansell v. Mansell*, 490 U.S. 581 (1989)), and a state court may not order the veteran to **indemnify** the former spouse for a post-divorce waiver (*Howell v. Howell*, 581 U.S. 214 (2017)) [S-423].
- **ERISA plans.** The anti-alienation rule (29 U.S.C. § 1056(d)(1)) yields only to a **qualified domestic relations order** (§ 1056(d)(3)). ERISA preempts a nonparticipant spouse's attempt to leave by will her community interest in undistributed pension benefits (*Boggs v. Boggs*, 520 U.S. 833 (1997)), and preempts state statutes that revoke a former spouse's beneficiary designation on divorce as applied to ERISA plans (*Egelhoff v. Egelhoff*, 532 U.S. 141 (2001)). (R-CMP-101)
- **Federal life insurance and savings bonds.** Beneficiary or survivorship designations under federal programs control over CP claims: National Service Life Insurance (*Wissner v. Wissner*, 338 U.S. 655 (1950)); Servicemembers' Group Life Insurance (*Ridgway v. Ridgway*, 454 U.S. 46 (1981)); Federal Employees' Group Life Insurance (*Hillman v. Maretta*, 569 U.S. 483 (2013)); U.S. savings bonds with survivorship (*Free v. Bland*, 369 U.S. 663 (1962)), subject to a fraud exception where CP was used without the other spouse's consent (*Yiatchos v. Yiatchos*, 376 U.S. 306 (1964)). (R-CMP-102)
- **Bankruptcy.** CP under the debtor's sole, equal or joint management and control becomes property of the bankruptcy estate (11 U.S.C. § 541(a)(2)); *In re Brace* (2020) answered the characterization question for such a case [S-422].
- **How to use on an essay.** One short paragraph: "Federal law preempts California CP law as to [benefit]; the court cannot divide it or award offsetting property to replace it." Then characterize the rest under California law.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: there is no PRC analogue to federal preemption of marital property; think of it as a higher-level statute (上位法) that displaces local marital-property rules for specific benefits (approx.).
