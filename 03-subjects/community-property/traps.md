# Community Property — traps

Community property is not on the MBE, so there are no MBE distractors here. These are the mistakes that cost points on California essays, with the rule that fixes each.

## Characterization traps

1. **Title = ownership.** Putting a CP-funded asset in one spouse's name does not make it SP; the Evid. Code § 662 title presumption yields to the transmutation statutes between spouses (R-CMP-012; Valli).
2. **Forgetting § 2640 after § 2581.** A jointly titled house is CP at divorce, but the SP down payment comes back first. Always follow § 2581 with § 2640 (R-CMP-013, R-CMP-042).
3. **Giving § 2640 reimbursement interest or appreciation.** Reimbursement is dollar for dollar, no interest, capped at net value (R-CMP-042). Contrast § 2641 (education), which **does** carry interest at the legal rate (R-CMP-046).
4. **Counting interest, taxes and insurance** as contributions. Only down payments, improvements and principal count for § 2640 and Moore/Marsden (R-CMP-037, R-CMP-040, R-CMP-042).
5. **Confusing Moore/Marsden with § 2640.** CP paying principal on SP property → **ownership share** (Moore). SP paying into CP property → **reimbursement** (§ 2640). Different direction, different remedy (R-CMP-037 vs. R-CMP-042).
6. **Forgetting Marsden's premarital appreciation.** Appreciation before marriage is all SP; only marital appreciation is shared (R-CMP-038).
7. **Using only Pereira or only Van Camp.** Apply both, then argue which fits (R-CMP-036).
8. **Mixing up the Pereira and Van Camp outputs.** Pereira computes the **SP** share (capital + return) and gives the rest to CP; Van Camp computes the **CP** share (salary − draws) and gives the rest to SP (R-CMP-034, R-CMP-035).
9. **Inheritance treated as joint (PRC instinct).** Gifts and inheritances to one spouse are SP regardless of what the will says about the spouse (R-CMP-002).
10. **"Total recapitulation" as tracing.** Showing more SP went in than came out over the years is not tracing; use direct tracing or exhaustion (R-CMP-032).
11. **Forgetting the family expense presumption.** SP spent on groceries and rent is a gift to the community; no reimbursement (R-CMP-031).
12. **Treating an oral transmutation as valid because the spouse relied on it.** No part-performance or estoppel exception (Benson) (R-CMP-022).
13. **Applying § 852(c) to anything given as a gift.** The exception covers only personal articles (clothing, jewelry and similar) used by the donee and not substantial in value; not cars, houses, boats, stock (R-CMP-024).
14. **Missing the undue-influence presumption** on an interspousal deed that satisfies § 852. The writing is only step one (R-CMP-016, R-CMP-026).
15. **Applying the fiduciary presumption to a premarital agreement.** Prospective spouses are not yet fiduciaries; use § 1615 instead (Bonds) (R-CMP-016, R-CMP-019).
16. **Skipping the § 1612(c) counsel rule** for spousal support waivers; a valid property agreement can still have an unenforceable support waiver (R-CMP-021).
17. **Assuming the degree is CP.** It is not; § 2641 reimbursement is the exclusive remedy (R-CMP-046).
18. **Calling a pension SP because it is unvested.** Vesting does not matter; use the time rule (R-CMP-048, R-CMP-049).
19. **Treating all disability pay as SP (or CP).** Split by what it replaces (R-CMP-051).
20. **Assuming loan proceeds follow the borrower's name.** Lender intent controls (R-CMP-058).

## Separation traps

21. **Using the date one spouse moved out without analysis.** § 70 requires expressed intent and consistent conduct; spouses may separate under one roof (Davis abrogated) (R-CMP-070).
22. **Treating pre-DOS earnings paid after DOS as SP.** A bonus earned during marriage but paid after DOS is CP (R-CMP-071).
23. **Forgetting Epstein and Watts together.** If one spouse paid the mortgage and lived in the house, raise both and net them (R-CMP-073, R-CMP-074).

## Management and remedy traps

24. **Saying a unilateral gift of CP is void.** It is **voidable** by the non-consenting spouse: wholly during life, half after death (R-CMP-064, R-CMP-099).
25. **Forgetting the BFP and one-year rules** for one-spouse conveyances of CP land (R-CMP-067).
26. **Applying § 1102 to QCP land during marriage.** QCP is generally managed by the acquiring spouse; the protection comes at death under Prob. Code § 102 (R-CMP-069, R-CMP-093).
27. **Missing the 100% remedy.** Fraud, oppression or malice → 100% of the asset under § 1101(h) (R-CMP-068).

## Division and debt traps

28. **Dividing PI damages equally.** § 2603 gives them to the injured spouse (at least half), unless commingled (R-CMP-078).
29. **Confusing creditor liability with allocation.** The community estate may be liable to the creditor (§ 910) even if the debt is assigned to one spouse at divorce (§§ 2620–2627) (R-CMP-084, R-CMP-089).
30. **Reaching the non-debtor spouse's SP** for an ordinary debt. Only necessaries (§ 914) (R-CMP-086, R-CMP-087).
31. **Forgetting the § 911 earnings exception** for a spouse's premarital creditors, and its "separate, uncommingled account" condition (R-CMP-085).
32. **Getting the tort order backwards.** Community-benefit activity → CP first; otherwise the tortfeasor's SP first (R-CMP-088).
33. **Dividing educational loans as community debts** (R-CMP-080).

## Death and QCP traps

34. **Using the dissolution QCP definition at death.** At death, out-of-state land is **not** QCP (Prob. Code § 66) (R-CMP-092).
35. **Splitting the survivor's own QCP at death.** Only the decedent's QCP is split (R-CMP-092).
36. **Letting the decedent will the whole CP asset.** The decedent controls only his or her half of each item (R-CMP-091).
37. **Forgetting § 102 recapture** when QCP was put into joint tenancy or a revocable trust (R-CMP-093).
38. **Giving the surviving spouse all the SP in intestacy.** The share depends on surviving issue, parents and siblings (R-CMP-094).
39. **Ignoring QCP at dissolution when one spouse lives elsewhere.** Roesch requires both spouses to be domiciled in California (R-CMP-003).
40. **Ignoring federal preemption.** Social Security, waived military pay, ERISA designations and federal insurance follow federal law (R-CMP-100 to R-CMP-103).
