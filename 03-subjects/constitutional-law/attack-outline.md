# Constitutional Law — attack outline

**Order:** Justiciability → Power → Federalism limits → State action → Right → Tier → Apply → Remedy.

## 1. Justiciability
- **Standing** (R-CON-008): injury in fact (concrete, particularized, actual or imminent) + fairly traceable + likely redressable. Do it per plaintiff and per remedy.
- **Third-party** (R-CON-011): own injury + close relationship + hindrance.
- **Association** (R-CON-012): members have standing + germane + no individual participation needed.
- **Taxpayer** (R-CON-013): none, except an Establishment Clause challenge to congressional spending (Flast).
- **Intervenor** (R-CON-014): needs its own standing if it seeks different relief; ideology is not enough.
- **Ripeness** (R-CON-015): fitness + hardship; pre-enforcement OK with a credible threat.
- **Mootness** (R-CON-016): live controversy throughout. Exceptions: capable of repetition yet evading review; voluntary cessation (absolutely clear it won't recur); damages or nominal damages; class action.
- **Political question** (R-CON-017): textual commitment or no manageable standards.
- **Eleventh Amendment** (R-CON-005, R-CON-006): no private damages suits against states; sue officers for prospective relief (Ex parte Young); cities not protected.

## 2. Federal power
- **N&P** (R-CON-020): rational means to an enumerated end.
- **Commerce** (R-CON-021, R-CON-022): channels; instrumentalities and things in commerce; substantial effects (aggregate economic activity only); no compelled purchase.
- **Tax** (R-CON-023): revenue-raising, not a penalty (NFIB functional test).
- **Spend** (R-CON-024): general welfare + unambiguous + related + no independent bar + not coercive.
- **§ 5** (R-CON-027): congruent and proportional remedy for state violations; no private conduct.
- **Thirteenth § 2** (R-CON-028): badges and incidents of slavery; reaches private actors.
- **War / foreign / treaty** (R-CON-025, R-CON-026): broad; treaty implementation via N&P.

## 3. Executive and interbranch
- **Youngstown** (R-CON-031): authorized = max; silence = twilight; contrary = lowest ebb (exclusive powers only).
- **Take Care / statutory authority** (R-CON-032): no lawmaking; no impoundment; clear authorization for major questions; IEEPA ≠ tariffs.
- **Commander in chief** (R-CON-033): repel attacks; Hamdi (plurality) process for detained citizens.
- **Treaties / executive agreements** (R-CON-034): later in time wins vs. statutes; agreements preempt state law; non-self-executing needs legislation.
- **Appointments** (R-CON-035): principal = President + Senate; inferior = President, courts, department heads; Congress cannot appoint enforcers.
- **Removal** (R-CON-036): at will for principal executive officers (Slaughter 2026 overrules Humphrey's); limited for-cause OK for inferior officers (Morrison); no double for-cause; Fed governors protected (Cook).
- **Pardon** (R-CON-037): federal crimes only; not impeachment.
- **Chadha / Bowsher** (R-CON-038): no legislative veto; no congressional removal except impeachment.
- **Presentment** (R-CON-039): bicameralism + presentment; no line-item veto.
- **Nondelegation** (R-CON-040): intelligible principle.
- **Immunities** (R-CON-041 to R-CON-043): President absolute civil immunity for official acts; criminal immunity per Trump v. US; privilege yields to criminal need; Speech or Debate for legislative acts; judges absolute; others qualified.

## 4. Federalism
- **Federal immunity** (R-CON-044): no direct state tax or regulation of federal operations; nondiscriminatory taxes on contractors OK.
- **Tenth Amendment** (R-CON-045): generally applicable laws bind states (Garcia).
- **Anti-commandeering** (R-CON-046): no orders to state legislatures or officers; no bans on state lawmaking.
- **DCC discrimination** (R-CON-047): virtually per se invalid unless legitimate local purpose + no nondiscriminatory alternative.
- **Pike** (R-CON-048): neutral law invalid only if burden clearly excessive vs. local benefits.
- **Exceptions** (R-CON-049, R-CON-052): market participant; public-entity function; congressional authorization (but not for individual rights).
- **State taxes** (R-CON-050): nexus, apportionment, nondiscrimination, fair relation.
- **Preemption** (R-CON-051): express; field; conflict (impossibility or obstacle).
- **Article IV P&I** (R-CON-053): individual citizens; fundamental (livelihood) rights; substantial reason + substantial relationship.

## 5. Individual rights
- **State action** (R-CON-054, R-CON-055): public function (exclusive) or entanglement (Shelley, Burton, joint action); officials' social media (Lindke two-part test).
- **Incorporation** (R-CON-056): almost all of the Bill of Rights via due process; not grand jury or civil jury.
- **Procedural due process** (R-CON-057, R-CON-058): protected interest (entitlement; liberty) → Mathews balancing → notice and opportunity before a neutral decisionmaker.
- **Substantive due process** (R-CON-060 to R-CON-063): fundamental (Glucksberg history-and-tradition) → strict scrutiny; otherwise rational basis; no abortion right (Dobbs).
- **Travel** (R-CON-064): new residents' basic necessities → strict scrutiny (Saenz).
- **Equal protection** (R-CON-066 to R-CON-076):
  - Strict: race, national origin, state alienage (except political functions), fundamental rights.
  - Intermediate: sex (exceedingly persuasive justification), nonmarital children.
  - Rational basis: all else; animus fails.
  - Purpose, not impact (Davis, Feeney, Arlington Heights).
- **Takings** (R-CON-077 to R-CON-081): physical (per se); total wipeout (Lucas, except nuisance); Penn Central factors; exactions (nexus + rough proportionality, including money and legislation); public use broad (Kelo); just compensation = FMV.
- **Contracts Clause** (R-CON-083): substantial impairment of existing contract → significant and legitimate purpose + reasonable means.
- **Ex post facto / attainder** (R-CON-084, R-CON-085): retroactive punitive laws; legislative punishment without trial.
- **Unconstitutional conditions** (R-CON-086): no benefit conditioned on waiving a right; program definition OK.
- **Second Amendment** (R-CON-065): text covers conduct → government must show historical tradition (Bruen, Rahimi).

## 6. First Amendment
- **Speech classify** (R-CON-092): content-based (topic, message, function or purpose) → strict; viewpoint → presumptively invalid; neutral → intermediate.
- **Strict scrutiny / compelled speech** (R-CON-093): necessary to a compelling interest, least restrictive means; no compelled messages.
- **TPM** (R-CON-094): content-neutral + narrowly tailored to a significant interest + ample alternatives.
- **Forums** (R-CON-095): traditional and designated → strict or TPM; limited and nonpublic → reasonable + viewpoint-neutral.
- **Government speech** (R-CON-096): may choose its message; check history, perception, control.
- **Unprotected** (R-CON-097 to R-CON-099): incitement (intent + imminent + likely); true threats (recklessness); fighting words (face-to-face); obscenity (Miller three prongs); child pornography.
- **Defamation** (R-CON-100): public figure → actual malice; private + public concern → negligence, actual malice for presumed or punitive damages.
- **Commercial** (R-CON-101): lawful + not misleading → substantial interest + directly advances + not more extensive than necessary.
- **Students** (R-CON-102): Tinker substantial disruption; Fraser lewd; Hazelwood school-sponsored; Morse drugs; Mahanoy off-campus.
- **Employees** (R-CON-103): citizen (not official duties) + public concern → Pickering balance → Mt. Healthy causation.
- **Licences / oaths** (R-CON-104): no denial for protected speech or membership; knowing membership + specific intent only.
- **Expressive conduct** (R-CON-105): Spence; O'Brien four parts.
- **Prior restraint** (R-CON-106): heavy presumption against; permits need objective standards; collateral bar for injunctions.
- **Vagueness / overbreadth** (R-CON-107, R-CON-108): fair notice and arbitrary enforcement; substantial overbreadth relative to legitimate sweep.
- **Press** (R-CON-109): no special privilege; no singling out; trials presumptively open.
- **Association** (R-CON-110, R-CON-111): disclosure → exacting scrutiny; forced inclusion → strict; campaign spending limits → strict; contribution limits → closely drawn.
- **Coercing platforms** (R-CON-112): no threats to intermediaries to suppress speech; curation is expression.
- **Free exercise** (R-CON-087, R-CON-088): neutral + generally applicable → rational basis; otherwise strict; no religious exclusions from neutral benefits; Yoder/Mahmoud parental rights; ministerial exception.
- **RFRA / RLUIPA** (R-CON-089): substantial burden → compelling interest + least restrictive means.
- **Establishment** (R-CON-090, R-CON-091): history and tradition + no coercion (Kennedy); no denominational preference (Larson); private-choice aid OK (Zelman).

## 7. Amendments XI–XV
- **Thirteenth** (R-CON-113): no slavery or involuntary servitude (physical or legal coercion) except criminal punishment; civic duties excepted.
- **Citizenship** (R-CON-114): born or naturalized in the U.S. and subject to its jurisdiction (Wong Kim Ark; Barbara 2026).
- **Fifteenth** (R-CON-115): no race-based denial of the vote; purpose required.
- **Prisoners** (R-CON-116): Turner reasonableness; strict scrutiny for race; RLUIPA.

## 8. Remedy
- **§ 1983 / relief** (R-CON-117): person acting under color of state law; Monell for cities; TRO/PI = likely success + irreparable harm + equities + public interest; no universal injunctions (CASA).
