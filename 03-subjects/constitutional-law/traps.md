# Constitutional Law — traps

Each trap names the mistake, the correct rule, and the rule ID.

## Justiciability
1. **Skipping standing on an essay.** Graders expect standing first, for **each** plaintiff and remedy, even when it is easy. → R-CON-008.
2. **"Taxpayer" standing for any spending.** Only Establishment Clause challenges to **congressional** taxing-and-spending appropriations (Flast); not executive spending, tax credits or property transfers. → R-CON-013.
3. **Treating ideological interest as injury.** A group "deeply committed" to an issue has no standing without a concrete injury; it can file an amicus brief. → R-CON-014.
4. **Graduation moots everything.** It moots the student's injunctive and declaratory claims, not damages (even nominal) or expungement. → R-CON-016.
5. **Voluntary cessation = moot.** The defendant must make it **absolutely clear** the conduct will not recur; suspending a rule during litigation is not enough. → R-CON-016.
6. **Capable of repetition for anyone.** The expectation of recurrence must be for the **same** plaintiff (outside class actions). → R-CON-016.
7. **Political question overuse.** Most foreign-affairs, election and congressional disputes are justiciable; the doctrine is narrow. → R-CON-017.
8. **Eleventh Amendment protects cities.** It does not; only states and arms of the state. → R-CON-005.
9. **Ex parte Young allows damages.** Only prospective injunctive or declaratory relief against officers for federal-law violations. → R-CON-006.

## Federal powers
10. **The Tenth Amendment invalidates any law touching state matters.** Only commandeering (or a law outside enumerated power) fails; generally applicable laws bind states (Garcia). → R-CON-045, R-CON-046.
11. **Aggregating noneconomic activity.** Wickard aggregation is for economic activity; Lopez and Morrison reject it for noneconomic conduct. → R-CON-022.
12. **Spending power limited to enumerated subjects.** It is not (Butler); Congress may spend for the general welfare. → R-CON-024.
13. **Forgetting the coercion limit.** Dole's mild-encouragement point vs. NFIB's "gun to the head." Analyse the share of existing funds at stake. → R-CON-024.
14. **§ 5 to reach private discrimination.** Section 5 reaches only state action (Morrison); use the Commerce Clause or the Thirteenth Amendment for private actors. → R-CON-027, R-CON-028.
15. **Humphrey's Executor as current law.** After *Trump v. Slaughter* (2026) [S-300], FTC-type commissioners are removable at will; but Morrison-type inferior officers and (per Cook) Federal Reserve governors are different. → R-CON-036.
16. **Pardons for state crimes.** The pardon power covers only federal offenses. → R-CON-037.
17. **Executive agreements override statutes.** They preempt **state** law, but not a prior federal statute. Treaties and statutes: last in time wins. → R-CON-034.
18. **Chevron deference.** Overruled (*Loper Bright* [VERIFY V-035]); courts decide statutory meaning independently. → R-CON-032.

## Federalism
19. **Corporations using Article IV P&I.** They cannot; they use the dormant Commerce Clause. → R-CON-053.
20. **Market participant defeats P&I.** It defeats the DCC claim, not a P&I claim (Camden). → R-CON-049, R-CON-053.
21. **Recreational activities as fundamental under P&I.** Higher hunting fees for nonresidents are valid (Baldwin). → R-CON-053.
22. **Applying Pike to a discriminatory law.** Discriminatory laws get the near-per-se rule; Pike is only for neutral laws. → R-CON-047, R-CON-048.
23. **Congress can authorize anything.** It can lift the DCC, but cannot authorize violations of equal protection or the right to travel. → R-CON-052.
24. **States can never regulate where federal law exists.** Absent express, field or conflict preemption, stricter state rules are fine. → R-CON-051.

## Individual rights
25. **No state action check.** Private schools, utilities and licensed clubs are not state actors merely because regulated, funded or licensed. → R-CON-054.
26. **Disparate impact = discrimination.** Equal protection requires discriminatory **purpose**; impact alone gets rational basis. → R-CON-067.
27. **Wrong burden under intermediate scrutiny.** The **government** must justify sex classifications. → R-CON-066, R-CON-071.
28. **Alienage always strict.** State political-function jobs (police, teachers, probation officers) get rational basis; federal alienage rules get rational basis. → R-CON-070.
29. **Age or disability as quasi-suspect.** Both get rational basis (with animus review). → R-CON-074.
30. **Education or wealth as fundamental or suspect.** Neither is under federal law (Rodriguez), apart from Plyler's total-denial rule. → R-CON-076.
31. **Due process hearing without a property interest.** An at-will employee or a one-year contract teacher has no entitlement (Roth). → R-CON-057.
32. **Abortion as a fundamental right.** Not after *Dobbs*; rational basis. → R-CON-062.
33. **Contracts Clause against the federal government or for future contracts.** It limits **state** legislation impairing **existing** contracts. → R-CON-083.
34. **Ex post facto for civil laws.** Only punitive laws; sex-offender registration was held civil (Smith v. Doe). → R-CON-084.
35. **Diminution in value = taking.** Mere loss in value is usually not a taking; check Lucas (100%) and Penn Central factors. → R-CON-079.
36. **Nollan/Dolan only for land dedications by administrators.** Also money exactions (Koontz) and legislative conditions (Sheetz [VERIFY V-035]). → R-CON-080.
37. **Second Amendment tiers of scrutiny.** Bruen rejects means-end scrutiny: text, then historical tradition. → R-CON-065.

## First Amendment
38. **Lemon test.** Abandoned (*Kennedy v. Bremerton*); use history, tradition and coercion. → R-CON-090.
39. **Free exercise strict scrutiny for every burden.** Only if the law is not neutral or not generally applicable (or in the Yoder/Mahmoud and religious-exclusion settings); otherwise Smith rational basis. → R-CON-087, R-CON-088.
40. **RFRA against the states.** RFRA binds only the federal government (Boerne); RLUIPA covers state and local land-use regulation and persons confined in state or local institutions (prisons, jails). → R-CON-089.
41. **Content-neutral = valid.** It still must be narrowly tailored and leave ample alternatives (McCullen; Ladue). → R-CON-094.
42. **Viewpoint discrimination allowed in nonpublic forums.** Never; nonpublic-forum rules must be reasonable **and** viewpoint-neutral. → R-CON-095.
43. **Offensive = fighting words.** Fighting words must be face-to-face personal insults likely to provoke violence (Cohen). → R-CON-098.
44. **True threats need only an objective test.** The speaker must at least be reckless (*Counterman* [VERIFY V-035]). → R-CON-098.
45. **Obscenity value judged locally.** The value prong uses a national reasonable-person standard (Pope). → R-CON-099.
46. **Overbreadth for commercial speech.** Not available (Bates). → R-CON-108.
47. **Vagueness vs. overbreadth confusion.** Vague = unclear; overbroad = clear but sweeps in protected speech. → R-CON-107, R-CON-108.
48. **Disobeying an injunction because it is unconstitutional.** The collateral bar rule requires obeying a court injunction until vacated; a void licensing statute may be ignored. → R-CON-106.
49. **Garcetti ignored.** Speech made pursuant to official duties is unprotected regardless of public concern. → R-CON-103.
50. **Tinker for school-sponsored newspapers.** Hazelwood (legitimate pedagogical concerns) governs school-sponsored speech. → R-CON-102.
51. **Membership alone punishable.** Requires knowing membership plus specific intent to further illegal aims. → R-CON-104, R-CON-110.
52. **"Garcia v. Ceballos."** The public-employee case is *Garcetti v. Ceballos*, 547 U.S. 410 (2006); *Garcia v. San Antonio MTA* is the Tenth Amendment case. → R-CON-103, R-CON-045.
53. **"The official must read the sign, so it is content-based."** Not the test: an on-premises/off-premises sign rule is location-based and content-neutral (*City of Austin*); ask whether the law singles out topic, idea or message. → R-CON-092.
54. **Citing a plurality as a holding.** *Hein*, *NFIB*'s commerce and coercion parts, *Kassel*, *Crawford*, *McCutcheon*, *Elrod* and *Town of Greece*'s coercion part had no majority opinion on the point; cite them for the result. → R-CON-013, R-CON-022, R-CON-024, R-CON-048, R-CON-075, R-CON-090, R-CON-103, R-CON-111.
