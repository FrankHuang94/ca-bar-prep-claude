# Constitutional Law — MBE rules and patterns

## 1. NCBE's assumptions for this subject

- **"Constitution" means the federal Constitution.** NCBE's outline note says that "Constitution," "constitutional" and "unconstitutional" refer to the federal Constitution unless the question indicates otherwise [S-040]. The exact wording is approximate [VERIFY V-053].
- **Distribution:** about half of the questions come from **IV Individual rights**, and about half from **I Judicial review, II Separation of powers and III Federalism** together [S-040]. With 25 scored questions, that is roughly 12–13 items from IV and 12–13 from I–III (see `05-mbe/subject-weighting.md`).
- **Current law.** The MBE tests generally accepted principles. NCBE has no stated lag rule for new decisions [VERIFY V-027]. Items are written well in advance, so older items may assume pre-2022 law. Apply current law when a question clearly turns on a changed point, such as Lemon, Chevron, Humphrey's Executor or Roe.

## 2. How the MBE asks questions

| Stem type | What it wants | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| "Is the statute constitutional?" with four reasons | The **correct reason** as well as the result | Eliminate answers with the right result but the wrong test or wrong burden |
| "The strongest argument in support of / against the law" | The doctrine most likely to win, not the most dramatic | Prefer the specific clause that fits the facts (e.g., the Commerce Clause over § 5 for private discrimination) |
| "Which standard of review applies?" | Tier plus burden | Identify classification or right first |
| "The court should dismiss because..." | A justiciability defect | Check standing, ripeness, mootness, political question, Eleventh Amendment in that order |
| "Which constitutional provision provides the best basis?" | The provision whose elements are all met | Article IV P&I needs an individual plaintiff; the DCC can be raised by corporations |

## 3. Black-letter rules the MBE favours

### Category I — Judicial review (outline I)
- **Standing elements** with fine distinctions: a generalized grievance as a citizen or taxpayer fails; Flast is limited to Establishment Clause challenges to congressional spending (R-CON-013); third-party standing for vendors and doctors (R-CON-011); associational standing (R-CON-012).
- **Mootness exceptions**: capable of repetition yet evading review (e.g., pregnancy, elections); voluntary cessation (R-CON-016).
- **Ripeness**: pre-enforcement challenge with a credible threat of prosecution (R-CON-015).
- **Eleventh Amendment**: no private damages suits against states in federal court, even under federal law, unless abrogated under § 5; officers can be enjoined (Ex parte Young); cities and counties are not protected (R-CON-005, R-CON-006).
- **Congress's control of jurisdiction**: Exceptions Clause power, but no dictating results in pending cases or reopening final judgments (R-CON-003, R-CON-004).
- **Adequate and independent state grounds** (R-CON-018).
- **Political questions**: impeachment procedures, the Guarantee Clause and partisan gerrymandering are nonjusticiable; most other disputes are justiciable (R-CON-017).

### Category II — Separation of powers (outline II)
- **Commerce power**: aggregation for economic activity (Wickard, Raich); no aggregation for noneconomic activity (Lopez, Morrison) (R-CON-021, R-CON-022).
- **Spending conditions**: Dole's factors and NFIB coercion (R-CON-024).
- **Taxing power**: a payment can be a tax even if it also discourages conduct (R-CON-023).
- **§ 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment**: congruence and proportionality; no reach to private conduct (R-CON-027). The **Thirteenth Amendment** reaches private racial discrimination (R-CON-028).
- **Youngstown categories** (R-CON-031); pardon power limited to federal crimes (R-CON-037).
- **Appointments**: Congress cannot appoint officers who exercise executive power; inferior officers may be appointed by heads of departments or courts (R-CON-035).
- **Removal**: at-will removal of principal executive officers; Congress cannot reserve removal for itself (R-CON-036). After *Trump v. Slaughter* (2026) [VERIFY V-034], an answer relying on *Humphrey's Executor* to protect an FTC-type commissioner is wrong for current law.
- **Legislative veto** (Chadha) and **line-item veto** (Clinton v. City of New York) are invalid (R-CON-038, R-CON-039).
- **Nondelegation**: an intelligible principle saves the delegation (R-CON-040).
- **Immunities**: Speech or Debate covers floor speeches but not press releases; presidential civil immunity covers official acts only (R-CON-041 to R-CON-043).

### Category III — Federalism (outline III)
- **Dormant Commerce Clause** (very frequent): facial discrimination → virtually per se invalid; neutral → Pike; market-participant exception; congressional authorization (R-CON-047 to R-CON-049, R-CON-052).
- **Preemption**: express, field and conflict; states may set stricter standards absent conflict or field occupation (R-CON-051).
- **Anti-commandeering** vs. generally applicable laws (Printz/New York vs. Garcia) (R-CON-045, R-CON-046).
- **Intergovernmental immunity**: no state tax or regulation of federal operations; nondiscriminatory taxes on contractors are valid (R-CON-044).

### Category IV — Individual rights (outline IV)
- **State action**: public function and entanglement; regulation or funding alone is not enough (R-CON-054, R-CON-055).
- **Equal protection tiers** and the purpose requirement (R-CON-066 to R-CON-074). The MBE loves alienage (state vs. federal; political-function exception), sex (intermediate, exceedingly persuasive justification), and rational basis (the law survives).
- **Fundamental rights**: travel, vote, marriage, family, parental rights; abortion is no longer fundamental (R-CON-060 to R-CON-064, R-CON-075).
- **Procedural due process**: property interest (Roth/Sindermann) and Mathews balancing (R-CON-057, R-CON-058).
- **Takings**: Loretto, Lucas, Penn Central, Nollan/Dolan (R-CON-077 to R-CON-080).
- **Contracts Clause** (state laws, existing contracts) and **ex post facto** (criminal only) (R-CON-083, R-CON-084).
- **Article IV P&I**: individuals only, livelihood-type rights, substantial-reason test (R-CON-053).
- **Free speech**: content-based vs. neutral; forums; unprotected categories with precise elements (Brandenburg, true threats, fighting words, Miller); commercial speech (Central Hudson); prior restraint, vagueness, overbreadth (R-CON-092 to R-CON-108).
- **Religion**: Smith/Lukumi; history-and-tradition and coercion for establishment (R-CON-087 to R-CON-091).
- **Association**: membership penalties need knowledge plus specific intent; disclosure needs exacting scrutiny (R-CON-110).

## 4. Recurring MBE fact patterns

| Pattern | Likely answer | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| State bans import of out-of-state garbage or requires local processing | Invalid under the DCC (discrimination; no nondiscriminatory alternative shown) | R-CON-047 |
| State-owned cement plant sells only to residents in a shortage | Valid: market participant | R-CON-049 |
| City requires contractors on city-funded projects to hire 40% city residents; out-of-state worker sues | DCC claim fails (market participant), but **Article IV P&I** may succeed | R-CON-049, R-CON-053 |
| Corporation challenges a law discriminating against nonresidents under P&I | Fails: corporations are not "citizens" for P&I; use the DCC | R-CON-053 |
| Federal statute requires state officials to enforce a federal program | Invalid: commandeering | R-CON-046 |
| Federal minimum wage applied to state employees | Valid: generally applicable law (Garcia) | R-CON-045 |
| Federal grant conditioned on a state drinking age, 5% of highway funds | Valid (Dole) | R-CON-024 |
| Private citizen sues a state for damages in federal court under a Commerce Clause statute | Barred by the Eleventh Amendment (Seminole Tribe) | R-CON-005 |
| Taxpayer challenges federal spending for a nonreligious program | No standing | R-CON-013 |
| Plaintiff's dispute ended but recurs quickly (pregnancy, election) | Not moot: capable of repetition, yet evading review | R-CON-016 |
| Senate tries an impeachment using a committee | Nonjusticiable political question | R-CON-017 |
| State law requiring a citizen police force or citizen public-school teachers | Valid under rational basis (political-function exception) | R-CON-070 |
| State law barring noncitizens from welfare or civil-service jobs | Invalid (strict scrutiny) | R-CON-070 |
| Facially neutral test with racially disparate results | Rational basis unless discriminatory purpose is proven | R-CON-067 |
| Teacher fired without a hearing; one-year contract, no tenure | No property interest: no hearing required | R-CON-057 |
| Speaker urges a crowd to "go now and burn the courthouse" | Incitement: unprotected | R-CON-097 |
| City bans all leafletting on public sidewalks | Invalid: not narrowly tailored; leafletting in a traditional public forum | R-CON-094 |
| Airport terminal bans solicitation of money | Valid: nonpublic forum, reasonable and viewpoint-neutral | R-CON-095 |
| School suspends a student for an armband protest without disruption | Invalid (Tinker) | R-CON-102 |
| Prosecutor fired over an internal memo written as part of the job | No First Amendment protection (Garcetti) | R-CON-103 |
| Statute retroactively increases the penalty for a past crime | Ex post facto | R-CON-084 |
| State law extends deadlines on existing private mortgages in an emergency | Valid Contracts Clause exercise if reasonable and temporary (Blaisdell) | R-CON-083 |
| Regulation leaves land with no economically beneficial use | Per se taking (Lucas), absent a nuisance exception | R-CON-079 |
| Permit conditioned on dedicating a bike path unrelated in degree to the impact | Taking unless rough proportionality is shown (Dolan) | R-CON-080 |
| Ordinance targets a religion's ritual animal killing while exempting hunting | Strict scrutiny; invalid (Lukumi) | R-CON-087 |
| Generally applicable drug law with no religious exemption | Valid under Smith (rational basis) | R-CON-087 |
| Prayer led by clergy at a public-school graduation | Establishment Clause violation (coercion) | R-CON-090 |
| Opening prayer at a town-council meeting | Valid by historical tradition | R-CON-090 |

## 5. Distractor families to watch

1. **Wrong tier.** An answer applies strict scrutiny to an age or disability classification, or rational basis to a sex classification.
2. **Wrong burden.** "The challenger must prove the law is not substantially related..." is wrong; under intermediate scrutiny the **government** bears the burden.
3. **Right result, wrong reason.** "Valid, because Congress has plenary power over interstate commerce" may be true in result but wrong if the real basis is the spending power or § 5.
4. **Tenth Amendment as a trump card.** The Tenth Amendment does not invalidate a law within an enumerated power unless it commandeers.
5. **§ 5 for private conduct.** Section 5 cannot reach purely private discrimination; the Commerce Clause or the Thirteenth Amendment can.
6. **P&I for corporations.** Corporations cannot invoke Article IV P&I.
7. **Lemon test.** An answer that applies the three-part Lemon test reflects pre-2022 law.
8. **"Due process requires a hearing" without a property interest.** Check for entitlement first (Roth).
9. **"Overbroad" vs. "vague."** Overbreadth means the law reaches substantial protected speech; vagueness means people cannot tell what is prohibited.
10. **Political question overuse.** Most disputes involving Congress or the President are justiciable.
