# Constitutional Law — essay templates

**General shape (all templates).**
- Top-level headings follow the **call**: one per question asked, or one per plaintiff or challenged law.
- Under each, put **threshold issues first**, then the merits, then the remedy if asked.
- Every issue gets: a short rule → specific facts on both sides → a hedged conclusion ("likely").
- Timing: about 15 minutes to outline, 45 minutes to write (`09-exam-strategy/time-management.md`).

The templates are informed by the California Con Law essays indexed in Phase 3 (`06-past-exams/index.csv`) [VERIFY V-071]. They describe structure only; no State Bar text is reproduced.

---

## Template 1 — Justiciability opener (use in nearly every Con Law essay)

Seen in: Jul 2019 (church TRO), Jul 2022 (mootness after graduation), Feb 2023 (intervenor), Feb 2024 (three plaintiffs).

```
I. [Plaintiff A]
   A. Standing
      1. Injury in fact — [specific harm: fine, lost sales, suspension, chilled practice]
      2. Causation — [defendant's rule caused it; no independent third party]
      3. Redressability — [injunction / declaration / damages would fix it]
      (If organization: Hunt test. If taxpayer: Flast limits. If intervenor: own standing.)
   B. Ripeness — [enforced yet? credible threat? fitness + hardship]
   C. Mootness — [what changed? exceptions: repetition/evading review, voluntary cessation, damages]
   D. [Eleventh Amendment if a state is a defendant → sue the officer; prospective relief]
   Conclusion: A may proceed on [claims/remedies].
```
**Paragraph pattern (standing):** "To have standing, a plaintiff must show an injury in fact that is concrete and particularized and actual or imminent, fairly traceable to the defendant, and likely to be redressed by a favorable decision (R-CON-008). Here, [facts]. Therefore, A likely has standing."

## Template 2 — Federal statute: power and Tenth Amendment

Seen in: Feb 2023 (federal physical-education funding conditions).

```
I. Source of congressional power
   A. Spending Clause — Dole factors
      1. General welfare
      2. Unambiguous condition
      3. Related to federal interest in the program
      4. No independent constitutional bar
      5. Not coercive (Dole 5% vs. NFIB "gun to the head")
   B. Alternative: Commerce Clause (three categories; economic? aggregation? Lopez/Morrison factors)
   C. Alternative: Taxing power (revenue? penalty?)
II. Tenth Amendment / anti-commandeering
   — Does the act order states to legislate or officers to enforce? Or is it a condition the state may refuse?
III. Other limits (individual rights) if raised
IV. Standing of any other party (e.g., intervenor) — Template 1
```

## Template 3 — State law burdening interstate business (multi-plaintiff)

Seen in: Jul 2018 (state-owned railroad favouring in-state shippers), Feb 2024 (state organic act and purchasing preference), Jul 2025 (P&I in the call).

```
I. [Plaintiff 1 — out-of-state corporation]
   A. Standing
   B. Preemption (if any federal law is mentioned)
   C. Dormant Commerce Clause
      1. Discriminatory on its face, purpose or effect? → virtually per se invalid
         (legitimate purpose + no nondiscriminatory alternative?)
      2. Exceptions — market participant / public entity / congressional authorization
      3. If neutral → Pike balancing
   D. Article IV P&I — corporation cannot invoke it (one sentence)
   E. Equal protection — economic classification → rational basis (state action first if needed)
II. [Plaintiff 2 — out-of-state individual]
   A. Standing  B. DCC (short; refer up)  C. Article IV P&I (full: fundamental right? substantial reason? substantial relationship? less restrictive means?)
   D. Fourteenth Amendment privileges or immunities (only for new residents / travel)
III. [Plaintiff 3 — in-state business harmed by the law]
   A. Standing  B. Due process / equal protection (rational basis)  C. Takings if property value destroyed
```
**Tip:** state who can raise each clause (corporations: DCC yes, P&I no).

## Template 4 — Free speech

Seen in: Feb 2012 (city refuses a flyer on its community board), Jul 2022 (student symbol rule; vagueness; content-based), Feb 2018 (inmate's religious book).

```
I. Threshold — standing / mootness (Template 1); state action
II. Is it protected speech?
   A. Speech or expressive conduct (Spence; O'Brien if conduct)
   B. Unprotected category claimed by government? (incitement, true threat, fighting words, obscenity) → elements
III. Setting
   A. Forum: traditional / designated / limited / nonpublic (or government speech?)
   B. Special setting: school (Tinker family), employee (Pickering/Garcetti), commercial (Central Hudson), prison (Turner)
IV. Content-based or content-neutral (viewpoint?)
   A. Content-based → strict scrutiny: compelling interest; necessary; least restrictive means
   B. Content-neutral → TPM: significant interest; narrowly tailored; ample alternatives
   (Limited/nonpublic forum → reasonable + viewpoint-neutral)
V. Facial challenges
   A. Prior restraint (permit scheme, unbridled discretion)
   B. Vagueness (undefined terms)
   C. Overbreadth (substantial protected applications)
VI. Conclusion (+ remedy if asked)
```

## Template 5 — Religion

Seen in: Feb 2014 (religious group's First Amendment arguments), Feb 2018 (jail: Ten Commandments postings; denial of a religious book and sacramental tea), Jul 2019 (candle ban in churches).

```
I. Threshold — standing (Flast for taxpayers if spending); ripeness
II. Free Exercise
   A. Sincere religious belief; burden on practice
   B. Neutral? (text, targeting, history, hostility — Lukumi, Masterpiece)
   C. Generally applicable? (individualized exemptions — Fulton; comparable secular activity — Tandon)
   D. If either fails → strict scrutiny (compelling interest; least restrictive means) — apply to this claimant
   E. If neutral and generally applicable → rational basis (Smith)
   F. Special settings: prison (Turner) + RLUIPA; federal action + RFRA; school opt-outs (Mahmoud); exclusion from benefits (Carson)
III. Establishment Clause
   A. Test: historical practices and understandings; coercion (Kennedy; note Lemon formerly applied)
   B. Apply: prayer, display, funding, denominational preference (Larson → strict)
   C. Analogies: Lee/Santa Fe (coercion), Town of Greece (tradition), Stone/Van Orden (displays)
IV. Equal protection / free speech overlap (religious speech excluded from a forum = viewpoint discrimination)
V. Conclusion
```

## Template 6 — Equal protection and due process (Fourteenth Amendment calls)

Seen in: Jul 2013 (youth labor corps), Feb 2018, Feb 2024 (rational basis), Feb 2025 retest (teacher), Jul 2025 (due process, equal protection, privileges or immunities, Article IV P&I).

```
I. State action
II. Equal Protection
   A. Classification — facial / as applied / purpose (Davis, Arlington Heights)
   B. Level of scrutiny — with burden of proof
   C. Ends — [compelling / important / legitimate] interest: identify the state's actual goals
   D. Means — [narrowly tailored / substantially related / rationally related]: over- and under-inclusiveness; alternatives
   E. Conclusion
III. Substantive Due Process
   A. Right asserted (carefully described) — fundamental? (Glucksberg history; recognized precedents)
   B. Tier and application
IV. Procedural Due Process (if a person lost a job, benefit, licence, schooling)
   A. Protected interest — property (entitlement) or liberty
   B. Process due — Mathews factors; Loudermill/Goss benchmarks
V. Privileges and Immunities
   A. Fourteenth Amendment P or I — national citizenship rights only (travel: Saenz)
   B. Article IV P&I — individuals, fundamental rights, substantial reason
VI. Conclusion for each clause
```

## Template 7 — Takings (Con Law + Real Property crossover)

Seen in: Jul 2015 (zoning and takings) [VERIFY V-071].

```
I. Standing / ripeness (final decision by the regulator; Knick — no state-court exhaustion for suits against local governments; State defendant → state court (Will))
II. Is there a taking?
   A. Physical occupation or access (Loretto; Cedar Point) → per se
   B. Exaction (Nollan nexus; Dolan proportionality; Koontz money; Sheetz legislative)
   C. Total wipeout (Lucas; nuisance exception)
   D. Penn Central factors (economic impact; investment-backed expectations; character)
III. Public use (Kelo; Midkiff)
IV. Just compensation; temporary takings (First English); remedy
V. Related Real Property issues: zoning validity (variance, nonconforming use) — see `03-subjects/real-property/outline.md` (Phase 4)
```

## Template 8 — Con Law + Remedies (TRO, preliminary injunction, declaratory relief)

Seen in: Jul 2019 (church seeks a TRO, a preliminary injunction and a declaratory judgment against a candle ban).

```
I. Justiciability — standing; ripeness (pre-enforcement); Eleventh Amendment (sue officers)
II. Merits (likelihood of success) — run the right template (4, 5 or 6) concisely
III. Temporary Restraining Order
   A. Likelihood of success (from II)
   B. Irreparable harm before a hearing (loss of First Amendment freedoms, even briefly — Elrod)
   C. Balance of hardships
   D. Public interest
   E. Notice / ex parte requirements; bond
IV. Preliminary Injunction — same four factors (Winter), with notice and a hearing; preserve the status quo
V. Permanent injunction (if asked): actual success + inadequate legal remedy + hardships + public interest + feasibility
VI. Declaratory Relief — actual controversy (28 U.S.C. § 2201); useful because it settles legality before enforcement
VII. Scope of relief — parties only; no universal injunction (Trump v. CASA [VERIFY V-035]); class relief via Rule 23
```
Coordinate with the Remedies files (`03-subjects/remedies/rule-statements.md`, Phase 4) for the provisional-relief rules.
