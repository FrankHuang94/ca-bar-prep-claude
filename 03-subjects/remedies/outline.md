# Remedies — comprehensive outline

**What this file is.** The complete reference for Remedies. It follows the State Bar's Remedies essay outline, sections I–VI [S-091] [VERIFY V-024], plus three author-added topics the outline line items did not capture: provisional relief (TROs and preliminary injunctions), limits on damages, and punitive damages.
- Each topic gives the rules with authority, the splits, and how the topic is tested on California essays.
- Essay-ready wording lives in [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md); rule IDs (R-REM-###) point there.
- California points are flagged **CA note**. The full comparison table is in [`ca-distinctions.md`](ca-distinctions.md).
- Contract measures of damages are taught in Contracts ([`../contracts/outline.md`](../contracts/outline.md) §V). This outline cross-references `R-CTR-###` rather than repeating them.

**Two rules of the road.**
1. **Remedies is essay-only.** It is not on the MBE, but it is one of the most frequent essay subjects: **16 of 31** administrations (Feb 2012 – Jul 2026), often as a "what remedies are available?" add-on to Contracts (4×), Torts (3×), Constitutional Law, Business Associations or Real Property ([`../../06-past-exams/frequency-analysis.md`](../../06-past-exams/frequency-analysis.md)).
2. **Governing law.** Answer under general principles (common law, the Restatements, the UCC, federal law where the case is federal) unless the call asks for California law [S-077]. Add "In California, …" where California differs and the point matters to the facts.

Code = California codes; FRCP = Federal Rules of Civil Procedure; SP = specific performance; PI = preliminary injunction; TRO = temporary restraining order; BFP = bona fide purchaser; Π/Δ = plaintiff/defendant.

## I. Basic principles (legal vs. equitable remedies; classification of remedies)

### I.A Classification of remedies (R-REM-001)
| Family | What it measures or does | Legal examples | Equitable examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compensatory | Π's loss | Damages (general, special, nominal), punitive damages | Equitable compensation for breach of fiduciary duty |
| Restitutionary | Δ's unjust gain | Quasi-contract, quantum meruit, replevin, ejectment, legal rescission | Constructive trust, equitable lien, resulting trust, subrogation, accounting, equitable rescission |
| Coercive | Orders Δ to act or stop | — | Injunctions (TRO, preliminary, permanent), specific performance |
| Declaratory | States rights | Declaratory judgment (sui generis), nominal damages | Quiet title, reformation, cancellation |

- **Why the label matters.** (1) Equitable remedies require an **inadequate legal remedy** and are **discretionary**. (2) Equitable defenses (laches, unclean hands) apply mainly to equitable remedies. (3) Equity is enforced by **contempt**; legal judgments by execution. (4) Jury trial attaches to legal claims.
- **Authority:** R2d § 344 (expectation, reliance and restitution interests); Civ. Code § 3274 (compensation is the general remedy; specific and preventive relief only as the Code allows) and § 3281 (damages defined).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC law lists 承担民事责任的方式 (stop infringement, remove obstruction, restitution, compensation, specific performance, etc.) without a law/equity split; US law sorts the same tools into legal and equitable families with different prerequisites (approx.).

### I.B Law vs. equity: history and the adequacy requirement (R-REM-002)
- **History.** English common-law courts gave money judgments and recovery of land or chattels; the Chancellor's court of equity gave personal orders where the common law was inadequate. U.S. federal courts merged law and equity procedurally in 1938 (one civil action, FRCP 2); California merged them in the 19th century. The **substantive** distinction survives.
- **Adequacy requirement.** Equity acts only if the legal remedy is inadequate: unique property; damages speculative or hard to measure; continuing harm needing many suits; Δ insolvent (split) (Civ. Code § 3422 [S-458]; R2d §§ 359–360).
- **Discretion.** Even when inadequacy is shown, the court balances hardships, considers the public interest and checks defenses (*Weinberger v. Romero-Barcelo*, 456 U.S. 305 (1982); *eBay Inc. v. MercExchange, L.L.C.*, 547 U.S. 388 (2006)).
- **Equitable maxims** used as shorthand in answers: equity acts in personam; equity follows the law; he who seeks equity must do equity; he who comes into equity must come with clean hands; equity aids the vigilant (laches); equity will not suffer a wrong without a remedy.

### I.C Enforcement: execution vs. contempt (equity acts in personam) (R-REM-003)
- **Legal judgment:** Π is a judgment creditor; enforcement by writ of execution, levy and judgment liens. No priority over Δ's other unsecured creditors.
- **Equitable decree:** an order to Δ personally; disobedience is **contempt** (R-REM-078). A court with personal jurisdiction over Δ may order acts concerning property in another state (convey land), though it cannot itself transfer title to out-of-state land. FRCP 70 lets the court appoint another to do the act or divest title within its jurisdiction.
- **Essay use.** Enforcement explains *why* Π wants equitable relief: a constructive trust gives the asset and priority over creditors; an injunction can be enforced by fines or jail.

### I.D Jury trial and procedure (R-REM-004)
- **Federal.** The Seventh Amendment preserves jury trial for legal claims. With mixed claims, common factual issues go to the jury first (*Beacon Theatres, Inc. v. Westover*, 359 U.S. 500 (1959); *Dairy Queen, Inc. v. Wood*, 369 U.S. 469 (1962)).
- **CA note:** the state jury right (Cal. Const., art. I, § 16) follows the historical law/equity line; equitable claims (e.g., promissory estoppel, even where Π seeks money: *C & K Engineering Contractors v. Amber Steel Co.* (1978) 23 Cal.3d 1 [S-1218]) are tried to the court.
- **Pleading in the alternative** is allowed (FRCP 8(d)); Π elects before judgment (I.E).

### I.E Election of remedies and no double recovery (R-REM-005)
- Π may plead inconsistent theories but **may not recover twice** for the same loss.
- **Affirm vs. disaffirm.** Expectation damages affirm the contract; rescission and restitution disaffirm it. Traditionally Π chose one.
- **Cumulative combinations.** Damages for past harm + injunction against future harm; specific performance + damages for delay; constructive trust on one asset + equitable lien on another.
- **CA note:** "A claim for damages is not inconsistent with a claim for relief based upon rescission"; the aggrieved party receives complete relief, including restitution and consequential damages, without duplicate items (Civ. Code § 1692 [S-462]; *Runyan v. Pacific Air Industries, Inc.* (1970) 2 Cal.3d 304 [S-462]). UCC § 2-721 similarly preserves damages after rescission for fraud.
- R2d § 378 (election among remedies).

### I.F How Remedies is tested on the California essay (R-REM-006)
- **Call types** (Phase 3 index [VERIFY V-071]):
  1. Open: "What remedies can Π obtain?" (often with "what defenses can Δ raise?").
  2. Named remedies: "Can Π obtain a TRO, a preliminary injunction and specific performance?" (Jul 2017 Q3); "TRO, PI and declaratory relief" (Jul 2019 Q2).
  3. Asset by asset: "Who prevails as to each of four assets?" (Jul 2026 Q3).
  4. Crossover add-on: liability in Contracts, Torts, Con Law or BA, then remedies.
- **Master order** for each claim: legal remedies (compensatory → limits → nominal → punitive) → restitution (legal → equitable) → equitable remedies (TRO/PI → permanent injunction or SP → reformation/rescission) → defenses → best remedy.
- **Grader expectations.** Answer the call in its order; a rule then the facts for each element; a conclusion per remedy (`00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §§ 4–5).

## II. Legal remedies (damages, including contract, real-property and tort measures)

### II.A Compensatory damages: purpose, general and special damages (R-REM-007)
- **Purpose.** Compensation for the detriment caused (Civ. Code §§ 3281–3282): in tort, restore Π to the pre-wrong position; in contract, put Π where performance would have.
- **General damages** flow naturally from the wrong (pain and suffering; market-value loss). **Special (consequential) damages** depend on Π's circumstances (medical bills, lost profits, lost wages) and must be specifically pleaded and proven.
- **Measures by statute (CA):**
  - contract: "all the detriment proximately caused thereby, or which, in the ordinary course of things, would be likely to result therefrom" (Civ. Code § 3300);
  - tort: "all the detriment proximately caused thereby, whether it could have been anticipated or not" (Civ. Code § 3333 [S-469]);
  - no recovery beyond what full performance would have yielded (Civ. Code § 3358); damages must be reasonable (§ 3359).
- **Future detriment** is recoverable if certain to result (Civ. Code § 3283); future economic loss is discounted to present value (R-REM-013).

### II.B Limits on damages: causation, foreseeability, certainty, mitigation (R-REM-008 to R-REM-013)
> These four limits appear in every damages answer. Give each item of damages a label (general / consequential), then one or two sentences per limit using the facts.

#### II.B.1 Causation (R-REM-008)
- Actual cause (but-for; substantial factor) and, in tort, proximate cause. Superseding causes cut off liability. The eggshell-plaintiff rule makes Δ liable for the full extent of physical injury.

#### II.B.2 Foreseeability (R-REM-009)
| | Contract | Tort |
|---|---|---|
| Test | Foreseeable at **contracting**: natural consequences, or special circumstances known to Δ (*Hadley v. Baxendale* (1854) 9 Ex. 341) | Proximate cause; Π recovers detriment "whether it could have been anticipated or not" (Civ. Code § 3333 [S-469]) |
| Authority | R2d § 351; UCC § 2-715(2)(a); Civ. Code § 3300; R-CTR-092 | Civ. Code § 3333; Torts proximate-cause rules |
| Extra limit | R2d § 351(3): court may limit to avoid disproportionate compensation | Intentional tortfeasors bear broader consequences |

#### II.B.3 Certainty (R-REM-010)
- Fact of loss certain; amount reasonably estimable (R2d § 352; Civ. Code § 3301).
- **Lost profits.** Established business: use history. New business: traditional bar, modern evidentiary hurdle — comparables, market data, expert testimony; California courts admit expert lost-profit projections only if the expert's method is reliable and the trial court, as gatekeeper, excludes speculative projections (*Sargon Enterprises, Inc. v. University of Southern California* (2012) 55 Cal.4th 747) [S-1213].
- **Fallbacks:** reliance damages (R-CTR-107) or restitution (III).

#### II.B.4 Mitigation / avoidable consequences (R-REM-011)
- No recovery for losses avoidable by reasonable effort without undue risk or burden; Δ bears the burden; reasonable mitigation costs are recoverable (R2d § 350; R2d Torts § 918 [VERIFY V-363]; R-CTR-095).
- Applications: stop work after repudiation (*Rockingham County v. Luten Bridge Co.*, 35 F.2d 301 (4th Cir. 1929)); seek comparable employment (*Parker v. Twentieth Century-Fox Film Corp.* (1970) 3 Cal.3d 176); cover for goods; seek medical treatment after injury; landlords must mitigate (majority and California).

#### II.B.5 Collateral source and present value (R-REM-012, R-REM-013)
- Benefits from sources independent of Δ (insurance, gifts) do not reduce damages (*Helfend v. Southern Cal. Rapid Transit Dist.* (1970) 2 Cal.3d 1 [S-472]; R2d Torts § 920A [VERIFY V-363]).
- **CA note:** recovery for past medical expenses is limited to amounts actually paid or incurred (*Howell v. Hamilton Meats & Provisions, Inc.* (2011) 52 Cal.4th 541 [S-472]).
- Future economic losses are reduced to present value.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Civil Code has parallel contract limits — 可预见性 (foreseeability) and 减损 (mitigation) — but no Hadley "special circumstances communicated" formula or new-business rule (approx.).

### II.C Contract damages (cross-reference Contracts) (R-REM-014, R-REM-017)
- **Expectation** (R-CTR-089): loss in value + incidental + consequential − cost avoided − loss avoided.
- **By contract type** (table in Contracts outline §V.A): goods (UCC §§ 2-706, 2-708, 2-709, 2-710 for sellers; 2-712, 2-713, 2-714, 2-715 for buyers; R-CTR-099 to R-CTR-103); construction (R-CTR-090); employment (R-CTR-091).
- **Reliance** (R-CTR-107) and **restitution** (R-CTR-108; breaching party R-CTR-109).
- **Liquidated damages** (R-REM-017; R-CTR-096): reasonable forecast + difficult to estimate; penalties void. **CA note:** Civ. Code § 1671(b) presumes validity in most contracts; (c)–(d) reverse it for consumer contracts and residential leases [S-200].
- **Limits on contract recovery:** no punitive damages (R2d § 355; R-REM-031); emotional distress only for bodily harm or emotionally sensitive contracts (R2d § 353; *Erlich v. Menezes* (1999) 21 Cal.4th 543; R-REM-025); fees only by contract or statute (Civ. Code § 1717 reciprocity; R-REM-033).
- **How tested.** Contracts + Remedies is the second most common crossover (4 essays). Graders reward computing the figure from the facts.

### II.D Real-property damages (land-sale contracts; harm to land; trespass and encroachment)
#### II.D.1 Land-sale contracts (R-REM-015, R-REM-016)
| Breaching party | Non-breaching party recovers | California |
|---|---|---|
| Seller refuses to convey | Market value at breach − contract price, + deposit, + incidental/consequential (American rule). English rule: good-faith seller with title defect liable only for deposit + expenses (*Flureau v. Thornhill* (1776)) | Civ. Code § 3306: price paid, title/document expenses, difference between price and value at breach, expenses preparing to enter, consequential damages, interest [S-461] [S-1221] |
| Buyer refuses to buy | Contract price − market value at breach, + incidental (resale costs); credit the deposit | Civ. Code § 3307: excess of amount due over value at breach, plus consequential damages and interest [S-461] [S-1221]; deposit forfeiture limited by § 1671 and *Freedman* [S-210] |
- **Alternative:** specific performance (IV.C.2); the § 3387 presumption makes it the usual buyer's remedy in California.

#### II.D.2 Physical harm to land (R-REM-019)
- Diminution in market value **or** reasonable cost of restoration, usually the lesser (R2d Torts § 929 [VERIFY V-363]), plus loss of use and, for occupants, discomfort and annoyance.
- **Personal-reason exception:** restoration cost exceeding diminution may be awarded if the owner has a personal reason to restore and the cost is reasonable (*Heninger v. Dunn* (1980) 101 Cal.App.3d 858 [S-477]).

#### II.D.3 Trespass, encroachment and trees (R-REM-020)
- Trespass is actionable per se → **nominal damages** at least; actual harm; **mesne profits** (rental value) for wrongful occupation (Civ. Code § 3334 [VERIFY V-357]).
- Permanent encroachment left in place: value of land taken or diminution of the parcel; often coupled with an equitable easement (IV.A.6).
- **CA note:** wrongful injury to trees or timber on another's land: damages **doubled** as a minimum (mandatory) and **trebled** in the court's discretion for a willful and malicious trespass; only **doubled** for a casual or involuntary trespass or one made with probable cause to believe the land was Δ's own (Civ. Code § 3346 [S-460]; CCP § 733; CACI 2002–2003; *Gould v. Madonna* (1970) 5 Cal.App.3d 404 [S-1206] [S-1207]); requires an actual trespass on another's land and does not reach negligently spread fires (*Scholes v. Lambirth Trucking Co.* (2020) [S-460]; *Russell v. Man* (2020) [S-1208]).

### II.E Tort damages (R-REM-018 to R-REM-025)
#### II.E.1 Personal injury (R-REM-018)
- **Economic:** past and future medical costs, lost earnings and earning capacity, out-of-pocket costs. **Non-economic:** pain and suffering, emotional distress, disfigurement, loss of enjoyment.
- Eggshell plaintiff; mitigation (seek treatment); comparative fault reduces recovery.
- **CA note:** liability for non-economic damages is several only, in proportion to fault (Civ. Code § 1431.2, Proposition 51) [VERIFY V-363]; medical damages limited to amounts paid (*Howell*) [S-472].

#### II.E.2 Personal property (R-REM-021, R-REM-022)
| Wrong | Measure | Alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| Conversion | Fair market value at time and place of conversion + interest (forced sale) (R2d Torts § 927 [VERIFY V-363]) | Replevin (III.B); restitution of Δ's sale price or profits (waiver of tort) |
| Trespass to chattels / damage | Cost of repair or diminution + loss of use | If repair > value, treat as total loss |
| **CA note** | Civ. Code § 3336: value at conversion + interest, **or** an amount sufficient to indemnify for the proximate loss, **plus** fair compensation for time and money properly spent pursuing the property [S-468] [VERIFY V-357]; peculiar value to owner recoverable against willful wrongdoer or one with notice (Civ. Code § 3355) [VERIFY V-357] | |

#### II.E.3 Fraud and misrepresentation (R-REM-023)
| Measure | Formula | Where used |
|---|---|---|
| Out-of-pocket | Value given − value received (+ consequential) | Negligent misrepresentation; **California** for fraud in the purchase, sale or exchange of property (Civ. Code § 3343; *Alliance Mortgage Co. v. Rothwell* (1995) 10 Cal.4th 1226 [S-453]) |
| Benefit of the bargain | Value as represented − value received (+ consequential) | Majority for intentional fraud in a bargain (R2d Torts § 549 [VERIFY V-363]); in California, some courts allow it against a **fiduciary** (split: *Fragale v. Faulkner* (2003) 110 Cal.App.4th 229 [S-1201] yes; *Hensley v. McSweeney* (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 1073 [S-1202] no) [S-454] |
| Punitive | R-REM-026 to R-REM-030 | Intentional fraud; Civ. Code § 3294 "fraud" |
| Alternative | Rescission + restitution (+ consequential damages in CA, § 1692) | Where Π wants out of the deal (III.B, IV.E) |
- **How tested.** Jul 2024 Q5 (fraud in sale of autographed baseballs: damages and equitable remedies) and Feb 2017 Q2 (condo sale) [VERIFY V-071]: compare both damages measures with numbers, then rescission/restitution, then punitive damages.

#### II.E.4 Nuisance (R-REM-024)
- **Permanent** nuisance: one action, diminution in market value. **Continuing** (abatable): past damages (loss of use, discomfort, annoyance) + later suits or injunction (R2d Torts §§ 929–930 [VERIFY V-363]).
- Injunction analysis in IV.A.6.

#### II.E.5 Emotional distress (R-REM-025)
- Parasitic to intentional torts; IIED/NIED within Torts limits; annoyance in trespass and nuisance. Contract: only bodily harm or emotionally sensitive contracts (R2d § 353; *Erlich*).

### II.F Punitive damages (R-REM-026 to R-REM-031)
#### II.F.1 General standard (R-REM-026)
- To punish and deter malicious, willful and wanton, or recklessly indifferent tortious conduct (R2d Torts § 908 [VERIFY V-363]). Not for negligence; **not for breach of contract** (R2d § 355; R-REM-031) unless an independent tort exists.
- Factors for amount: reprehensibility, harm, Δ's wealth.

#### II.F.2 Due-process limits (R-REM-027)
- *BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore*, 517 U.S. 559 (1996): three guideposts — reprehensibility (most important), ratio to actual or potential harm, comparable civil penalties.
- *State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co. v. Campbell*, 538 U.S. 408 (2003): few awards above a single-digit ratio satisfy due process; with substantial compensatory damages, ~1:1 may be the limit; reprehensibility factors (physical harm; indifference to health or safety; financially vulnerable target; repetition; intentional malice, trickery or deceit); no punishment for dissimilar or out-of-state conduct.
- *Philip Morris USA v. Williams*, 549 U.S. 346 (2007): no direct punishment for harm to non-parties. *Pacific Mut. Life Ins. Co. v. Haslip*, 499 U.S. 1 (1991): jury guidance and post-verdict review. *Exxon Shipping Co. v. Baker*, 554 U.S. 471 (2008): 1:1 ceiling under federal maritime law (not constitutional).

#### II.F.3 California (R-REM-028 to R-REM-030)
- **Civ. Code § 3294(a):** non-contract obligation; **clear and convincing evidence** of **oppression, fraud or malice** [S-451].
- **§ 3294(c) definitions** [S-451] [S-1200]:
  - *malice* — conduct intended to injure, or despicable conduct carried on with willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others;
  - *oppression* — despicable conduct subjecting a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person's rights;
  - *fraud* — intentional misrepresentation, deceit or concealment of a material fact known to Δ, intended to deprive a person of property or legal rights or otherwise cause injury.
- **Employers (§ 3294(b)):** advance knowledge and conscious disregard, authorization or ratification, or personal guilt — for a corporation, by an officer, director or **managing agent** (one with substantial discretionary authority over decisions that ultimately determine corporate policy: *White v. Ultramar, Inc.* (1999) 21 Cal.4th 563 [S-452]).
- **Predicates:** actual damages (*Cheung v. Daley* (1995) 35 Cal.App.4th 1673 [S-470]; *Kizer v. County of San Mateo* (1991) 53 Cal.3d 139; *Mother Cobb's Chicken Turnovers, Inc. v. Fox* (1937) 10 Cal.2d 203 [S-1209]); a small or nominal award can suffice where real harm was hard to measure (*Kluge v. O'Gara* (1964) 227 Cal.App.2d 207 [S-1210]); Π must prove Δ's **financial condition** (*Adams v. Murakami* (1991) 54 Cal.3d 105 [S-464]); bifurcated financial-condition evidence on Δ's request (Civ. Code § 3295(d)); pretrial discovery of it only on court order (§ 3295(c)) [S-1200].

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC 惩罚性赔偿 exists only in listed statutory settings and is usually a multiple of loss; US punitive damages are a general tort remedy, bounded by due process (approx.).

### II.G Other monetary relief (R-REM-033, R-REM-034, R-REM-094)
- **Attorney's fees:** American rule; exceptions by contract (CA: Civ. Code § 1717 reciprocity), statute (42 U.S.C. § 1988; CCP § 1021.5 private attorney general), common fund, bad-faith litigation; CCP § 1021.
- **Prejudgment interest:** liquidated sums from vesting (Civ. Code § 3287); discretionary in tort and fraud/malice cases (Civ. Code § 3288) [VERIFY V-359]; conversion carries interest from conversion (§ 3336) [S-468].
- **Statutory multiples:** trees and timber (§ 3346 [S-460]); other statutes (e.g., antitrust treble damages) are outside the bar outline.
- **Nominal damages:** VI.D.

### II.H Economic loss rule (tort vs. contract recovery) (R-REM-032)
- Purely economic loss from a contracting party's failure to perform is recoverable in contract only. Tort remains available for an **independent duty** (fraud in the inducement; personal injury or damage to other property).
- **CA note:** *Robinson Helicopter Co., Inc. v. Dana Corp.* (2004) 34 Cal.4th 979 (affirmative misrepresentations exposing Π to independent liability); *Rattagan v. Uber Technologies, Inc.* (2024) 17 Cal.5th 1 [S-132] (fraudulent concealment during performance actionable if the tort's elements exist independently of the contract and Π faced a risk of harm beyond the bargain). Cross-reference R-CTR-117.
- **Why it matters in Remedies:** opening tort recovery opens § 3333 damages, emotional distress and **punitive damages**.

## III. Restitution (purpose of restitution; legal and equitable restitutionary remedies)

### III.A Purpose of restitution and measuring the benefit (R-REM-035 to R-REM-037)
- **Principle.** A person unjustly enriched at another's expense must make restitution (R3RUE § 1 [VERIFY V-350]). Restitution measures **Δ's gain**, not Π's loss.
- **Elements:** (1) benefit conferred on Δ; (2) at Π's expense; (3) unjust for Δ to retain it without paying.
- **When restitution beats damages:**
  - Δ's gain exceeds Π's loss (Δ resold Π's property at a profit; Δ used Π's machine and saved labor: *Olwell v. Nye & Nissen Co.*, 26 Wash.2d 282 (1946));
  - Π's loss is hard to prove;
  - no enforceable contract exists (statute of frauds, indefiniteness, illegality not in pari delicto);
  - Π wants specific property or priority over creditors (equitable restitution).
- **Measure depends on Δ's culpability (R-REM-036):**

| Δ | Measure | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Innocent recipient (mistake) | Reasonable value of the benefit, or the actual increase in Δ's wealth if that is more just (lower) | R2d § 371; R3RUE § 49 [VERIFY V-350] |
| Conscious wrongdoer / fiduciary in breach | All gain, including profits from the property ("disgorgement") | R3RUE § 51 [VERIFY V-350] |
| Contract setting | Reasonable value of performance or increase in Δ's wealth; split on capping at contract price | R2d §§ 371, 373; R-CTR-108 |

- **CA note:** Civ. Code §§ 2223–2224 make a wrongful detainer or one who gains a thing by fraud, accident, mistake, undue influence, violation of a trust or other wrongful act an "involuntary trustee" [S-463]. California appellate courts disagree whether "unjust enrichment" is a standalone cause of action; frame the claim as quasi-contract or constructive trust [VERIFY V-361].

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: 不当得利 (unjust enrichment) and 无因管理 (negotiorum gestio) cover similar ground, but PRC restitution is a personal claim; US equitable restitution can give a proprietary claim (approx.).

### III.B Legal restitutionary remedies: quasi-contract/unjust enrichment, quantum meruit, replevin, ejectment, legal rescission (R-REM-038 to R-REM-043)
#### III.B.1 Quasi-contract (implied-in-law contract) (R-REM-038)
- An obligation imposed by law to prevent unjust enrichment, not a real agreement. Elements: benefit conferred; Δ's knowledge and acceptance or retention; injustice of retention without payment; no enforceable contract on the subject.
- Typical settings: mistaken payments; benefits under an unenforceable or void contract; failed contracts (impracticability, frustration) (R2d §§ 375–377); emergency services.
- **Remedy:** money judgment for the value of the benefit. **Limit:** no priority over creditors; no claim to specific property.
- Distinguish **implied-in-fact** contracts (real agreement inferred from conduct; expectation measure).

#### III.B.2 Quantum meruit (R-REM-039)
- Reasonable value of services rendered at Δ's request or with Δ's knowledge, with both expecting payment.
- Used for: unenforceable contracts; no agreed price; Π's election of restitution after Δ's total breach (R-CTR-108); a breaching Π's recovery net of Δ's damages and capped at the pro rata contract price (R-CTR-109; *Britton v. Turner*, 6 N.H. 481 (1834)).

#### III.B.3 Limits: volunteers and officious intermeddlers (R-REM-040)
- No restitution for gifts or benefits forced on a person without a chance to decline. Exceptions: emergency services protecting life or health (by a professional who expects payment), performing another's duty to supply necessaries, mistaken payments and improvements.
- **CA note:** good-faith improvers of another's land have a statutory remedy (CCP § 871.1 et seq.) [VERIFY V-368].

#### III.B.4 Replevin (CA: claim and delivery) (R-REM-041)
- Legal action to recover a **specific chattel** wrongfully detained, plus damages for detention. Π must show a right to immediate possession.
- **Provisional writ:** pre-judgment seizure requires notice and hearing except in narrow cases (*Fuentes v. Shevin*, 407 U.S. 67 (1972)) and a bond. Enforced by the sheriff.
- **CA note:** "claim and delivery," CCP § 511.010 et seq.; the court issues a writ of possession after a hearing on a showing of the probable validity of Π's claim (§ 512.010) [S-475].
- **UCC:** buyer's replevin of identified goods it cannot cover (§ 2-716(3)).
- **Essay use:** best for a unique chattel still in Δ's hands (a painting, a family heirloom). If Δ sold it, switch to a constructive trust on the proceeds or conversion damages.

#### III.B.5 Ejectment (R-REM-042)
- Legal action to recover possession of land from one wrongfully in possession, with **mesne profits** (fair rental value) (Civ. Code § 3334 [VERIFY V-357]). Against tenants: unlawful detainer (CCP § 1159 et seq.). For an encroaching structure, a mandatory injunction is usually more practical.

#### III.B.6 Legal rescission (R-REM-043)
- At law, Π rescinds unilaterally by **notice** and **tender** of what it received, then sues to recover what it gave (money in quasi-contract; property by replevin or ejectment).
- **CA note:** since 1961 California has a unified procedure: rescission by prompt notice and restoration or offer to restore (Civ. Code § 1691), then an action for relief based on rescission, with consequential damages allowed (§ 1692) [S-462]; grounds include mistake, duress, menace, fraud, undue influence, failure of consideration and illegality (§ 1689).

### III.C Equitable restitutionary remedies: constructive and resulting trusts, equitable lien, equitable rescission, subrogation, indemnity, contribution, accounting (R-REM-044 to R-REM-054)
#### III.C.1 Constructive trust (R-REM-044)
- **Elements:** Δ holds **title** to specific property; obtained by **wrongful conduct** or unjust enrichment (fraud, theft/conversion, breach of fiduciary duty, mistake, undue influence); property is Π's or **traceable** to it; legal remedy inadequate (often shown by uniqueness, Δ's insolvency, or appreciation Π wants to capture; the traditional formulation — California cases list only specific property, Π's right to it, and Δ's wrongful acquisition or detention [S-1801], and the Restatement (Third) may not require it [VERIFY V-940]); no BFP.
- **Advantages:** Π gets the asset and all **appreciation and income**; **priority** over Δ's unsecured creditors; enforceable by contempt; may reach a donee.
- **Disadvantages:** if the asset fell in value, Π bears the loss (choose a lien); BFPs take free.
- **Authority:** Civ. Code §§ 2223–2224 [S-463]; R3RUE § 55 [VERIFY V-350].

#### III.C.2 Equitable lien (R-REM-045)
- A court-imposed **security interest** in specific property to secure Π's restitution claim where Π's money or property was used to acquire, improve or pay down debt on it.
- **Advantages:** Π forces a sale and can obtain a **deficiency judgment** if proceeds fall short; works for improvements to property Δ already owned. **Limit:** no share of appreciation beyond the amount secured.
- R3RUE § 56 [VERIFY V-350].

#### III.C.3 Choosing trust vs. lien (R-REM-046)
| Facts | Better remedy |
|---|---|
| Asset bought entirely with Π's money; value up | Constructive trust (asset + gain) |
| Asset bought entirely with Π's money; value down | Equitable lien + deficiency judgment |
| Π's money paid part of the price | Proportionate constructive trust (share) or lien |
| Π's money improved Δ's existing property / paid Δ's mortgage | Equitable lien (or subrogation to the mortgage) |
| Asset transferred to a donee | Constructive trust or lien follows it |
| Asset transferred to a BFP | Neither on the asset; trace into proceeds in Δ's hands |

#### III.C.4 Resulting trust (R-REM-050)
- Arises from presumed intent: (1) purchase-money resulting trust (A pays, title to B, no gift intended; gift presumed between close family); (2) failed or surplus express trust. Title holder must convey. Contrast the constructive trust, which rests on wrongdoing.

#### III.C.5 Equitable rescission and cancellation (R-REM-043, R-REM-077)
- Court decree rescinding the contract, ordering mutual restoration and, where needed, **cancellation** of instruments (deeds, notes) that cloud Π's title (Civ. Code § 3412 [VERIFY V-368]). Used where Π cannot simply rescind and sue at law (e.g., Π conveyed land and needs the deed set aside).
- **Bars:** affirmation after knowledge; unreasonable delay; BFP; unclean hands; inability to restore (courts may adjust with money).
- Full treatment in IV.E.

#### III.C.6 Subrogation (R-REM-051)
- One whose money paid another's debt (under compulsion, to protect its own interest, or because the money was wrongfully taken) steps into the creditor's shoes, including its **security and priority** (R3RUE § 57 [VERIFY V-350]).
- **Essay use:** embezzled funds used to pay off Δ's mortgage → Π subrogated to the bank's first lien (useful where Δ's equity in a homestead would otherwise be protected).

#### III.C.7 Indemnity and contribution (R-REM-052, R-REM-053)
- **Indemnity** shifts the whole loss to the party primarily responsible (express contract; implied/equitable for vicarious or secondary liability). **CA note:** comparative equitable indemnity among joint tortfeasors (*American Motorcycle Assn. v. Superior Court* (1978) 20 Cal.3d 578) [S-1214].
- **Contribution** lets a joint tortfeasor who paid more than its share recover the excess (pro rata or by comparative fault). **CA note:** CCP § 875 et seq.; a court-determined good-faith settlement bars other tortfeasors' claims against the settling tortfeasor for comparative contribution or partial/comparative indemnity (CCP § 877.6(c)) [S-1215].

#### III.C.8 Accounting (R-REM-054)
- Equitable order requiring a fiduciary (trustee, partner, agent) or a party with complex accounts to account for receipts and disgorge profits. Often paired with a constructive trust on profits.

### III.D Tracing, commingled funds and bona fide purchasers (R-REM-047 to R-REM-049)
#### III.D.1 Tracing through exchanges (R-REM-047)
- Follow Π's property through each substitution to its present product (R3RUE § 58 [VERIFY V-350]). Write the chain: "Π's $40,000 → Δ's checking account → $40,000 down payment on the condo."
- Tracing ends at a BFP or when the property is dissipated (spent on consumption).

#### III.D.2 Commingled funds (R-REM-048)
- **Presumption:** the wrongdoer spends its own money first.
- **Lowest intermediate balance rule:** Π's claim to the account cannot exceed the lowest balance between Π's deposit and the claim; later deposits of Δ's own money do not replenish it unless intended as restitution (R3RUE § 59 [S-465] [VERIFY V-350]).
- **Election:** if Δ bought an asset with withdrawals and then dissipated the rest, Π may claim the asset.
- **Multiple victims:** pro rata distribution is common.
- **Worked table (essay format):**

| Date | Event | Balance | Π's traceable share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Δ's own funds | $2,000 | $0 |
| 2 | Deposits $10,000 stolen from Π | $12,000 | $10,000 |
| 3 | Withdraws $9,000 (cruise) | $3,000 | $3,000 (Δ's $2,000 deemed spent first, then $7,000 of Π's) |
| 4 | Deposits $5,000 salary | $8,000 | $3,000 (lowest intermediate balance) |

#### III.D.3 Bona fide purchasers and change of position (R-REM-049)
- A **BFP** (value + good faith + no notice) takes free of equitable claims; Π then pursues the proceeds or the wrongdoer.
- A **donee** takes subject to the claim, but an innocent donee may raise **change of position** to the extent it relied in good faith (not available to wrongdoers) (R3RUE §§ 65–66 [VERIFY V-350]).
- **Goods:** a thief has void title (owner recovers even from a good-faith buyer); one who obtained goods by fraud has voidable title and can pass good title to a good-faith purchaser for value (UCC § 2-403(1)).
- **How tested.** Jul 2026 Q3 asked who prevails as to each of four assets (baseball, condominium, stock, bank account) in a fraud-based restitution claim [VERIFY V-071]: for each asset, trace, check transferee status, and choose trust or lien.

### III.E Choosing the restitutionary remedy (decision table)
| Π's goal | Remedy |
|---|---|
| Money equal to Δ's gain; Δ solvent | Quasi-contract / quantum meruit (legal) |
| Get back the specific chattel | Replevin (legal) or constructive trust (equitable) |
| Get back land | Ejectment (legal); rescission + cancellation (equitable) |
| Capture appreciation or income | Constructive trust |
| Asset lost value | Equitable lien + deficiency |
| Δ insolvent; priority needed | Constructive trust, equitable lien or subrogation |
| Δ's profits from Π's property | Disgorgement / accounting |
| Unwind the deal | Rescission (legal or equitable; CA unified, § 1692) |

## IV. Equitable remedies (injunctions; specific performance; reformation; rescission; contempt enforcement)

### IV.A Permanent injunctions (R-REM-055 to R-REM-064)
- **Definition.** A final order after trial on the merits, **prohibitory** (stop) or **mandatory** (act). Grounds in California: Civ. Code § 3422 (damages inadequate; compensation extremely difficult to ascertain; necessary to prevent a multiplicity of proceedings; obligation arising from a trust) [S-458]; CCP § 526.
- **Essay elements — I-P-F-B-D** (R-REM-055): Inadequate legal remedy; Protectable interest; Feasibility; Balancing of hardships (and public interest); no Defenses.
- **Federal test** (*eBay*, 547 U.S. 388 (2006); R-REM-056): irreparable injury; inadequate legal remedies; balance of hardships; public interest not disserved.
- **Statutory limits (CA):** Civ. Code § 3423 lists cases where injunctions may not issue, including staying a pending judicial proceeding (except to prevent a multiplicity of proceedings) and preventing breach of a contract that would not be specifically enforced (subject to the personal-service exception in § 3423(e)) [S-459] [S-1204].

#### IV.A.1 Inadequate legal remedy / irreparable harm (R-REM-057)
- Unique subject matter (land, art, heirlooms); speculative or unmeasurable harm (goodwill, environmental, aesthetic, constitutional rights); continuing harm (multiplicity of suits); Δ insolvent (split); irreversible harm (demolition, felling a tree).
- On the essay, name **the specific reason** damages fail.

#### IV.A.2 Property right / protectable interest (R-REM-058)
- Traditional requirement; modern courts protect personal, contract and constitutional rights. State it and find it satisfied.

#### IV.A.3 Feasibility of enforcement (R-REM-059)
- Prohibitory easier than mandatory; one-time acts easy; continuing supervision, cooperation, skill or taste hard; personal services not compelled (R2d §§ 366–367); acts abroad can be ordered in personam.

#### IV.A.4 Balancing of hardships and public interest (R-REM-060)
- Compare Π's harm if denied with Δ's (and the public's) harm if granted. Deny or tailor relief if Δ's hardship is **greatly disproportionate**, unless Δ acted **willfully** (no balancing for willful wrongdoers).
- Public interest: jobs, public health, free speech, environmental protection.

#### IV.A.5 Defenses
- See V (laches, unclean hands, estoppel, waiver, undue hardship, unconscionability, changed circumstances).

#### IV.A.6 Recurring injunction settings
| Setting | Rule | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Encroachment** | Willful/negligent encroacher → removal ordered. Innocent encroacher + no irreparable injury to Π + removal hardship greatly disproportionate → damages; in California an **equitable easement** on payment of compensation (R-REM-061) | *Christensen v. Tucker* (1952) 114 Cal.App.2d 554; *Hirshfield v. Schwartz* (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 749 [S-466] |
| **Nuisance** | Balance; options: full, partial or conditional injunction; permanent damages in lieu; compensated injunction where Π came to the nuisance (R-REM-062) | *Boomer v. Atlantic Cement Co.*, 26 N.Y.2d 219 (1970); *Spur Industries, Inc. v. Del E. Webb Development Co.*, 108 Ariz. 178 (1972); R2d Torts § 840D [VERIFY V-363] |
| **Trespass (continuing)** | Injunction against repeated entries; damages inadequate because of multiplicity | Civ. Code § 3422 [S-458] |
| **Waste / trees** | Injunction to prevent irreparable destruction (timber, historic structures); damages multiplied for trees in CA (§ 3346) | [S-460] |
| **Speech / defamation** | No pre-trial injunction (prior restraint); California allows a narrow post-trial injunction against repeating statements adjudicated defamatory (R-REM-063) | *Balboa Island Village Inn, Inc. v. Lemen* (2007) 40 Cal.4th 1141 [S-476] |
| **Personal services** | No SP; negative injunction possible for unique services during the term (*Lumley v. Wagner* (1852)); in California only for qualifying written contracts with minimum compensation (Civ. Code § 3423(e)); no end run by enjoining the new employer (*Beverly Glen Music, Inc. v. Warner Communications, Inc.* (1986) 178 Cal.App.3d 1142) (R-REM-064) | [S-459] [S-1203] [S-1204] |
| **Non-competes** | California voids post-employment non-competes (Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600; R-CTR-041); trade-secret injunctions remain available | Contracts folder |
| **Government action** | Injunction against enforcement of an unconstitutional law; pair with declaratory relief (*Steffel v. Thompson*, 415 U.S. 452 (1974)) | VI.A |

### IV.B Provisional relief: temporary restraining orders and preliminary injunctions (R-REM-065 to R-REM-069)
> Tested on Jul 2017 Q3 (chess queen: TRO, PI, then SP), Jul 2019 Q2 (church: TRO, PI, declaratory relief) and Mar 2025 Q5 (fence: PI, permanent injunction, damages) [VERIFY V-071]. When the call lists them, analyze **TRO → PI → permanent relief** in that order.

#### IV.B.1 Temporary restraining order (R-REM-065)
- **Purpose:** preserve the status quo until a PI hearing.
- **Federal (FRCP 65(b)):** ex parte TRO only if (A) specific facts in an affidavit or verified complaint clearly show **immediate and irreparable injury** before Δ can be heard and (B) the movant's attorney **certifies** notice efforts and why notice should not be required; expires at the time set, **≤ 14 days**, extendable once for good cause or by consent; Δ may move to dissolve on **2 days'** notice; security under 65(c).
- **Merits:** courts apply the PI factors (*Winter*) in compressed form: likely success, irreparable harm before the hearing, balance of hardships, public interest.
- A TRO extended beyond the rule's limit becomes an appealable PI (*Granny Goose Foods, Inc. v. Teamsters*, 415 U.S. 423 (1974)).

#### IV.B.2 Preliminary injunction — federal (R-REM-066)
- **Notice** required (65(a)(1)); evidentiary hearing; court may consolidate with trial (65(a)(2)).
- ***Winter v. NRDC*, 555 U.S. 7 (2008):** Π must establish (1) **likely** success on the merits; (2) **likely** irreparable harm absent relief (possibility is not enough); (3) balance of equities tips in Π's favor; (4) public interest. Extraordinary remedy, never of right.
- Some circuits keep a sliding scale ("serious questions" + hardships tipping sharply) after *Winter*.

#### IV.B.3 California standard (R-REM-067)
- **Two interrelated factors:** likelihood Π will prevail on the merits, and the **relative interim harm** to the parties; a stronger showing on one lessens the showing needed on the other (*IT Corp. v. County of Imperial* (1983) 35 Cal.3d 63 [S-455]); but some possibility of success on the merits is always required (*Butt v. State of California* (1992) 4 Cal.4th 668 [S-456]).
- **TRO (CCP § 527):** may issue on a verified complaint or affidavit showing great or irreparable injury before the matter can be heard on notice; the applicant certifies under oath that it gave notice, tried in good faith to do so, or why notice should be excused (§ 527(c)); the OSC re: preliminary injunction must be heard within **15 days** (up to **22** for good cause), with service within 5 days of issuance or 2 days before the hearing, whichever is earlier (§ 527(d)) [S-457] [S-1205].
- **Undertaking mandatory** (CCP § 529) [S-478].
- **Essay tip:** state the general four factors, then "In California, courts weigh likelihood of success and relative interim harm on a sliding scale (*IT Corp.*)."

#### IV.B.4 Bond, status quo and mandatory interim relief (R-REM-068, R-REM-069)
- **Security** (FRCP 65(c); CCP § 529): covers Δ's damages if wrongfully enjoined; recovery generally capped at the bond.
- **Status quo ante litem** = last peaceable, uncontested status. Prohibitory interim orders preserve it; **mandatory** PIs (affirmative acts) are disfavored and need a clear showing.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: 行为保全 (conduct preservation) under the PRC Civil Procedure Law is the nearest analogue to a TRO/PI; it also turns on urgency and usually requires security (approx.).

### IV.C Specific performance (R-REM-070 to R-REM-074)
#### IV.C.1 Elements (R-REM-070; R-CTR-104)
- **CCIFD:** valid Contract with certain terms; Π's Conditions satisfied (ready, willing, able); Inadequate legal remedy; Feasibility; no Defenses. Mutuality of remedy no longer required if Π's performance is assured (Civ. Code § 3386 [VERIFY V-108]).
- **Authority:** R2d §§ 357–369; UCC § 2-716; Civ. Code §§ 3384–3395.

#### IV.C.2 Land (R-REM-071)
- Land is unique → buyer's damages presumed inadequate. Most courts let a seller compel payment (mutuality).
- **CA note:** Civ. Code § 3387: breach of an agreement to transfer real property is **presumed** not adequately relieved by money; the presumption is **conclusive** for a **single-family dwelling** the party seeking performance **intends to occupy**; otherwise it affects the burden of proof [S-450]. A seller may also get SP against a defaulting buyer (*Laske v. Lampasona*, 89 Cal.App.2d 284) [S-1802]; whether courts grant it less readily than a buyer's suit is unconfirmed [VERIFY V-355]. SP unavailable against a party who did not receive adequate consideration or for whom the contract is not just and reasonable (§ 3391 [S-474]).
- **Equitable conversion** (Real Property): buyer bears risk of loss in some states after contracting.

#### IV.C.3 Unique goods (R-REM-072)
- UCC § 2-716(1): goods **unique or in other proper circumstances** (inability to cover; output or requirements contracts). § 2-716(3): replevin of identified goods if cover impossible. § 2-502: buyer's right to goods on seller's insolvency or, for consumer goods, repudiation.
- Uniqueness facts: one-of-a-kind, antique, provenance, sentimental tie, no market substitute.

#### IV.C.4 Personal services and other contracts (R-REM-064)
- No SP of personal services (Thirteenth Amendment; supervision; R2d § 367; Civ. Code § 3390 [S-1354]); negative injunction possible (IV.A.6).
- Construction contracts: SP rare (supervision), except simple, well-defined work or where Δ is the landowner.
- Contracts to make loans, sell shares of a close corporation, or convey a business: SP possible where the subject is unique.

#### IV.C.5 Abatement; protecting the claim (R-REM-073, R-REM-074)
- **Abatement:** buyer may take what the seller can convey with a price reduction for a deficiency (acreage, encumbrance) (R2d § 358 [VERIFY V-371]).
- **Lis pendens:** recording a notice of pendency of action gives constructive notice and prevents later buyers from being BFPs (CCP § 405.20 et seq.) [VERIFY V-360]. For goods, seek a TRO/PI against sale.

### IV.D Reformation (R-REM-075)
- **Grounds:** mutual mistake in **expression** (scrivener's error); fraud as to the writing's contents; unilateral mistake known to the other party (R2d §§ 155, 166; Civ. Code § 3399).
- **Proof:** prior agreement, departure in the writing, usually **clear and convincing** evidence.
- **Not barred by** the parol evidence rule, the statute of frauds or Π's negligence in failing to read (R2d § 157). **Barred by** BFP reliance, laches, unclean hands.
- **Sequencing on the essay:** reform the writing first, then enforce it as reformed (SP, injunction, damages).
- **How tested:** Oct 2020 Q5 (contract omitted one of two paintings), Jul 2023 Q5 (omitted mineral rights and access), Feb 2016 Q4 (insured's cross-claim to reform coverage), Feb 2015 Q4 (road description) [VERIFY V-071].

### IV.E Rescission and cancellation (R-REM-043, R-REM-076, R-REM-077)
- **Grounds:** fraud or material misrepresentation; mutual mistake on a basic assumption; unilateral mistake (R2d § 153); duress; undue influence; incapacity; material breach; mutual agreement (R-CTR-105).
- **Requirements:** prompt notice; restoration or offer to restore; court decree (equitable) or tender and suit (legal). **CA note:** §§ 1689, 1691, 1692 [S-462].
- **Insurance (CA):** insurer may rescind for concealment, intentional or unintentional (Ins. Code § 331), or a representation false in a material point (§ 359) [S-471] [S-1211]; materiality judged subjectively by the probable and reasonable effect on the particular insurer's decision (§ 334) [S-1212]; premiums returned; waiver/estoppel if the insurer accepted premiums after learning the truth (R-REM-076).
- **Cancellation of instruments:** Civ. Code § 3412 [VERIFY V-368] (R-REM-077).

### IV.F Contempt enforcement and scope of decrees (R-REM-078 to R-REM-080)
| Type | Purpose | Sanction | Procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Civil coercive | Compel compliance | Conditional daily fine or jail until compliance | Civil; ability to comply required |
| Civil compensatory | Compensate Π for loss from violation | Fine paid to Π | Civil |
| Criminal | Punish completed defiance | Fixed fine or jail | Criminal protections; proof beyond reasonable doubt; jury for serious penalties (*Int'l Union, UMWA v. Bagwell*, 512 U.S. 821 (1994)) |
- **Elements:** valid and clear order, knowledge, ability to comply, willful disobedience.
- **CA note:** CCP § 1209 (contempts) [VERIFY V-358], § 1218 (fine up to $1,000 and up to 5 days per act) [S-473]; § 1219(a) (coercive imprisonment until an act within the person's power is performed; the act is specified in the commitment warrant) [S-1803]; punitive contempt sanctions require proof beyond a reasonable doubt (*Mitchell v. Superior Court* (1989) 49 Cal.3d 1230) [S-1803] [S-1804].
- **Collateral bar** (*United States v. United Mine Workers*, 330 U.S. 258 (1947); *Walker v. City of Birmingham*, 388 U.S. 307 (1967), upholding a state's use of the rule): Δ must obey an order issued with jurisdiction and challenge it on appeal; **CA note:** California permits a challenge to a void (e.g., unconstitutional) order in the contempt proceeding (*In re Berry* (1968) 68 Cal.2d 137) [S-1219].
- **Who is bound** (FRCP 65(d)(2)): parties; their officers, agents, servants, employees, attorneys; persons in active concert — with actual notice. **Form:** reasons, specific terms, reasonable detail, no incorporation by reference (65(d)(1)).
- **Scope:** no broader than necessary for complete relief to the plaintiffs; federal courts may not issue universal injunctions (*Trump v. CASA, Inc.* (2025)) [VERIFY V-035].

## V. Equitable defenses (laches; unclean hands; in pari delicto; estoppel; undue hardship; waiver; changed circumstances; unconscionability)

> Raise defenses under each equitable remedy (or once, under a "Defenses" heading, if the call asks "what defenses can Δ raise?"). Tested on Feb 2013 Q3, Feb 2016 Q4 and Oct 2020 Q5 [VERIFY V-071].

| Defense | Elements | Bars | Authority / rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Laches** | Π knew or should have known; unreasonable delay; prejudice to Δ | Equitable relief (not legal damages within the limitations period) | R-REM-081, R-REM-090; *Petrella v. MGM*, 572 U.S. 663 (2014) |
| **Unclean hands** | Π's inequitable conduct directly related to the claim | Equitable relief (CA: legal claims too, *Kendall-Jackson Winery, Ltd. v. Superior Court* (1999) 76 Cal.App.4th 970 [S-1216]) | R-REM-082 |
| **In pari delicto** | Illegal transaction; equal fault | Enforcement and restitution | R-REM-083 |
| **Estoppel** | Representation or conduct; reasonable detrimental reliance | Any remedy inconsistent with the representation | R-REM-084; Evid. Code § 623 |
| **Waiver** | Intentional relinquishment of a known right | The waived right | R-REM-085; Civ. Code § 3516 |
| **Undue hardship** | Δ's burden greatly disproportionate; Δ not willful | Injunction / SP (damages remain) | R-REM-086; R2d § 364 |
| **Changed circumstances** | Significant change in fact or law | Continued enforcement of a decree; SP | R-REM-088; *Rufo*, 502 U.S. 367 (1992); FRCP 60(b)(5); CCP § 533 [VERIFY V-365] |
| **Unconscionability / inadequate consideration** | Unfair terms or grossly inadequate price at formation | SP (damages may remain) | R-REM-087; R2d § 364; Civ. Code § 3391 [S-474] |

### V.A Laches (and the statute of limitations) (R-REM-081, R-REM-090)
- Delay is measured from when Π knew or should have known. Prejudice: lost evidence, Δ's investment in reliance (building), changed market.
- Legal claims: statute of limitations; within it, laches generally cannot bar damages (*Petrella v. Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, Inc.*, 572 U.S. 663 (2014); *SCA Hygiene Products Aktiebolag v. First Quality Baby Products, LLC*, 580 U.S. 328 (2017)).

### V.B Unclean hands (R-REM-082)
- "He who comes into equity must come with clean hands." The misconduct must relate to the same transaction. Unrelated bad acts are irrelevant.

### V.C In pari delicto (R-REM-083)
- Court leaves equally guilty parties where it finds them; exceptions for less-culpable plaintiffs, protected classes and timely withdrawal (R2d §§ 197–199 [VERIFY V-371]).

### V.D Estoppel (R-REM-084)
- Π's statement or conduct (or silence with a duty to speak) + Δ's reasonable reliance + detriment. Often pleaded with laches when Π watched Δ build (Evid. Code § 623).

### V.E Waiver (R-REM-085)
- Knowing relinquishment, express or by conduct (accepting late payments; accepting premiums after discovering a misstatement). No reliance needed.

### V.F Undue hardship (R-REM-086)
- The balancing element as a defense. Not available to a willful wrongdoer. Usually leads to damages instead of an injunction, or a tailored decree.

### V.G Changed circumstances (R-REM-088)
- Courts may modify or dissolve injunctions on a significant change in fact or law (*Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail*, 502 U.S. 367 (1992); FRCP 60(b)(5); CCP § 533 [VERIFY V-365]).

### V.H Unconscionability and inadequate consideration (R-REM-087)
- SP refused where the bargain was unfair when made, even if damages remain available (R2d § 364; UCC § 2-302). **CA note:** Civ. Code § 3391 bars SP against a party who did not receive adequate consideration or as to whom the contract is not just and reasonable [S-474].

### V.I Other defenses to specific performance and injunction (R-REM-089)
- Any contract defense: no contract, indefiniteness, statute of frauds (part performance exception), mistake, misrepresentation, impossibility or impracticability, illegality.
- Π's own breach or failure of conditions; sale to a BFP; feasibility objections.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC law has no general "equitable defenses"; delay is handled by 诉讼时效 and good-faith principles (诚信原则), which are the closest analogue to unclean hands and estoppel (approx.).

## VI. Declaratory remedies (declaratory judgments; reformation; nominal damages)

### VI.A Declaratory judgments (R-REM-091, R-REM-092)
- **Federal:** 28 U.S.C. § 2201(a): in a case of actual controversy within its jurisdiction, a federal court may declare the rights and other legal relations of any interested party, whether or not further relief is or could be sought; § 2202 further relief; FRCP 57. The Act is procedural; it does not create jurisdiction.
- **Actual controversy:** definite and concrete, between adverse parties, real and substantial (*MedImmune, Inc. v. Genentech, Inc.*, 549 U.S. 118 (2007)). No advisory opinions.
- **Discretion:** courts may decline (*Wilton v. Seven Falls Co.*, 515 U.S. 277 (1995)).
- **Against threatened enforcement:** a person threatened with prosecution under an allegedly unconstitutional statute may seek a declaration without showing the irreparable harm an injunction requires (*Steffel v. Thompson*, 415 U.S. 452 (1974)).
- **CA note:** CCP § 1060 (any person interested under a written instrument or contract, or regarding property, may seek a declaration in cases of actual controversy, including before breach); § 1061 (court may refuse where a declaration is not necessary or proper at the time) [S-467].
- **How tested:** Jul 2019 Q2 (church: declaratory relief that the candle ban is unconstitutional) [VERIFY V-071].

### VI.B Quiet title and cancellation (R-REM-093, R-REM-077)
- **Quiet title** declares title against adverse claims (CA: CCP § 760.010 et seq.) [VERIFY V-368]. **Cancellation** removes a void or voidable instrument that clouds title (Civ. Code § 3412) [VERIFY V-368].

### VI.C Reformation as a declaratory-type remedy (R-REM-075)
- The State Bar outline lists reformation here as well as under equitable remedies: reformation declares what the parties' writing should say. Treat it fully under IV.D; on the essay, discuss it where the call places it.

### VI.D Nominal damages (R-REM-094)
- A trivial sum (e.g., $1) for violation of a right without proven loss: trespass, battery, breach of contract, constitutional violations (Civ. Code § 3360; R2d § 346(2)). Not available for negligence (actual damage is an element).
- Nominal damages redress a completed injury for Article III standing (*Uzuegbunam v. Preczewski*, 592 U.S. 279 (2021)).
- **CA note:** a nominal award reflecting no actual harm likely does not support punitive damages; a small award given because real harm was hard to measure may (R-REM-030) [S-1209] [S-1210].

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: a US declaratory judgment resembles a PRC 确认之诉; nominal damages have no PRC counterpart, since PRC compensation requires actual loss (approx.).
