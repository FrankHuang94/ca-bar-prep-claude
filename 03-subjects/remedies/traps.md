# Remedies — traps

Each item gives the trap, the correct rule, and the rule ID. Remedies is not on the MBE, so these are **essay** traps: issues missed, rules confused, or structure that costs points.

## Structure
1. **Jumping straight to the injunction.** Equity asks whether the legal remedy is adequate, so discuss damages first, unless the call names only equitable remedies (R-REM-006, R-REM-002).
2. **Ignoring the call's list.** If the call says "TRO, preliminary injunction and specific performance," use exactly those three headings in that order (R-REM-006).
3. **No conclusion on which remedy is best.** End with an election paragraph: which remedy or combination serves Π best without double recovery (R-REM-005).
4. **Analyzing remedies without the parties.** With several claimants or defendants, organize claim by claim, as the selected answers do (`00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §5).
5. **Treating the call as a liability question only.** In crossovers (Torts + Remedies), spend at least half the time on remedies when the call asks for them.

## Damages
6. **Using contract foreseeability in a tort case.** Tort damages run to all detriment proximately caused, "whether it could have been anticipated or not" (Civ. Code § 3333); *Hadley* is the contract test (R-REM-009).
7. **Forgetting the four limits.** Each item of damages needs causation, foreseeability, certainty and mitigation, each in a sentence or two (R-REM-008 to R-REM-011).
8. **Mitigation burden on Π.** The defendant must prove Π could have avoided the loss; reasonable mitigation costs are recoverable even if the effort failed (R-REM-011).
9. **Automatic restoration cost for harm to land.** The general rule is the lesser of diminution and restoration; restoration above diminution needs a personal reason (R-REM-019).
10. **Missing the California tree statute.** Cutting or damaging trees on another's land triggers multiple damages under Civ. Code § 3346: double is mandatory; treble is discretionary and needs a willful and malicious trespass (R-REM-020).
11. **One measure for fraud.** Give out-of-pocket and benefit-of-the-bargain figures; in California, § 3343 limits property-transaction fraud to out-of-pocket unless the defendant is a fiduciary (split) (R-REM-023).
12. **Permanent vs. continuing nuisance confused.** Permanent: one award of diminution in value. Continuing: past damages plus an injunction or later suits (R-REM-024).
13. **Recovering billed medical expenses in California.** *Howell* limits past medical damages to amounts paid or incurred; the collateral source rule still bars credit for insurance (R-REM-012).

## Punitive damages
14. **Punitive damages for breach of contract.** Never for breach alone, however willful; look for an independent tort (fraud) (R-REM-031, R-REM-032).
15. **Preponderance standard in California.** Section 3294 requires clear and convincing evidence of oppression, fraud or malice (R-REM-028).
16. **Corporate punitive damages without a managing agent.** Ratification or authorization must come from an officer, director or managing agent (substantial discretionary authority over corporate policy), not just a supervisor who can hire and fire (R-REM-029).
17. **No evidence of financial condition.** In California, Π must introduce it, or the award fails (*Adams*). Actual damages are also a predicate; a bare nominal award reflecting no real harm likely will not do (R-REM-030).
18. **Ignoring the ratio.** If the facts give a punitive figure, test it against the single-digit guidance and the *Gore* guideposts (R-REM-027).

## Restitution
19. **Measuring restitution by Π's loss.** Restitution measures Δ's gain: a converter who resold for more than value owes the sale price (R-REM-035, R-REM-037).
20. **Constructive trust without tracing.** The plaintiff must trace its property into a specific asset; dissipated funds support only a money judgment (R-REM-044, R-REM-047).
21. **Choosing a constructive trust over a depreciated asset.** Use an equitable lien plus a deficiency judgment when the asset lost value (R-REM-046).
22. **Letting later deposits restore the plaintiff's share.** Lowest intermediate balance: the wrongdoer's later deposits do not replenish the trust funds (R-REM-048).
23. **Treating a donee like a BFP.** A gratuitous transferee takes subject to the constructive trust; only a purchaser for value without notice takes free (R-REM-049).
24. **Theft vs. fraud for goods.** A thief passes no title, even to a good-faith buyer; a defrauder has voidable title and can pass good title to a good-faith purchaser for value (UCC § 2-403(1)) (R-REM-049).
25. **Calling a constructive trust a "trust" with fiduciary duties.** It is a remedy, not an express trust; contrast the resulting trust, based on presumed intent (R-REM-044, R-REM-050).
26. **Quasi-contract for an officious benefit.** No restitution for benefits forced on someone without a chance to decline, except emergencies, necessaries and mistakes (R-REM-040).

## Equitable remedies
27. **"Damages are inadequate" without saying why.** Name the reason: uniqueness, irreparable harm, speculative damages, multiplicity, insolvency (R-REM-057).
28. **Balancing for a willful wrongdoer.** No balancing of hardships for a defendant who built knowing of the encroachment; removal is ordered (R-REM-060, R-REM-061).
29. **TRO and preliminary injunction blurred.** TRO: may issue without notice, short duration (federal ≤ 14 days), irreparable harm before a hearing. PI: notice and hearing, lasts until trial. Both need a bond (R-REM-065 to R-REM-068).
30. **Using *Winter* alone in California state court.** Add the *IT Corp.* two-factor sliding scale (R-REM-067).
31. **"Possibility" of irreparable harm.** *Winter* requires that irreparable harm be likely (R-REM-066).
32. **Specific performance of personal services.** Never; consider a negative injunction, and in California the § 3423(e) limits (R-REM-064).
33. **Specific performance of ordinary goods.** Fungible goods can be bought elsewhere: cover and damages. Only unique goods, or goods where cover is impossible, qualify (R-REM-072).
34. **Forgetting § 3387.** On a California land-sale SP issue, cite the presumption and whether it is conclusive (single-family, buyer will occupy) (R-REM-071).
35. **Forgetting mutuality is gone.** Modern courts (and Civ. Code § 3386) do not require mutuality of remedy if Π's performance is assured (R-REM-070).
36. **Reformation vs. rescission confused.** Reformation fixes a writing that misstates a real agreement; rescission unwinds a flawed agreement (R-REM-075, R-REM-043).
37. **Parol evidence rule as a bar to reformation.** It does not bar reformation; neither does Π's negligence in failing to read (R-REM-075).
38. **Enjoining defamation before trial.** That is a prior restraint; California allows only a narrow post-trial injunction against repeating adjudicated statements (R-REM-063).

## Defenses and declaratory relief
39. **Laches without prejudice.** Delay alone is not laches; the defendant must show prejudice. Laches generally does not bar damages filed within the limitations period (R-REM-081, R-REM-090).
40. **Unclean hands from unrelated misconduct.** The misconduct must relate to the same transaction. In California the defense can bar legal claims too (R-REM-082).
41. **Estoppel vs. waiver.** Estoppel needs detrimental reliance; waiver does not (R-REM-084, R-REM-085).
42. **Declaratory relief on a hypothetical.** It needs an actual, ripe controversy between adverse parties (R-REM-092).
