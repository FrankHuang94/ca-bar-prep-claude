# Remedies — primer

Read this first. It explains what Remedies is about, why US law is organized the way it is, and the handful of ideas that decide most essays. Detail is in [`outline.md`](outline.md); exam wording is in [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md).

## 1. What the subject is
Every other subject asks **"is the defendant liable?"** Remedies asks **"so what does the plaintiff get?"** The answer is one or more of four things:

| Remedy family | Question it answers | Typical forms |
|---|---|---|
| **Damages** | How much did the plaintiff lose? | Compensatory, nominal, punitive |
| **Restitution** | How much did the defendant unjustly gain? | Quasi-contract, replevin, constructive trust, equitable lien |
| **Coercive relief** | Must the defendant do or stop something? | Injunctions (TRO, preliminary, permanent), specific performance |
| **Declaratory relief** | What are the parties' rights? | Declaratory judgment, quiet title, reformation |

## 2. Why "law" and "equity" still matter
English law had two court systems. The **common-law courts** gave money (damages) or returned land and chattels. The **Court of Chancery (equity)** stepped in when money was not enough and gave personal orders: do this, stop that, hand over that property. US courts merged the two procedurally, but the old line still decides four practical things:
1. **Prerequisite.** Equity acts only when the legal remedy (money) is **inadequate**.
2. **Discretion.** Damages follow liability as of right; equitable relief is **discretionary** — the judge balances hardships and the public interest.
3. **Defenses.** Equitable remedies are subject to **equitable defenses** (laches, unclean hands, estoppel, hardship).
4. **Enforcement.** A damages judgment is collected like a debt. An equitable order is enforced by **contempt** — fines or jail for disobedience.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC law treats 继续履行 (specific performance) and 停止侵害 (stop infringement) as ordinary forms of civil liability. US law treats their counterparts as exceptional, discretionary equitable remedies that need an "inadequate damages" showing first (approx.).

## 3. Key vocabulary
| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Expectation damages | Money putting Π where full contract performance would have |
| Consequential (special) damages | Losses from Π's particular circumstances (lost profits), limited by foreseeability |
| Mitigation | Π must take reasonable steps to limit its loss |
| Punitive damages | Extra money to punish malicious conduct; tort only |
| Unjust enrichment | Δ got a benefit at Π's expense that it would be unfair to keep |
| Quasi-contract | A legal claim for the value of a benefit where there is no real contract |
| Replevin / ejectment | Legal actions to get back a chattel / land |
| Constructive trust | Court orders Δ to hand over specific property obtained wrongfully (and its gains) |
| Equitable lien | Court gives Π a security interest in Δ's property bought or improved with Π's money |
| Tracing | Following Π's property through exchanges into what Δ now holds |
| Injunction | Court order to do (mandatory) or stop (prohibitory) something |
| TRO / preliminary injunction | Short-term orders preserving the status quo before trial |
| Specific performance | Court order to perform a contract (land, unique goods) |
| Reformation | Rewriting a document to match the parties' actual deal |
| Rescission | Unwinding a contract and returning each side's performance |
| Laches | Unreasonable delay that prejudices Δ, barring equitable relief |
| Unclean hands | Π's own misconduct in the same matter bars equitable relief |
| Declaratory judgment | Court declares rights without ordering anything |

More translations are in `02-foundations/legal-english-glossary.md`.

## 4. How the pieces connect: the essay flow
For each claim the plaintiff has, work left to right:

```
Damages (compensatory → limits → nominal → punitive)
   → Restitution (legal: quasi-contract, replevin, ejectment / equitable: constructive trust, lien)
      → Equitable remedies (TRO → preliminary injunction → permanent injunction or specific performance;
                            reformation; rescission)
         → Defenses (laches, unclean hands, estoppel, hardship...)
            → Which remedy is best for the plaintiff?
```
The legal remedies come first because equity asks whether they are adequate. Restitution sits in the middle because it has both a legal and an equitable side.

## 5. The ideas that matter most
1. **Measure the right thing.** Damages measure **Π's loss**; restitution measures **Δ's gain**. When Δ profited more than Π lost (Δ resold Π's painting for double its value), restitution wins. When Π lost more, damages win.
2. **Every damages item passes four limits:** causation, foreseeability (contract: *Hadley*; tort: proximate cause), certainty (especially lost profits) and mitigation. Graders expect a sentence on each for each item.
3. **Punitive damages are for torts, not contracts.** They need aggravated conduct (in California: clear and convincing evidence of oppression, fraud or malice, Civ. Code § 3294) and must respect due-process ratios (*Gore*, *State Farm*). A breach of contract earns none unless there is an independent tort such as fraud.
4. **Equity's gate is "inadequate legal remedy."** Land, unique goods (art, heirlooms, collectibles), irreversible harm (cutting a tree, demolishing a building), continuing harm, and speculative damages open the gate. Say *why* money fails.
5. **Injunction elements are a checklist:** inadequate remedy, protectable interest, feasibility, balancing of hardships, defenses (I-P-F-B-D). For interim relief, add likelihood of success and urgency: a TRO lasts days, a preliminary injunction lasts until trial, a permanent injunction follows judgment.
6. **Constructive trust vs. equitable lien is a math problem.** Trace Π's money into the asset. If the asset went **up**, take it (constructive trust). If it went **down**, take a lien and sue for the rest. A **bona fide purchaser** cuts off both.
7. **Reform first, then enforce.** When a writing leaves out what the parties agreed (a painting, mineral rights), reformation fixes the document; specific performance or an injunction then enforces it.

## 6. How California fits in
Remedies essays use **general principles**. California adds a few high-value sentences: § 3294 for punitive damages, § 3387 for specific performance of a home, the *IT Corp.* sliding scale for preliminary injunctions, § 3343 out-of-pocket damages for fraud in property deals, § 1692 allowing damages with rescission, and equitable easements for innocent encroachers. See [`ca-distinctions.md`](ca-distinctions.md).

## 7. Where this connects to your other subjects
- **Contracts:** damages measures, specific performance, rescission, reformation (shared rules R-CTR-089 to R-CTR-109).
- **Torts:** the liability half of fraud, trespass, conversion and nuisance essays.
- **Real Property:** land-sale remedies, encroachments, recording (BFPs, lis pendens).
- **Trusts:** constructive and resulting trusts; remedies for breach of trust.
- **Civil Procedure:** FRCP 65 (TROs, preliminary injunctions), jury trial, declaratory judgments.
- **Constitutional Law:** injunctions and declaratory relief against unconstitutional laws; prior restraints.
