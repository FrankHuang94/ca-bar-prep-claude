# Remedies — essay templates

**Basis.** The Phase 3 index of the 16 administrations with a Remedies essay (Feb 2012 – Jul 2026) shows five recurring call types [VERIFY V-071]:
1. **Open call:** "What remedies can Π obtain (and what defenses can Δ raise)?" — Feb 2013 Q3, Jul 2013 Q6, Feb 2014 Q6, Feb 2017 Q2, Oct 2020 Q5, Feb 2025 Q4.
2. **Named interim and final relief:** "TRO, preliminary injunction, specific performance" (Jul 2017 Q3); "TRO, preliminary injunction, declaratory relief" (Jul 2019 Q2); "preliminary injunction, permanent injunction, damages" (Mar 2025 Q5).
3. **Contract equitable remedies:** specific performance, reformation, rescission (Feb 2016 Q4, Feb 2020 Q3, Jul 2023 Q5).
4. **Restitution by asset / tracing:** Jul 2023 Q5 (checking account), Jul 2026 Q3 (four assets).
5. **Tort + remedies:** fraud (Jul 2024 Q5), nuisance/trespass (Feb 2022 Q3), intentional torts (Jul 2013 Q6).

**How to use these templates.** They are skeletons; fill them with the rules in [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md). Selected answers follow the call's structure and give a conclusion for each remedy (`00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §5).

**Timing (60 minutes).** About 12–15 minutes to read and outline, 45 minutes to write. In a remedies essay the points sit in the **elements** of each remedy, so give each element its own short paragraph with facts.

**The three paragraph shapes you will use repeatedly.**
- **Element paragraph:** "**Inadequate legal remedy.** [Rule in one sentence.] Here, [specific facts]. [Counter-argument.] Therefore, [hedged conclusion]."
- **Remedy conclusion:** "Because all elements are met and no defense applies, the court will likely grant [remedy]."
- **California add-on:** "In California, [statute or case] [what it changes]; here, [effect]."

## Template 1 — The full remedies answer ("What remedies can Π obtain?")
Use for open calls. Repeat the block for each claim (Π v. Δ1, Π v. Δ2) if there are several.

```
I. [Π] v. [Δ] — underlying claim (one paragraph unless the call asks for liability)
   [Name the claim: breach / fraud / trespass / conversion / nuisance; one-line rule; conclude liable.]

II. LEGAL REMEDIES
   A. Compensatory damages (R-REM-007)
      1. Measure — state the measure for this claim and compute it:
         contract: expectation (R-REM-014) / land (R-REM-015, -016)
         tort: injury (R-REM-018), land (R-REM-019), trespass (R-REM-020), conversion (R-REM-021),
               fraud — out-of-pocket vs. benefit of bargain; CA § 3343 (R-REM-023),
               nuisance — permanent vs. continuing (R-REM-024)
      2. Consequential damages — list each item
      3. Limits (one short paragraph each, per item):
         a. Causation (R-REM-008)
         b. Foreseeability — contract: Hadley; tort: proximate cause, CA § 3333 (R-REM-009)
         c. Certainty — lost profits; new business (R-REM-010)
         d. Mitigation — what did Π do? (R-REM-011)
      → Conclusion with a figure if the facts allow
   B. Nominal damages (R-REM-094) — if loss is small or unproven
   C. Punitive damages (R-REM-026 to R-REM-030)
      tort? → malice / oppression / fraud → CA § 3294 clear and convincing → company: managing agent
      → due process (Gore / State Farm ratio) → conclusion
   D. Liquidated damages clause, if any (R-REM-017)

III. RESTITUTION
   A. Legal restitution
      1. Quasi-contract / unjust enrichment — measure Δ's gain (R-REM-035 to R-REM-038)
      2. Replevin (chattel) / ejectment (land) — if Π wants the thing back (R-REM-041, -042)
      3. Rescission + restitution — if Π wants out of the deal (R-REM-043)
   B. Equitable restitution
      1. Constructive trust — title + wrongful acquisition + tracing + inadequate legal remedy (R-REM-044)
      2. Equitable lien — if value fell or Π's money improved Δ's property (R-REM-045, -046)
      3. BFP / donee check (R-REM-049)

IV. EQUITABLE REMEDIES
   A. TRO and preliminary injunction (Template 2) — only if facts show urgency
   B. Permanent injunction — I-P-F-B-D (Template 2, Part C)
      or Specific performance — CCIFD (Templates 3 and 4)
   C. Reformation / rescission (Template 6) — if a writing is wrong or the deal is tainted

V. DEFENSES (R-REM-081 to R-REM-090)
   Laches · unclean hands · estoppel / waiver · undue hardship · unconscionability · contract defenses
   (Only those the facts raise; one paragraph each with facts.)

VI. ELECTION — BEST REMEDY (R-REM-005)
   "Π should seek [X] because [reason: priority / appreciation / uniqueness]; Π cannot also recover [Y]
    for the same loss." In California, rescission + consequential damages may be combined (§ 1692).
```

**Model element paragraph (original).**
> **Inadequate legal remedy.** Equity acts only when money cannot make the plaintiff whole, as where the subject matter is unique or the harm is irreparable. Here, Owner's 150-year-old oak cannot be replaced by any amount of money; once felled, the harm is permanent. Δ may argue that a mature tree can be valued by an arborist and replanted, but a replacement would take generations to grow. The legal remedy is therefore inadequate.

## Template 2 — TRO → preliminary injunction → permanent injunction
Use when the call names interim relief, or when the facts show an imminent, irreversible act (an auction tomorrow, bulldozers arriving, an ordinance taking effect). Analyze in the call's order.

```
I. Temporary restraining order (R-REM-065; CA R-REM-067)
   Rule: preserves the status quo until a PI hearing; may issue without notice if specific facts show
         immediate irreparable harm before Δ can be heard + certified notice efforts; federal ≤ 14 days;
         CA OSC hearing within 15 (22) days; bond.
   A. Irreparable harm before a hearing — the clock in the facts ("auction Friday")
   B. Likelihood of success on the merits — short merits preview (contract formed? ordinance invalid?)
   C. Balance of hardships — short delay for Δ vs. permanent loss for Π
   D. Public interest
   E. Notice — given, or why excused
   F. Bond
   → Conclusion: TRO likely granted / denied

II. Preliminary injunction (R-REM-066, R-REM-067, R-REM-068, R-REM-069)
   Rule (federal, Winter): likely success; likely irreparable harm; balance of equities; public interest.
   California: likelihood of prevailing + relative interim harm, sliding scale (IT Corp.); some chance of
   success always required (Butt).
   A. Likelihood of success — fuller merits analysis than for the TRO
   B. Irreparable harm pending trial
   C. Balance of hardships / relative interim harm
   D. Public interest
   E. Status quo — prohibitory or mandatory? (mandatory = heightened showing)
   F. Bond
   → Conclusion

III. Permanent injunction (R-REM-055 to R-REM-060) — after trial, if Π prevails
   A. Inadequate legal remedy — unique / irreparable / speculative / multiplicity / insolvency
   B. Property right or protectable interest
   C. Feasibility — prohibitory vs. mandatory; supervision; personal services
   D. Balancing of hardships — dollar figures; willful Δ gets no balancing; public interest
   E. Defenses — laches, unclean hands, estoppel, undue hardship
   [Federal case: add the eBay four factors in one sentence]
   → Conclusion; note enforcement by contempt (R-REM-078)

IV. Declaratory relief (if asked) (R-REM-091, R-REM-092)
   Actual controversy; ripeness; discretion; against threatened enforcement (Steffel)
```

**Setting-specific add-ons.**
| Setting | Add this sub-heading |
|---|---|
| Encroaching fence, wall or garage (Mar 2025 Q5 pattern) | "Relative hardship / equitable easement" — innocence, irreparable injury, disproportion (R-REM-061) |
| Nuisance (Feb 2022 Q3 pattern) | "Scope of the injunction" — hours, volume, *Boomer* damages, *Spur* compensated injunction (R-REM-062) |
| Government action, First Amendment (Jul 2019 Q2 pattern) | Merits preview uses the Con Law test; irreparable harm = loss of constitutional freedoms; public interest favors rights; add declaratory relief |
| Speech by Δ | "Prior restraint" (R-REM-063) |

**Model California sentence.**
> In California state court, the judge weighs two interrelated factors — the likelihood that Π will prevail on the merits and the relative interim harm to the parties — and a strong showing on one can offset a weaker showing on the other (*IT Corp. v. County of Imperial*). Here, …

## Template 3 — Specific performance of a land contract
Use for buyer-v.-seller disputes over land (or a seller seeking to force a purchase).

```
I. Damages (brief) — market value at breach − contract price + deposit + expenses (R-REM-015);
   CA § 3306 if California law. Conclude: available, but Π prefers the land.

II. Specific performance (R-REM-070, R-REM-071)
   A. Valid contract with definite and certain terms — parties, property, price; SOF writing
      (or part performance) (R-REM-089)
   B. Π's conditions satisfied — ready, willing and able (financing, tender, deposit)
   C. Inadequate legal remedy — land is unique;
      CA § 3387: presumption; CONCLUSIVE if single-family dwelling Π intends to occupy
   D. Mutuality (one sentence) — no longer required if Π's performance is assured (CA § 3386)
   E. Feasibility — ordering a deed is simple; out-of-state land: order Δ in personam
   F. Defenses
      1. Inadequate consideration / not just and reasonable (CA § 3391) (R-REM-087)
      2. Laches / unclean hands
      3. Mistake / misrepresentation / SOF
      4. Sale to a BFP — record a lis pendens now (R-REM-074)
   → Conclusion; abatement if the seller cannot convey all that was promised (R-REM-073)

III. Interim protection — lis pendens; TRO / PI against conveyance if needed (Template 2)
```

**Model § 3387 paragraph.**
> **Inadequate legal remedy.** Every parcel of land is considered unique, so money damages are generally inadequate when a seller refuses to convey. In California, Civil Code § 3387 presumes that the breach of an agreement to transfer real property cannot be adequately relieved by money, and the presumption is conclusive when the property is a single-family dwelling that the buyer intends to occupy. Here, Buyer planned to move her family into the three-bedroom house, so the presumption is conclusive and this element is met.

## Template 4 — Unique goods: TRO, specific performance, replevin (and reformation)
Use for a chess piece, painting, heirloom or collectible (Jul 2017 Q3, Oct 2020 Q5 patterns).

```
I. Threshold — UCC Article 2 governs (sale of goods) (R-CTR-001)
II. Was the item part of the contract?
   If the writing omits it → Reformation first (R-REM-075): prior agreement; mutual mistake in
   expression or fraud; clear and convincing; PER and SOF no bar; defenses (negligence is no bar)
III. TRO / preliminary injunction to stop sale, auction or removal (Template 2, Parts I–II)
IV. Specific performance (R-REM-070, R-REM-072)
   A. Contract with definite terms
   B. Π ready, willing and able (price tendered?)
   C. Inadequate legal remedy — UCC § 2-716: unique (one of a kind, provenance, sentimental value,
      no market substitute) or other proper circumstances (cover impossible)
   D. Feasibility — delivery of a chattel is a one-time act
   E. Defenses — laches, unclean hands, unconscionability, BFP
V. Replevin (R-REM-041) — legal alternative to recover the chattel; UCC § 2-716(3) if identified and
   cover impossible; CA claim and delivery (writ of possession on probable validity)
VI. Damages — market − contract price (UCC § 2-713) + incidental/consequential; conclude they are
    inadequate for a unique item
VII. Best remedy — usually SP or replevin plus a TRO to freeze the item
```

## Template 5 — Constructive trust / equitable lien: tracing asset by asset
Use when Δ obtained money or property by fraud, embezzlement, theft or breach of fiduciary duty and then bought or transferred things (Jul 2026 Q3; Jul 2023 Q5 patterns).

```
I. Basis for restitution — Δ's wrongful acquisition and unjust enrichment (R-REM-035); legal remedy
   (quasi-contract money judgment) inadequate if Δ is insolvent, the asset is unique, or it appreciated

II. Asset 1: [e.g., autographed baseball]
   A. Tracing — write the chain: "Π's $X → Δ's account → purchase of the baseball" (R-REM-047)
   B. Who holds it? Δ / donee / purchaser for value without notice (R-REM-049)
      - thief: void title (Π recovers even from a good-faith buyer)
      - fraud: voidable title (good-faith purchaser for value takes free; UCC § 2-403)
   C. Value now vs. amount taken
      up → constructive trust (asset + gain) (R-REM-044)
      down → equitable lien + deficiency (R-REM-045)
      partly Π's money → proportionate share or lien (R-REM-046)
   D. Alternatives — replevin (if the chattel is Π's own original property); subrogation (if a debt
      was paid off) (R-REM-051)
   → Conclusion: who prevails as to this asset

III. Asset 2: [e.g., condominium bought with a down payment of Π's money] — same A–D

IV. Asset 3: [e.g., stock that rose in value] — same A–D

V. Asset 4: [e.g., commingled bank account] (R-REM-048)
   A. Table of balances (date | event | balance | Π's traceable share)
   B. Δ spends own money first; lowest intermediate balance caps Π's claim to the account
   C. Later deposits of Δ's own money do not restore Π's share
   D. Withdrawals used to buy identifiable assets → trace into them
   → Conclusion: amount recoverable from the account

VI. Defenses — BFP; change of position (innocent recipients only); laches; unclean hands
VII. Priority — constructive trust / lien beats Δ's unsecured creditors
```

**Model asset paragraph.**
> **The stock.** Dana used $40,000 of the stolen funds, and nothing else, to buy the shares, which are now worth $70,000. Because Dana holds title to property acquired with Pam's money through fraud and the shares are directly traceable to that money, a constructive trust is appropriate. A money judgment would give Pam only $40,000 and would leave her competing with Dana's other creditors; a constructive trust gives her the shares and their $30,000 gain. Pam should choose the constructive trust over an equitable lien.

## Template 6 — Reformation, rescission and defenses (contracts and insurance)
Use when a writing omits or misstates terms, or a party wants to undo a deal for fraud or mistake (Feb 2016 Q4 insurance; Jul 2023 Q5 mineral rights; Feb 2015 Q4 road description).

```
I. [Party A]'s claim for rescission (R-REM-043; insurance R-REM-076)
   A. Ground — fraud, material misrepresentation, mutual mistake, (insurance: concealment or material
      misrepresentation, intentional or not — Ins. Code §§ 331, 359)
   B. Materiality / reliance
   C. Prompt notice and restoration (return of premiums or price) — CA § 1691
   D. Relief — restitution; CA § 1692 consequential damages
   E. [Party B]'s defenses — waiver (accepted premiums or payments after learning the truth),
      estoppel, laches, unclean hands, affirmation (R-REM-081 to R-REM-085)
   → Conclusion

II. [Party B]'s claim for reformation (R-REM-075)
   A. Prior agreement on the true terms
   B. Writing departs from it
   C. Mutual mistake in expression, or fraud / known unilateral mistake
   D. Clear and convincing evidence
   E. Defenses — BFP reliance; laches; negligence in not reading is NOT a defense
   → Conclusion: writing reformed to [term]

III. Enforcement of the reformed contract — specific performance / injunction / damages (Templates 3–4)
```

## Template 7 — Tort remedies (fraud, trespass, conversion, nuisance)
Use for Torts + Remedies crossovers (Jul 2024 Q5 fraud; Feb 2022 Q3 nuisance and trespass; Jul 2013 Q6 intentional torts).

```
I. Liability — one paragraph per tort (elements; conclusion)

II. Damages
   A. Measure for each tort
      Fraud: out-of-pocket vs. benefit of the bargain; CA § 3343 (R-REM-023)
      Trespass: nominal; harm; rental value; trees CA § 3346 (R-REM-020)
      Conversion: FMV at conversion + interest; CA § 3336 (R-REM-021)
      Nuisance: permanent vs. continuing (R-REM-024)
      Emotional distress / annoyance (R-REM-025)
   B. Limits — proximate cause (CA § 3333), certainty, mitigation
   C. Punitive — § 3294 malice/oppression/fraud, clear and convincing; financial condition;
      due-process ratio
   D. Economic loss rule if the parties had a contract (R-REM-032)

III. Restitution
   A. Disgorgement / waiver of tort — Δ's profits or savings (R-REM-037)
   B. Replevin (conversion) / ejectment (occupation)
   C. Rescission + restitution (fraud in a sale) (R-REM-043)
   D. Constructive trust on proceeds (R-REM-044)

IV. Injunction (Template 2, Part III)
   Continuing trespass or nuisance → multiplicity of suits; balancing; coming to the nuisance; tailored
   relief (R-REM-062)

V. Defenses and best remedy
```

## Template 8 — Personal-services and contract remedies crossover
Use when a performer, artist or employee walks out (Feb 2020 Q3 pattern) or a party seeks SP of a services contract.

```
I. Breach — material? (Contracts R-CTR-066)
II. Damages — cost of a replacement; lost profits (certainty); mitigation (R-REM-014, R-REM-010)
III. Specific performance — DENIED for personal services: Thirteenth Amendment, supervision, quality
     (R-REM-064; R2d § 367; CA Civ. Code § 3390)
IV. Negative injunction — unique services; during the term; reasonable scope; employee can still earn
    a living (Lumley); CA § 3423(e) written-contract and minimum-compensation conditions
V. Restitution — recover advances paid (R-REM-038)
VI. Best remedy
```

## Checklist before you stop writing
- Did every remedy get a **conclusion**?
- Did you **compute** damages where the facts give numbers?
- Did each equitable remedy address **inadequacy, feasibility, balancing and defenses**?
- Did you add the **California sentence** where it matters (§ 3294, § 3387, *IT Corp.*, § 3343, § 1692)?
- Did you end with the **best remedy / election**?
