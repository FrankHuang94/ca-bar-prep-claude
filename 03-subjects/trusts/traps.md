# Trusts — traps

Trusts is not on the MBE, so these are **essay traps**: rules that applicants misstate, mix up or forget to raise. Rule IDs point to [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md).

## Creation and formalities

1. **Precatory words.** "I hope," "I wish" usually means **no trust**: write the gift analysis too, not only the trust analysis (R-TRS-004, R-TRS-039).
2. **"No trustee, no trust" is wrong.** A trust never fails for want of a trustee unless the settlor intended only that person to serve (R-TRS-007).
3. **Merger is rare.** A settlor who is trustee and lifetime beneficiary is fine if anyone else holds a remainder (R-TRS-008).
4. **Unfunded ≠ invalid.** A living trust with no assets can be filled by a pour-over at death; a signed schedule may fund land under *Heggstad* (R-TRS-005, R-TRS-010).
5. **Oral trusts.** Personal property: valid on clear and convincing evidence, but not on the settlor's own words alone. Land: writing required, though a constructive or resulting trust can still arise (R-TRS-030, R-TRS-031).
6. **Secret vs. semi-secret.** Secret (absolute gift on the face of the will) → constructive trust for the intended beneficiary. Semi-secret (the will says "in trust" but no terms) → resulting trust to the estate under the majority view. Students reverse these (R-TRS-032, R-TRS-033).
7. **Pour-over window.** California allows the trust instrument to be signed up to **60 days after** the will; don't fail a pour-over just because the trust came second (R-TRS-010).

## Revocation and modification

8. **Default revocability is reversed in California.** Traditional rule: irrevocable unless reserved. California § 15400: revocable unless **expressly** irrevocable. State both (R-TRS-011).
9. **A will is not a § 15401(a)(2) writing.** The statute says "other than a will." Don't conclude a later will revoked the trust without arguing the trust's own terms and the UTC view (R-TRS-013).
10. **"Exclusive" must be explicit.** A trust method that is merely stated, not labelled exclusive, does not block the statutory method (R-TRS-012).
11. ***Haggerty* is about amendments.** Pre-2024 cases such as *King v. Lynch* required strict compliance with the stated amendment method; they were disapproved. Do not cite them as current law (R-TRS-014).
12. **Community property in a joint trust.** One spouse can **revoke** as to CP alone, but **modification** needs both (R-TRS-015).
13. **Agents can't amend by default.** An agent under a power of attorney may modify or revoke a trust only if the **trust instrument** expressly permits it (§ 15401(c)); broad language in the power of attorney alone is not enough (R-TRS-016).

## Termination

14. **Settlor alive changes everything.** With the settlor's and all beneficiaries' consent, material purpose is irrelevant (§ 15404). Material-purpose analysis applies only when beneficiaries act **without** the settlor (R-TRS-024, R-TRS-025).
15. ***Claflin* vs. § 15403.** *Claflin* bars termination while a material purpose remains; California lets the court balance, and a trust with a valid spendthrift clause may be terminated only for good cause. Write both (R-TRS-025).
16. **"All beneficiaries"** includes contingent remaindermen, minors and the unborn; missing consent defeats § 15403 (R-TRS-025).
17. **Uneconomic-trust threshold changed.** The trustee may terminate if principal ≤ **$100,000** (SB 1127, effective Jan. 1, 2025). Older outlines show a lower figure (R-TRS-027).

## Charitable trusts

18. **Cy pres needs general charitable intent** (California and the common law). Don't skip the intent analysis just because the Restatement (Third) presumes it (R-TRS-021).
19. **The court, not the trustee, picks the new purpose**, and it must be as near as possible. A trustee's preferred project can be rejected (R-TRS-021).
20. **Members of the public lack standing** to enforce a charitable trust; the AG does (R-TRS-020).
21. **Charitable vs. honorary.** A pet trust or a trust to maintain a car is not charitable; it is valid only under §§ 15211–15212 (21 years for purpose trusts) (R-TRS-022, R-TRS-023).

## Creditors

22. **California spendthrift exceptions are broader than the general rule.** General creditors can reach principal already due and payable in full and up to 25% of future payments (reduced for support needs); under general law they get nothing. Don't add § 15307's "excess over support" on top of the 25%: *Carmack* puts both inside the same cap (R-TRS-042, R-TRS-046).
23. **Self-settled = no protection.** The settlor's creditors reach the maximum the trustee could pay the settlor, whatever the spendthrift clause says (R-TRS-047).
24. **Revocable trusts don't shield the settlor**, during life (§ 18200) or after death if the probate estate is short (§ 19001) (R-TRS-092, R-TRS-093).
25. **Discretionary vs. support.** Discretionary: no enforceable right to any amount. Support: right to what is needed. Many trusts are hybrids; analyze both (R-TRS-048, R-TRS-049).

## Trustee duties and liability

26. **Fair price is no defense to self-dealing.** The no-further-inquiry rule ends the analysis unless there is authorization, informed consent or court approval (R-TRS-053).
27. **One act, two breaches.** Investing in the trustee's own business is a **loyalty** breach and a **prudence** breach. Give each its own heading (R-TRS-055, R-TRS-057).
28. **Hindsight.** Prudence is judged when the decision is made, not by the outcome; a loss alone does not prove a breach (R-TRS-058).
29. **Revocable-trust period.** While the settlor is alive and competent, duties run to the settlor; beneficiaries can sue only after death (*Giraldin*), and the settlor's written direction is a defense (R-TRS-065).
30. **Duty to account is not owed while the trust is revocable** (§ 16069) and a power-holder is competent. Don't find a breach for failing to account to remainder beneficiaries then; but if no one holding the power to revoke is competent, notice and accounts are owed to the beneficiaries who would take at death (§ 15800, AB 1079) (R-TRS-063, R-TRS-065).
31. **"Sole and absolute discretion" is not unlimited.** Good faith and the trust's purposes still apply; a trustee-beneficiary is limited to HEMS for himself (R-TRS-069).
32. **Exculpatory clauses never cover profit or bad faith** (R-TRS-084).
33. **Successor trustees can be liable** for failing to fix or pursue a predecessor's breach they knew or should have known about (R-TRS-083).
34. **Third parties.** California protects a good-faith purchaser for value unless it had **actual knowledge** (§ 18100); constructive notice is not enough. The general BFP rule is stricter (R-TRS-095).
35. **Trustee contracts.** Sign "as trustee" and identify the trust, or face personal liability (R-TRS-090).

## Crossovers

36. **Omitted heirs reach revocable trusts** (§ 21601). In a Wills + Trusts essay, a spouse married or a child born after the trust and will may take from trust assets (R-TRS-077; R-WIL-089).
37. **§ 21380 applies to trust amendments.** A gift to the drafter, or to a caregiver of a dependent adult, in a trust amendment is presumptively invalid (R-TRS-076).
38. **A petition against the trustee is not a "contest."** No-contest clauses don't reach petitions to remove a trustee or construe the trust (R-TRS-075).
39. **Don't forget distribution.** After finding a trust failed, say who takes: residue, other residuary takers or heirs (R-TRS-040).
40. **Read the call for "California law."** Trusts calls vary; if silent, lead with general principles and add California (ca-distinctions §1).
