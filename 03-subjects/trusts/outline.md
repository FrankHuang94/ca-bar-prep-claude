The coverage matrix flags that outline's wording as paraphrased from search [VERIFY V-024], so headings below use the matrix text, with three author-added topics (revocability, pour-over, charitable trusts and cy pres). ## I Express trusts

An **express trust** is one the settlor intends to create. It contrasts with **resulting** trusts (implied from presumed intent) and **constructive** trusts (imposed as a remedy for wrongdoing), which are covered in III. Essays almost always begin here: *was a valid trust created, and is it still in force?*

### I.x Creating a valid express trust (definition; parties; creation; purposes; transfer of property; inter vivos and testamentary; pour-over; revocable vs. irrevocable)

#### I.A Definition and required parties

- **Definition.** A trust is a fiduciary relationship with respect to property: the **trustee** holds legal title and owes equitable duties to manage the property for the **beneficiaries**; the **settlor** (trustor, grantor) creates it by manifesting intent (Restatement (Second) of Trusts § 2; Prob. Code §§ 15200–15205) [S-668]. R-TRS-001.
- **Parties.**
  - *Settlor*: must have capacity and intent (R-TRS-003, R-TRS-004).
  - *Trustee*: holds legal title, owes fiduciary duties (V). An individual, a bank or trust company, or co-trustees.
  - *Beneficiaries*: hold equitable interests: present (income) and future (remainder) interests; ascertainable for a private trust (I.x2).
- **One person, several roles.** A settlor can be trustee (declaration of trust) and a beneficiary. The only bar is **merger**: a sole trustee who is also the sole beneficiary of every interest. A typical California living trust (settlor = trustee = lifetime beneficiary; children = remainder beneficiaries) is valid because the children hold equitable remainders (common law; Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 69 [VERIFY V-562]). R-TRS-008.
- **A trust never fails for want of a trustee.** If the named trustee dies, declines, or is removed, the court fills the vacancy (Prob. Code § 15660), unless the settlor intended the trust to exist only if that person served. Acceptance is by signing or by knowingly acting (§ 15600); silence for an unreasonable time is rejection (§ 15601) [VERIFY V-569]. R-TRS-007.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Trust Law's three-party structure (委托人 / 受托人 / 受益人) matches settlor / trustee / beneficiary, but the PRC statute's "entrust" (委托给) language leaves the trustee's title less clear than the common-law split of legal and equitable title (approx.).

#### I.B Creation: the five requirements

Write: *"A valid express trust requires (1) a settlor with capacity, (2) intent to create a trust, (3) trust property, (4) a lawful purpose and (5) an ascertainable beneficiary (or a charitable purpose)."* Then go element by element.

1. **Methods of creation (§ 15200)** [S-668]. A trust may be created by: (a) the owner's **declaration** that he or she holds property as trustee; (b) an **inter vivos transfer** to another as trustee; (c) a **transfer by will**; (d) the **exercise of a power of appointment** in trust; (e) an **enforceable promise** to create a trust. **No consideration** is required (§ 15208). R-TRS-002.
2. **Capacity.** The settlor must have capacity to create, amend or revoke. California uses the decision-specific test of §§ 810–812, but where a trust or amendment closely resembles a will or codicil in content and complexity, courts apply the testamentary test of § 6100.5 (*Andersen v. Hunt* (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 722 [S-1111]; applied in *Eyford v. Nord* (2021) to an amendment that disinherited one set of beneficiaries [S-1112]). Undue influence (Prob. Code § 86; R-WIL-039, R-WIL-040) and the § 21380 presumption (R-TRS-076) apply to trusts. R-TRS-003.
3. **Intent (§ 15201)** [S-668]. A **present** manifestation of intent to impose **enforceable duties**. No magic words ("trust" or "trustee" are neither required nor conclusive).
   - **Precatory words** ("wish," "hope," "request," "desire") ordinarily create no trust; the recipient takes outright (R-TRS-039). Context matters: a direction to a fiduciary, or a gift that would otherwise be pointless, may show a duty.
   - **Future intent** ("I will set up a trust for you next year") creates nothing unless an enforceable promise.
   - R-TRS-004.
4. **Trust property (res) (§ 15202)** [S-668]. Must be an **existing, identifiable** interest the settlor owns.
   - **Expectancies** (a hoped-for inheritance, profits not yet earned) cannot be trust property until they exist and the settlor reaffirms intent (common law).
   - **Funding.** A living trust is created only as to property actually transferred or declared. A **signed trust instrument in which the settlor-trustee declares that listed property is held in trust** can make that property trust property without a separate deed; for land, the signed declaration satisfies the statute of frauds (*Estate of Heggstad* (1993) 16 Cal.App.4th 943 [S-1109]). Unfunded assets otherwise pass by will (often a pour-over, I.F) or intestacy.
   - R-TRS-005.
5. **Lawful purpose (§ 15203)** [S-668]. Any purpose not illegal or against public policy. Invalid: trusts to defraud creditors, to induce crime, conditions intended to **restrain marriage** (Civ. Code § 710 [VERIFY V-589]) or to **encourage divorce**. A limitation "until she marries" can be valid if it reflects support rather than an intent to forbid marriage. R-TRS-006.
6. **Beneficiary (§ 15205)**: see I.x2.
7. **Trustee**: see I.A (not strictly required at creation).

**How tested.** Feb 2015 Q6 (a testamentary trust for grandchildren with a zoo as remainder beneficiary) [VERIFY V-071]. Expect creation issues in Wills + Trusts crossovers: a testamentary trust whose validity depends on the will, a living trust partly unfunded, precatory language in a will.

#### I.C Valid trust purposes

- **Private trusts** may serve any lawful purpose: support of family, management for a minor or spendthrift, tax planning, holding a business.
- **Charitable trusts** (I.x2) must serve a charitable purpose that benefits the public.
- **Honorary (noncharitable purpose) trusts** and **pet trusts** (I.x2) are allowed by statute with limits.
- **Illegal or public-policy purposes** fail, in whole or in part (§ 15203). If a single condition is invalid, courts strike the condition and enforce the rest when that matches intent.
- **Effect of failure:** resulting trust to the settlor or estate (III; R-TRS-036).

#### I.D Transfer of property (funding the trust)

- **Declaration of trust:** no transfer needed; the owner becomes trustee. For land, the signed declaration satisfies § 15206 (II.A).
- **Transfer to a third-party trustee:** requires an effective conveyance: deed for land, assignment or re-registration for securities, delivery for chattels, change of account title for bank accounts.
- **Unfunded or partly funded living trust:** valid as to what is funded. Assets left outside pass by will or intestacy; the usual fix is a pour-over will (I.F) or a § 850 petition (XII.C) using *Heggstad*.
- **Additions:** a settlor may add property later; a revocable trust may be funded by pour-over at death.
- **Totten trusts** ("A in trust for B" bank accounts): revocable will substitutes; at death the balance passes to the beneficiary unless clear and convincing evidence shows a different intent (Prob. Code §§ 80, 5301–5302 [VERIFY V-538]). R-TRS-017; R-WIL-101.

#### I.E Inter vivos and testamentary trusts

| | Inter vivos (living) trust | Testamentary trust |
|---|---|---|
| Created | During life, by declaration or transfer | By will, effective at death |
| Formalities | Writing for land (§ 15206); oral trust of personalty provable by clear and convincing evidence (§ 15207) | Will formalities (§ 6110, § 6111) |
| Probate | Funded assets avoid probate | Assets pass through probate first |
| Revocability | California default: revocable unless expressly irrevocable (§ 15400) | Irrevocable once the testator dies |
| Court supervision | Only on petition (§ 17200) | Probate court has continuing jurisdiction on petition |

- **Will-substitute treatment.** A revocable living trust is treated like a will for omitted spouse and omitted child purposes (§ 21601; R-WIL-089), the disqualified-donee presumption (§ 21380), Division 11 construction rules, and former-spouse rules (§ 5040) [S-605]. R-TRS-077; R-WIL-102.
- **Feb 2015 pattern.** Testamentary trust for grandchildren with a charitable remainder; the testator later came under a conservatorship. Validity of the will (and the conservator's acts, R-WIL-074) determine what reaches the trust. R-TRS-009.

#### I.F Pour-over wills and testamentary additions to trusts (pour-over trusts)

*Author-added row TRS-SUP-002. The full rule is a Wills rule, R-WIL-061; this section gives the Trusts angle.*

- **Rule (§ 6300, as amended effective Jan 1, 2018).** A will may devise property to the **trustee of a trust identified in the will** whose terms are in a **written instrument executed before, concurrently with, or within 60 days after** the will (or in the valid will of a person who predeceased the testator), regardless of the existence, size or character of the trust corpus [S-611] [S-612]. R-TRS-010; R-WIL-061.
- **Unfunded trust is fine.** The pour-over itself funds the trust at death.
- **Amendments count.** The devise is valid even though the trust is amendable or revocable, and the property is administered under the trust **as amended before or after** the testator's death [S-611]. This is the key difference from incorporation by reference, which captures only the document as it existed when the will was signed (R-WIL-058).
- **Not a testamentary trust.** Unless the will provides otherwise, the poured-over property becomes part of the existing trust; it is not a separate testamentary trust.
- **Revoked trust → lapse.** If the trust is revoked or terminated before the testator's death, the devise lapses unless the will provides otherwise [S-611] [VERIFY V-533].
- **Validity chain on an essay.** (1) Will valid? (2) § 6300 satisfied? (3) Trust validly created and not revoked? (4) Which version of the trust (amendments: *Haggerty*, I.G)? (5) Gifts in the trust valid (§ 21380, omitted heirs)?
- **CA note.** The 60-day post-will window is California's 2018 addition; the traditional Uniform Testamentary Additions to Trusts Act required the trust to exist before or with the will.

#### I.G Revocable vs. irrevocable trusts

##### Revocability of trusts: California default rule vs. majority rule; methods of revocation and modification

*Author-added row TRS-SUP-001. Tested Feb 2016 Q1 (was the trust revoked by a later will?) [VERIFY V-071]; the 2024 *Haggerty* decision makes modification high-yield (recent-changes §7, Apply).*

**1. The default.**

| Rule | Content | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional common law (majority of older cases) | A trust is **irrevocable** unless the settlor expressly reserved a power to revoke | Restatement (Second) of Trusts § 330 [VERIFY V-923] |
| Uniform Trust Code (modern) | Revocable unless the terms expressly make it irrevocable (for trusts created after enactment) | UTC § 602(a) [VERIFY V-594] |
| **California** | "Unless a trust is expressly made irrevocable by the trust instrument, the trust is revocable by the settlor." Applies where the settlor was domiciled in California at creation, the instrument was executed in California, or the instrument selects California law | Prob. Code § 15400 [S-1113] |

**Write:** "Under the traditional rule a trust is irrevocable unless the settlor reserved a power to revoke; California, like the UTC, presumes the opposite: a trust is revocable unless expressly made irrevocable (§ 15400). Because W created the trust in California and it does not say it is irrevocable, it is revocable." R-TRS-011.

**2. Methods of revocation (§ 15401(a))** [S-660]. A revocable trust may be revoked in whole or in part:
1. by **compliance with any method stated in the trust instrument**; or
2. by a **writing, other than a will, signed by the settlor** (or another person holding the power) **and delivered to the trustee during the settlor's lifetime**.

- **Exclusivity.** If the instrument **explicitly** makes its method the exclusive method, only that method works. A stated method that is not labelled exclusive leaves the statutory method available [S-660].
- **Settlor as trustee.** Where the settlor is the sole trustee, argue that holding the signed writing in her capacity as trustee satisfies delivery; *Masry v. Masry* (2008) 166 Cal.App.4th 738 is cited on § 15401 exclusivity, but no case holding on settlor-trustee delivery has been verified for this repo [VERIFY V-553]. With co-trustees (Feb 2016: settlor and her daughter), argue whether delivery to the other co-trustee was needed.
- **Revocation by will.** The statutory method excludes wills. A later will revokes the trust only if the trust instrument allows revocation by will (and then only at death). Property already in a funded trust is not in the probate estate, so the will cannot pass it. **Other side:** UTC § 602(c) allows revocation by a later will or codicil that expressly refers to the trust or specifically devises trust property, if the trust's method is not exclusive [VERIFY V-594]. Feb 2016 selected answers reached opposite conclusions; argue both. R-TRS-012, R-TRS-013.
- **Partial revocation** (withdrawing a single asset) uses the same methods.
- **Joint settlors and community property.** Unless the instrument says otherwise, each settlor may revoke as to the portion he or she contributed, except as Fam. Code § 761 provides (§ 15401(b) [S-1114]). For **community property** in a revocable trust, **either spouse alone may revoke** as to CP, but **modification** as to CP during marriage requires **both spouses' joinder or consent** (Fam. Code § 761) [S-681] [VERIFY V-585]. R-TRS-015.
- **Who may exercise.** The settlor (or another person to whom the instrument gives the power). An **agent under a power of attorney** may modify or revoke only if the **trust instrument** expressly permits it (§ 15401(c)) [S-1114]; the power of attorney must also grant the authority expressly (§ 4264), and a **conservator** needs court approval (substituted judgment, § 2580) [VERIFY V-922]. R-TRS-016.
- **At death** the trust becomes irrevocable; the trustee must then give the § 16061.7 notice (V.H).

**3. Modification (§ 15402) and *Haggerty v. Thornton*.**
- § 15402: "Unless the trust instrument provides otherwise, if a trust is revocable by the settlor, the settlor may modify the trust by the procedure for revocation."
- **Before 2024**, Courts of Appeal split. *King v. Lynch* (2012) 204 Cal.App.4th 1186 held that where the trust specified a modification method, that method had to be used [S-129].
- ***Haggerty v. Thornton* (2024) 15 Cal.5th 729**: a revocable trust may be modified by the statutory revocation procedure in § 15401(a)(2) (a signed writing delivered to the trustee) **unless the trust instrument provides a method of modification and explicitly makes that method exclusive**. The Court disapproved *King v. Lynch* and similar decisions [S-129] [S-650] [S-129]. R-TRS-014.
- **Write:** "Under § 15402 and *Haggerty*, S may amend by any signed writing delivered to the trustee unless the trust's amendment clause is explicitly exclusive. The clause here says only that amendments 'may' be made by notarized writing; it is not exclusive, so S's signed, delivered amendment is valid."
- **General-law contrast.** The traditional rule required strict compliance with a stated method; UTC § 602(c) permits any method manifesting clear and convincing intent unless the stated method is exclusive [VERIFY V-594].

**4. Irrevocable trusts** can be modified or terminated only as in I.J (consent, changed circumstances, uneconomic size, cy pres).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: under the PRC Trust Law a settlor generally cannot unilaterally revoke a trust that benefits others unless the trust document reserves the right; that is closer to the traditional common-law rule than to California's revocable-by-default rule (approx.).

### I.x2 Ascertainable beneficiaries (and charitable trusts)

- **Private trusts need a beneficiary** identified or ascertainable under the instrument, now or within the perpetuities period (§ 15205 [S-668] [VERIFY V-589]). R-TRS-018.
  - **Class gifts** ("my children," "my grandchildren," "my nieces and nephews") are ascertainable even if membership changes.
  - **Indefinite groups** ("my friends," "worthy people") fail as private trusts at common law. California allows a beneficiary to be identified by a **power to select** from a sufficiently described class [VERIFY V-589]. If the class is truly indefinite, test whether the trust can be **charitable** or **honorary**.
  - **Unborn beneficiaries** are fine; they are represented by a guardian ad litem in proceedings.
- **Consequence of failure:** resulting trust for the settlor or estate (R-TRS-036).

#### Charitable trusts and cy pres

*Author-added row TRS-SUP-003. Tested Feb 2015 Q6 (zoo remainder) and Feb 2025 Q2 (petition to redirect a research trust after a cure was found) [VERIFY V-071].*

**1. What is charitable.** Purposes recognized as charitable: relief of poverty; advancement of education; advancement of religion; promotion of health; governmental or municipal purposes; and other purposes beneficial to the community (Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 28 [VERIFY V-559]). The beneficiaries must be the **public or an indefinite class**, not named individuals. The court first decides whether a charitable trust was created at all; if not, cy pres cannot apply (*Estate of Faulkner* (1954) 128 Cal.App.2d 575) [S-682]. R-TRS-019.

**2. Special features.**
- **No ascertainable beneficiary needed.**
- **Perpetual duration allowed**; the rule against perpetuities does not limit a charitable trust (II.B; R-TRS-035).
- **Enforcement by the Attorney General**, which has primary responsibility for supervising charitable trusts in California (Gov. Code § 12598) [S-671]; co-trustees and persons with a special interest may also sue, but not members of the public (*Holt v. College of Osteopathic Physicians & Surgeons* (1964) 61 Cal.2d 750 [VERIFY V-560]). R-TRS-020.
- **Favored construction**: courts construe ambiguous gifts in favor of charity [S-682].

**3. Cy pres.**
- **Rule.** If the specific charitable purpose becomes **impossible, impracticable or illegal** (the Restatement (Third) adds **wasteful**), and the settlor had a **general charitable intent**, the court directs the property to another charitable purpose **as near as possible** to the original. Cy pres is "an equitable power" that lets a court carry out a testamentary charitable trust where the testator expressed a general charitable intent (*Estate of Gatlin* (1971) 16 Cal.App.3d 644) [S-670]. R-TRS-021.
- **Steps on an essay:**
  1. Is it a valid charitable trust?
  2. Has the specific purpose become impossible, impracticable, illegal (or wasteful)? Accomplishment of the purpose (a cure is found) counts as impossibility of continuing it.
  3. **General or specific intent?** Evidence: breadth of the stated purpose; the settlor's other charitable giving; any **gift over** on failure (a gift over shows specific intent and defeats cy pres); the settlor's ties to the cause.
  4. **Nearest purpose.** The court, not the trustee, picks a purpose as near as possible. A trustee's preferred project that is only loosely related may be rejected.
  5. **If cy pres fails**: resulting trust to the settlor's estate (III).
- **Modern presumption.** The Restatement (Third) § 67 and UTC § 413 presume a general charitable intent [VERIFY V-559] [VERIFY V-594]. California applies the common-law test (general charitable intent required) through case law; on the essay, state the test, then note the modern presumption.
- **Petition.** The trustee or AG petitions the probate court (Prob. Code § 17200 [VERIFY V-559]); the AG must be given notice in matters affecting charitable trusts [VERIFY V-560].
- **Administrative deviation vs. cy pres.** Changing *how* a charitable trust is run (investment limits, location of a scholarship office) is deviation (§ 15409; R-TRS-026). Changing *what* charitable purpose it serves is cy pres.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Trust Law also allows a failed or impossible charitable (公益) trust's property to be applied to a similar charitable purpose with official approval, a cy pres analogue; under the 2016 Charity Law such trusts are called 慈善信托 and have a supervisor (监察人) rather than an Attorney General (approx.).

#### Honorary and pet trusts

- **Honorary (noncharitable purpose) trusts (§ 15211).** A trust for a lawful noncharitable purpose, or for a noncharitable corporation or unincorporated society, may be performed by the trustee for **only 21 years**, whether or not there is a beneficiary who can enforce it [S-676] [VERIFY V-558]. Example: maintaining a vintage car or a private garden. R-TRS-022.
- **Pet trusts (§ 15212).** A trust for the care of a **domestic or pet animal** is a valid trust for a lawful noncharitable purpose, governed by its own rules: it may be enforced by a person **named** in the instrument or **appointed** by the court; unless the instrument provides otherwise it **terminates when no animal covered by the trust remains alive** (whether the covered animals are those living at the settlor's death or during the settlor's lifetime is unconfirmed); and accounting rules apply above a value threshold [S-676] [VERIFY V-558]. R-TRS-023.
- **Common-law background.** Without a statute, a trust with no ascertainable beneficiary and no charitable purpose fails; some courts allowed the trustee to perform voluntarily ("honorary"). California's statutes replace that uncertainty.

### I.J Modification and termination of trust

*Tested Feb 2012 Q1 (can the beneficiaries terminate?) and Jul 2025 Q1 (petition to dissolve two testamentary trusts) [VERIFY V-071].* Work through these routes in order.

**1. Revocable trust, settlor alive and competent:** the settlor revokes or modifies (I.G). No one else's consent is needed (except both spouses for CP modification).

**2. Settlor plus all beneficiaries (§ 15404)** [S-1102]. By the **written** consent of the settlor and all beneficiaries, even an irrevocable trust may be modified or terminated **without court approval, without the trustee's consent, and even if a material purpose remains** (§ 15404(a)). If a beneficiary does not consent, the other beneficiaries, with the settlor's consent, may petition, and the court may modify or terminate if the nonconsenting beneficiaries' interests are not substantially impaired (§ 15404(b)). R-TRS-024.

**3. All beneficiaries without the settlor (§ 15403)** [S-674] [S-1100].
- All beneficiaries must consent: watch for **minors, unborn or unascertained remainder beneficiaries**, who cannot consent and must be represented.
- **Material purpose.** If continuing the trust is necessary to carry out a material purpose, the court may modify or terminate only if, in its discretion, the reason for doing so **outweighs** the interest in accomplishing that purpose.
- **Spendthrift trusts.** If the trust is subject to a valid spendthrift restraint, it may not be terminated under § 15403 unless the court finds **good cause** [S-1100].
- **Classic material purposes:** a **spendthrift** clause; **discretionary** or **support** provisions; a **postponed age** of distribution ("until age 35"); successive interests designed to protect a beneficiary; a purpose to keep a family business or farm in the family. Older text of § 15403 gave the court no discretion to terminate a trust subject to a valid spendthrift restraint; the current text permits it for good cause [S-1100].
- **General-law contrast (*Claflin v. Claflin* (Mass. 1889) 149 Mass. 19):** beneficiaries cannot compel termination while a material purpose remains unaccomplished, full stop. California's balancing test is more flexible; write both. R-TRS-025.
- *Boys & Girls Club of Petaluma v. Walsh* (2008) is reported to hold that broad trustee discretion was not itself a material purpose barring a consensual modification; cite and holding unconfirmed, so do not rely on it on the exam [VERIFY V-920].

**4. Changed circumstances (§ 15409)** [S-1101]. On petition by a trustee or beneficiary, the court may modify the **administrative or dispositive** provisions or terminate if, **owing to circumstances not known to and not anticipated by the settlor**, continuing under the trust's terms would **defeat or substantially impair** its purposes; the court may order acts the instrument forbids. A spendthrift restraint is a factor the court must consider, but it does not preclude modification or termination (§ 15409(b)). R-TRS-026.

**5. Uneconomic trust (§ 15408)** [S-656] [S-657] [S-1103].
- **Court route:** on petition, the court may modify or terminate if the principal's value has become so low relative to the cost of administration that continuing would defeat or substantially impair the purposes.
- **Trustee route:** unless the instrument provides otherwise, the trustee of a trust whose principal does **not exceed $100,000** in fair market value may terminate it without a court order, distributing consistently with the purposes. The threshold was **$20,000** when enacted [S-657] and was raised to **$100,000 by SB 1127 (Stats. 2024, ch. 76, effective January 1, 2025)** [S-656] [S-1103]; the figure immediately before SB 1127 ($40,000 or $50,000) and whether the new figure is inflation-indexed are unconfirmed [VERIFY V-921]. **Recent change**: confirm the figure in the June 2027 update.
- R-TRS-027.

**6. Termination by its own terms (§§ 15407, 15410)** [VERIFY V-557]: expiration of the term, accomplishment of the purpose, purpose becoming unlawful or impossible, merger. On termination the trustee winds up and distributes as the instrument directs; on revocation, to the settlor. R-TRS-028.

**7. Combination and division (§§ 15411–15412)** [VERIFY V-557]: the court may combine trusts with substantially similar terms, or divide a trust, if purposes and beneficiaries' interests are not impaired. Relevant where, as in Jul 2025 Q1, there are two trusts from one estate. R-TRS-029.

**8. Charitable trusts:** cy pres (I.x2) or deviation.

**9. Decanting.** California's Uniform Trust Decanting Act (Prob. Code §§ 19501 et seq.) lets a trustee with distribution discretion distribute to a second trust with modified terms, within limits [VERIFY V-592]. Nice-to-know.

**Essay structure for "can the trust be terminated?":** (1) Is the settlor alive and is the trust revocable? (2) Who must consent and have they? (3) Material purpose: identify it, then balance (§ 15403) or bar (*Claflin*). (4) Changed circumstances (§ 15409). (5) Uneconomic (§ 15408). (6) Conclusion with distribution consequences.

## II Statute of frauds and rule against perpetuities

### II.A Statute of frauds

- **Real property (§ 15206)** [S-668] [S-684]. A trust in relation to real property is valid only if evidenced by: (a) a written instrument signed by the **trustee** (or the trustee's agent authorized in writing); (b) a written instrument **conveying** the property signed by the **settlor** (or the settlor's agent authorized in writing); or (c) **operation of law**. R-TRS-030.
  - "Operation of law" covers **resulting and constructive trusts**, so an oral promise to hold land in trust, though unenforceable as an express trust, may support a constructive trust if the promisor obtained the land by fraud or abuse of a confidential relationship.
  - A trust of land may be recorded (§ 15210).
- **Personal property (§ 15207)** [S-669]. An **oral** trust of personal property is valid, but its existence and terms may be established **only by clear and convincing evidence**, and **the settlor's oral declaration, standing alone, is not sufficient**. R-TRS-031.
- **Testamentary trusts** must meet will formalities; their terms must be in the will or a validly incorporated document (R-WIL-058).
- **Secret trusts** (absolute gift on the face of the will; devisee secretly promised to hold for another): the court admits extrinsic evidence and imposes a **constructive trust** for the intended beneficiary (common law; Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 18 [VERIFY V-561]). R-TRS-032.
- **Semi-secret trusts** (will shows a trust but omits the beneficiary or purpose): traditional majority → express trust fails, **resulting trust** for the testator's estate; modern Restatement view → **constructive trust** for the intended beneficiary [VERIFY V-561]. R-TRS-033.

| | Secret trust | Semi-secret trust |
|---|---|---|
| Face of will | Absolute gift to D | Gift "to D in trust" without terms |
| Extrinsic evidence? | Yes, to prove D's promise | Majority: no; modern: yes |
| Result | Constructive trust for intended beneficiary | Majority: resulting trust for the estate; modern: constructive trust |

### II.B Rule against perpetuities

- **California: Uniform Statutory Rule Against Perpetuities (Prob. Code §§ 21200 et seq.).** A nonvested property interest is valid if (1) when created it is **certain to vest or terminate within a life in being plus 21 years**, or (2) it **actually vests or terminates within 90 years** after creation (wait-and-see) (§ 21205). If an interest violates the rule, the court **reforms** the disposition to approximate the transferor's plan within the 90-year period (§ 21220) [VERIFY V-563]. R-TRS-034.
- **When the period starts.** For an interest in a **revocable** trust, the period starts when the settlor's power to revoke ends (usually at death), because until then the settlor is effectively the owner [VERIFY V-563].
- **Trust applications.** Typical traps: gifts to grandchildren "who reach 25"; gifts to a class that can reopen; administrative contingencies ("when my estate is distributed"). Under USRAP almost all of these are saved by waiting 90 years or by reformation.
- **Charitable trusts** may last forever; a gift over from one charity to another is exempt; a gift over between private and charitable interests is subject to the rule (R-TRS-035) [VERIFY V-563].
- **Honorary trusts** are capped at 21 years (§ 15211); pet trusts last until no animal covered by the trust remains alive (§ 15212) [VERIFY V-558] (I.x2).
- **MBE vs. essay.** Real Property MBE questions use the common-law rule (no wait-and-see). On a California Trusts essay, state the common-law test, then USRAP.

## III Failure of express trust (resulting trusts; constructive trusts; gifts; intestacy)

When an express trust fails, the essay asks **where the property goes**. Work through four possible answers in this order: resulting trust → constructive trust → gift → will residue or intestacy.

### III.A Resulting trusts

- **Nature.** Arises by **operation of law** from the **presumed intent** of the transferor that the transferee was not meant to take the beneficial interest; no writing needed (§ 15206(c)). The resulting trustee's only duty is to reconvey. R-REM-050.
- **Situations:**
  1. **Failure of an express trust** in whole or part: no ascertainable beneficiary, illegal purpose, perpetuities violation, charitable purpose fails and cy pres is unavailable, a semi-secret trust (majority view) (R-TRS-036).
  2. **Excess corpus**: purposes fully accomplished with property left over (e.g., a trust to pay for a child's education when the child dies young).
  3. **Purchase-money resulting trust**: A pays for property titled in B's name → resulting trust for A presumed, unless gift or loan intended; **gift presumed** if B is A's spouse, child or other natural object of bounty (R-TRS-037) (common law; Restatement (Third) of Trusts §§ 7–9 [VERIFY V-564]).
- **Who takes:** the settlor or, if dead, the settlor's estate (will residue or heirs).
- **CA note.** Between spouses, the community property presumptions decide characterization (R-CMP rules); purchase-money resulting trust arguments rarely displace them.

### III.B Constructive trusts

- **Statute.** "One who gains a thing by fraud, accident, mistake, undue influence, the violation of a trust, or other wrongful act, is, unless he or she has some other and better right thereto, an involuntary trustee of the thing gained, for the benefit of the person who would otherwise have had it" (Civ. Code § 2224); one who wrongfully detains a thing is an involuntary trustee for the owner (Civ. Code § 2223). R-TRS-038; R-REM-044.
- **It is a remedy, not a trust relationship**: it compels reconveyance of specific property or its traceable product (R-REM-047, R-REM-048) and captures appreciation.
- **Trust settings where it arises:**
  - a trustee who buys property with trust funds in his own name;
  - a **secret trust** devisee (II.A);
  - an oral promise to hold land, where the promisor obtained it by fraud or in a confidential relationship;
  - a person who obtained a trust amendment by **undue influence**;
  - a third party who knowingly received trust property in breach (XII).

| | Resulting trust | Constructive trust |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Presumed intent | Wrongdoing / unjust enrichment |
| Typical trigger | Failed express trust; purchase-money | Fraud, undue influence, breach of trust, secret trust |
| Who benefits | Settlor or settlor's estate | Person who would otherwise have had the property |
| Writing needed? | No (operation of law) | No (operation of law) |

### III.C Gifts

- If the transferor **did not intend a trust** (precatory words; the transferee was meant to benefit), the transferee takes an **outright gift** (R-TRS-039).
- An **imperfect lifetime gift** (no delivery) is not saved by calling it a declaration of trust unless the donor intended to become a trustee.
- **Precatory language on an essay:** discuss both: (1) no trust because only a wish → gift; (2) trust because the context imposes a duty → apply trust rules.

### III.D Where the property goes: will residue or intestacy

- **Failed testamentary trust** → property falls into the will's **residue** (Prob. Code § 21111; R-WIL-065). If the failed trust *was* the residue, the share passes to **other residuary beneficiaries**; if none, by **intestacy** (§§ 6400–6402; R-WIL-015, R-WIL-016, R-WIL-018). Check **anti-lapse** first if the failure is a beneficiary's predeceasing (R-WIL-063).
- **Failed inter vivos trust** → resulting trust to the **settlor**, or to the settlor's **estate** (passing by will or intestacy).
- **Failed pour-over** → if § 6300 fails and incorporation by reference fails, the devise fails and passes under the residuary clause or intestacy. R-TRS-040.
- **Essay finish.** End every failure analysis with the destination: "Therefore the $X passes by resulting trust to T's estate and, because the will's residue is the failed trust, to T's heirs by intestacy: half to W (CP half already hers)..."

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: when a PRC trust fails or terminates without a named recipient, the property generally returns to the settlor or the settlor's heirs, which resembles the US resulting trust (approx.).

## IV Restrictions on beneficial interest (spendthrift provisions; discretionary and support trusts)

*Tested Feb 2019 Q1 (could creditors reach trust assets?) [VERIFY V-071]. The State Bar outline lists spendthrift, discretionary and support trusts.*

### IV.A Spendthrift provisions

- **Validity (§§ 15300–15301)** [S-672]. If the instrument provides that a beneficiary's interest in **income** (§ 15300) or **principal** (§ 15301(a)) is not subject to **voluntary or involuntary transfer**, the restraint is valid: the beneficiary cannot assign, and creditors cannot reach the interest **before payment**. R-TRS-041.
- **Must restrain both** voluntary and involuntary transfer to be a true spendthrift clause (common law; the statute speaks of both).
- **Once paid**, money in the beneficiary's hands is reachable like any other property.
- **Principal due and payable (§ 15301(b)).** After an amount of principal has become due and payable, a judgment creditor may petition, and the court may order the trustee to satisfy the judgment out of it [S-672]. Under *Carmack v. Reynolds* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 844, a general creditor may reach up to the **full amount** of distributions already due and payable but still in the trustee's hands (except amounts explicitly intended or actually required for support); the 25% cap does not apply to them [S-655]. R-TRS-042.
- **Material purpose.** A spendthrift clause is a classic material purpose that weighs against termination by beneficiaries (I.J).
- **Self-settled**: invalid as to the settlor (IV.B).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Trust Law's principle that trust property is independent (信托财产独立性) and generally immune from enforcement against the beneficiary's creditors resembles spendthrift protection, but it lacks California's detailed exception list (approx.).

### IV.B Exceptions to spendthrift protection (California)

| Creditor | Rule | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Settlor's own creditors** (self-settled trust) | Restraint invalid against the settlor's creditors; if discretionary or support, creditors reach the **maximum the trustee could pay** the settlor, up to the settlor's proportionate contribution | § 15304 [S-672] [S-1107] (R-TRS-047) |
| **Child or spousal support** | Court may order trustee to pay support from income or principal payable (or that the trustee has decided to pay) to the beneficiary, as equitable | § 15305 [S-672] (R-TRS-043) |
| **Restitution / damages for a felony** of which the beneficiary was convicted | Reachable notwithstanding the restraint | § 15305.5 [S-673] [S-1122] (R-TRS-044) |
| **Public support reimbursement** (State or county) | Reachable within statutory limits; not distributions required for a disabled beneficiary's care (§ 15306(b)) | § 15306 [S-655] (R-TRS-045) |
| **General judgment creditors**: 25% | Court may order up to **25%** of each future payment otherwise due to the beneficiary paid to the creditor, considering the support needs of the beneficiary and dependents | § 15306.5 [S-654] [S-655] (R-TRS-046) |
| **General judgment creditors**: excess over support | Amounts payable that **exceed what is necessary for the beneficiary's education and support** may be applied to the judgment, but § 15307 orders count toward the same 25% cap (*Carmack*) | § 15307 [S-672] [S-655] (R-TRS-046) |
| **Principal due and payable** | Court may order payment to the creditor, up to the full amount due (no 25% cap) | § 15301(b) [S-672] [S-655] (R-TRS-042) |

- **General-law contrast.** Under the traditional rule, a valid spendthrift clause bars general creditors entirely; only **exception creditors** (child or spousal support, certain governmental claims, and, in some states, suppliers of necessaries) can reach the interest (UTC § 503 [VERIFY V-594]). California's **due-principal**, **25%** and **excess-over-support** rules are distinctive. Write the general rule, then: "California, however, allows a general creditor to reach due principal and up to 25% of future payments..."
- *Carmack v. Reynolds* (2017): the California Supreme Court held the Probate Code does not impose an absolute 25% limit on a general creditor's access to a spendthrift trust. The creditor (there, a bankruptcy trustee) may reach due-and-payable distributions in full (§ 15301(b)) and, separately, up to 25% of anticipated payments, reduced by what the beneficiary needs for support; orders under §§ 15306.5 and 15307 together may not exceed 25% of a payment [S-655] [S-1105].

### IV.C Discretionary trusts

- **Rule (§ 15303)** [S-672] [S-1106]. Where the trustee pays "so much as the trustee in its discretion sees fit," a creditor or transferee **cannot compel** the trustee to pay any amount payable only in the exercise of discretion. R-TRS-048.
- **After the trustee decides to pay**, the payment becomes subject to the support, restitution and general-creditor provisions (§§ 15305–15307).
- **Notice of transfer or levy.** If the trustee knows of a transfer or has been served with process by a judgment creditor and still makes a discretionary payment to the beneficiary, the trustee is liable to the transferee or creditor to the extent the payment impairs its rights, **unless** the interest is subject to a valid spendthrift restraint (§ 15303(b)) [S-1106].
- **Self-settled discretionary trust**: creditors reach the maximum the trustee could pay the settlor (§ 15304).
- **Beneficiary's rights:** the beneficiary can compel the trustee only to exercise discretion in good faith and reasonably (VI).

### IV.D Support trusts

- **Rule (§ 15302)** [S-672] [S-1108]. Where the instrument directs payments **for the education or support** of the beneficiary, the interest, **to the extent necessary** for education or support, cannot be transferred and is not subject to enforcement of a money judgment until paid, subject to §§ 15304–15307. R-TRS-049.
- **General law:** suppliers of **necessaries** (food, shelter, medical care furnished to the beneficiary) may reach a support trust.
- **Distinguish:** a **support** trust gives the beneficiary an enforceable right to support (trustee must pay what is needed); a **discretionary** trust gives no enforceable right to any amount, only to good-faith exercise of discretion. Many trusts are hybrids ("in its discretion, for support"): analyze both.

### IV.E Creditors of the settlor (revocable trusts)

See XI.D. In brief: a settlor's creditors reach a **revocable** trust during life to the extent of the power to revoke (§ 18200) [S-664], and after death to the extent the probate estate is insufficient (§ 19001) [S-665]. A spendthrift clause never protects a settlor from his or her own creditors (§ 15304). R-TRS-047, R-TRS-092, R-TRS-093.

## V Duties of trustee (standard of care; loyalty; prudent investing; self-dealing; duty to account)

*Tested Feb 2016 Q1 (self-dealing purchase at fair market value; investing trust assets in the trustee's own business) and Jul 2025 Q1 (trustee's handling of two trusts) [VERIFY V-071].* On an essay, take **one duty per heading**: state the duty, apply the facts, conclude breach or no breach, then remedies (VIII–IX).

### V.A Duty to administer according to the trust; acceptance

- On accepting the trust (§ 15600), the trustee must administer it **according to the trust instrument** and, except as the instrument validly provides otherwise, the statutory duties (§ 16000) [VERIFY V-569]. R-TRS-050.
- The instrument can expand or restrict many duties (e.g., authorize retention of a family business), but it cannot license **bad faith** or excuse intentional or reckless breaches (§ 16461; IX.C).
- A trustee who has not accepted owes no duties; a trustee may resign as the instrument allows, with beneficiary consent, or with court approval.

### V.B Standard of care (§ 16040)

- "The trustee shall administer the trust with **reasonable care, skill, and caution** under the circumstances then prevailing that a **prudent person acting in a like capacity** would use in the conduct of an enterprise of like character and with like aims to accomplish the purposes of the trust" (§ 16040) [VERIFY V-569]. R-TRS-051.
- **Professional and expert trustees**: a trustee who has **special skills**, or who was named because of representations of special skills, must use them (§ 16014) [VERIFY V-569].
- **Unpaid family trustee**: same standard; lack of pay does not lower it.

### V.C Duty of loyalty and self-dealing

**1. Loyalty (§ 16002).** The trustee must administer the trust **solely in the interest of the beneficiaries**. R-TRS-052.

**2. Self-dealing and conflicts (§ 16004(a))** [S-661]. The trustee must not:
- **use or deal with trust property for the trustee's own profit** or any purpose unconnected with the trust; or
- **take part in any transaction in which the trustee has an interest adverse to the beneficiary**.

**3. "No further inquiry."** A self-dealing transaction (trustee buys trust property, sells own property to the trust, borrows trust funds, pays himself unauthorized fees) is a breach **regardless of fairness, price or good faith**. The only defenses are **authorization in the instrument, informed consent of the beneficiaries (with capacity and full disclosure), or court approval**. R-TRS-053.
- *Feb 2016 pattern:* the trustee bought the trust's house **at fair market value**. Write: "Even though Sam paid fair market value, buying trust property for himself is self-dealing, and under the no-further-inquiry rule fairness is no defense."

**4. Presumption against trustee–beneficiary transactions (§ 16004(c))** [S-661]. A transaction between trustee and beneficiary, during the trust or while the trustee's influence remains, by which the trustee obtains an advantage, is **presumed to violate** the trustee's duties; the presumption affects the **burden of proof**; it does not apply to hiring and compensation agreements. R-TRS-054.

**5. Conflicts short of self-dealing.** Investing trust funds in a business the trustee owns or manages; hiring the trustee's own company, law firm or relatives; buying assets from an affiliate; competing with the trust. These are loyalty breaches unless authorized, and they usually also fail prudence (V.E). R-TRS-055.
- *Feb 2016 pattern:* the trustee put trust assets into his own new business: a loyalty breach (conflict) and a prudence breach (speculative, undiversified).

**6. Attorney-trustees and drafters** (PR crossover):
- **Dual compensation**: an attorney-trustee (and the trustee's relatives and law firm) may not take both trustee fees and legal fees unless the trustee waives trustee fees or the dual compensation is approved, after full disclosure, by court order or by 30 days' advance notice without objection; the section does not apply if the trustee is related by blood or marriage to, or a cohabitant of, the settlor, and a waiver of the section is void (§ 15687) [S-677] [S-1116]. R-TRS-066.
- **Drafter as beneficiary**: donative transfer presumed invalid; conclusive for the drafter (§ 21380) (R-TRS-076; R-WIL-095).
- **Drafter as sole trustee**: removal ground (§ 15642(b)(6)) (R-TRS-074).
- **PR rules**: CRPC rule 1.8.1 (business transactions, R-PRF-026), rule 1.8.3 (gifts, R-PRF-027).

### V.D Duty of impartiality (§ 16003)

- "If a trust has two or more beneficiaries, the trustee has a duty to deal impartially with them and shall act impartially in investing and managing the trust property, taking into account any differing interests of the beneficiaries" (§ 16003). R-TRS-056.
- **Typical tension:** income beneficiary (wants high yield) vs. remainder beneficiary (wants growth). Allocation of receipts and expenses between income and principal follows the trust and California's principal and income statute (Prob. Code §§ 16320 et seq.), which includes a power to adjust between income and principal in some circumstances [VERIFY V-570].
- **Impartial ≠ equal.** The instrument may favor one beneficiary; the trustee follows it.
- **Jul 2025 pattern:** one trustee for two trusts with different beneficiaries or purposes must not favor one trust over the other; using one trust's money for the other's expenses is a loyalty and impartiality problem [VERIFY V-071].

### V.E Prudent investing (Uniform Prudent Investor Act, Prob. Code §§ 16045–16054)

- **Standard (§ 16047)** [S-1119]. Invest and manage as a **prudent investor** would, considering the purposes, terms, distribution requirements and other circumstances; exercise reasonable care, skill and caution. R-TRS-057.
- **Portfolio approach.** Decisions about individual assets are evaluated in the context of the **portfolio as a whole** and as part of an **overall strategy** with risk and return objectives reasonably suited to the trust. No investment is per se imprudent; the question is fit.
- **Factors** include general economic conditions, inflation or deflation, tax consequences, the role each investment plays in the portfolio, expected total return, the beneficiaries' other resources, liquidity and income needs, and an asset's special relationship to the trust's purposes (§ 16047(c)) [S-1119].
- **Diversification (§ 16048).** The trustee must diversify unless, under the circumstances, it is prudent not to (e.g., large tax cost, a family business the settlor wanted kept, a small trust better served by a single fund) [S-1119]. R-TRS-058.
- **Review on acceptance (§ 16049).** Within a reasonable time after accepting the trust or receiving assets, decide whether to retain or dispose of them to bring the portfolio into compliance [S-1119].
- **No hindsight (§ 16051).** Compliance is judged by the facts and circumstances at the time of the decision, not by outcome [S-1119].
- **Costs (§ 16050)**: incur only reasonable costs [VERIFY V-924].
- **Delegation (§ 16052)**: may delegate investment functions prudently (V.G).
- **Instrument controls**: the prudent investor rule is a default; the instrument may expand or restrict it (§ 16046 [VERIFY V-924]), but a retention clause does not license imprudence in the face of obvious danger.
- **Older rule.** The "prudent man" rule judged investments one by one and disfavored speculation; some instruments still use legal-list language. On an essay, apply the UPIA.

**Essay checklist for an investment breach:** (1) what the trust needs (income for a widow? growth for minors?); (2) concentration and risk; (3) any instrument authorization; (4) timing of review; (5) conflict of interest (V.C); (6) hindsight defense; (7) conclusion and measure of loss (IX).

### V.F Duties to take control, preserve, enforce claims, make productive, and earmark

- **Take control and preserve (§ 16006)**: collect assets, secure and insure them [VERIFY V-569].
- **Make productive (§ 16007)**: make trust property productive under the circumstances and consistent with the purposes (e.g., rent the vacant house, invest idle cash) [VERIFY V-569].
- **Enforce claims and defend actions (§§ 16010–16011)**: pursue the trust's claims, including against a predecessor or co-trustee [VERIFY V-569].
- **Keep trust property separate and earmarked (§ 16009)**: no **commingling** with the trustee's own funds; title in the trustee's fiduciary name [VERIFY V-569]. Commingling is a breach even without loss; losses that follow are charged to the trustee; tracing rules apply (R-REM-048). R-TRS-059, R-TRS-060.

### V.G Delegation and co-trustees

- **Delegation (§ 16052)** [VERIFY V-569]. A trustee may delegate investment and management functions a prudent trustee could properly delegate, using reasonable care in **selecting** the agent, **establishing the scope and terms**, and **periodically reviewing** performance. A trustee may not abdicate the entire administration.
- **Co-trustees.**
  - Unless the instrument provides otherwise, powers vested in co-trustees are exercised by **unanimous** action (§ 15620).
  - Each co-trustee must **participate** in administration and use **reasonable care to prevent** a co-trustee's breach and to **compel redress** (§ 16013) [VERIFY V-569].
  - A passive co-trustee who lets the other loot the trust is liable (§ 16402; IX.B).
  - R-TRS-061.
- **Feb 2016 pattern**: settlor and daughter were co-trustees; later the son was named trustee. Ask who had authority, whether delivery of a revocation to the co-trustee was needed, and whether the daughter's inaction breached her duties [VERIFY V-071].

### V.H Duty to inform and account

- **Inform (§ 16060)**: keep beneficiaries **reasonably informed** of the trust and its administration [S-1126].
- **Report on request (§ 16061)**: on reasonable request, report information about assets, liabilities, receipts and disbursements relevant to the beneficiary's interest [S-1126].
- **Terms (§ 16061.5)**: when a revocable trust becomes irrevocable at the settlor's death, provide the irrevocable terms to beneficiaries and heirs who request them [S-1125].
- **Notification on death of settlor (§ 16061.7)**: when a revocable trust becomes irrevocable because of the settlor's death, the trustee must serve a notification on each beneficiary and each heir **within 60 days** (or within 60 days after learning of a person entitled to notice), including a boldface warning about the contest deadline; the settlor cannot waive the notice. A change of trustee of an irrevocable trust also triggers notice [S-652] [S-1125].
- **Contest period (§ 16061.8)**: no person served may bring an action to contest the trust more than **120 days** after service of the notification, or **60 days** after delivery of trust terms requested during the 120-day period, **whichever is later** [S-652]. Since 2022 (AB 1745) this bar applies only where the notice was given because a revocable trust became irrevocable on a settlor's death [S-653]. R-TRS-064.
- **Account (§ 16062)** [S-679]: at least **annually**, at **termination**, and on a **change of trustee**, to each beneficiary to whom income or principal is required or authorized to be currently distributed. Contents: receipts, disbursements, assets, liabilities, compensation, agents hired. Waiver in writing is possible within limits (§ 16064) [VERIFY V-593]. R-TRS-063.
- **Exceptions (§ 16069)** [S-683]: no duty to account (or to provide terms or information) while the trust is **revocable** (unless no person holding the power to revoke is competent: § 15800, AB 1079 [S-1115]), or where the beneficiary and trustee are the same person.
- **Remedy for failure**: petition to compel (§ 17200), removal (§ 15642), and the statute of limitations does not start without an adequate account (§ 16460).

### V.I Duties while the trust is revocable (§ 15800)

- While a trust is revocable and the settlor is competent, **the beneficiaries' rights are subject to the settlor's control**, and the trustee's duties are owed to the **settlor** (§ 15800) [S-680] [S-1115]. A trustee of a revocable trust must follow the power-holder's written directions acceptable to the trustee (§ 16001) and is not liable to beneficiaries for acts done on them (§ 16462) [S-1127] [S-1121]. R-TRS-065.
- **Incompetent settlor.** AB 1079 (Stats. 2021, ch. 749) amended §§ 15800 and 16069: when no person holding the power to revoke is competent, the trustee must, within 60 days after learning of the incompetence, notify the beneficiaries who would take if the settlor had died and give them a copy of the trust, and the duties to account and to report run to those beneficiaries. Incompetence may be shown by the trust's own method or a judicial determination [S-1115] [S-680].
- **After death.** *Estate of Giraldin* (2012) 55 Cal.4th 1058: after the settlor's death, beneficiaries have **standing** to sue the trustee for breaches committed during the settlor's lifetime that harmed the trust; the trustee may defend by showing the settlor consented or directed the acts.
- **Feb 2012 pattern**: a revocable trust with the settlor as trustee and a successor trustee; after death the beneficiaries sue the successor (IX.B) [VERIFY V-071].

### V.J Trustee compensation and reimbursement

- Compensation as provided in the instrument or, if none, **reasonable** compensation (§§ 15680–15681) [VERIFY V-924]; the court may reduce or deny it for breach (§ 16420).
- Reimbursement for expenses **properly incurred** (§ 15684) [VERIFY V-581]; no reimbursement for the trustee's own torts or breaches.
- Attorney-trustee dual compensation (§ 15687): V.C.6.

## VI Trustee's discretionary powers (discretion granted by trust language; implied powers; limits on discretion)

### VI.A Sources of powers

- **Express powers** in the instrument.
- **Statutory powers** (Prob. Code §§ 16200 et seq.): unless limited by the instrument, a trustee may, for example, collect, hold and retain assets; sell, exchange, lease and encumber property; borrow; invest; insure; settle claims; employ agents; and make distributions in cash or kind [VERIFY V-924]. R-TRS-067.
- **Implied powers**: those necessary or appropriate to carry out the trust's purposes (common law).
- **Court-granted powers**: § 15409 (changed circumstances) and § 17200 petitions for instructions.
- **Limit:** every power is a fiduciary power; having the power to sell does not make a particular sale proper (§ 16202 [VERIFY V-924]).

### VI.B Discretion granted by trust language and its limits

- **Reasonableness (§ 16080)** [S-659]: except as provided in § 16081, a discretionary power is **not left to the trustee's arbitrary discretion** but must be **exercised reasonably**. R-TRS-068.
- **"Sole," "absolute" or "uncontrolled" discretion (§ 16081)** [S-659] [S-1117]: the trustee must still **act in accordance with fiduciary principles** and **not in bad faith or in disregard of the purposes** of the trust. Such words relax the reasonableness standard but do not eliminate review. R-TRS-069.
- **Trustee-beneficiary.** A beneficiary who, as trustee or co-trustee, may make discretionary distributions to himself or herself may exercise that power in his or her own favor only for **health, education, support or maintenance**, unless the settlor clearly indicates a broader power **by express reference to § 16081(c)**; the limit does not apply to a settlor's power over a revocable trust or to certain marital-deduction trusts (§ 16081(c)–(d)) [S-1117].
- **Abuse of discretion** includes: bad faith; dishonesty; improper motive (spite, self-interest, favoring the trustee's family); failure to exercise any judgment (a blanket refusal); acting beyond the bounds of reasonable judgment; misreading the standard. R-TRS-070.
- **Other resources.** Whether the trustee should consider the beneficiary's other income and assets depends on the instrument and the settlor's intent (Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 50 [VERIFY V-923]); absent direction, argue both.
- **Courts do not substitute judgment**: if the trustee acts in good faith within the instrument's standard, the court will not interfere even if it would have decided differently.

### VI.C How to write a discretion issue

1. Quote the discretion language and the standard ("for support in accustomed manner of living").
2. State § 16080 (reasonable) or § 16081 (sole/absolute: good faith, purposes).
3. Apply: motive, process (did the trustee ask about needs?), result.
4. Creditor angle: discretionary interests are protected from compelled distribution (IV.C).
5. Conclusion and remedy: court order compelling a proper exercise, surcharge, removal.

## VII Rights of beneficiary

### VII.A The beneficial interest

- An **equitable property interest**: present (income), future (remainder), vested or contingent, fixed or discretionary.
- **Transferable** and **reachable by creditors** unless restrained by a valid spendthrift clause (IV). R-TRS-071.
- **Revocable trust**: while the settlor is alive and competent, beneficiaries' rights are subject to the settlor's control (§ 15800); a beneficiary cannot sue the trustee then (V.I).

### VII.B Information, accountings and enforcement

- Rights to information, terms and accounts: V.H.
- **Petitions (§ 17200)**: beneficiary or trustee may petition concerning the trust's internal affairs or to determine its existence, including to construe the instrument, determine validity, settle accounts, compel accounts or information, instruct the trustee, approve or modify, terminate, appoint or remove a trustee, compel redress of a breach, and fix compensation. R-TRS-072.
- **Right to compel distributions** due under the instrument, and to compel a good-faith exercise of discretion (VI).

### VII.C Removal and replacement of the trustee

- **Grounds (§ 15642)** [S-658] [S-1118]: removal under the instrument's terms, on the court's own motion, or on petition of a settlor, co-trustee or beneficiary, including for:
  - breach of trust;
  - insolvency or other unfitness;
  - hostility or lack of cooperation among co-trustees impairing administration;
  - failure or refusal to act;
  - excessive compensation;
  - a **sole trustee who is a § 21380(a) disqualified person** (drafter, fiduciary-transcriber, care custodian of a dependent adult), unless the court finds the settlor intended that person to serve and that intent was not the product of fraud or undue influence (§ 15642(b)(6)); the ground does not apply where the settlor is related by blood or marriage to, or a cohabitant of, the trustee or drafter, where a court approved the designation, or where an independent attorney signed a certificate of review [S-658] [S-1118];
  - other good cause.
- The court may suspend the trustee and appoint a temporary trustee pending the hearing. R-TRS-073, R-TRS-074.

### VII.D Contesting a trust; no-contest clauses; disqualified donees

- **Grounds to contest a trust or amendment**: lack of capacity (R-TRS-003), undue influence, fraud, duress (R-WIL-038 to R-WIL-042), improper execution or revocation (I.G), forgery.
- **Deadline**: 120-day / 60-day period after the § 16061.7 notification (V.H; R-TRS-064).
- **No-contest clauses (§§ 21310–21315)** apply to trusts: enforced only against a **direct contest without probable cause** (and, if expressly stated, ownership challenges and creditor's claims) [S-608] [S-1056]. Petitions to construe the trust or challenge the trustee's conduct are not direct contests. R-TRS-075; R-WIL-093, R-WIL-094.
- **Presumptively disqualified donees (§§ 21380–21392)** apply to trusts and amendments: gifts to the drafter, a fiduciary-transcriber, a care custodian of a dependent adult, and related persons are presumed the product of fraud or undue influence [S-645] [S-936]. R-TRS-076; R-WIL-095 to R-WIL-098.
- **Omitted spouse and omitted child** statutes reach revocable trusts (§ 21601); a former spouse's nonprobate gift fails on dissolution (§ 5040) [S-605]. R-TRS-077; R-WIL-083, R-WIL-089.
- **Feb 2012 pattern**: a child born after the trust was signed (Dave) claimed against trust assets [VERIFY V-071].

### VII.E Remedies available to beneficiaries

Covered in VIII–IX: compel, enjoin, surcharge, rescind, trace, remove, reduce compensation.

## VIII Remedying breach of trust

*Tested Feb 2016 Q1 (what remedies does Bob have?) [VERIFY V-071].*

### VIII.A Breach and the statutory remedies (§§ 16400, 16420)

- **Breach (§ 16400)**: a violation by the trustee of **any duty** owed to the beneficiaries [S-678].
- **Remedies (§ 16420)** [S-678]. To remedy a breach that has occurred or is threatened, a beneficiary or co-trustee may petition the court to:
  1. **compel** the trustee to perform the duties;
  2. **enjoin** the trustee from committing a breach;
  3. **compel the trustee to redress** a breach by **paying money or restoring property** (surcharge);
  4. **appoint a receiver or temporary trustee**;
  5. **remove** the trustee;
  6. **reduce or deny compensation**;
  7. **set aside** acts of the trustee;
  8. **impose an equitable lien or a constructive trust** on trust property or proceeds;
  9. **trace** trust property wrongfully disposed of and **recover** it or its proceeds, subject to § 18100 (protection of good-faith third parties).
- These remedies are **not exclusive**; other statutory and common-law remedies remain (§ 16420(b); § 16442). R-TRS-078.

### VIII.B Remedies for self-dealing: elect to rescind or affirm

- The beneficiaries may **set aside** the transaction and recover the property (if the trustee or a transferee with notice still holds it), or **affirm** it and recover the trustee's **profit** and any **loss**; they choose the larger recovery. R-TRS-079.
- *Feb 2016 pattern*: if the trustee still owns the house, rescission and reconveyance (with credit for the price he paid); if he resold at a profit, disgorgement; if the property fell in value, surcharge for the loss.

### VIII.C Tracing, constructive trust and equitable lien

- Follow trust property into its product; claim it by **constructive trust** (asset rose) or **equitable lien** (asset fell), including through commingled accounts (lowest intermediate balance) until it reaches a **bona fide purchaser** (R-REM-044 to R-REM-049). R-TRS-080.
- Constructive trust also gives **priority over the trustee's unsecured creditors**, important if the trustee is insolvent.

### VIII.D Removal and compensation

- Removal (VII.C) is often the key remedy for repeated or loyalty breaches.
- The court may **reduce or deny compensation** for the period of breach (§ 16420).

### VIII.E Recovering property from others

- Petitions under **§ 850** and **double damages under § 859** (XII.C).

## IX Trustee liability to beneficiary

*Tested Feb 2012 Q1 (suit against the successor trustee) and Jul 2025 Q1 (must the trustee's estate repay a trust?) [VERIFY V-071].*

### IX.A Measure of liability (§ 16440)

- **(a)** A trustee who commits a breach of trust is chargeable with any of the following that is appropriate under the circumstances [S-678]:
  1. any **loss or depreciation in value** of the trust estate resulting from the breach, **with interest**;
  2. any **profit made by the trustee** through the breach, **with interest**;
  3. any **profit that would have accrued** to the trust estate if the loss of profit is the result of the breach.
- **(b)** The court may **excuse** the trustee in whole or in part if the trustee acted **reasonably and in good faith** under the circumstances as known to the trustee and it would be equitable to do so [S-678] [VERIFY V-925]. R-TRS-081, R-TRS-082.
- **Interest** at the legal rate or the amount actually earned, whichever the statute directs [VERIFY V-925].
- **No netting** of a gain from one breach against a loss from a separate breach (general rule) [VERIFY V-925].

### IX.B Liability for others: co-trustees, predecessors, agents

- **Co-trustee (§ 16402)** [VERIFY V-579]: liable for a co-trustee's breach only if the trustee **participated**, **improperly delegated**, **approved, acquiesced in or concealed** it, **negligently enabled** it, or **neglected to take reasonable steps to compel redress**.
- **Successor trustee (§ 16403)** [VERIFY V-579]: not liable for a predecessor's breach unless the successor **knew or should have known** of it and **improperly permitted it to continue**, or **neglected to take reasonable steps to compel the predecessor to deliver** trust property or **to redress** the breach.
- **Agents (§ 16401)** [VERIFY V-579]: liable for an agent's acts only if the trustee directed the act, failed to use reasonable care in selecting or monitoring the agent, or otherwise is at fault.
- R-TRS-083.
- *Feb 2012 pattern:* the beneficiaries' claim against successor trustee Tara turns on what she knew and what she did once in office.

### IX.C Defenses

| Defense | Rule | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Exculpatory clause** | Enforceable, but **not** for breaches committed **intentionally, with gross negligence, in bad faith, or with reckless indifference**, or for **profit** derived from the breach; construed strictly | § 16461(b) [S-662] [S-1120] (R-TRS-084) |
| **Beneficiary consent** | Bars that beneficiary, unless induced by improper conduct, lack of capacity, or ignorance of material facts or rights | § 16463 [S-662] [VERIFY V-925] (R-TRS-085) |
| **Release; affirmance** | Same limits; releases favoring the trustee also face § 16004(c) | §§ 16464, 16465 [S-662] [VERIFY V-925] |
| **Settlor's written direction** (revocable trust) | Trustee not liable for following written directions of the holder of the power to revoke | § 16462 [S-662] [S-1121] |
| **Statute of limitations** | **3 years** after receipt of an account or report that adequately discloses the claim; otherwise 3 years after the beneficiary discovered or reasonably should have discovered it | § 16460 [S-662] [VERIFY V-577] (R-TRS-086) |
| **Court excuse** | Reasonable and good-faith conduct | § 16440(b) [S-678] (R-TRS-082) |
| **Laches** | Equitable delay with prejudice | Common law |

### IX.D Liability survives the trustee's death

- The trustee's liability is personal; claims survive death (Code Civ. Proc. § 377.20 [VERIFY V-579]) and may be pursued against the trustee's estate (creditor-claim procedures apply [VERIFY V-579]). The **successor trustee** must take reasonable steps to compel redress (§ 16403). R-TRS-087.
- *Jul 2025 pattern:* whether a deceased trustee's estate must repay one of two trusts [VERIFY V-071].

## X Beneficiary liability

- **Participating or consenting beneficiary.** A beneficiary who consents to, joins in, or knowingly benefits from a breach **cannot sue** the trustee for it (§ 16463) [S-662], and the court may **impound** that beneficiary's interest to indemnify the trust or the other beneficiaries for the loss (Restatement (Second) of Trusts § 257 [VERIFY V-580]). R-TRS-088.
- **Overpayments and improper distributions.** A beneficiary who receives more than his share must **restore** it; the trustee may **offset** it against future distributions; a beneficiary who receives trust property with notice of a breach holds it on constructive trust (XII).
- **No personal liability to third parties** merely as a beneficiary: contract and tort creditors of the trust look to the trust property (through the trustee in a representative capacity) and to an at-fault trustee (§§ 18000–18004) [S-663] [VERIFY V-580]. R-TRS-089.
- **Beneficiary who is also trustee** is liable in the trustee capacity.
- **Beneficiary who induces a breach by fraud** may be liable in tort as well.

## XI Trust or trustee liability to third party

*Tested Feb 2019 Q1 (can creditors reach the trust assets?) [VERIFY V-071].*

### XI.A Contracts (§ 18000)

- **Rule** [S-663]. Unless the contract or the chapter provides otherwise, a trustee is **not personally liable on a contract properly entered into in the trustee's fiduciary capacity** in the course of administration, **unless the trustee fails to reveal the representative capacity and identify the trust** in the contract. R-TRS-090.
- **Common-law contrast.** The traditional rule made the trustee personally liable on trust contracts (with a right of indemnity from the trust) unless the contract excluded personal liability. California (and UTC § 1010 [VERIFY V-594]) shields a trustee who signs properly.
- **Indemnity.** A trustee personally liable on a proper contract may be reimbursed from the trust (§ 15684) [VERIFY V-581].

### XI.B Torts and ownership liabilities (§§ 18001–18002)

- A trustee is personally liable for obligations arising from **ownership or control** of trust property, and for **torts** committed in the course of administration, **only if personally at fault** [S-663] [VERIFY V-581]. R-TRS-091.
- **No indemnity** for liabilities caused by the trustee's own fault.

### XI.C Claims against the trust (§ 18004)

- A claim based on a contract, an obligation arising from ownership or control, or a tort may be asserted **against the trust by proceeding against the trustee in the trustee's representative capacity**, whether or not the trustee is personally liable [S-663].
- **Issues between trustee and trust** (who bears the loss) are resolved in a separate accounting or proceeding.

### XI.D Creditors of the settlor reaching trust assets

- **Revocable trust, settlor alive (§ 18200)** [S-664]: if the settlor retains the power to revoke in whole or in part, the trust property is **subject to the claims of the settlor's creditors** to the extent of the power of revocation, during the settlor's lifetime. A spendthrift clause does not help the settlor. R-TRS-092.
- **Revocable trust, settlor dead (§ 19001)** [S-665]: property that was subject to the power of revocation at death is subject to the claims of the deceased settlor's creditors and expenses of administration **to the extent the probate estate is inadequate**. Sections 19000 et seq. provide an optional trust claims procedure. R-TRS-093.
- **Irrevocable self-settled trust (§ 15304)**: creditors reach the settlor's interest, or the maximum the trustee could pay the settlor (IV.B).
- **Irrevocable trust for others**: the settlor's creditors cannot reach it unless the transfer was fraudulent (Civ. Code §§ 3439 et seq., the Uniform Voidable Transactions Act [VERIFY V-924]).
- **Trustee's duty to the settlor's creditors** after death is limited: the trustee's only duty to creditors with pending claims is to avoid affirmative misconduct that defeats their reasonable expectation of payment from trust assets; there is no general duty to preserve assets for them (*Arluk Medical Center Industrial Group, Inc. v. Dobler* (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 1324 [S-1123] [S-1124]).
- **CP note.** Community property in a revocable trust remains CP; community debts can reach it under the Family Code liability rules (R-CMP rules) (Feb 2019 crossover).

## XII Third party liability to trust

### XII.A Third persons who participate in a breach

- A third person who **knowingly participates** in a trustee's breach (aids and abets), for example by buying trust property knowing the sale is a breach for the trustee's benefit, or by knowingly helping divert trust funds, is **liable** to the beneficiaries for the loss and holds any trust property received on **constructive trust** (common law; Civ. Code § 2224; Restatement (Second) of Trusts § 326 [VERIFY V-582]). R-TRS-094.
- The trustee has the duty to sue such persons (§ 16010); if the trustee will not (because the trustee is the wrongdoer), beneficiaries may sue, or a successor or temporary trustee will.

### XII.B Transferees of trust property; protection of good-faith third parties

- **§ 18100** [S-666]: with respect to a third person **dealing with or assisting** a trustee, if the third person acts **in good faith and for a valuable consideration** and **without actual knowledge** that the trustee is exceeding or improperly exercising its powers, the third person is **fully protected** as if the trustee had and properly exercised the power, and is **not bound to inquire** into the trustee's authority. **Constructive knowledge or inquiry notice does not defeat** the protection (*Adler v. Manor Healthcare Corp.* (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 1110) [S-667]. R-TRS-095.
- **Donees** (no value) and **transferees with actual knowledge** take subject to the trust and must restore the property or its value.
- **Certification of trust** (§ 18100.5) lets third parties rely on a trustee's summary certificate instead of the whole instrument [VERIFY V-581].
- **General-law contrast.** The common-law bona fide purchaser rule protects only a purchaser for value **without notice**, actual or constructive. California's statute is more protective of third parties.

### XII.C Recovering trust property (§§ 850–859)

- **§ 850 petition**: the trustee (or an interested person) may petition for an order that property held by another, or titled in the decedent's name, belongs to the trust and must be transferred to it; used for *Heggstad* claims and for wrongful takings [S-675].
- **§ 859 double damages**: a person found to have **in bad faith wrongfully taken, concealed or disposed of** trust property, or to have taken it by **undue influence** or **elder or dependent adult financial abuse**, is liable for **twice the value** of the property recovered, and may be liable for reasonable **attorney's fees and costs** [S-675] [VERIFY V-584]. R-TRS-096.
- **Interference with expected inheritance** (tort) is available only where probate remedies are inadequate (R-WIL-105).
