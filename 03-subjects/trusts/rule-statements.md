# Trusts — rule statements

**How to use this file.** Each block is a rule you can write verbatim on an essay, followed by elements, exceptions, authority and a short example. `build_anki.py` reads the bold labels. The order follows [`outline.md`](outline.md) and the State Bar's Trusts outline (I–XII).

**Governing law.** Trusts is **not on the MBE**. Some Trusts calls say "according to California law" (Feb 2012, Feb 2015); others do not, and then the official default is "principles of general application" (see [`ca-distinctions.md`](ca-distinctions.md) §1 and [`README.md`](README.md)). California's Trust Law (Probate Code Division 9) codifies most general principles, so the two usually agree. Each "MBE vs. CA essay" line below says **same** or **differs** and what to write.

**Cross-references to Wills.** Pour-over devises, no-contest clauses, presumptively disqualified donees, omitted heirs and nonprobate transfers are taught in [`../wills-succession/rule-statements.md`](../wills-succession/rule-statements.md) (R-WIL-061, R-WIL-089, R-WIL-093 to R-WIL-098, R-WIL-101, R-WIL-102). The Trusts rules below state only the trust-specific angle and point to those IDs.

**Abbreviations.** Prob. Code = California Probate Code; UTC = Uniform Trust Code (not adopted in California; comparison only); UPIA = Uniform Prudent Investor Act (adopted in California); AG = Attorney General; HEMS = health, education, maintenance or support.

## I Express trusts — creation (I.A–I.G)

### R-TRS-001 — Trust defined; required parties
**Rule (write this):** A trust is a fiduciary relationship in which a trustee holds legal title to property (the res) under equitable duties to manage it for the benefit of one or more beneficiaries, created by a settlor who manifests the intent to create it. One person may fill more than one role, but a sole trustee cannot also be the sole beneficiary.

**Elements:** 1. Settlor with capacity and intent. 2. Trustee (legal title, fiduciary duties). 3. Beneficiary (equitable interest). 4. Trust property. 5. Lawful purpose.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A trust does not fail for want of a trustee (R-TRS-007). Charitable and honorary trusts need no ascertainable beneficiary (R-TRS-019, R-TRS-022). Merger (R-TRS-008).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 15200–15205 [S-668]; Restatement (Second) of Trusts § 2.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule under California and general law.

**Example:** Mom transfers $500,000 to Bank "in trust for my son Sam for life, remainder to Sam's children." Mom is the settlor, Bank the trustee with legal title, and Sam (life income) and his children (remainder) hold the equitable interests.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Trust Law (信托法) describes the settlor "entrusting" (委托给) property rights to the trustee and leaves title transfer unclear; in US law the trustee plainly holds legal title (approx.).

### R-TRS-002 — Methods of creating a trust
**Rule (write this):** A trust may be created by (1) the owner's declaration that he or she holds property as trustee, (2) a lifetime transfer of property to another person as trustee, (3) a transfer by will, (4) the exercise of a power of appointment in trust, or (5) an enforceable promise to create a trust. No consideration is required.

**Elements:** 1. Identify the method. 2. Declaration: present-tense intent to hold as trustee. 3. Transfer: delivery or conveyance to the trustee. 4. Will: valid will. 5. Promise: enforceable under contract law.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A promise to create a trust in the future is enforceable only if it is a binding contract; a gratuitous promise creates nothing.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 15200, 15208 [S-668].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** Dad signs a note: "From today I hold my 1,000 Apple shares as trustee for my daughter." A valid declaration of trust; no delivery to anyone else is needed because Dad is the trustee.

### R-TRS-003 — Capacity to create, amend or revoke a trust
**Rule (write this):** A settlor must have capacity when creating, amending or revoking a trust. California measures capacity by the decision-specific test of Probate Code §§ 810–812, but where a trust or amendment closely resembles a will or codicil in content and complexity, courts apply the testamentary-capacity test of § 6100.5. Fraud, duress and undue influence invalidate a trust or amendment as they do a will.

**Elements:** 1. Identify the instrument and its complexity. 2. Choose the test (§ 6100.5 for will-like instruments; § 812 for complex ones). 3. Apply facts about mental state at signing. 4. Check undue influence (R-WIL-039, R-WIL-040) and the § 21380 presumption (R-TRS-076).

**Exceptions / defenses:** A lucid interval suffices. A conservatorship does not by itself prove incapacity.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 810–812, 6100.5; *Andersen v. Hunt* (2011) 196 Cal.App.4th 722 [S-1111]; *Eyford v. Nord* (2021) (applying § 6100.5 to a trust amendment that disinherited one set of beneficiaries) [S-1112].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific choice of test. Under general law, the capacity to make a revocable trust is usually equated with testamentary capacity.

**Example:** An 88-year-old settlor with early dementia signs a one-page amendment changing the remainder beneficiary. Apply § 6100.5: if she understood the nature of the act, her property and her family, the amendment is valid even though she could no longer manage complex finances.

### R-TRS-004 — Intent to create a trust; precatory words
**Rule (write this):** The settlor must manifest a present intention to create a trust, that is, to impose enforceable duties on a trustee to hold property for another. No particular words are required, but precatory words such as "wish," "hope" or "request" ordinarily create no trust unless the context shows the settlor meant to impose a duty.

**Elements:** 1. Words or conduct. 2. Intent to impose duties (not a moral obligation). 3. Present intent (not a future plan).

**Exceptions / defenses:** If no trust is intended, the transferee takes an outright gift (R-TRS-039).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15201 [S-668]; common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A will leaves "$100,000 to my wife, hoping she will help my nephew." Precatory: the wife takes outright. "To my wife, who shall use it to pay my nephew's tuition" imposes a duty and creates a trust.

### R-TRS-005 — Trust property and funding
**Rule (write this):** A trust exists only if there is trust property: an existing, identifiable property interest that the settlor transfers to a trustee or declares to be held in trust. A mere expectancy, such as a hoped-for inheritance or profits not yet earned, cannot be trust property until it comes into existence and the settlor then manifests the intent again. In California, a settlor-trustee's signed trust instrument that identifies an asset as trust property can make it trust property without a separate deed.

**Elements:** 1. Identifiable property. 2. Owned by the settlor. 3. Transferred or declared in trust. 4. Not a mere expectancy.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A pour-over will can fund an empty trust at death (R-TRS-010). A nominally funded trust still has a res if the trustee holds a token asset or the trust is named beneficiary of a contract right (e.g., life insurance).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15202 [S-668]; *Estate of Heggstad* (1993) 16 Cal.App.4th 943 [S-1109]; common law (expectancies).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same core rule. The *Heggstad* funding rule is California case law; mention it when land was listed on a trust schedule but never deeded.

**Example:** Settlor signs a living trust declaring herself trustee and listing "my house at 12 Elm St." on Schedule A, but never records a deed. Under *Heggstad* the house is trust property, and the signed instrument satisfies § 15206.

### R-TRS-006 — Lawful trust purpose
**Rule (write this):** A trust may be created for any purpose that is not illegal or contrary to public policy. A trust, or a term, that requires a crime, induces illegal conduct, or is intended to restrain marriage or encourage divorce is invalid to that extent.

**Elements:** 1. Identify the purpose and conditions. 2. Illegal or against public policy? 3. Sever the invalid term if possible.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A gift "until she marries" may be a valid limit on duration (support until marriage) rather than a restraint, depending on intent.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15203 [S-668]; Civ. Code § 710 (conditions in restraint of marriage) [VERIFY V-589].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** "$500,000 in trust for my granddaughter, but if she ever marries, her interest passes to my brother," with evidence the settlor wanted to keep her from marrying, is a condition in restraint of marriage and is void, so she keeps her interest free of it. "Income to my granddaughter until she marries" is a valid limitation if the settlor meant only to support her while single; argue intent both ways.

### R-TRS-007 — Trustee: acceptance, rejection, no failure for want of a trustee
**Rule (write this):** A trust does not fail for want of a trustee: if the named trustee dies, declines or is removed, the court appoints a successor unless the settlor intended the trust to exist only if that person served. A named trustee accepts by signing the instrument or a written acceptance, or by knowingly exercising powers or performing duties; one who does not accept within a reasonable time is deemed to have rejected the trust.

**Elements:** 1. Named trustee accepted? 2. If not, vacancy. 3. Successor named in the instrument, or appointed by the court.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A trustee who has rejected cannot be forced to serve and owes no duties.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 15600, 15601, 15660 [VERIFY V-569]; common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A will names Brother as trustee for T's minor children; Brother declines. The court appoints a successor, and the trust survives.

### R-TRS-008 — Merger
**Rule (write this):** When the sole trustee is also the sole beneficiary, legal and equitable title merge and no trust exists. There is no merger if there are co-trustees or other beneficiaries, including remainder beneficiaries, so the usual living trust, with the settlor as trustee and lifetime beneficiary and others as remainder beneficiaries, is valid.

**Elements:** 1. One trustee? 2. Same person is every beneficiary, present and future? 3. If yes, merger; if no, valid trust.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Merger can occur later (e.g., the remainder beneficiary becomes sole trustee and sole beneficiary), ending the trust.

**Authority:** Common law; Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 69 [VERIFY V-562].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** Sam declares himself trustee "for myself for life, then for my daughters." Valid: the daughters hold equitable remainders, so there is no merger.

### R-TRS-009 — Inter vivos and testamentary trusts
**Rule (write this):** An inter vivos (living) trust is created during the settlor's life by declaration or transfer and takes effect at once; property in a funded living trust passes outside probate. A testamentary trust is created by will, takes effect only at death, and is valid only if the will is valid; its property passes through probate before reaching the trustee.

**Elements:** 1. When created and how. 2. Living: declaration or transfer and funding. 3. Testamentary: valid will (R-WIL-047, R-WIL-050) and terms stated in it or validly incorporated.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A revocable living trust is a will substitute but is treated like a will for many purposes (R-TRS-077; R-WIL-102).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15200 [S-668]; Prob. Code § 6110.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** T's will leaves her estate "in trust for my grandchildren, remainder to the Zoo" (Feb 2015 pattern). It is a testamentary trust; if the will fails, so does the trust.

### R-TRS-010 — Pour-over to a living trust (Trusts angle)
**Rule (write this):** A will may devise property to the trustee of a trust identified in the will whose terms are in a writing executed before, concurrently with, or within 60 days after the will, even if the trust is unfunded, revocable or amendable. The poured-over property is administered under the trust as amended before or after the testator's death, and if the trust is revoked before death the devise lapses unless the will provides otherwise. See R-WIL-061.

**Elements:** 1. Valid will. 2. Trust identified in the will. 3. Trust instrument signed within the § 6300 window. 4. Amendments apply. 5. Trust revoked before death → lapse.

**Exceptions / defenses:** If § 6300 fails, incorporation by reference may save the trust terms that existed when the will was signed, but not later amendments (R-WIL-058).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6300 (as amended eff. 2018) [S-611] [S-612]; R-WIL-061.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California's 60-day window is broader than the traditional Uniform Testamentary Additions to Trusts Act rule (trust executed before or with the will).

**Example:** T's 2020 will pours the residue into "the T Trust." In 2024 T amends the trust to add a gift to her caregiver. The poured-over residue follows the 2024 terms, but the caregiver's gift must survive § 21380 (R-TRS-076).

### R-TRS-011 — Revocability: California default
**Rule (write this):** In California a trust is revocable by the settlor unless the trust instrument expressly makes it irrevocable. This reverses the traditional common-law rule, under which a trust is irrevocable unless the settlor expressly reserves a power to revoke; the Uniform Trust Code adopts California's approach for new trusts.

**Elements:** 1. Does the instrument expressly say "irrevocable"? 2. If silent: revocable in California, irrevocable under the traditional rule. 3. California's default applies where the settlor was domiciled in California when the trust was created, the instrument was executed in California, or the instrument chooses California law.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The trust becomes irrevocable at the settlor's death. An express irrevocability clause controls.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15400 [S-1113]; UTC § 602(a) [VERIFY V-594]; Restatement (Second) of Trusts § 330 (traditional rule) [VERIFY V-923].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs:** traditional rule = irrevocable unless reserved; California and UTC = revocable unless expressly irrevocable. Write both, then apply California where the call says California law or the settlor is a Californian.

**Example:** Wendy's trust says nothing about revocation. Under § 15400 she may revoke it; under the traditional rule she could not.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Trust Law lets a settlor who is the sole beneficiary terminate the trust but has no general presumption of revocability; California treats a silent living trust as revocable (approx.).

### R-TRS-012 — Methods of revoking a revocable trust
**Rule (write this):** A revocable trust may be revoked in whole or in part (1) by any method stated in the trust instrument, or (2) by a writing, other than a will, signed by the settlor (or another person holding the power of revocation) and delivered to the trustee during the settlor's lifetime. If the instrument explicitly makes its own method the exclusive method, only that method works.

**Elements:** 1. Trust revocable (R-TRS-011). 2. Instrument method followed? 3. If not, is the instrument method explicitly exclusive? 4. If not exclusive, statutory method: signed writing, not a will, delivered to the trustee while the settlor is alive.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Where the settlor is the sole trustee, argue that holding the signed writing in her capacity as trustee satisfies delivery; no California case confirming this point has been verified for this repo [VERIFY V-553]. Community property held in trust: R-TRS-015.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15401(a) [S-660] [S-1114]; *Haggerty v. Thornton* (2024) 15 Cal.5th 729 [S-129] [S-650] [S-129]; *Masry v. Masry* (2008) 166 Cal.App.4th 738 [VERIFY V-553].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs** from the traditional rule, which required strict compliance with any method the instrument stated. Under UTC § 602(c) the settlor may use any method showing clear and convincing intent if the instrument's method is not exclusive [VERIFY V-594].

**Example:** The trust allows revocation "by a signed writing delivered to the trustee and acknowledged before a notary." The settlor signs and delivers an unnotarized revocation. The instrument method fails, but because it is not explicitly exclusive, the § 15401(a)(2) method succeeds.

### R-TRS-013 — Attempted revocation by will
**Rule (write this):** In California a later will does not revoke a living trust under the statutory method, because § 15401(a)(2) requires a writing "other than a will" delivered to the trustee during the settlor's life; a will revokes the trust only if the trust instrument itself permits revocation by will. A will that purports to dispose of property already in a funded trust does not reach it, because that property is not part of the probate estate.

**Elements:** 1. Did the trust instrument permit revocation by will? 2. If not, the will is not a statutory writing. 3. Trust property stays in the trust.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Argue the other side under the UTC-type rule: a later will that expressly refers to the trust or specifically devises trust property can revoke if the trust's method is not exclusive [VERIFY V-594].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15401(a)(2) [S-660]; UTC § 602(c) [VERIFY V-594].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs:** California, no; UTC, yes in some cases. The Feb 2016 selected answers reached opposite conclusions on this issue, so graders reward the two-sided analysis.

**Example:** Wendy later signs a will saying "I revoke my trust and leave the ranch to Sam as trustee for Bob." Unless her trust allowed revocation by will, the trust stands in California and the ranch passes under the trust; under the UTC-type rule the will might revoke it.

### R-TRS-014 — Modifying a revocable trust (Haggerty)
**Rule (write this):** Unless the trust instrument provides otherwise, a settlor may modify a revocable trust by the procedure for revocation. Under *Haggerty v. Thornton* (2024), the settlor may therefore amend by the § 15401(a)(2) statutory method, a signed writing delivered to the trustee, unless the instrument provides a method of modification and explicitly makes it exclusive.

**Elements:** 1. Trust revocable. 2. Instrument's amendment method followed? 3. If not, is that method explicitly exclusive? 4. If not, a signed writing delivered to the trustee suffices.

**Exceptions / defenses:** An explicitly exclusive amendment clause; modification as to community property needs both spouses (R-TRS-015).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15402; *Haggerty v. Thornton* (2024) 15 Cal.5th 729 [S-129] [S-650] [S-129] (disapproving *King v. Lynch* (2012) 204 Cal.App.4th 1186 [S-129]).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. California-specific and a **recent change** (recent-changes §7, Apply). Before *Haggerty*, some Courts of Appeal required strict compliance with any stated amendment method.

**Example:** The trust allows amendment "by a writing signed by the settlor and acknowledged before a notary." The settlor signs an unnotarized amendment and hands it to the trustee. The notary clause is not explicitly exclusive, so the amendment is valid under the statutory method (signed writing delivered to the trustee).

### R-TRS-015 — Joint settlors and community property in a revocable trust
**Rule (write this):** Unless the instrument provides otherwise, when a trust has more than one settlor each may revoke it as to the portion he or she contributed. For community property in a revocable trust, either spouse acting alone may revoke as to that property, but the trust may be modified as to community property during the marriage only with the joinder or consent of both spouses.

**Elements:** 1. Joint settlors? 2. Character of the property (R-CMP rules). 3. Revocation: one spouse may act alone. 4. Modification: both spouses.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The instrument may require both spouses to revoke, or allow one to modify.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15401(b) (each settlor may revoke as to the portion he or she contributed, "except as provided in Section 761 of the Family Code") [S-1114]; Fam. Code § 761 [S-681] [VERIFY V-585].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific (CP crossover).

**Example:** H and W put community property in a joint revocable trust that is silent on revocation. W alone signs and delivers a revocation; it is effective as to the CP. A one-spouse amendment cutting out H's relatives would need H's consent.

### R-TRS-016 — Who may exercise the power to revoke or modify
**Rule (write this):** The power to revoke or modify belongs to the settlor (or another person to whom the instrument gives it). A trust may not be modified or revoked by an agent under a power of attorney unless the trust instrument expressly permits it. A conservator may act only with court approval. On the settlor's death the trust becomes irrevocable.

**Elements:** 1. Who acted? 2. Settlor personally, or a fiduciary? 3. Agent: does the trust instrument expressly permit an agent to revoke or modify? 4. Conservator: court approval?

**Exceptions / defenses:** The power of attorney itself must also grant authority over trusts expressly [VERIFY V-922].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15401(c) [S-1114]; Prob. Code §§ 2580, 4264 (conservator; power of attorney) [VERIFY V-922].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific details.

**Example:** Daughter, acting under a general durable power of attorney, amends Mom's trust to make herself sole remainder beneficiary. Unless the trust instrument expressly permits amendment by an agent, the amendment is void under § 15401(c), whatever the power of attorney says; it is also self-dealing by a fiduciary.

### R-TRS-017 — Totten trust (bank-account trust)
**Rule (write this):** A bank account in the depositor's name "in trust for" another is a revocable will substitute: the depositor owns and may withdraw the funds during life, and at death the beneficiary takes the remaining balance unless clear and convincing evidence shows a different intent. No will formalities are required.

**Elements:** 1. Account form "A in trust for B." 2. Depositor's lifetime control. 3. Balance to B at death. 4. Clear and convincing evidence of contrary intent?

**Exceptions / defenses:** Community property limits (R-CMP-096); a later will cannot redirect it (R-WIL-104).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 80, 5301–5302 [VERIFY V-538]; R-WIL-101.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance as general law.

**Example:** "Mom, in trust for Ben." Mom withdraws $20,000 to buy a car; Ben cannot object. At Mom's death Ben takes the remaining balance outside probate.

## I.x2 Ascertainable beneficiaries; charitable, honorary and pet trusts

### R-TRS-018 — Ascertainable beneficiaries
**Rule (write this):** A private trust must have a beneficiary who is identified, or ascertainable under the terms of the instrument, when the trust is created or within the perpetuities period. A class whose members can be determined ("my children," "my grandchildren") suffices, as does a power given to someone to select beneficiaries from a sufficiently described class; a gift to an indefinite group ("my friends") fails as a private trust unless it can stand as a charitable or honorary trust or as a power of selection.

**Elements:** 1. Named or described beneficiaries. 2. Class can be determined. 3. If indefinite: charitable? honorary? power of selection?

**Exceptions / defenses:** Afterborn class members can be included; the class closes under the instrument or the rule of convenience.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15205 [S-668] [VERIFY V-589]; common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule in substance.

**Example:** A trust "for my grandchildren" is valid even though more grandchildren may be born. A trust "for my good friends" is too indefinite at common law and fails, leaving a resulting trust (R-TRS-036).

### R-TRS-019 — Charitable trust: definition and validity
**Rule (write this):** A charitable trust is a trust for a charitable purpose, such as the relief of poverty, the advancement of education, religion or health, governmental or municipal purposes, or another purpose beneficial to the community, that benefits the public or an indefinite class rather than specific individuals. It needs no ascertainable beneficiary, may last indefinitely, and is enforced by the Attorney General.

**Elements:** 1. Charitable purpose. 2. Public or indefinite class of beneficiaries. 3. Intent and property (R-TRS-004, R-TRS-005).

**Exceptions / defenses:** A trust for named persons is private even if they are poor. A mixed purpose ("charitable or benevolent") may fail if the trustee could apply the funds to non-charitable ends.

**Authority:** Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 28 [VERIFY V-559]; *Estate of Faulkner* (1954) 128 Cal.App.2d 575 [S-682]; Gov. Code § 12598 [S-671].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A residue "to fund research to cure RG syndrome" serves health and science for the public; it is a valid charitable trust (Feb 2025 pattern).

### R-TRS-020 — Enforcing a charitable trust
**Rule (write this):** The Attorney General has primary responsibility to supervise and enforce charitable trusts. Co-trustees and persons with a special interest (such as a designated recipient charity) may also sue, but members of the general public who might benefit may not.

**Elements:** 1. Charitable trust. 2. Plaintiff: AG, co-trustee, or person with a special interest.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The settlor's retained right to enforce depends on the instrument and jurisdiction.

**Authority:** Gov. Code § 12598 [S-671]; *Holt v. College of Osteopathic Physicians & Surgeons* (1964) 61 Cal.2d 750 [VERIFY V-560].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A museum's trustees divert endowment funds to a for-profit café. The AG and a dissenting co-trustee may sue; an art lover who visits the museum may not.

### R-TRS-021 — Cy pres
**Rule (write this):** When a charitable trust's specific purpose becomes impossible, impracticable or illegal, and the settlor had a general charitable intent rather than an intent limited to that particular purpose, the court will not let the trust fail but will direct the property to a charitable purpose that comes as near as possible to the settlor's original purpose. Without a general charitable intent the trust fails, and a resulting trust returns the property to the settlor or the settlor's estate.

**Elements:** 1. Valid charitable trust (R-TRS-019). 2. Original purpose impossible, impracticable or illegal (the Restatement (Third) adds wasteful). 3. General charitable intent. 4. Substitute purpose as near as possible. 5. Court order on petition.

**Exceptions / defenses:** An express gift over on failure shows a specific intent and defeats cy pres. Mere inconvenience, or a trustee's belief that another purpose is better, is not enough.

**Authority:** *Estate of Gatlin* (1971) 16 Cal.App.3d 644 [S-670]; Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 67 [VERIFY V-559]; Prob. Code § 17200 [VERIFY V-559].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Mostly same:** California applies the common-law doctrine and requires a general charitable intent; the Restatement (Third) and UTC § 413 presume one [VERIFY V-594]. Write the general-intent test, then note the modern presumption.

**Example:** A trust funds research to cure RG syndrome; a cure is found, and the trustee asks to redirect the money to an ophthalmology scholarship (Feb 2025 pattern). The purpose is accomplished, so cy pres is available if the testator's intent was general (fighting eye disease). The court then asks whether the proposed scholarship is as near as possible, or whether research on a related eye disease is closer.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Trust Law also lets the authority approve applying a failed charitable (公益) trust's property to a similar purpose, which resembles cy pres; California uses the court and the AG instead (approx.).

### R-TRS-022 — Honorary (noncharitable purpose) trusts
**Rule (write this):** A trust for a lawful noncharitable purpose with no ascertainable beneficiary, or for a noncharitable corporation or unincorporated society, may be carried out by the trustee for only 21 years, whether or not anyone can enforce it.

**Elements:** 1. Noncharitable purpose. 2. No ascertainable beneficiary. 3. Trustee willing to perform. 4. 21-year limit.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Trusts for animals are governed separately (R-TRS-023).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15211 [S-676] [VERIFY V-558].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; similar to UTC § 409 [VERIFY V-594].

**Example:** $40,000 "to keep my vintage car in running order forever." The trustee may do so for 21 years; after that, the remaining funds return to the settlor's estate by resulting trust.

### R-TRS-023 — Trusts for the care of animals
**Rule (write this):** A trust for the care of a domestic or pet animal is valid. It may be enforced by a person named in the instrument or, if none, by a person the court appoints, and, unless the instrument provides otherwise, it ends when no animal covered by the trust remains alive [VERIFY V-558]; the remaining property then passes as the instrument directs or to the settlor's successors.

**Elements:** 1. Domestic or pet animal. 2. Enforcer named or appointed. 3. Duration: until no covered animal remains alive (which animals are covered, those living at the settlor's death or during the settlor's lifetime, is unconfirmed [VERIFY V-558]). 4. Use limited to the animal's care.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The court may reduce property that substantially exceeds what the care requires [VERIFY V-558].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15212 [S-676] [VERIFY V-558].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; similar to UTC § 408 [VERIFY V-594].

**Example:** $200,000 for the care of the settlor's cat. Valid and enforceable by the named caregiver; on the cat's death the remainder passes as the trust directs.

## I.J Modification and termination

### R-TRS-024 — Modification or termination with the settlor's and all beneficiaries' consent
**Rule (write this):** By the written consent of the settlor and all beneficiaries, even an irrevocable trust may be modified or terminated without court approval, without regard to the trustee's wishes or to any material purpose of the trust. If a beneficiary does not consent, the other beneficiaries, with the settlor's consent, may petition the court, which may modify or terminate the trust if the interests of the nonconsenting beneficiaries are not substantially impaired.

**Elements:** 1. Settlor alive and consenting. 2. All beneficiaries consent in writing (capacity; minors and unborns need representation). 3. Or, on petition, nonconsenting interests not substantially impaired.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Consent obtained by undue influence is invalid.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15404(a), (b) [S-1102].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same as general law (UTC § 411(a)) [VERIFY V-594].

**Example:** Grandma's irrevocable trust pays income to Son, remainder to adult Grandson. All three sign a termination agreement. The trust ends even though the trustee objects.

### R-TRS-025 — Termination or modification by the beneficiaries alone (material purpose)
**Rule (write this):** If all beneficiaries of an irrevocable trust consent, they may petition the court to modify or terminate it. But if continuing the trust is necessary to carry out a material purpose, the court may grant the petition only if it finds, in its discretion, that the reason for modifying or terminating outweighs the interest in accomplishing that purpose. If the trust is subject to a valid spendthrift restraint, the court may terminate it under this section only if there is good cause.

**Elements:** 1. Settlor dead or not consenting. 2. All beneficiaries consent. 3. Identify any material purpose (spendthrift, discretionary or support provisions, postponed age of distribution are classic examples). 4. Balance the reason for the change against that purpose. 5. Termination of a spendthrift trust: good cause.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Under the traditional *Claflin* rule, a material purpose is an absolute bar. Earlier text of § 15403 gave the court no discretion to terminate a trust subject to a valid spendthrift restraint; the current text allows it for good cause [S-1100].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15403 [S-674] [S-1100]; *Claflin v. Claflin* (Mass. 1889) 149 Mass. 19.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs:** *Claflin*/majority = no termination while a material purpose remains; California = judicial balancing (plus good cause for a spendthrift trust). Write both.

**Example:** After the settlor's death, beneficiaries Beth and Carol want to end a trust that pays them income until age 35 and has a spendthrift clause (Feb 2012 pattern). Under *Claflin* they lose. Under § 15403 they must show a reason, such as an urgent medical need, that outweighs those purposes and amounts to good cause to end a spendthrift trust.

### R-TRS-026 — Modification or termination for changed circumstances
**Rule (write this):** On petition by a trustee or beneficiary, the court may modify the administrative or dispositive provisions of a trust, or terminate it, if, because of circumstances not known to and not anticipated by the settlor, continuing the trust under its terms would defeat or substantially impair the accomplishment of its purposes. The court may authorize acts the instrument forbids, and it must consider a spendthrift restraint as a factor but is not barred by it.

**Elements:** 1. Circumstances unknown to and unanticipated by the settlor. 2. Continuing the trust defeats or substantially impairs its purposes. 3. Modification tailored to the purposes.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A change the settlor foresaw, or a mere wish for a better return, is not enough.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15409(a), (b) [S-1101]; common law (equitable deviation).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance (UTC § 412 [VERIFY V-594]).

**Example:** The trust forbids selling the family farm, which now loses money every year while the income beneficiary is impoverished. The court may authorize a sale because unforeseen conditions defeat the purpose of supporting the family.

### R-TRS-027 — Uneconomic trusts
**Rule (write this):** On petition, the court may modify or terminate a trust if the value of its principal has become so low in relation to the cost of administration that continuing it would defeat or substantially impair its purposes. Unless the instrument provides otherwise, the trustee may also terminate, without a court order, a trust whose principal does not exceed $100,000 in fair market value (threshold raised by SB 1127, Stats. 2024, ch. 76, effective January 1, 2025), distributing the property consistently with the trust's purposes.

**Elements:** 1. Principal value versus administration cost. 2. Court route (any size) or trustee route (≤ $100,000). 3. Instrument does not forbid. 4. Distribution consistent with purposes.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The instrument may forbid early termination.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15408 [S-656] [S-657] [S-1103] [S-1104].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs** in amount: UTC § 414 uses $50,000 [VERIFY V-594]. Recent California change; check the figure in June 2027 (whether it is inflation-indexed is unconfirmed [VERIFY V-921]).

**Example:** One of two testamentary trusts now holds $60,000 and pays $8,000 a year in fees. Unless the will forbids it, the trustee may terminate it under § 15408(b), and a beneficiary may petition under § 15408(a).

### R-TRS-028 — Termination by its terms; distribution on termination
**Rule (write this):** A trust terminates when its term expires or its purpose is accomplished, when its purpose becomes unlawful or impossible, or when it is revoked; the trustee keeps the powers needed to wind up. On revocation the property goes to the settlor or as the settlor directs; on other terminations it goes to the persons entitled under the trust, or, if the instrument is silent, back to the settlor or the settlor's estate by resulting trust.

**Elements:** 1. Terminating event. 2. Winding up. 3. Distribution: instrument, settlor, or resulting trust.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Merger also ends a trust (R-TRS-008).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 15407, 15410 [VERIFY V-557].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** "To hold until my daughter reaches 30, then to her outright." The trust ends at 30; the trustee has a reasonable time to distribute.

### R-TRS-029 — Combining and dividing trusts
**Rule (write this):** The court may combine two or more trusts with substantially similar terms into a single trust, or divide a trust into separate trusts, if doing so will not defeat or substantially impair the accomplishment of the trust purposes or the interests of the beneficiaries.

**Elements:** 1. Similar terms (to combine) or good reason (to divide). 2. No impairment of purposes or interests.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Distinct purposes (e.g., one trust to run a farm, another to fund family-history research) weigh against combining.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 15411, 15412 [VERIFY V-557].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; nice-to-know.

**Example:** Two testamentary trusts have the same beneficiaries but different purposes. A petition to merge them may be denied because a combined trust would dilute one settled purpose.

## II Statute of frauds and rule against perpetuities

### R-TRS-030 — Statute of frauds: trusts of real property
**Rule (write this):** A trust in relation to real property is valid only if evidenced (1) by a written instrument signed by the trustee or the trustee's agent authorized in writing, (2) by a written instrument conveying the property signed by the settlor or the settlor's agent authorized in writing, or (3) by operation of law.

**Elements:** 1. Real property? 2. Qualifying signed writing? 3. If not, a resulting or constructive trust by operation of law?

**Exceptions / defenses:** Resulting and constructive trusts arise by operation of law and need no writing (R-TRS-036 to R-TRS-038).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15206 [S-668] [S-684].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule (general statute of frauds).

**Example:** Uncle orally promises to hold a lot "for my niece." No writing, so no express trust of land; if Uncle obtained the lot by fraud, Niece may still obtain a constructive trust.

### R-TRS-031 — Oral trusts of personal property
**Rule (write this):** An oral trust of personal property is valid, but its existence and terms may be established only by clear and convincing evidence, and the settlor's oral declaration, standing alone, is not sufficient evidence of its creation.

**Elements:** 1. Personal property. 2. Oral declaration or transfer. 3. Clear and convincing evidence. 4. Corroboration beyond the settlor's own words.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Land requires a writing (R-TRS-030); testamentary trusts require a will (R-TRS-032).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15207 [S-669].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs slightly:** general law accepts oral trusts of personalty, usually on clear and convincing evidence; California adds the express "declaration alone is not enough" rule.

**Example:** Grandpa tells friends at dinner that he is "holding my coin collection for my grandson." His statement alone is not enough; a labelled cabinet, letters, or a separate ledger could supply the corroboration.

### R-TRS-032 — Testamentary trust formalities; secret trusts
**Rule (write this):** A trust created by will must satisfy the formalities for wills, and its terms must appear in the will or in a document validly incorporated by reference. Where a will makes an absolute gift to a person who secretly promised the testator to hold it for another (a secret trust), the court admits extrinsic evidence of the promise and imposes a constructive trust on the devisee for the intended beneficiary, to prevent unjust enrichment.

**Elements:** 1. Absolute gift on the face of the will. 2. Devisee's promise to hold for another. 3. Clear proof. 4. Constructive trust.

**Exceptions / defenses:** If the promise cannot be proved, the devisee keeps the gift.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6110 (R-WIL-047); common law; Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 18 [VERIFY V-561].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** T's will leaves "$50,000 to my friend Fran," after Fran promised to give it to T's nonmarital child. Fran holds it on constructive trust for the child.

### R-TRS-033 — Semi-secret trusts
**Rule (write this):** Where the will shows on its face that the devisee is to hold in trust but does not identify the beneficiary or the purpose (a semi-secret trust), the traditional majority rule is that the express trust fails and the devisee holds the property on a resulting trust for the testator's estate, because the devisee was never meant to benefit. The modern Restatement view admits extrinsic evidence and imposes a constructive trust for the intended beneficiary.

**Elements:** 1. Will shows a trust. 2. Beneficiary or purpose missing. 3. Majority: resulting trust to the estate. 4. Modern: constructive trust for the intended beneficiary.

**Exceptions / defenses:** If the terms are in an existing writing referred to in the will, incorporation by reference may save the trust (R-WIL-058).

**Authority:** Common law; Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 18 [VERIFY V-561].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; split of authority. Write both views.

**Example:** "$50,000 to Fran, in trust for the purposes I have told her." Majority: Fran holds on resulting trust for the residue. Modern view: Fran holds on constructive trust for the person T named to her.

### R-TRS-034 — Rule against perpetuities (USRAP) and trusts
**Rule (write this):** In California a nonvested property interest, including a beneficial interest in a private trust, is valid if, when created, it is certain to vest or terminate within a life in being plus 21 years, or if it actually vests or terminates within 90 years after its creation. If an interest violates the rule, the court reforms the disposition to approximate the transferor's plan within the 90 years.

**Elements:** 1. Nonvested interest. 2. Common-law test (lives in being + 21). 3. If it fails, wait and see 90 years. 4. If still invalid, reform.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Charitable trusts (R-TRS-035). Interests created by exercising a power of appointment run from the power's creation in some cases.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21205, 21220 [VERIFY V-563].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE for Trusts; the Real Property MBE tests the common-law rule. On a Trusts essay, apply USRAP.

**Example:** "Income to my children for life, then principal to my grandchildren who reach 25." Void under the common-law rule (an afterborn grandchild might reach 25 too late), but under USRAP it is valid if all interests vest within 90 years, which is almost certain; if not, the court reforms.

### R-TRS-035 — Charitable trusts and perpetuities
**Rule (write this):** A charitable trust may last indefinitely; the rule against perpetuities does not limit its duration. A gift over from a private interest to a charity, or from a charity to a private interest, on a remote condition is subject to the rule, but a gift over from one charity to another is not.

**Elements:** 1. Charitable trust: unlimited duration. 2. Gift over: private ↔ charity → apply the rule; charity → charity → exempt.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Honorary trusts are limited to 21 years (R-TRS-022).

**Authority:** Common law; Prob. Code § 21225 [VERIFY V-563].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A perpetual scholarship fund is valid. "To the Red Cross, but if it ever stops disaster relief, to the Salvation Army" is a charity-to-charity gift over and is not void.

## III Failure of express trust

### R-TRS-036 — Resulting trust when an express trust fails
**Rule (write this):** When an express trust fails in whole or in part, for example for lack of a beneficiary, illegality, a perpetuities violation, or failure of a charitable purpose without cy pres, or when the trust's purposes are fully accomplished without exhausting the property, the trustee holds the property on a resulting trust for the settlor or, if the settlor has died, for the settlor's estate.

**Elements:** 1. Express trust fails or has surplus. 2. Trustee was not meant to benefit. 3. Property returns to settlor or estate.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A charitable trust may be saved by cy pres (R-TRS-021). The instrument may name an alternate taker.

**Authority:** Common law; Restatement (Third) of Trusts §§ 7–8 [VERIFY V-564]; R-REM-050.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A testamentary trust for the testator's dog, "remainder for such good works as my trustee chooses," fails as to the vague remainder; the trustee holds it on resulting trust for the estate.

### R-TRS-037 — Purchase-money resulting trust
**Rule (write this):** When one person pays the purchase price for property and title is taken in another's name, a resulting trust for the payor is presumed, unless the payor intended a gift or a loan. Where title is taken in the name of the payor's spouse, child or other natural object of bounty, a gift is presumed instead. Both presumptions are rebuttable.

**Elements:** 1. A pays. 2. Title to B. 3. No intent to give or lend. 4. Family-gift presumption?

**Exceptions / defenses:** A payor who put title in another's name to defraud creditors may be denied relief (unclean hands).

**Authority:** Common law; Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 9 [VERIFY V-564]; R-REM-050.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule. Between spouses, the community property presumptions control characterization instead (R-CMP rules).

**Example:** Aunt pays $300,000 for a condo titled in her business partner's name "for convenience." The partner holds it on a resulting trust for Aunt.

### R-TRS-038 — Constructive trust
**Rule (write this):** One who gains a thing by fraud, accident, mistake, undue influence, the violation of a trust or other wrongful act is, unless he or she has some other and better right to it, an involuntary trustee of the thing for the person who would otherwise have had it. Courts use the constructive trust to compel a wrongdoer, whether a trustee, a devisee or a third party, to convey the specific property or its traceable product.

**Elements:** 1. Defendant holds specific property. 2. Acquired by wrongful conduct or unjust enrichment. 3. Traceable to plaintiff's interest. 4. No bona fide purchaser.

**Exceptions / defenses:** BFP (R-TRS-095); tracing fails if the property is dissipated (R-REM-047).

**Authority:** Civ. Code §§ 2223, 2224; R-REM-044.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule. It is a remedy, not an express trust: no intent, no fiduciary duties beyond reconveyance.

**Example:** Trustee buys land with trust funds and takes title in his own name. The beneficiaries obtain a constructive trust over the land and its appreciation.

### R-TRS-039 — Gift rather than trust
**Rule (write this):** If the transferor did not intend to impose enforceable duties, as with precatory words or where the transferee was meant to benefit, there is no trust and the transferee takes an outright gift. Conversely, a lifetime gift that fails for lack of delivery will not be saved by treating it as a declaration of trust unless the owner intended to become a trustee.

**Elements:** 1. Intent to benefit the transferee outright? → gift. 2. Failed gift + no intent to be trustee → nothing passes.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Context can turn hopeful words into a duty (R-TRS-004).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15201 [S-668]; common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** Mom tells Son "this painting is yours" but keeps it and never delivers it; she dies. There is no completed gift, and a court will not recast it as a declaration of trust because she never meant to hold it as trustee.

### R-TRS-040 — Where failed-trust property goes
**Rule (write this):** Property in a failed testamentary trust falls into the will's residue, or, if the residuary gift itself fails, passes to the other residuary beneficiaries or by intestacy. Property in a failed inter vivos trust returns by resulting trust to the settlor or the settlor's estate, and then passes under the settlor's will (often by pour-over) or by intestacy.

**Elements:** 1. Testamentary or inter vivos? 2. Residue? 3. Other residuary takers? 4. Intestacy.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Anti-lapse may substitute issue of a predeceased kindred beneficiary first (R-WIL-063).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21111 (R-WIL-065); Prob. Code §§ 6400–6402 (R-WIL-015, R-WIL-016, R-WIL-018); common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California rules (residue of a residue passes to other residuary takers, unlike the old common-law rule).

**Example:** A testamentary trust's remainder to a zoo that has closed fails and cy pres is unavailable. If the trust was the residue, the remainder passes by intestacy to the testator's heirs.

## IV Restrictions on beneficial interest

### R-TRS-041 — Spendthrift provisions
**Rule (write this):** If the trust instrument provides that a beneficiary's interest in income or principal is not subject to voluntary or involuntary transfer, the restraint is valid: the beneficiary cannot assign the interest, and the beneficiary's creditors cannot reach it before it is paid to the beneficiary, subject to the statutory exceptions.

**Elements:** 1. Instrument restrains both voluntary and involuntary transfer. 2. Interest not yet paid. 3. No exception applies (R-TRS-043 to R-TRS-047).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Self-settled interests (R-TRS-047); distributed funds (R-TRS-042).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 15300, 15301(a) [S-672].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Same core rule**, but California's statutory exceptions are broader than the general-law list. Write the general rule, then the California exceptions.

**Example:** A trust pays Ann $5,000 a month and has a spendthrift clause. Ann's credit-card creditor cannot garnish the trust, and her assignment of future payments to a lender is void.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Trust Law makes trust property independent of the beneficiary's own estate and limits enforcement against it, but it has no detailed list of spendthrift exceptions like California's (approx.).

### R-TRS-042 — Distributed funds and principal that is due
**Rule (write this):** Once income or principal has been paid to the beneficiary, it is the beneficiary's own property and creditors may reach it. After an amount of principal has become due and payable under the trust instrument, a judgment creditor may petition for an order directing the trustee to satisfy the judgment out of that principal, notwithstanding the restraint; the 25 percent cap on general creditors does not apply to these due-and-payable amounts.

**Elements:** 1. Paid → reachable in the beneficiary's hands. 2. Principal due and payable → court may order payment to creditor, up to the full amount due.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The court has discretion over the amount. Distributions explicitly intended or actually required for the beneficiary's support remain protected (*Carmack*).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15301(b) [S-672]; *Carmack v. Reynolds* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 844 [S-655] [S-1105].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific on due principal.

**Example:** The trust requires distribution of $100,000 principal to Ben at age 30. Ben turns 30; before the trustee pays, Ben's judgment creditor may seek an order directing payment of that principal to the creditor.

### R-TRS-043 — Exception: child and spousal support
**Rule (write this):** Notwithstanding a spendthrift provision, a beneficiary's interest may be reached to satisfy a judgment for support of the beneficiary's child, spouse or former spouse; the court may order the trustee to pay the support from income or principal that is payable, or that the trustee in its discretion has decided to pay, to the beneficiary, as is equitable.

**Elements:** 1. Support judgment. 2. Owed by the beneficiary to a child, spouse or former spouse. 3. Amounts payable or determined to be paid. 4. Equitable order.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Purely discretionary amounts the trustee has not decided to pay are harder to reach.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15305 [S-672].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same as the general-law exception (UTC § 503 [VERIFY V-594]).

**Example:** Ken's spendthrift trust pays him $4,000 a month, and he owes $2,000 a month in child support. The court may order the trustee to pay the support directly.

### R-TRS-044 — Exception: restitution for crimes
**Rule (write this):** A spendthrift restraint does not protect a beneficiary's interest from a judgment for restitution or damages owed to the victim of a felony of which the beneficiary was convicted; the court may order the trustee to pay the judgment from amounts payable to the beneficiary.

**Elements:** 1. Felony conviction. 2. Restitution or damages judgment for the crime. 3. Amounts payable to the beneficiary.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Limited to the statutory categories.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15305.5 [S-673] [S-1122].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific.

**Example:** A beneficiary convicted of felony assault owes the victim $80,000. The victim can reach trust payments despite the spendthrift clause.

### R-TRS-045 — Exception: reimbursement for public support
**Rule (write this):** A spendthrift restraint does not prevent the State or a county from reaching the beneficiary's interest to recover the cost of public support provided to the beneficiary, to the extent and in the manner the statute allows.

**Elements:** 1. Public support provided. 2. Statutory claim by the public entity. 3. Statutory limits.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Section 15306(b) bars recovery where trust distributions are required for the care of a disabled beneficiary (as *Carmack* describes the subdivision) [S-655].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15306(a), (b) [S-655].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; nice-to-know.

**Example:** A county that paid for a beneficiary's institutional care may petition to recover the cost from his trust interest.

### R-TRS-046 — General creditors: the 25 percent cap, excess over support, and *Carmack*
**Rule (write this):** Notwithstanding a spendthrift provision, a general judgment creditor may reach (1) up to the full amount of principal that is already due and payable but still held by the trustee (§ 15301(b)), and (2) up to 25 percent of each future payment to the beneficiary, reduced as needed for the support of the beneficiary and dependents (§ 15306.5). Section 15307 (amounts in excess of the beneficiary's education and support needs) does not let a general creditor take more than that 25 percent of future payments: under *Carmack v. Reynolds* (2017), orders under §§ 15306.5 and 15307 together are capped at 25 percent of each payment, and the cap does not apply to due-and-payable principal.

**Elements:** 1. Money judgment. 2. Principal already due and payable → reachable in full (support carve-out). 3. Future payments → order of up to 25 percent of each payment, considering support needs. 4. § 15307 excess-over-support orders count toward the same 25 percent cap.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Support needs of the beneficiary and dependents; amounts explicitly intended or actually required for support; court discretion.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 15301(b), 15306.5, 15307 [S-654] [S-672]; *Carmack v. Reynolds* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 844 [S-655]; *Frealy v. Reynolds* (9th Cir. 2017) (applying *Carmack*) [S-1105].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs:** under general law a valid spendthrift clause bars general creditors entirely (only "exception creditors" can reach the interest); California lets general creditors reach due principal and up to 25 percent of future payments. Write the general rule, then California's rule and *Carmack*.

**Example:** A trust pays Lou $10,000 a month, and a contract creditor holds a $90,000 judgment. The court may order up to $2,500 a month paid to the creditor, less if Lou and his dependents need more than $7,500 a month for support; § 15307 cannot push the order above $2,500. If a $50,000 principal distribution has already come due and the trustee still holds it, the creditor may reach all of it under § 15301(b).

### R-TRS-047 — Self-settled trusts
**Rule (write this):** If the settlor is also a beneficiary, a restraint on the transfer of the settlor's interest is invalid against the settlor's creditors; and if the trustee has discretion (or a support standard) to pay income or principal to or for the settlor, the settlor's creditors may reach the maximum amount the trustee could pay to or for the settlor under the trust's terms, not exceeding the settlor's proportionate contribution to the trust.

**Elements:** 1. Settlor = beneficiary. 2. Spendthrift clause ineffective as to the settlor's interest. 3. Discretionary or support trust: creditors reach the maximum the trustee could pay. 4. Cap: the settlor's proportionate contribution.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The rule reaches only the settlor's own interest, not interests of others; the trust itself remains valid.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15304(a), (b) [S-672] [S-1107].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same as the majority rule. California does not allow self-settled asset-protection trusts.

**Example:** A surgeon alone puts $2 million in an irrevocable trust for herself with a spendthrift clause and discretionary distributions. Her malpractice creditors may reach the full $2 million the trustee could pay her (she contributed all of it).

### R-TRS-048 — Discretionary trusts
**Rule (write this):** Where the trustee has discretion whether and how much to pay a beneficiary, a transferee or creditor of the beneficiary cannot compel the trustee to pay any amount that may be paid only in the exercise of that discretion. Once the trustee decides to pay, the payment becomes subject to the support, restitution and general-creditor exceptions. If the trustee knows of a transfer or has been served in a creditor's proceeding and still makes a discretionary payment to the beneficiary, the trustee is liable to the transferee or creditor to the extent the payment impairs its rights, unless the interest is protected by a valid spendthrift restraint.

**Elements:** 1. Pure discretion? 2. Creditor cannot compel. 3. Amounts the trustee decides to pay → §§ 15305–15307. 4. Notice of transfer or service of process → trustee liability, unless a valid spendthrift clause applies.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Self-settled discretionary trusts (R-TRS-047). The rule applies whether or not the instrument states a standard for the discretion.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15303(a), (b) [S-672] [S-1106].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same core rule.

**Example:** A trust without a spendthrift clause gives Trustee "sole discretion" to pay Pat income. Pat's creditor serves process. The creditor cannot force any payment, but if Trustee then pays Pat, Trustee is liable to the creditor to the extent the payment impairs the creditor's rights. With a valid spendthrift clause, that trustee liability does not arise.

### R-TRS-049 — Support trusts
**Rule (write this):** Where the instrument directs the trustee to pay income or principal for the education or support of a beneficiary, the beneficiary's interest, to the extent necessary for education or support, cannot be transferred and is not subject to enforcement of a money judgment until paid, except as provided in the self-settled, support-judgment, restitution, public-support and general-creditor provisions.

**Elements:** 1. Support or education standard. 2. Protected to the extent necessary for support. 3. Statutory exceptions. 4. Excess over support reachable (§ 15307).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Under general law, suppliers of necessaries to the beneficiary can reach a support trust.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15302 [S-672] [S-1108].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance.

**Example:** A trust pays "so much as needed for Ella's support and education." Ella's credit-card creditor cannot reach the amounts needed for her support; any surplus is reachable only within the 25 percent cap on future payments (R-TRS-046).

## V Duties of trustee

### R-TRS-050 — Duty to administer according to the trust and the law
**Rule (write this):** On accepting the trust, the trustee must administer it according to the trust instrument and, except as the instrument validly provides otherwise, according to the duties imposed by statute. The instrument may relax many duties but cannot authorize bad faith or excuse the trustee from acting in the beneficiaries' interest.

**Elements:** 1. Acceptance (R-TRS-007). 2. Follow the instrument's terms. 3. Statutory duties fill the gaps.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Court-authorized deviation (R-TRS-026); written directions of the settlor of a revocable trust (R-TRS-065).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16000, 15600 [VERIFY V-569].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** The trust directs the trustee to keep the family home for the widow's use. The trustee sells it to invest more profitably. Breach, absent court approval.

### R-TRS-051 — Standard of care
**Rule (write this):** A trustee must administer the trust with the reasonable care, skill and caution under the circumstances then prevailing that a prudent person acting in a like capacity would use in conducting an enterprise of like character and aims to accomplish the trust's purposes. A trustee who has special skills, or was appointed because of representations of special skills, must use them.

**Elements:** 1. Prudent person in a like capacity. 2. Circumstances at the time. 3. Higher standard for professionals and experts.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The standard is not lowered because the trustee serves without pay.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16040, 16014 [VERIFY V-569].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A bank trustee with an investment department lets a bond portfolio mature into cash that sits idle for three years. It is measured against a professional's standard and breached it.

### R-TRS-052 — Duty of loyalty
**Rule (write this):** A trustee must administer the trust solely in the interest of the beneficiaries, putting their interests ahead of the trustee's own interests and those of third persons.

**Elements:** 1. Trustee's conduct. 2. Any personal or third-party interest? 3. Was the beneficiaries' interest the sole motive?

**Exceptions / defenses:** Authorization in the instrument, informed beneficiary consent, or court approval.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16002.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** The trustee steers the trust's property sales to her brother's brokerage at above-market commissions. Disloyal even if the brother does good work.

### R-TRS-053 — Self-dealing (no further inquiry)
**Rule (write this):** A trustee must not use or deal with trust property for the trustee's own profit or for any purpose unconnected with the trust, and must not take part in any transaction in which the trustee has an interest adverse to the beneficiary. Self-dealing, such as buying trust property, selling one's own property to the trust or borrowing trust funds, is a breach regardless of fairness or good faith unless the instrument authorizes it, the beneficiaries give informed consent, or a court approves.

**Elements:** 1. Trustee on both sides, or personally interested. 2. No authorization, informed consent or court approval. 3. Breach; no inquiry into fairness.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Authorization, informed consent (R-TRS-085), court approval.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16004(a) [S-661]; common law ("no further inquiry" rule).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** Trustee Sam buys the trust's house for himself at fair market value (Feb 2016 pattern). A breach of loyalty: fair price is no defense. Bob may set the sale aside or recover Sam's profit (R-TRS-079).

### R-TRS-054 — Presumption against trustee–beneficiary transactions
**Rule (write this):** A transaction between the trustee and a beneficiary, made during the trust or while the trustee's influence over the beneficiary remains, by which the trustee obtains an advantage from the beneficiary, is presumed to be a violation of the trustee's fiduciary duties. The presumption affects the burden of proof and does not apply to agreements about hiring or compensating the trustee.

**Elements:** 1. Trustee–beneficiary transaction. 2. During the trust or while influence remains. 3. Advantage to the trustee. 4. Trustee must prove fairness and full disclosure.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Compensation agreements.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16004(c) [S-661].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance as general law (heightened scrutiny).

**Example:** The trustee buys a beneficiary's remainder interest for 60 percent of its appraised value. The deal is presumed a breach; the trustee must prove full disclosure and fairness.

### R-TRS-055 — Conflicts of interest short of direct self-dealing
**Rule (write this):** A trustee also breaches the duty of loyalty by entering a transaction in which the trustee has a conflicting personal interest, such as investing trust funds in a business the trustee owns or manages, hiring the trustee's own firm or relatives, or competing with the trust, unless the instrument authorizes it or the beneficiaries give informed consent; such investments usually also fail the prudent-investor standard.

**Elements:** 1. Trustee's personal stake. 2. No authorization or consent. 3. Loyalty breach. 4. Separately test prudence (R-TRS-057).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Express authorization; informed consent; court approval.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16002, 16004(a) [S-661]; common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** Sam invests trust cash in his own new start-up (Feb 2016 pattern). That is a loyalty breach (conflict) and a prudence breach (speculative and undiversified).

### R-TRS-056 — Duty of impartiality
**Rule (write this):** If a trust has two or more beneficiaries, the trustee must deal impartially with them and act impartially in investing and managing the trust property, taking into account their differing interests, notably balancing income for the life beneficiary against growth for the remainder beneficiaries.

**Elements:** 1. Successive or concurrent beneficiaries. 2. Investment and distribution decisions. 3. Due regard for each interest.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The instrument may expressly favor one beneficiary (e.g., "primary concern is my wife's comfort").

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16003; California principal and income statute (Prob. Code §§ 16320 et seq.) [VERIFY V-570].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** The trustee of a trust for Widow (income) and Kids (remainder) puts everything in non-dividend growth stock at the Kids' urging. Widow receives almost no income: breach of impartiality.

### R-TRS-057 — Prudent investor rule
**Rule (write this):** A trustee must invest and manage trust assets as a prudent investor would, considering the trust's purposes, terms, distribution requirements and other circumstances, and exercising reasonable care, skill and caution. Each investment decision is judged not in isolation but in the context of the trust portfolio as a whole and as part of an overall strategy with risk and return objectives reasonably suited to the trust.

**Elements:** 1. Purposes and distribution needs. 2. Portfolio as a whole. 3. Risk and return. 4. Relevant factors: economic conditions, inflation, taxes, liquidity, expected return, the beneficiaries' other resources, an asset's special relationship to the trust. 5. Judged at the time, not by hindsight (R-TRS-058).

**Exceptions / defenses:** The instrument may expand or restrict the rule; a trustee who reasonably relies on the instrument's terms is protected (§ 16046 [VERIFY V-924]).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16045–16054, esp. § 16047(a)–(c) [S-1119].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule (California adopted the UPIA). The older "prudent man" rule judged each investment alone.

**Example:** The trustee puts 40 percent of a trust that must pay a widow's living expenses into one biotech start-up. Even with safe holdings elsewhere, the portfolio's risk is unsuited to the trust's purpose.

### R-TRS-058 — Duty to diversify; reviewing inherited assets; no hindsight
**Rule (write this):** A trustee must diversify the trust's investments unless, under the circumstances, it is prudent not to. Within a reasonable time after accepting the trust, the trustee must review the assets and decide whether to retain or dispose of them, and compliance with the prudent-investor rule is judged on the facts and circumstances existing when the decision was made, not by hindsight.

**Elements:** 1. Concentration? 2. Reason not to diversify (tax cost, special asset, instrument authorization)? 3. Prompt review after acceptance. 4. Judge at the time.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The instrument may authorize retention ("retain the family company stock"), but the trustee must still act prudently.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16048, 16049, 16051 [S-1119].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A trust receives 90 percent of its value in the settlor's employer's stock, with no clause authorizing retention. The trustee waits two years while the stock collapses: breach of the duty to diversify.

### R-TRS-059 — Duties to take control, preserve, enforce and make productive
**Rule (write this):** The trustee must take reasonable steps to take control of and preserve the trust property, to enforce claims of the trust and defend actions against it, and to make the trust property productive under the circumstances and in furtherance of the trust's purposes.

**Elements:** 1. Collect and secure assets. 2. Insure and maintain. 3. Pursue claims. 4. Productive use.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Assets the instrument directs be held for use (a residence) need not produce income.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16006, 16007, 16010, 16011 [VERIFY V-569].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** The trustee leaves the trust's rental house vacant for a year and lets the insurance lapse before a fire. Breach of the duties to preserve and to make the property productive.

### R-TRS-060 — Duty to earmark; no commingling
**Rule (write this):** The trustee must keep trust property separate from other property and see that it is designated as trust property. Commingling trust funds with the trustee's own is a breach even if no loss results, and any loss that follows is charged to the trustee.

**Elements:** 1. Separate accounts and titles. 2. Designation as trust property. 3. Commingling → breach; tracing (R-REM-048).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Statutory exceptions for certain pooled or nominee holdings [VERIFY V-569].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16009 [VERIFY V-569].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** The trustee deposits trust rents in his personal checking account "temporarily." That is a breach whether or not he later repays.

### R-TRS-061 — Delegation and co-trustees
**Rule (write this):** A trustee may delegate investment and management functions that a prudent trustee could properly delegate, but must use reasonable care in selecting the agent, setting the scope and terms of the delegation, and monitoring the agent; the trustee may not hand over the whole administration. Co-trustees must act unanimously unless the instrument provides otherwise, each must participate in the administration, and each must use reasonable care to prevent a co-trustee's breach and to compel redress of one.

**Elements:** 1. Delegable function? 2. Care in selection, terms, monitoring. 3. Co-trustees: unanimity, participation, vigilance.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The instrument may allow majority action or assign duties among co-trustees.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16052, 15620, 16013 [VERIFY V-569].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule (the older rule barred delegating discretionary functions).

**Example:** Dot and Sam are co-trustees. Dot lets Sam manage everything and never reviews a statement while Sam diverts funds. Dot is liable for failing to prevent the breach (R-TRS-083).

### R-TRS-062 — Duty to inform and report
**Rule (write this):** The trustee must keep the beneficiaries reasonably informed of the trust and its administration, must on reasonable request report information about the trust's assets, liabilities, receipts and disbursements, and must provide the terms of an irrevocable trust to a beneficiary who requests them.

**Elements:** 1. Beneficiary of an irrevocable trust. 2. Reasonable information and request. 3. Terms on request.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No duty to remainder beneficiaries while the trust is revocable and a person holding the power to revoke is competent; when no such person is competent, the duties run to the beneficiaries who would take if the settlor had died (§ 15800; R-TRS-065).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16060, 16061, 16061.5 [S-1126] [S-1125].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance (UTC § 813 [VERIFY V-594]).

**Example:** A remainder beneficiary of an irrevocable trust asks for a balance sheet; the trustee refuses because "you get nothing until Mom dies." Breach.

### R-TRS-063 — Duty to account
**Rule (write this):** The trustee must account at least annually, at the termination of the trust, and on a change of trustee, to each beneficiary to whom income or principal is required or authorized to be currently distributed. No account is owed while the trust is revocable (unless no person holding the power to revoke is competent) or where the beneficiary is also the trustee, and a beneficiary may waive accounts in writing.

**Elements:** 1. Current beneficiary. 2. Timing: annually, termination, change of trustee. 3. Exceptions: revocable with a competent power-holder; beneficiary is trustee; waiver.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Waivers have limits, and the court can still order an account.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16062, 16064, 16069 [S-679] [S-683] [VERIFY V-593]; § 15800 (as amended by AB 1079, Stats. 2021, ch. 749) [S-1115].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific schedule.

**Example:** The trustee of an irrevocable trust for three siblings never accounts in five years. The siblings may petition to compel an account (R-TRS-072) and seek removal (R-TRS-073).

### R-TRS-064 — Notification on irrevocability; 120-day contest period
**Rule (write this):** When a revocable trust becomes irrevocable because of the settlor's death, the trustee must serve a statutory notification on each beneficiary and each heir within 60 days. A person served may not bring an action to contest the trust more than 120 days after service of the notification, or 60 days after delivery of a copy of the trust terms requested during that 120-day period, whichever is later.

**Elements:** 1. Settlor's death made the trust irrevocable. 2. Notification served. 3. Count 120 days, or 60 days from delivery of requested terms. 4. Later date controls.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The 120-day bar applies only to the death-of-settlor notice (2022 amendment) [S-653]. A defective notice may not start the period.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16061.7, 16061.8 [S-652] [S-653] [S-1125].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs:** California-specific; UTC § 604 uses the earlier of three years after death or 120 days after notice [VERIFY V-594].

**Example:** Mom dies; the trustee serves the notice on Son on March 1. Son requests the trust terms and receives them on June 10. His deadline is the later of June 29 (120 days) and August 9 (60 days after June 10): August 9.

### R-TRS-065 — Revocable trusts: duties run to the settlor
**Rule (write this):** While a trust is revocable and the person holding the power to revoke is competent, that person, not the beneficiaries, holds the beneficiaries' rights and the trustee's duties are owed to that person; a trustee who acts on the written directions of the holder of the power to revoke is not liable to the beneficiaries. After the settlor dies, the beneficiaries have standing to sue the trustee for breaches committed during the settlor's life that harmed the trust.

**Elements:** 1. Trust revocable and power-holder competent? 2. Duties to the power-holder. 3. Power-holder's written direction a defense. 4. After death: beneficiaries may sue.

**Exceptions / defenses:** If no person holding the power to revoke is competent, the trustee must, within 60 days after learning of the incompetence, notify the beneficiaries who would take if the settlor had died and give them a copy of the trust, and the duties to account and to report run to those beneficiaries (AB 1079, Stats. 2021, ch. 749) [S-1115] [S-680].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15800 [S-1115] [S-680]; Prob. Code §§ 16001, 16462 [S-1127] [S-1121]; *Estate of Giraldin* (2012) 55 Cal.4th 1058.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific (*Giraldin*).

**Example:** Son, trustee of Mom's revocable trust, makes risky investments while Mom is alive. After her death his siblings may sue; Son's best defense is Mom's written direction or consent.

### R-TRS-066 — Trustee compensation; attorney as trustee
**Rule (write this):** A trustee is entitled to the compensation the instrument provides or, if none, to reasonable compensation. Notwithstanding any contrary trust provision, a trustee who is a lawyer, and the trustee's close relatives and law firm, may not also be paid for legal services to the trust unless the trustee waives trustee compensation or the dual compensation is approved, after full disclosure, by court order or by 30 days' advance written notice to which no one entitled to notice objects. The rule does not apply if the trustee is related by blood or marriage to, or is a cohabitant of, the settlor.

**Elements:** 1. Compensation source (instrument or reasonable). 2. Attorney-trustee (or relative or firm paid for legal work)? 3. Dual compensation only by waiver of trustee fees, court order, or unopposed advance notice. 4. Settlor-relative exception.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Excessive compensation is a removal ground (R-TRS-073). A trust clause cannot waive § 15687; such a waiver is void.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 15680, 15681 [VERIFY V-924]; § 15687 [S-677] [S-1116].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. California-specific (PR crossover: rule 1.8.1 business transactions; R-PRF-026).

**Example:** A lawyer-trustee unrelated to the settlor bills the trust $400 an hour for legal work and also takes a 1 percent trustee fee without a court order or notice to the beneficiaries. That violates § 15687.

## VI Trustee's discretionary powers

### R-TRS-067 — Trustee's powers: express, statutory and implied
**Rule (write this):** A trustee has the powers conferred by the trust instrument, the powers conferred by statute (to sell, lease, borrow, invest, insure, settle claims, employ agents and similar powers) unless the instrument limits them, and implied powers necessary or appropriate to carry out the trust's purposes. Every power is exercised subject to the trustee's fiduciary duties.

**Elements:** 1. Source of the power. 2. Limits in the instrument. 3. Exercise consistent with duties.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A court may authorize acts beyond the powers (R-TRS-026).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16200, 16202 [VERIFY V-924]; common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** The trust is silent on selling land. The trustee has the statutory power to sell but must sell prudently and loyally (fair price, proper marketing, no insider buyer).

### R-TRS-068 — Discretion must be exercised reasonably
**Rule (write this):** A discretionary power conferred on a trustee is not left to the trustee's arbitrary discretion but must be exercised reasonably, in good faith, and in accordance with the trust's terms and purposes. A court will not substitute its judgment for a reasonable exercise of discretion, but it will intervene to prevent an abuse.

**Elements:** 1. Discretion granted. 2. Exercised in good faith. 3. Consistent with purposes and standards. 4. Not arbitrary.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Broader language: R-TRS-069.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16080 [S-659].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** The trust allows payments for "my grandson's education." The trustee refuses all tuition because he dislikes the art school the grandson chose. Abuse of discretion: an improper motive.

### R-TRS-069 — "Sole," "absolute" or "uncontrolled" discretion
**Rule (write this):** Even when the instrument grants "absolute," "sole" or "uncontrolled" discretion, the trustee must act in accordance with fiduciary principles and may not act in bad faith or in disregard of the trust's purposes. A beneficiary who, as trustee or co-trustee, may make discretionary distributions to himself or herself may exercise that power in his or her own favor only for health, education, support or maintenance, unless the settlor clearly indicates a broader power by express reference to § 16081(c).

**Elements:** 1. Expanded discretion language. 2. Good faith and purposes still required. 3. Trustee-beneficiary: HEMS limit unless the instrument expressly refers to § 16081(c).

**Exceptions / defenses:** The court's review is deferential but not absent. The HEMS limit does not apply to a settlor's power over a revocable trust or to certain marital-deduction trusts (§ 16081(d)).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16081(a)–(d) [S-659] [S-1117].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance as general law.

**Example:** A trustee with "sole and absolute discretion" over distributions to his sister and himself pays himself $300,000 for a yacht and his sister nothing. That is bad faith and outside the HEMS limit.

### R-TRS-070 — Reviewing discretion; the beneficiary's other resources
**Rule (write this):** In making discretionary distributions, the trustee must apply the standards and purposes in the instrument. Whether the trustee should consider the beneficiary's other resources depends on the instrument and the settlor's intent. A court reviews only for abuse: bad faith, improper motive, failure to use judgment, or a decision outside the bounds of reasonable judgment.

**Elements:** 1. Standard in the instrument (support, comfort, accustomed manner of living). 2. Other resources: per instrument and intent. 3. Abuse test.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Express direction to ignore other resources.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16080 [S-659]; Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 50 [VERIFY V-923].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** The trust pays "for Ann's support in her accustomed manner of living." The trustee may consider Ann's salary, but cannot deny support simply because her husband is wealthy if the instrument points the other way.

## VII Rights of beneficiary

### R-TRS-071 — The beneficial interest; who may enforce
**Rule (write this):** A beneficiary holds an equitable interest that is freely transferable and reachable by creditors unless restrained by a valid spendthrift provision. Beneficiaries, co-trustees and successor trustees may enforce the trust, but while a trust is revocable and the settlor is competent, its beneficiaries have no enforceable rights against the trustee.

**Elements:** 1. Nature of the interest. 2. Transferable unless restrained. 3. Standing.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Charitable trusts: AG enforcement (R-TRS-020).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 15300–15301, 15800, 17200 [S-672].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A remainder beneficiary sells her interest to a buyer for cash. Valid unless the trust has a spendthrift clause.

### R-TRS-072 — Petitions to the court
**Rule (write this):** A trustee or beneficiary may petition the probate court concerning the internal affairs of a trust or to determine its existence, for example to construe the instrument, determine validity, settle or compel accounts, compel information, instruct the trustee, modify or terminate the trust, appoint or remove a trustee, compel redress of a breach, or fix compensation.

**Elements:** 1. Proper petitioner. 2. Internal-affairs subject. 3. Relief sought.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Contests of a trust after the settlor's death are subject to the 120-day period (R-TRS-064).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 17200.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California procedure.

**Example:** Beneficiaries petition under § 17200 to compel the trustee to account and to remove him.

### R-TRS-073 — Removal of a trustee
**Rule (write this):** A trustee may be removed under the trust's terms, by the court on its own motion, or on petition of a settlor, co-trustee or beneficiary. Grounds include breach of trust, insolvency or other unfitness, hostility or lack of cooperation among co-trustees that impairs administration, failure or refusal to act, excessive compensation, and any other good cause.

**Elements:** 1. Proper petitioner. 2. Ground. 3. Court's discretion; interim relief (suspend, appoint temporary trustee).

**Exceptions / defenses:** The settlor's choice of trustee carries weight; friction alone may not justify removal.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15642 [S-658] [S-1118].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance.

**Example:** Sibling co-trustees refuse to speak to each other, and the administration stalls for a year. The court may remove one or both.

### R-TRS-074 — Drafter or other disqualified person as sole trustee
**Rule (write this):** A sole trustee who is a person described in § 21380(a), such as the drafter of the instrument, a fiduciary who transcribed it, or a care custodian of a dependent-adult settlor, may be removed on that ground, whether or not the person also receives a gift, unless the court finds that the settlor intended that person to serve and that this intent was not the product of fraud or undue influence. Any donative gift to such a person is separately presumed invalid (R-TRS-076).

**Elements:** 1. Sole trustee. 2. § 21380(a) category. 3. Statutory exclusion? 4. Settlor's untainted intent shown?

**Exceptions / defenses:** Court finding of the settlor's intent free of fraud or undue influence. The ground does not apply if the settlor is related by blood or marriage to, or is a cohabitant of, the trustee or the drafter or transcriber; if a court order approved the designation; or if an independent attorney counseled the settlor and signed a certificate of independent review. A trustee removed on this ground may be charged the costs of the proceeding, including attorney's fees.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 15642(b)(6), (c) [S-658] [S-1118]; Prob. Code § 21380 [S-645].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific (PR crossover: R-PRF-027).

**Example:** The settlor's paid caregiver arranges a trust naming herself sole trustee. The beneficiaries petition to remove her, and she must prove the settlor's independent intent.

### R-TRS-075 — No-contest clauses in trusts
**Rule (write this):** No-contest clauses in trusts are governed by the same statute as wills: they are enforced only against a direct contest brought without probable cause and, if the clause expressly so provides, against a challenge to the transferor's ownership of property or a creditor's claim. A petition to construe the trust or to challenge the trustee's conduct is not a direct contest.

**Elements:** 1. Protected instrument. 2. Direct contest? 3. Probable cause? 4. Forfeiture only if no probable cause.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Strict construction; probable cause.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21310, 21311 [S-608] [S-1056]; R-WIL-093, R-WIL-094.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific.

**Example:** Daughter petitions to remove the trustee for breach. That is not a direct contest, so the no-contest clause cannot forfeit her share.

### R-TRS-076 — Presumptively disqualified donees in trust instruments
**Rule (write this):** The presumption of fraud or undue influence for donative transfers to the drafter, to a fiduciary who transcribed the instrument, to a care custodian of a dependent adult, and to their relatives and associates applies to trusts and trust amendments as it does to wills. It is rebuttable only by clear and convincing evidence and is conclusive for the drafter and persons related to or associated with the drafter, subject to the § 21382 exceptions and the § 21384 certificate of independent review; a failed gift passes as if the donee predeceased without issue.

**Elements:** 1. Donative transfer in a trust instrument. 2. Disqualified category. 3. Exception or certificate? 4. Rebuttal (none for the drafter). 5. Effect.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Relatives within the fourth degree, cohabitants, court-approved instruments, charities, small gifts, certificate of independent review.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21380, 21382, 21384, 21386 [S-645] [S-621] [S-635] [S-646]; R-WIL-095 to R-WIL-098.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific (PR crossover: R-PRF-027).

**Example:** The settlor's lawyer drafts a trust amendment giving the lawyer's son $100,000. The son is related to the drafter and not to the settlor, so the gift is conclusively presumed invalid (no rebuttal evidence is allowed) and passes as if he predeceased.

### R-TRS-077 — Omitted heirs and divorce rules applied to revocable trusts
**Rule (write this):** California applies the omitted spouse and omitted child statutes to a revocable trust that becomes irrevocable at the settlor's death, computing the share on both probate and trust property, and a nonprobate transfer to a former spouse in a revocable trust fails on dissolution as if the former spouse had predeceased.

**Elements:** 1. Revocable trust as a testamentary instrument. 2. Spouse married or child born after all instruments. 3. Exceptions (R-WIL-087, R-WIL-091). 4. Former spouse → gift fails.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The omitted-heir exceptions; express contrary provision for the former spouse.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21601, 21610, 21620, 5040 [S-605]; R-WIL-083, R-WIL-089, R-WIL-102.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific (Wills crossover).

**Example:** Sam's trust predates the birth of his son Dave, who is not provided for (Feb 2012 pattern). Dave may claim an omitted-child share from the trust assets unless an exception applies.

## VIII Remedying breach of trust

### R-TRS-078 — Breach of trust and the menu of remedies
**Rule (write this):** A violation by a trustee of any duty owed to the beneficiaries is a breach of trust. For a breach or threatened breach, a beneficiary or co-trustee may petition the court to compel performance, enjoin the breach, compel the trustee to redress it by paying money or restoring property, appoint a receiver or temporary trustee, remove the trustee, reduce or deny compensation, set aside the trustee's acts, impose an equitable lien or constructive trust, or trace and recover trust property, subject to the protection of good-faith third parties. These remedies are cumulative with other remedies.

**Elements:** 1. Duty. 2. Breach. 3. Choose remedies to fit the harm (money, property, removal, compensation).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Trustee defenses (R-TRS-082, R-TRS-084 to R-TRS-086).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16400, 16420 [S-678].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance (UTC § 1001 [VERIFY V-594]).

**Example:** Bob asks the court to remove Sam, surcharge him, set aside the house purchase and trace the start-up investment (Feb 2016 pattern).

### R-TRS-079 — Remedies for self-dealing: rescind or recover profit
**Rule (write this):** When a trustee self-deals, the beneficiaries may elect either to set the transaction aside and recover the property (if the trustee or a transferee with notice still holds it), or to affirm it and hold the trustee liable for any profit made and any loss caused, choosing whichever gives the greater recovery.

**Elements:** 1. Self-dealing (R-TRS-053). 2. Property still held? → rescission. 3. Resold at a profit? → disgorgement. 4. Loss? → surcharge.

**Exceptions / defenses:** BFP protection (R-TRS-095); beneficiary affirmance with full knowledge (R-TRS-085).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16420, 16440(a) [S-678]; common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** Sam bought the trust's house for $500,000 (its market value) and resold it a year later for $650,000. Bob can recover Sam's $150,000 profit; had Sam kept the house, Bob could have the sale set aside.

### R-TRS-080 — Tracing; constructive trust and equitable lien
**Rule (write this):** Beneficiaries may trace misappropriated trust property into its product and impose a constructive trust or an equitable lien on it, including into a commingled account under the lowest-intermediate-balance rule, until the property reaches a bona fide purchaser for value.

**Elements:** 1. Identify trust property. 2. Follow each exchange. 3. Choose constructive trust (asset rose) or lien (asset fell). 4. BFP cut-off.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Dissipation ends tracing; BFP (R-TRS-095).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16420 [S-678]; Civ. Code § 2224; R-REM-044, R-REM-045, R-REM-047, R-REM-048.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** The trustee uses $100,000 of trust cash to buy stock in his own name, now worth $160,000. The beneficiaries claim the stock and its gain by constructive trust.

## IX Trustee liability to beneficiary

### R-TRS-081 — Measure of the trustee's liability
**Rule (write this):** A trustee who commits a breach of trust is chargeable with whichever of the following is appropriate: (1) any loss or depreciation in value of the trust estate resulting from the breach, with interest; (2) any profit the trustee made through the breach, with interest; or (3) any profit that would have accrued to the trust estate if the loss of profit resulted from the breach.

**Elements:** 1. Loss caused. 2. Trustee's profit. 3. Lost profit. 4. Interest. 5. No double recovery.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Excuse (R-TRS-082); exculpation (R-TRS-084).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16440(a) [S-678].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance.

**Example:** The trustee sells blue-chip stock worth $200,000 to buy into a friend's restaurant, which fails; the stake is now worth $20,000. The trustee owes the $180,000 loss with interest, and more if the stock would have risen.

### R-TRS-082 — Court's power to excuse liability
**Rule (write this):** The court may excuse a trustee in whole or in part from liability for a breach if the trustee acted reasonably and in good faith under the circumstances as known to the trustee, and it would be equitable to excuse the trustee.

**Elements:** 1. Reasonable conduct. 2. Good faith. 3. Equity favors excuse.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Rarely available for loyalty breaches or where the trustee profited.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16440(b) [S-678] [VERIFY V-925].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific in form.

**Example:** A lay trustee relied on the trust's accountant's written (wrong) advice about a tax deadline. The court may excuse liability for the penalty.

### R-TRS-083 — Liability for co-trustees, predecessors and agents
**Rule (write this):** A trustee is not liable for a co-trustee's breach unless the trustee participated in it, improperly delegated to the co-trustee, approved, acquiesced in or concealed it, negligently enabled it, or neglected to take reasonable steps to compel redress. A successor trustee is not liable for a predecessor's breach unless the successor knew or should have known of it and improperly permitted it to continue or neglected to compel redress or delivery of trust property. A trustee is liable for an agent's acts only if the trustee directed them or failed to use reasonable care in selecting or supervising the agent.

**Elements:** 1. Whose breach? 2. Trustee's own fault (participation, negligence, inaction). 3. Successor: knowledge plus failure to act.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Reasonable steps taken.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16401, 16402, 16403 [VERIFY V-579].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance.

**Example:** Tara, the successor trustee, discovers that the prior trustee left the trust undiversified and that he took a loan from the trust, and does nothing for two years (Feb 2012 pattern). She is liable for her own failure to correct the portfolio and to pursue the predecessor.

### R-TRS-084 — Exculpatory clauses
**Rule (write this):** A trust provision relieving the trustee of liability for breach is enforceable, except that it cannot relieve the trustee of liability for a breach committed intentionally, with gross negligence, in bad faith or with reckless indifference to the beneficiary's interest, or for any profit the trustee derived from the breach. Courts construe such clauses strictly.

**Elements:** 1. Clause covers the conduct? 2. Conduct only ordinary negligence? 3. No profit to the trustee.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Section 16461(c)–(d) also restricts trust clauses that treat a beneficiary's failure to object to an account within a short period as a release [VERIFY V-926]. The UTC also refuses to enforce a clause inserted by abuse of a fiduciary or confidential relationship; § 16461 has no such express rule, but argue undue influence or § 16004(c) if the trustee drafted the clause.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16461(a)–(d) [S-662] [S-1120].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance (UTC § 1008 [VERIFY V-594]).

**Example:** The clause says "Trustee shall not be liable for any act or omission." It may excuse a negligent failure to diversify, but never profitable self-dealing.

### R-TRS-085 — Beneficiary consent, release and affirmance
**Rule (write this):** A beneficiary who consents to the trustee's conduct, releases the trustee, or affirms a transaction cannot later hold the trustee liable for it, unless the consent, release or affirmance was induced by the trustee's improper conduct, the beneficiary lacked capacity, or the beneficiary did not know the material facts or his or her rights.

**Elements:** 1. Consent, release or affirmance. 2. Capacity. 3. Full knowledge of facts and rights. 4. No improper inducement.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A release obtained by a trustee who gains an advantage is also subject to the § 16004(c) presumption (R-TRS-054).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 16463, 16464, 16465 [S-662] [VERIFY V-925].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance.

**Example:** A beneficiary signs a release after the trustee shows her a summary that omits the self-dealing sale. The release is ineffective because she did not know the material facts.

### R-TRS-086 — Limitations period
**Rule (write this):** A beneficiary's claim for breach of trust must be brought within three years after the beneficiary receives an interim or final account, or other written report, that adequately discloses the existence of the claim; if no adequate account or report was received, within three years after the beneficiary discovered, or reasonably should have discovered, the subject of the claim.

**Elements:** 1. Adequate written disclosure? 2. Date received. 3. Otherwise discovery rule. 4. Three years.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Concealment by the trustee delays discovery.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16460 [S-662] [VERIFY V-577].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs:** UTC § 1005 uses one year after an adequate report and a five-year outside limit [VERIFY V-594].

**Example:** The trustee's 2021 annual account clearly reports the sale of trust land to the trustee's wife. A beneficiary who received it had to sue by 2024.

### R-TRS-087 — Liability survives the trustee's death
**Rule (write this):** A trustee's liability for breach is personal and survives the trustee's death: the claim may be pursued against the deceased trustee's estate, and the successor trustee must take reasonable steps to compel redress of the predecessor's breach.

**Elements:** 1. Breach by the deceased trustee. 2. Claim survives. 3. Successor's duty to pursue it.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Limitations (R-TRS-086); creditor-claim procedure in the trustee's probate estate [VERIFY V-579].

**Authority:** Code Civ. Proc. § 377.20 [VERIFY V-579]; Prob. Code §§ 16403, 16420 [S-678].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A trustee who managed two family trusts used one trust's cash to cover the other's farm expenses, then died. The successor trustee should seek repayment with interest from his estate (Jul 2025 pattern [VERIFY V-071]).

## X Beneficiary liability

### R-TRS-088 — Beneficiary who consents to or joins a breach
**Rule (write this):** A beneficiary who consents to, participates in or knowingly benefits from a breach of trust cannot hold the trustee liable for it, and the trustee or the other beneficiaries may have that beneficiary's interest impounded to indemnify the trust for the loss. A beneficiary who receives an overpayment or improper distribution must restore it, or have it offset against future distributions.

**Elements:** 1. Beneficiary's consent, participation or knowing benefit. 2. Bar to suit. 3. Impounding of the interest. 4. Restitution of overpayments.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A beneficiary without knowledge of the facts or capacity is not barred.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 16463 [S-662]; Restatement (Second) of Trusts § 257 [VERIFY V-580]; common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance.

**Example:** Bea urges the trustee to buy her friend's speculative bonds, which collapse. Bea cannot sue, and the court may charge her income interest to make good the remainder beneficiaries' loss.

### R-TRS-089 — Beneficiary not liable to third parties
**Rule (write this):** A beneficiary is not personally liable to third parties on contracts or for torts arising from the trust's administration merely because he or she is a beneficiary; third parties look to the trust property, through the trustee in a representative capacity, and to the trustee personally if at fault.

**Elements:** 1. Claim arises from trust administration. 2. Defendant is only a beneficiary. 3. Recourse is the trust and the at-fault trustee.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A beneficiary who is also the trustee, or who personally commits the tort, is liable in that capacity.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 18000–18004 [S-663]; common law [VERIFY V-580].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** A visitor injured at a trust-owned apartment building sues the remainder beneficiary. The beneficiary is not liable; the claim runs against the trust through the trustee.

## XI Trust or trustee liability to third party

### R-TRS-090 — Contracts made by the trustee
**Rule (write this):** Unless the contract provides otherwise, a trustee is not personally liable on a contract properly entered into in the trustee's fiduciary capacity in the course of administration, unless the trustee fails to reveal the representative capacity and identify the trust in the contract. The creditor may collect from trust property by proceeding against the trustee in the representative capacity.

**Elements:** 1. Contract within administration. 2. Signed in fiduciary capacity. 3. Capacity revealed and trust identified? 4. Recourse to trust property.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A trustee who signs without disclosing capacity may be personally liable, with reimbursement from the trust if the contract was proper.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 18000, 18004 [S-663].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs:** at common law the trustee was personally liable on trust contracts (then indemnified) unless the contract excluded liability; California and the UTC reverse this.

**Example:** The trustee signs a roofing contract as "Tara Smith" without "as trustee of the Jones Trust." She may be personally liable, then seek reimbursement from the trust.

### R-TRS-091 — Torts and ownership liabilities; claims against the trust
**Rule (write this):** A trustee is personally liable for obligations arising from ownership or control of trust property, and for torts committed in the course of administration, only if the trustee is personally at fault. A claim may be asserted against the trust by proceeding against the trustee in the representative capacity, whether or not the trustee is personally liable, and the trustee is entitled to reimbursement from the trust for expenses properly incurred.

**Elements:** 1. Liability arising from administration or ownership. 2. Trustee personally at fault? 3. Trust liable through the trustee. 4. Reimbursement only for proper expenses.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No reimbursement for liabilities caused by the trustee's own fault.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 18001, 18002, 18004 [S-663] [VERIFY V-581]; Prob. Code § 15684 [VERIFY V-581].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs** from the old common-law rule of personal liability with indemnity.

**Example:** A tenant slips on a broken stair the trustee knew about for months. The trust is liable through the trustee, and the trustee is personally liable because she was at fault.

### R-TRS-092 — Settlor's creditors during life: revocable trusts
**Rule (write this):** If the settlor retains the power to revoke the trust in whole or in part, the trust property is subject to the claims of the settlor's creditors during the settlor's lifetime to the extent of the power of revocation; a spendthrift clause does not change this.

**Elements:** 1. Retained power to revoke. 2. Settlor alive. 3. Creditors reach property to the extent of the power.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Statutory exemptions the settlor could claim apply.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 18200 [S-664]; Prob. Code § 15304 [S-672].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same as general law (UTC § 505(a)(1) [VERIFY V-594]).

**Example:** Hank's creditor can reach his revocable trust's assets while Hank is alive (Feb 2019 pattern).

### R-TRS-093 — Settlor's creditors after death
**Rule (write this):** On the settlor's death, property that was subject to the settlor's power of revocation at death is subject to the claims of the deceased settlor's creditors and to the expenses of administration, to the extent the probate estate is inadequate to satisfy them.

**Elements:** 1. Revocable at death. 2. Probate estate insufficient. 3. Trust pays the shortfall.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Property that became irrevocable before death is outside the rule (unless a fraudulent transfer).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 19001 [S-665]; *Arluk Medical Center Industrial Group, Inc. v. Dobler* (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 1324 (trustee's only duty to creditors with pending claims is to avoid affirmative misconduct that defeats their reasonable expectation of payment) [S-1123] [S-1124].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same in substance (UTC § 505(a)(3) [VERIFY V-594]).

**Example:** Hank dies with a $20,000 probate estate, $200,000 in his revocable trust and $90,000 of debts. The creditors reach $70,000 from the trust.

## XII Third party liability to trust

### R-TRS-094 — Third party who knowingly participates in a breach
**Rule (write this):** A third person who knowingly participates in a trustee's breach of trust, such as a buyer who knows the trustee is selling trust property in breach for the trustee's benefit, or a bank that knowingly lets the trustee divert trust funds, is liable to the beneficiaries for the resulting loss and holds any trust property received on a constructive trust.

**Elements:** 1. Trustee's breach. 2. Third party's knowledge. 3. Participation or assistance. 4. Loss or property received.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Good-faith purchaser for value without actual knowledge (R-TRS-095).

**Authority:** Common law (aiding and abetting a breach of fiduciary duty); Civ. Code § 2224; Restatement (Second) of Trusts § 326 [VERIFY V-582].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; same rule.

**Example:** The trustee sells trust land to his girlfriend for half its value, and she knows the facts. She holds the land on constructive trust and is liable for the loss.

### R-TRS-095 — Transferees of trust property; protection of good-faith third parties
**Rule (write this):** A third person who acts in good faith and for value, without actual knowledge that the trustee is exceeding or improperly exercising the trustee's powers, is fully protected as if the trustee had and properly exercised the power, and need not inquire into the trustee's authority. A donee, or a transferee with actual knowledge, takes subject to the beneficiaries' claims and may be compelled to restore the property.

**Elements:** 1. Good faith. 2. Value. 3. No actual knowledge of the impropriety. 4. If protected, beneficiaries pursue the trustee and the proceeds.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Constructive knowledge or inquiry notice does not defeat protection under § 18100.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 18100 [S-666]; *Adler v. Manor Healthcare Corp.* (1992) 7 Cal.App.4th 1110 [S-667]; Prob. Code § 16420 [S-678].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE. **Differs:** the general bona fide purchaser rule denies protection to a buyer with notice (actual or constructive); California's § 18100 removes protection only for actual knowledge.

**Example:** The trustee sells trust stock at market price to a broker who does not know the trust forbids sales. The broker is protected; the beneficiaries pursue the trustee and the sale proceeds.

### R-TRS-096 — Recovering trust property: § 850 petitions and § 859 double damages
**Rule (write this):** A trustee or beneficiary may petition under Probate Code § 850 for an order that property held by another belongs to the trust and must be transferred to it. A person who in bad faith wrongfully took, concealed or disposed of trust property, or took it by undue influence or elder financial abuse, is liable for twice the value of the property recovered and may be liable for attorney's fees.

**Elements:** 1. Property claimed by the trust. 2. § 850 petition. 3. Bad-faith taking (or undue influence or elder financial abuse). 4. Double damages.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Good-faith claim of right defeats § 859.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 850, 859 [S-675] [VERIFY V-584].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California-specific.

**Example:** After Mom's death, Son moves $300,000 from the trust account into his own name, claiming it was a gift. The successor trustee petitions under § 850; if Son acted in bad faith, § 859 adds double damages.
