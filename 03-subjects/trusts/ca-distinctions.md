# Trusts — California distinctions

**Why this file matters.** Trusts is **not on the MBE**, so there is no "MBE rule" column. The comparison that matters is **California's Trust Law** (Probate Code Division 9, enacted 1986) against the **traditional common law** (what older cases and the Restatement (Second) say) and the **Uniform Trust Code** (UTC, the modern model most states now use; California has **not** adopted it). Your Fall 2026 Trusts & Estates course will likely teach the UTC and Restatement (Third); on the bar, know where California differs. UTC and Restatement section numbers in this file are given for orientation and were not re-checked against the uniform act text [VERIFY V-594].

## 1. Which law to write on a Trusts essay

**Finding (resolves the open question in V-057 as far as current evidence allows):**
- The official essay instruction says to answer "according to legal theories and principles of general application" **unless the question expressly asks for California law** [S-077].
- **Some Trusts calls do ask for California law.** The Phase 3 index records the Feb 2012 Q1 call as "Under California law: …" and the Feb 2015 Q6 call as "Under California law, what rights …" (both confirmed against State Bar text via search) [S-079].
- **Others apparently do not.** A secondary bar-prep source describes the Feb 2016 and Feb 2019 Trusts essays as applying general law and the Feb 2015 essay as applying California law [S-651]. The Feb 2016 call as indexed ("Discuss whether the trust was effectively revoked, what duties Sam breached…") contains no California-law instruction. The Jul 2025 call wording has not been seen [VERIFY V-550].
- So: **read the call**. The State Bar does not always ask for California law in Trusts, unlike Community Property and (usually) Wills.

**What to write:**
1. **Call says "California law"** → lead with the Probate Code rule and section; mention the general rule only where California departs from it and the contrast helps your analysis.
2. **Call is silent** → lead with the general principle (common law / Restatement / modern UTC view), then add "California follows this rule (Prob. Code § ___)" or "California differs: …" in one sentence. Because California codified most general trust principles, this costs little time and covers both readings.
3. **Always cite the Probate Code section** where you know it. Graders reward precise authority, and the facts are almost always set in California.
4. **Where the rules really differ** (rows marked ★ below), state both and apply each; then give your conclusion under the law the call requires.

## 3. Revocation and modification after *Haggerty*: how to write it

1. **Default** (§ 15400): revocable unless expressly irrevocable. Contrast the traditional rule in one sentence.
2. **Instrument method** followed?
3. **Exclusive?** The instrument must **explicitly** make its method exclusive. "May be amended by…" is not exclusive.
4. **Statutory method** (§ 15401(a)(2)): signed writing, not a will, delivered to the trustee during life. Settlor-trustee delivery issue (unsettled; argue both).
5. **Modification** (§ 15402; *Haggerty*): the same analysis applies to amendments.
6. **Community property** (Fam. Code § 761): one spouse may revoke; both must agree to modify.
7. **Who acted?** Settlor personally, or an agent (only if the **trust instrument** expressly permits, § 15401(c)) or a conservator (court approval) (R-TRS-016).
8. **Effect**: if valid, which version governs the poured-over property (§ 6300).

## 4. Spendthrift and creditor problems: how to write them

1. **Who is the creditor's debtor?** Beneficiary (go to 2) or settlor (go to 5).
2. **Type of interest**: mandatory (fixed), support, discretionary, or hybrid.
3. **Spendthrift clause valid?** (§§ 15300–15301). Restrains voluntary and involuntary transfer?
4. **Exceptions**: support (§ 15305); felony restitution (§ 15305.5); public support (§ 15306); due principal in full (§ 15301(b)); general creditors up to 25% of each future payment, reduced for support needs, with § 15307 excess-over-support orders inside the same cap (§§ 15306.5, 15307; *Carmack*). Contrast the general rule that spendthrift clauses bar general creditors.
5. **Settlor's creditors**: self-settled (§ 15304); revocable trust during life (§ 18200) and after death (§ 19001).

## 5. Trustee-lawyer conflicts (PR crossover)

California layers Probate Code rules on top of the Rules of Professional Conduct:
- **Gift to the drafting lawyer** (or the lawyer's relatives and firm): conclusively presumed invalid (§ 21380(c)); exceptions and independent-review certificate in §§ 21382, 21384 (R-WIL-095, R-WIL-096); CRPC rule 1.8.3 (R-PRF-027).
- **Lawyer as sole trustee of an instrument he drafted**: removal ground unless the settlor's untainted intent is shown (§ 15642(b)(6)) (R-TRS-074).
- **Dual compensation**: no trustee fees plus legal fees without waiver or court approval (§ 15687) (R-TRS-066).
- **Business dealings with the trust or beneficiaries**: self-dealing (§ 16004) and CRPC rule 1.8.1 (R-PRF-026).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC law has no statutory presumption that a gift to the drafting lawyer is invalid; California's § 21380 is a protective device with no close PRC counterpart (approx.).
