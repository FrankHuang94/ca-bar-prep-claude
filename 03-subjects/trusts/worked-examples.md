# Trusts — worked examples

Original fact patterns. Read the facts, decide the answer, then check the analysis. Each analysis follows **Rule → Application → Conclusion** and names the rule ID in [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md).

---

### 1. The hopeful widower (intent)
**Facts.** Hal's will leaves "$200,000 to my sister Sue, in the hope that she will look after my son's education." Sue spends the money on a house.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* a trust requires a present intent to impose enforceable duties; precatory words ("hope," "wish") ordinarily create no trust (R-TRS-004, R-TRS-039).
- *Application:* "in the hope that" expresses a wish, not a command. On the other side, the son is identified and the gift's stated reason is his education, which suggests Hal expected the money to be used for him. But Hal used no mandatory words and named no amount for the son.
- *Conclusion:* probably an outright gift to Sue, who may keep the house.

### 2. The schedule but no deed (res and funding)
**Facts.** Rosa signs a living trust naming herself trustee. Schedule A lists "my condominium at 4 Bay St." She never records a deed. At her death her will leaves everything to her nephew; the trust leaves the condo to her niece.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* a trust needs identifiable property transferred or declared in trust. A settlor-trustee's signed declaration that listed property is held in trust can make it trust property without a deed (R-TRS-005; *Heggstad*). A trust of land must be evidenced by a writing signed by the settlor or trustee (R-TRS-030).
- *Application:* Rosa, as both settlor and trustee, signed an instrument that declares the condo trust property. The signed trust satisfies § 15206.
- *Conclusion:* the condo is trust property and passes to the niece; the nephew takes only probate assets. The successor trustee can confirm title by a § 850 petition (R-TRS-096).

### 3. "My good friends" (beneficiary)
**Facts.** Ted's will gives $100,000 "to Tina, in trust, to distribute among my good friends as she sees fit."

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* a private trust needs ascertainable beneficiaries or a power to select from a sufficiently described class (R-TRS-018). A failed express trust produces a resulting trust (R-TRS-036).
- *Application:* "good friends" has no objective test, but Tina is given a power to choose. Under the traditional view the class is too indefinite and the trust fails. California permits a power of selection if the class is described with reasonable certainty, and "friends" is borderline [VERIFY V-589]. The gift is not charitable (no public benefit), and not honorary or a pet trust.
- *Conclusion:* likely fails. Tina holds the $100,000 on a resulting trust for Ted's estate, so it falls into the residue (R-TRS-040).

### 4. Revoked by a later will? (Feb 2016 pattern)
**Facts.** Wanda creates a revocable trust of her ranch with herself and her daughter Dina as co-trustees; it is silent on how to revoke. Later her will says: "I revoke my trust; my ranch goes to my son." Wanda dies.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* in California a trust is revocable unless expressly irrevocable (§ 15400). If the instrument states no method, the settlor revokes by a signed writing, **other than a will**, delivered to the trustee during her life (§ 15401(a)(2)). Under the UTC-type view, a later will that expressly refers to the trust can revoke it (R-TRS-011 to R-TRS-013).
- *Application:* the trust is revocable. But the will is expressly excluded by § 15401(a)(2). It was also not "delivered" to Dina during Wanda's life; it speaks only at death. On the other side, the will is a signed writing that clearly expresses the intent to revoke, and under the UTC rule it would succeed because it expressly refers to the trust.
- *Conclusion:* under California law the trust probably was **not** revoked, so the ranch passes under the trust. Under the UTC-type rule the son would take.

### 5. The notarized-amendment clause (Haggerty)
**Facts.** Paula's revocable trust says: "This trust may be amended by a writing signed by the settlor and acknowledged before a notary." Paula, the sole trustee, signs an unnotarized amendment adding her caregiver's daughter, Ivy, as a beneficiary, and keeps it in her desk.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* a revocable trust may be modified by the procedure for revocation unless the instrument provides otherwise. Under *Haggerty*, the statutory method (a signed writing delivered to the trustee) is available unless the trust's amendment method is explicitly exclusive (R-TRS-014).
- *Application:* the clause says "may"; it does not say the method is exclusive. Paula signed. Because she is the sole trustee, keeping the writing herself likely counts as delivery (R-TRS-012).
- *Conclusion:* the amendment is probably valid as an amendment. Ivy is the daughter of a care custodian, so test § 21380 if Paula was a dependent adult (R-TRS-076).

### 6. One spouse revokes (CP crossover)
**Facts.** Ken and Lia put their community-property home in a joint revocable trust that is silent on revocation. Lia, separated from Ken, signs and delivers to both trustees a revocation of the trust "as to the house."

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* unless the trust provides otherwise, either spouse alone may revoke as to community property, but modification as to CP requires both spouses (Fam. Code § 761; R-TRS-015).
- *Application:* Lia revoked rather than modified; she signed and delivered (§ 15401(a)(2)).
- *Conclusion:* the revocation is effective. The house returns to Ken and Lia as community property and will be divided under the Family Code.

### 7. Beneficiaries want out (Feb 2012 pattern)
**Facts.** Sam's trust, now irrevocable on his death, pays income to his daughters Beth (28) and Cara (31) until each turns 40, then principal. It has a spendthrift clause. Both daughters want to end it now to buy a business together.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* all beneficiaries may petition to terminate, but if continuance is needed to carry out a material purpose, the court may terminate only if the reason outweighs that purpose (§ 15403). Under *Claflin*, a material purpose is an absolute bar (R-TRS-025).
- *Application:* both beneficiaries consent, and no one else holds an interest (check for any gift over to their children). The age-40 delay and the spendthrift clause show Sam wanted to protect his daughters from rash spending: material purposes. Buying a business is the kind of risk Sam guarded against.
- *Conclusion:* likely denied under both *Claflin* and § 15403.

### 8. The money-losing farm (changed circumstances)
**Facts.** Gus's testamentary trust directs the trustee to "never sell the family farm" and to pay its income to his widow. A decade-long drought makes the farm lose money; the widow has no income.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* the court may modify or terminate if circumstances not known to or anticipated by the settlor mean continuing under the trust's terms would defeat or substantially impair its purposes (§ 15409; R-TRS-026).
- *Application:* the trust's main purpose is to support the widow, and the no-sale clause now defeats it. Gus may not have foreseen a decade-long drought. On the other side, keeping the farm in the family may itself be a purpose.
- *Conclusion:* the court will likely authorize a sale, perhaps with a right of first refusal for family members.

### 9. The tiny trust (uneconomic)
**Facts.** A trust for Nora now holds $75,000 and pays $6,000 a year in fees. The instrument is silent on early termination.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* unless the instrument provides otherwise, the trustee may terminate a trust whose principal does not exceed $100,000; the court may terminate any trust whose value is too low relative to costs (§ 15408; R-TRS-027).
- *Application:* $75,000 is under $100,000, and fees consume 8% a year.
- *Conclusion:* the trustee may terminate without a court order and distribute to Nora consistently with the trust's purposes.

### 10. The cure is found (Feb 2025 pattern; cy pres)
**Facts.** Tess's will leaves her residue in trust "to fund research for a cure for Q-disease, a rare eye disorder." A cure is found. The trustee petitions to use the funds for scholarships for ophthalmology students.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* if a charitable purpose becomes impossible or impracticable and the settlor had a general charitable intent, the court applies the property to the purpose nearest the original (R-TRS-021). Without general intent, a resulting trust arises (R-TRS-036).
- *Application:* research on Q-disease is no longer needed. Evidence of general intent: the gift went to medical research generally, eye disease is the theme, and there is no gift over. Is the scholarship the nearest purpose? Research on other rare eye diseases is closer to "research for a cure" than scholarships are.
- *Conclusion:* cy pres applies, but the court may choose eye-disease research rather than the trustee's scholarship proposal.

### 11. The museum café (charitable enforcement)
**Facts.** A charitable trust supports a public art museum. Two of three trustees vote to lease the museum's garden to their friend's for-profit restaurant at a below-market rent. A museum member sues.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* the Attorney General enforces charitable trusts; co-trustees and persons with a special interest may sue; members of the public may not (R-TRS-020).
- *Application:* a museum member is a member of the public. The third trustee, or the AG, can sue. The below-market lease to a friend raises loyalty and prudence issues (R-TRS-052, R-TRS-057).
- *Conclusion:* the member likely lacks standing; the dissenting trustee and the AG have it.

### 12. Spendthrift and the credit-card judgment
**Facts.** Ana is entitled to $6,000 a month from a spendthrift trust; she needs $4,000 a month to live. Bank holds a $50,000 judgment against her. Her ex-husband holds a child-support judgment.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* a valid spendthrift clause blocks creditors before payment (R-TRS-041), but California allows support creditors (R-TRS-043) and, for general creditors, up to 25% of payments considering support needs, or amounts over what is needed for support (R-TRS-046). General law bars general creditors entirely.
- *Application:* the ex-husband can obtain an order for the support payments. Bank may obtain up to $1,500 a month (25%), and arguably the $2,000 a month above Ana's needs under § 15307. The support order and Ana's needs reduce what Bank gets.
- *Conclusion:* the ex-husband takes first; Bank gets a partial order. Under general law Bank would get nothing.

### 13. The self-settled shelter
**Facts.** Dr. Vu puts $3 million into an irrevocable trust for herself and her children, with distributions to her "in the trustee's sole discretion" and a spendthrift clause. A patient wins a $1 million malpractice judgment.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* a spendthrift restraint is invalid as to a settlor-beneficiary; creditors reach the maximum amount the trustee could pay the settlor (R-TRS-047).
- *Application:* the trustee could pay Dr. Vu the whole $3 million in its discretion.
- *Conclusion:* the patient may reach the trust to satisfy the $1 million judgment.

### 14. Creditors after death (Feb 2019 pattern)
**Facts.** Hank dies with $15,000 in his probate estate, $400,000 in his revocable trust and $60,000 in debts.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* property subject to the settlor's power of revocation at death is subject to the deceased settlor's creditors to the extent the probate estate is inadequate (§ 19001; R-TRS-093).
- *Application:* the estate is short by $45,000 plus administration costs.
- *Conclusion:* creditors may reach about $45,000 (plus expenses) from the trust.

### 15. Buying the house at market value (self-dealing)
**Facts.** Sid, trustee, buys the trust's house for himself at its appraised value of $700,000. Two years later he sells it for $900,000.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* a trustee may not deal with trust property for his own profit; self-dealing is a breach regardless of fairness unless authorized, consented to or court-approved (R-TRS-053). Beneficiaries may rescind or affirm and take the profit (R-TRS-079).
- *Application:* nothing authorized the purchase. Fair price is irrelevant under the no-further-inquiry rule. Sid has resold, so rescission against him is no longer possible (unless the buyer had actual knowledge, R-TRS-095).
- *Conclusion:* Sid is liable for the $200,000 profit plus interest, and the court may remove him and reduce his compensation (R-TRS-078).

### 16. Trust money in the trustee's start-up
**Facts.** Trustee Mo invests 30% of a trust that supports an elderly widow in his own new software company. It fails.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* loyalty forbids transactions in which the trustee has a conflicting interest (R-TRS-055). The prudent investor rule requires suitable risk and diversification (R-TRS-057, R-TRS-058).
- *Application:* conflict: Mo profits as owner. Imprudence: a start-up is speculative, 30% is a heavy concentration, and the widow needs income.
- *Conclusion:* two breaches. Mo is liable for the loss with interest (R-TRS-081); removal likely.

### 17. Growth stocks for the children (impartiality)
**Facts.** The trust pays income to widow Wen for life, remainder to the children. At the children's urging, the trustee moves everything into non-dividend growth stocks.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* the trustee must act impartially, considering the differing interests of income and remainder beneficiaries (R-TRS-056).
- *Application:* Wen receives almost no income while the children gain. Unless the trustee uses a power to adjust between income and principal, the allocation favors the remainder.
- *Conclusion:* breach of impartiality; Wen can seek surcharge or an order rebalancing the portfolio.

### 18. Sole and absolute discretion
**Facts.** Trustee Ray has "sole and absolute discretion" to distribute to himself and his brother Leo. He pays himself $250,000 for a boat and refuses Leo's request for medical bills.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* even "sole and absolute" discretion must be exercised in good faith and consistent with the trust's purposes. A trustee-beneficiary may pay himself only for health, education, support or maintenance unless the instrument clearly says otherwise (R-TRS-069).
- *Application:* a boat is not HEMS. Refusing Leo's medical needs while enriching himself shows bad faith.
- *Conclusion:* abuse of discretion. Ray must repay the $250,000, Leo can compel a proper exercise, and Ray may be removed.

### 19. The 120-day letter
**Facts.** Mom's trust became irrevocable on her death. The trustee served the § 16061.7 notification on Son on January 5. Son requested the trust terms on April 20 and received them on April 25. He files a contest on June 20.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* the contest must be filed within 120 days of service of the notice, or 60 days after delivery of terms requested within that 120-day period, whichever is later (R-TRS-064).
- *Application:* 120 days from January 5 is May 5. The request (April 20) was within the 120 days. 60 days from April 25 is June 24, which is later.
- *Conclusion:* the June 20 filing is timely.

### 20. The passive co-trustee
**Facts.** Dot and Sam are co-trustees. For three years Dot signs every document Sam gives her without reading it; Sam drains $150,000 into his own accounts.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* a co-trustee is liable for a co-trustee's breach if she improperly delegated, negligently enabled it, or failed to take reasonable steps to prevent it or compel redress (R-TRS-061, R-TRS-083).
- *Application:* Dot abdicated her duty to participate and monitor, and her signatures enabled the diversion.
- *Conclusion:* Dot and Sam are jointly liable for the $150,000 plus interest; Dot may seek contribution from Sam.

### 21. The exculpatory clause
**Facts.** The trust says: "No trustee shall be liable for any loss except for fraud." The trustee negligently left cash uninvested for two years, and also borrowed $50,000 from the trust at no interest.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* exculpatory clauses are enforceable except for intentional, grossly negligent, bad-faith or reckless breaches and for profit the trustee derives from the breach (R-TRS-084).
- *Application:* idle cash is probably ordinary negligence; the clause likely covers it unless two years is gross negligence. The interest-free loan is self-dealing that benefited the trustee.
- *Conclusion:* the trustee is likely excused for the idle cash but liable for the loan benefit and interest.

### 22. The trustee's unsigned contract
**Facts.** Trustee Tara hires a roofer for a trust building, signing "Tara Lee" without mentioning the trust. The trust cannot pay.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* a trustee is not personally liable on a contract properly made in the fiduciary capacity unless she fails to reveal her capacity and identify the trust (R-TRS-090).
- *Application:* Tara did neither.
- *Conclusion:* Tara is personally liable to the roofer. She may seek reimbursement from the trust because the contract was proper (R-TRS-091).

### 23. The girlfriend's bargain (third party)
**Facts.** Trustee Vic sells trust land worth $500,000 to his girlfriend for $250,000. She knows it is trust land and that the price is half its value. She later gives it to her brother.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* a third party who knowingly participates in a breach is liable and holds the property on constructive trust; only a good-faith purchaser for value without actual knowledge is protected (R-TRS-094, R-TRS-095).
- *Application:* the girlfriend had actual knowledge. The brother is a donee and gave no value.
- *Conclusion:* the beneficiaries recover the land from the brother by constructive trust, and the girlfriend and Vic are liable for any loss. If the taking was in bad faith, § 859 double damages may apply against her (R-TRS-096).

### 24. The pour-over signed later (Wills crossover)
**Facts.** On March 1 Ann signs a will pouring her residue into "the Ann Trust." She signs the trust instrument on April 15. In 2026 she amends the trust to add a gift to her church.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* § 6300 validates a pour-over to a trust whose instrument is signed before, with, or within 60 days after the will; amendments made before or after death apply (R-TRS-010; R-WIL-061).
- *Application:* April 15 is 45 days after March 1, inside the window. The 2026 amendment governs.
- *Conclusion:* the residue pours over and is distributed under the amended trust, including the church gift.

### 25. The secret promise
**Facts.** Ed's will leaves $80,000 "to my friend Fred." Before signing, Ed told Fred, who agreed, that the money was for Ed's nonmarital daughter. Fred now keeps it.

**Analysis.**
- *Rule:* where a devisee promises the testator to hold an apparently absolute gift for another, the court imposes a constructive trust for the intended beneficiary (R-TRS-032).
- *Application:* Fred's promise induced the gift; letting him keep it is unjust enrichment. Proof will come from witnesses and Fred's admissions.
- *Conclusion:* Fred holds the $80,000 on constructive trust for the daughter.
