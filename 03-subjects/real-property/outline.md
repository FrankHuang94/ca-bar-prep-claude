## I Ownership of real property (State Bar outline I: nature and ownership of land)

**Weight:** about one-fifth of the Real Property MBE (about 5 of 25 scored questions) [S-040]. The essay favorites in this category are concurrent ownership and landlord-tenant.

### I.A Present estates and future interests

The system is a timeline. A **present estate** gives possession now; a **future interest** gives a right to possession later. Classify every interest by asking, in order: (1) what present estate was created (look at the words of duration or condition); (2) who holds each future interest (the grantor or a transferee); (3) what kind of future interest it is; (4) whether any rule (RAP, destructibility, Shelley, worthier title, restraints) changes the result.

| Present estate | Creating words | Future interest in grantor | Future interest in transferee | RAP applies to the future interest? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fee simple absolute | "to A" / "to A and her heirs" | none | none | — |
| Fee simple determinable | "so long as," "while," "until" | possibility of reverter | executory interest (estate then is FS subject to executory limitation) | reverter: no; executory interest: yes |
| Fee simple subject to condition subsequent | "but if," "provided that" + right to re-enter | right of entry (power of termination) | — (a transferee's interest would be an executory interest) | right of entry: no |
| Fee simple subject to executory limitation | "to A, but if X, to B" | — | shifting executory interest | yes |
| Life estate | "to A for life" | reversion | remainder (vested or contingent) | contingent remainder and vested subject to open: yes |
| Term of years / periodic tenancy | leasehold | reversion | remainder | as above |

#### I.A.1 Present estates

##### I.A.1.a Fees simple
- **Fee simple absolute:** largest estate; infinite duration; freely alienable, devisable, descendible; heirs of a living person have only an expectancy. Words of inheritance are no longer needed; a grant presumptively passes a fee simple (Civ. Code §§ 1072, 1105). R-PRP-001.
- **Fee tail** ("to A and the heirs of his body"): abolished in nearly all states; in California a would-be fee tail is a fee simple (Civ. Code § 763) [S-774].
- **Precatory language** ("in the hope that she keeps it in the family") does not limit the fee.
- **How tested:** MBE uses "to A and her heirs" to test whether A's heirs take anything (they do not). Essays use the fee simple as the baseline against which a defeasible fee is compared.

##### I.A.1.b Defeasible fees
- **Fee simple determinable (FSD):** ends **automatically** on the stated event; grantor holds a **possibility of reverter** (implied). R-PRP-002.
- **Fee simple subject to condition subsequent (FSSCS):** continues until the grantor **elects** to terminate by exercising a **right of entry** (power of termination); delay can waive. R-PRP-003.
- **Fee simple subject to executory limitation:** ends automatically and passes to a **third party** (shifting executory interest); RAP applies. R-PRP-004.
- **Construction preferences** (R-PRP-005): (1) words of mere purpose or motive ("for use as a park") create a fee simple absolute; (2) ambiguity → FSSCS over FSD (avoid automatic forfeiture); (3) a restriction may be read as a covenant instead.
- **RAP fallback:** if a third party's executory interest is void, strike it: durational words leave an FSD with a possibility of reverter in the grantor; conditional words leave a fee simple absolute.
- **CA note:** California abolished the FSD and the possibility of reverter; a would-be FSD is a fee simple subject to a restriction in the form of a condition subsequent, enforceable by a power of termination; a recorded power of termination expires 30 years after recording unless a notice of intent to preserve is recorded, and an obsolete power is unenforceable (Civ. Code §§ 885.010–885.070) [S-758] [S-759].
- **How tested:** Jul 2015 Q2 (defeasible fee and possibility of reverter); Jul 2024 Q2 (residential-use condition with a gift over to a third party's descendants) [VERIFY V-071].

##### I.A.1.c Life estates
- Measured by the grantee's life or another's (**pur autre vie**); alienable only for the measuring life; a lease by a life tenant ends at the life tenant's death. R-PRP-006.
- **Waste** (R-PRP-007): affirmative (voluntary harm; exploiting resources, subject to the open-mines doctrine); permissive (failure to repair, pay taxes, pay mortgage **interest**, limited to income or rental value); ameliorative (value-increasing change; allowed today only if remaindermen would not reasonably object or conditions have changed: *Melms v. Pabst Brewing Co.*, 104 Wis. 7, 79 N.W. 738 (1899)). Remedies: damages, injunction; treble damages by statute in some states, including California's waste statute (CCP § 732) [VERIFY V-663].
- **Sale of the fee:** a court may order sale of the whole only if necessary to protect all interests (*Baker v. Weedon*, 262 So. 2d 641 (Miss. 1972)).
- **Defeasible life estates** ("to W for life or until remarriage") are valid.
- **How tested:** Feb 2026 Q1 (life estates appear in some summaries; unconfirmed) [VERIFY V-071]; MBE asks who must pay taxes, interest and repairs.

#### I.A.2 Future interests

##### I.A.2.a Reversions
- Left in a grantor who conveys a lesser estate (life estate, term) without naming who takes next, or where a named remainder is contingent. Always vested; transferable; not subject to RAP. R-PRP-008.

##### I.A.2.b Remainders
- A future interest in a **transferee** that can take on the **natural end** of a prior life estate or term. Never follows a fee.
- **Vested** (ascertained taker, no condition precedent): indefeasibly vested; **vested subject to open** (class can grow); **vested subject to complete divestment** (condition subsequent). R-PRP-009.
- **Contingent** (unborn or unascertained taker, or condition precedent): the grantor keeps a reversion; alternative contingent remainders are common ("to B if …, and if not, to C"). R-PRP-010.
- **Comma test:** condition in the same clause as the gift ("to B if B survives A") = condition precedent → contingent; condition after a gift that is complete on its face ("to B, but if B dies before A, to C") = condition subsequent → vested subject to divestment.
- **Statutory definitions:** Civ. Code §§ 767–769 (future interests vested or contingent).

##### I.A.2.c Executory interests
- A transferee's future interest that **divests** a prior transferee (**shifting**) or the grantor (**springing**), or that follows a gap. Subject to the RAP. R-PRP-011.
- Preference: if an interest can be a remainder, it is one.
- **How tested:** Jul 2024 Q2 (shifting executory interest after a residential-use condition) [VERIFY V-071].

##### I.A.2.d Possibilities of reverter and powers of termination
- **Possibility of reverter** follows an FSD; automatic; not subject to RAP; devisable and descendible, and in most states transferable.
- **Right of entry / power of termination** follows an FSSCS; requires election; not subject to RAP; at common law not transferable inter vivos (some states held an attempted transfer destroyed it).
- Both are generally barred from forfeiture when the grantor waives or is estopped; statutes of limitations run from the triggering event (FSD) or from the election (FSSCS, with reasonable-time limits).
- **CA note:** only the power of termination exists; 30-year recording limit; obsolescence (Civ. Code §§ 885.020–885.040) [S-758].

##### I.A.2.e Rules affecting these interests
| Rule | Pattern | Common-law result | Modern / California |
|---|---|---|---|
| Destructibility of contingent remainders | Legal contingent remainder not vested when the prior estate ends | Destroyed; grantor's reversion takes | Abolished in most states; California: abolished (Civ. Code § 741) [VERIFY V-652] |
| Rule in Shelley's Case | "to A for life, then to A's heirs" (land) | A takes fee simple (merger) | Abolished in most states; California: heirs take as remaindermen (Civ. Code § 779) [S-774] |
| Doctrine of Worthier Title | "to A for life, then to O's heirs" (inter vivos) | O keeps a reversion | Rule of construction at most; California: not part of state law (Prob. Code § 21108) [S-774] |
| Merger | Life estate and next vested estate in one person | Merge into a larger estate (could destroy intervening contingent remainder) | Still applies, but no destruction where destructibility abolished |
| Class gifts / rule of convenience | "to B's children" | Class closes when any member can demand possession | Same (R-PRP-013; wills: R-WIL-066) |
| Rule against perpetuities | See I.D.1 | Void if might vest too late | USRAP wait-and-see 90 years (California) |
| Restraints on alienation | See I.D.2 | Disabling restraints on fee void | Same; reasonable restraints valid |

R-PRP-012, R-PRP-013.

### I.B Cotenancy

| Feature | Tenancy in common | Joint tenancy | Tenancy by the entirety | California community property (cross-ref) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Creation | Default presumption (Civ. Code § 686) | Four unities + express language (Civ. Code § 683) | Spouses + four unities (where recognized) | Spouses; acquisition during marriage (R-CMP-011) |
| Survivorship | No | Yes | Yes | No (unless CP with right of survivorship, Civ. Code § 682.1) |
| Unilateral transfer | Yes, of share | Yes; severs as to that share | No | Joinder required for real property (Fam. Code § 1102) |
| Creditors of one owner | Reach that share | Reach share while debtor lives; lien ends at debtor's death (lien theory) | Cannot reach (most entirety states) | Community estate liable for either spouse's debts (R-CMP-084) |
| Exists in California? | Yes | Yes | **No** | Yes |

#### I.B.1 Types: tenancy in common and joint tenancy
- **Tenancy in common:** separate undivided shares; unity of possession only; presumed (R-PRP-019).
- **Joint tenancy:** four unities (time, title, interest, possession) plus clear survivorship language; most states (and California) allow a self-conveyance to oneself and another to create it (R-PRP-020).
- **Severance by conveyance:** a joint tenant's inter vivos conveyance to a third party severs as to that share; the transferee is a tenant in common; remaining joint tenants stay joint among themselves (R-PRP-021).
- **Severance by self-conveyance (CA):** *Riddle v. Harmon* (1980) 102 Cal.App.3d 524; recording requirement: severance effective against survivorship only if recorded before death, or executed and acknowledged within three days before death and recorded within seven days after (Civ. Code § 683.2(c)) [S-760]. A will never severs (*Estate of England* (1991) 233 Cal.App.3d 1) [S-761].
- **Mortgage, lease, contract, killing** (R-PRP-022): title-theory mortgage severs; lien-theory mortgage does not, and in California the survivor takes free of a co-tenant's sole mortgage or deed of trust (*People v. Nogarr* (1958) 164 Cal.App.2d 591; *Hamel v. Gootkin* (1962) 202 Cal.App.2d 27) [S-765]; a lease by one joint tenant does not sever and ends at the lessor's death (*Tenhet v. Boswell* (1976) 18 Cal.3d 150) [S-765]; an enforceable contract to sell severs in most states; a killer forfeits survivorship (Prob. Code § 251) [VERIFY V-664].
- **Tenancy by the entirety:** not recognized in California (R-PRP-023). For spouses in California, analyze title under Community Property (R-CMP-012 to R-CMP-014, R-CMP-098) and simultaneous death of joint tenants under Wills (R-WIL-009).
- **How tested:** Feb 2015 Q2, Jul 2015 Q2, Oct 2020 Q3 [VERIFY V-071]. Typical call: "What interests do A, B, C and D hold?" Draw the timeline and re-state the tenancy after every transfer.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: 按份共有 ≈ tenancy in common; 共同共有 is joint ownership without a US-style automatic survivorship right (approx.).

#### I.B.2 Rights and obligations of cotenants
- **Possession:** each may possess the whole; no rent owed to the others absent **ouster** or agreement (majority) (R-PRP-024); *Spiller v. Mackereth*, 334 So. 2d 859 (Ala. 1976).
- **Third-party rents and resource profits:** must be shared (net of expenses).
- **Carrying costs:** contribution for taxes and mortgage payments, offset by the value of exclusive occupancy (R-PRP-025).
- **Repairs and improvements:** no forced contribution; credit in partition or accounting (improver gets value added, bears loss); CA partition court may adjust equities (CCP § 872.140) [VERIFY V-672].
- **Waste:** a cotenant who commits waste is liable to the others.
- **Adverse possession between cotenants:** requires ouster plus the statutory period.
- **Fiduciary duty:** generally none, except a cotenant who buys an outstanding title or tax title is often treated as holding it for all.
- **Partition** (R-PRP-026): voluntary or judicial; preference for partition in kind; sale when division is impracticable or unfair. **CA note:** Partition of Real Property Act for tenancies in common (CCP § 874.311 et seq., actions filed on or after Jan 1, 2023): buyout right, preference for partition in kind, open-market sale [S-772].
- **How tested:** Jul 2015 Q2 (accounting and contribution), Oct 2020 Q3 (claims for taxes and rental fees) [VERIFY V-071].

### I.C Landlord-tenant law (State Bar outline II: landlord and tenant)

Essay favorite: four of the 14 Real Property essays turned on landlord-tenant issues (Feb 2012 Q6, Feb 2015 Q2 (lease covenant), Feb 2019 Q3, Feb 2023 Q3) [VERIFY V-071]. Typical calls: "What arguments can L and T each make?" and "What claims does T have and what counterclaims may L assert?"

#### I.C.1 Types of tenancies
| Tenancy | Duration | How it ends | Notice | Statute of frauds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Term of years | Fixed period | Automatically at end | None | Writing if more than one year (Civ. Code §§ 1091, 1624(a)(3)) |
| Periodic | Successive periods | Notice by either party | Common law: one period (six months for year-to-year), ending at period end; CA: Civ. Code §§ 1946, 1946.1 | Usually oral is fine |
| At will | No fixed term | Notice by either, or death | Common law: none or reasonable; CA: at least 30 days (Civ. Code § 789) | — |
| At sufferance | Holdover | Landlord's election | — | — |

- Implied periodic tenancies arise from possession plus periodic rent (including under an oral lease void under the statute of frauds); California presumes the period by the rent interval, month to month by default (Civ. Code §§ 1943, 1944). R-PRP-027, R-PRP-028.
- **Holdover** (R-PRP-029): landlord elects eviction (with damages) or a new periodic tenancy (majority: year to year if original term was a year or more); CA: acceptance of rent presumes renewal for the rent period, max one month if rent monthly, max one year (Civ. Code § 1945).
- **CA note (residential):** Tenant Protection Act just-cause requirement after 12 months and rent cap (Civ. Code §§ 1946.2, 1947.12; sunset Jan 1, 2030) [S-752] [S-755]; R-PRP-046.

#### I.C.2 Possession and rent
- **Delivery of possession** (R-PRP-030): **English rule** (majority): landlord must deliver actual possession; tenant may terminate or affirm, with rent abatement and damages. **American rule** (minority): only legal right; tenant sues the holdover (*Hannan v. Dusch*, 154 Va. 356, 153 S.E. 824 (1930)). **CA note:** no clear California holding; Civ. Code § 1927's duty to secure "quiet possession" supports the English rule [S-778] [VERIFY V-651]. Tested Feb 2019 Q3, Feb 2023 Q3 [VERIFY V-071].
- **Quiet enjoyment** (R-PRP-034): implied in every lease (Civ. Code § 1927); actual eviction (total: no rent; partial by landlord: all rent suspended at common law; by paramount title: pro rata).
- **Constructive eviction** (R-PRP-035): substantial interference from landlord's act or breach of duty → notice → failure to cure → vacate within a reasonable time (*Reste Realty Corp. v. Cooper*, 53 N.J. 444, 251 A.2d 268 (1969)); CA statutory termination right (Civ. Code § 1932).
- **Rent** (R-PRP-031): independent covenant at common law; modern residential law makes it dependent on habitability. Rent acceleration valid if not a penalty.
- **Security deposits:** return with itemization; **CA note:** 21-day return; one-month cap (two months for qualifying small landlords; one month for service members) for residential tenancies after July 1, 2024 (Civ. Code § 1950.5, amended by AB 12) [S-750] [S-751].
- **Tenant's duties** (R-PRP-032): no waste; ordinary repairs; no illegal use; CA residential tenant duties (Civ. Code § 1941.2).
- **Landlord's entry:** reasonable notice (CA: generally 24 hours' written notice for nonemergency entry, Civ. Code § 1954) [VERIFY V-662].
- **Landlord's remedies for nonpayment** (R-PRP-044): terminate if lease or statute allows, then unlawful detainer (CCP § 1161: three-day notice to pay or quit); self-help is barred in California (Civ. Code § 789.3) [VERIFY V-662].
- **Landlord tort liability** (R-PRP-045): common-law no-duty rule with exceptions (latent defects known, common areas, negligent repairs, public use, short-term furnished, covenant to repair); modern/CA general negligence (*Rowland v. Christian* (1968) 69 Cal.2d 108; *Uccello v. Laudenslayer* (1975) 44 Cal.App.3d 504; *Peterson v. Superior Court* (1995) 10 Cal.4th 1185; *Ann M. v. Pacific Plaza Shopping Center* (1993) 6 Cal.4th 666). Cross-reference Torts.

#### I.C.3 Transfers by landlord or tenant
- **Assignment vs. sublease** (R-PRP-039): whole remaining term = assignment; less = sublease (majority objective test; minority intent: *Ernst v. Conditt*, 54 Tenn. App. 328, 390 S.W.2d 703 (1964)).

| Relationship | Privity of contract | Privity of estate | Who can L sue for rent? |
|---|---|---|---|
| L – original T (after assignment) | Yes | No | T (as surety) |
| L – assignee who did not assume | No | Yes, while it holds the leasehold (until reassignment) | Assignee, for rent accruing during its tenure |
| L – assignee who assumed | Yes | Yes (while it holds the leasehold) | Assignee, for the whole term, even after reassignment |
| L – sublessee | No | No | Not directly (only via the master tenant), unless sublessee assumed |

- **Liability after transfer** (R-PRP-040); **covenants running with the leasehold** (R-PRP-041): intent + touch and concern + privity of estate (*Spencer's Case* (1583) 5 Co. Rep. 16a); covenants to pay rent, repair, insure (when proceeds benefit the premises), options to renew or purchase usually run.
- **Restrictions on transfer** (R-PRP-042): strictly construed; *Dumpor's Case* (1603) 4 Co. Rep. 119b (waiver after one consent; rejected in many states); traditional rule allows arbitrary refusal; modern trend requires reasonableness. **CA note:** *Kendall v. Ernest Pestana, Inc.* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 488; Civ. Code §§ 1995.010–1995.340 (absolute prohibition must be express; no stated standard → not unreasonably withheld, § 1995.260) [S-762].
- **Transfers by landlord** (R-PRP-043): grantee takes subject to the lease; covenants that touch and concern bind and benefit the grantee; tenant attorns.
- **How tested:** Feb 2012 Q6 (transfers), Feb 2015 Q2 (covenant to insure) [VERIFY V-071].

#### I.C.4 Termination
- **Expiration; notice** (periodic); **holdover** (above).
- **Abandonment and mitigation** (R-PRP-033): landlord's options (accept surrender; relet for tenant's account; traditional: let it sit). Modern majority: duty to mitigate (*Sommer v. Kridel*, 74 N.J. 446, 378 A.2d 767 (1977)). **CA note:** Civ. Code § 1951.2 (damages = unpaid rent plus excess of remaining rent over what the tenant proves could reasonably have been avoided, discounted); § 1951.4 lock-in remedy if the lease so provides and permits reasonable transfer; § 1951.3 abandonment notice procedure [VERIFY V-662].
- **Surrender** (R-PRP-047): express (writing if remaining term over a year) or by operation of law (landlord's inconsistent acceptance).
- **Destruction, condemnation, illegality, frustration, merger** (R-PRP-047): Civ. Code §§ 1932, 1933.
- **Anticipatory breach:** tenant's repudiation lets the landlord sue for damages for the whole term (net of mitigation) in many states.
- **Retaliatory eviction** (R-PRP-038): Civ. Code § 1942.5 (180-day presumption).
- **How tested:** Feb 2019 Q3 (past-due and future rent), Feb 2023 Q3 (landlord's counterclaims: rent, abandonment, mitigation) [VERIFY V-071].

#### I.C.5 Habitability and suitability
- **Implied warranty of habitability** (residential) (R-PRP-036): *Javins v. First National Realty Corp.*, 428 F.2d 1071 (D.C. Cir. 1970); *Green v. Superior Court* (1974) 10 Cal.3d 616; Civ. Code §§ 1941, 1941.1 (untenantable conditions), 1942.1 (waiver void).
- **Remedies** (R-PRP-037): terminate; repair and deduct (CA: up to one month's rent, twice in 12 months, Civ. Code § 1942); withhold or reduce rent; damages (reduced rental value); defense to eviction (CCP § 1174.2) [VERIFY V-662]; rent not collectible after a public-officer citation left unrepaired 35 days (Civ. Code § 1942.4) [VERIFY V-662].
- **Commercial leases:** no implied warranty of habitability (majority); tenant relies on constructive eviction, express covenants, and (some states) an implied covenant of suitability where the landlord knew of a latent defect making the premises unsuitable for the known purpose.
- **Illegality:** a lease for an illegal use is unenforceable; if the planned use becomes illegal, the tenant may terminate (frustration).
- **How tested:** Feb 2023 Q3 (actual eviction and possession issues framed as tenant's claims) [VERIFY V-071].

### I.D Special problems

#### I.D.1 Rule against perpetuities: common law rule and statutory reforms
- **Statement:** "No interest is good unless it must vest, if at all, not later than twenty-one years after some life in being at the creation of the interest." R-PRP-014.
- **Applies to:** contingent remainders; executory interests; vested remainders subject to open (class gifts); options in gross; rights of first refusal (majority); powers of appointment (special rules).
- **Does not apply to:** reversions, possibilities of reverter, rights of entry; indefeasibly vested remainders; vested remainders subject to complete divestment; charity-to-charity gifts over; options appendant to leases.
- **Method:** (1) identify the interest; (2) when created (deed: delivery; will: death; revocable trust: when irrevocable); (3) what must happen for it to vest; (4) look for a validating life among persons alive at creation; (5) if any possible chain of events vests it later, strike it.

| Trap | Fact pattern | Common-law result |
|---|---|---|
| Fertile octogenarian | "to A for life, then to A's children for life, then to A's grandchildren," A age 80 | Grandchildren's gift void (A presumed able to have more children) |
| Unborn widow | "to A for life, then to A's widow for life, then to A's children then living" | Children's gift void (A's widow may be born after the grant) |
| Administrative contingency | "to my issue living when my estate is distributed" | Void (distribution might take more than 21 years) |
| Age contingency over 21 | "to the first of A's children to reach 25" (A alive) | Void |
| Class gift all-or-nothing | One possible late-vesting member | Whole class gift void |
| Option in gross | "to B, an option to buy Blackacre at any time" | Void at common law (USRAP excludes commercial options) |

- **Reforms** (R-PRP-016): wait-and-see (common-law period or 90 years); cy pres/reformation; specific fixes (age reduction to 21; fertility presumptions). **USRAP** (adopted in California, Prob. Code § 21200 et seq.): valid if it satisfies the common-law rule **or** actually vests or terminates within **90 years** (§ 21205); otherwise reform (§ 21220); excludes most nondonative transfers (§ 21225) [S-766]. Trusts cross-reference: R-TRS-034, R-TRS-035.
- **How tested:** MBE tests the traps under the common law unless a statute is given; CA essays rarely test the RAP in depth (Jul 2024 Q2 involved a gift over that raises a perpetuities issue; whether the answers discussed it is unconfirmed) [VERIFY V-071].

#### I.D.2 Alienability, descendibility, and devisability of present and future interests
- Present estates and vested future interests: transferable, devisable, descendible (R-PRP-018).
- Contingent remainders and executory interests: transferable at modern law (California: Civ. Code § 699), unless survival is a condition.
- Possibilities of reverter and rights of entry: descendible and devisable; inter vivos transfer of a right of entry was restricted at common law.
- **Restraints on alienation** (R-PRP-017): disabling restraints on a fee void; forfeiture and promissory restraints on a fee generally void; on life estates and leaseholds generally valid; reasonable restraints valid (ROFRs, lease consent clauses, cooperative approvals); Civ. Code § 711.

#### I.D.3 Fair housing/discrimination
- **Fair Housing Act** (42 U.S.C. § 3601 et seq.): race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, national origin; refusals, terms, advertising (§ 3604(c), applies even to exempt owners), steering, blockbusting, reasonable accommodations and modifications (§ 3604(f)). Exemptions: § 3603(b) (owner of three or fewer single-family houses selling or renting without a broker or discriminatory ads; owner-occupied four-or-fewer-unit dwellings) and § 3607 (religious organizations, private clubs, housing for older persons). Disparate impact is cognizable (*Texas Dep't of Housing & Community Affairs v. Inclusive Communities Project, Inc.*, 576 U.S. 519 (2015)). R-PRP-048.
- **42 U.S.C. § 1982:** race discrimination in all property transactions, no exemptions (*Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co.*, 392 U.S. 409 (1968)).
- **Covenants:** *Shelley v. Kraemer*, 334 U.S. 1 (1948) (no judicial enforcement of racial covenants); R-PRP-057.
- **CA note:** FEHA (Gov. Code §§ 12927, 12955) adds classes (including source of income, which covers housing vouchers after SB 329 (2019)) and has a narrower exemption (owner-occupied single-family home with one roomer or boarder) [S-776]; Unruh Act (Civ. Code § 51); restrictive covenant modification (Gov. Code § 12956.2) [S-771].

#### I.D.4 Conflicts of law related to disputes involving real property
- **Situs rule:** the law of the place where the land lies governs the validity, formalities and effect of conveyances, the estates created, recording, liens, adverse possession and succession to land by will or intestacy (Restatement (Second) of Conflict of Laws §§ 223, 236, 239) [VERIFY V-659]. R-PRP-049.
- Contract issues (e.g., enforceability of a land-sale contract's non-title terms) may follow the parties' chosen law or the most-significant-relationship test.
- Only the situs can transfer title in rem; a court with personal jurisdiction may order a party to convey (*Fall v. Eastin*, 215 U.S. 1 (1909)).
- **CA note:** out-of-state land at dissolution and quasi-community property: R-CMP-003, R-CMP-083.

## II Rights in real property (State Bar outline IV: non-possessory interests in land)

**Weight:** about one-fifth of the Real Property MBE [S-040]. Essay favorites: easements (Feb 2021 Q5, Jul 2016 Q2, Jul 2024 Q2) and covenants/servitudes (Feb 2021 Q5, Feb 2026 Q1) [VERIFY V-071].

### II.A Restrictive covenants

#### II.A.1 Nature and type
- A **covenant** is a promise about the use of land. Enforced **at law** (damages) it is a **real covenant**; enforced **in equity** (injunction) it is an **equitable servitude**. The same promise can be both; analyze both on an essay unless the call limits the remedy.
- **Negative** (restrict use: "residential only") vs. **affirmative** (require an act or payment: "pay annual road assessments").
- The Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes treats easements, covenants and servitudes as one category ("servitudes") and drops horizontal privity; it is influential but not the MBE default.

| Requirement | Real covenant: burden runs | Real covenant: benefit runs | Equitable servitude: burden runs | Equitable servitude: benefit runs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Writing | Yes | Yes | Yes, or common scheme | Yes, or common scheme |
| Intent to run | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Touch and concern | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Horizontal privity | **Yes** | No | No | No |
| Vertical privity | Yes (strict: entire estate) | Yes (relaxed) | No | No |
| Notice | Yes (to bind a BFP) | No | **Yes** | No |
| Remedy | Damages | Damages | Injunction | Injunction |

Rules: R-PRP-050 (burden), R-PRP-051 (benefit), R-PRP-052 (equitable servitude), R-PRP-054 (touch and concern).

#### II.A.2 Creation
- **Express:** in a deed or recorded declaration signed by the promisor (deed-poll acceptance binds the grantee who accepts). Statute of frauds applies.
- **Implied from a common scheme** (implied reciprocal negative servitude) (R-PRP-053): general plan when sales began (recorded plat, advertising, restrictions in most deeds) + notice (record, actual, inquiry from uniform appearance) → binds lots sold without the restriction; enforceable by all lot owners in the plan (*Sanborn v. McLean*, 233 Mich. 227, 206 N.W. 496 (1925)).
- **CA note:** California will not imply a restriction from a common plan alone; it must be in a recorded instrument (*Riley v. Bear Creek Planning Committee* (1976) 17 Cal.3d 500), but a declaration of CC&Rs recorded before the sales, describing the property and stating it binds successors, binds later buyers even if their deeds do not mention it (*Citizens for Covenant Compliance v. Anderson* (1995) 12 Cal.4th 345). California's statutory covenant rule: Civ. Code § 1468 (describe both parcels; successors expressly bound; relates to use, repair or maintenance; recorded) [S-769]; CID declarations are enforceable as equitable servitudes unless unreasonable (Civ. Code § 5975) [S-775].
- **Horizontal privity forms:** grantor–grantee (instantaneous), landlord–tenant (tenurial), mutual simultaneous interests (e.g., easement holders) in some states; simple neighbor agreements lack it.
- **How tested:** Feb 2026 Q1 ("for residential purposes" in each recorded deed of 15 lots) [VERIFY V-071].

#### II.A.3 Scope
- Interpret by the words and purpose; older cases resolve true ambiguity in favor of free use (R-PRP-055).
- "Residential purposes," "single-family dwelling," "no trade or business": ask whether the use changes the residential character (customers, employees, signage, traffic). An occasional or purely internal home office usually does not violate; a daycare, store or rental business usually does.
- Affirmative covenants to pay assessments: amount and purposes limited by the instrument; HOA board discretion (II.A.6).
- **Remedies and who may sue:** original promisee; successors to the benefited land; any lot owner under a common scheme; association if the declaration authorizes.

#### II.A.4 Transfer
- **Burden** passes to successors of the burdened land if the running requirements are met (horizontal and vertical privity and notice for real covenants; notice for equitable servitudes).
- **Benefit** passes with the benefited land (relaxed vertical privity; no horizontal privity).
- **Adverse possessors and lessees:** an adverse possessor lacks vertical privity for real-covenant burdens but is bound by equitable servitudes with notice; a tenant of the burdened land is bound by negative equitable servitudes with notice.
- **Benefit in gross:** older rule refused to let the burden run when the benefit was personal; modern rule allows it.
- **How tested:** Feb 2021 Q5 (covenant by a former neighbor enforced against a third party) [VERIFY V-071].

#### II.A.5 Termination
- Release (writing); merger; expiration by terms; abandonment; acquiescence; changed conditions (radical change defeating the purpose, judged mainly inside the restricted area; Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes § 7.10 [VERIFY V-653]); laches; unclean hands; estoppel; prescription (open violation for the statutory period); condemnation (majority compensates benefited owners); unenforceability for illegality or public policy (R-PRP-056, R-PRP-057).
- **Defense checklist for essays:** (1) changed conditions; (2) acquiescence or abandonment (how many violations, how long, how similar, who tolerated them); (3) laches (delay + prejudice); (4) unclean hands (plaintiff's own violation); (5) relative hardship (for the injunction).
- **How tested:** Feb 2026 Q1 (long-running home businesses without objection) [VERIFY V-071].

#### II.A.6 Property owners' associations and common interest ownership communities
- Associations enforce recorded declarations, adopt rules, levy assessments (secured by liens) and manage common areas. Directors owe fiduciary duties; courts defer to good-faith board decisions within their authority (*Lamden v. La Jolla Shores Clubdominium Homeowners Assn.* (1999) 21 Cal.4th 249).
- **Recorded use restrictions** are presumed reasonable; the challenger must prove they are arbitrary, impose burdens substantially outweighing benefits, or violate fundamental public policy, judged for the development as a whole (*Nahrstedt v. Lakeside Village Condominium Assn.* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 361) [S-775]. R-PRP-058.
- **Statutory overrides (CA):** at least one pet (Civ. Code § 4715) [S-775]; solar energy systems (Civ. Code § 714) [VERIFY V-655]; Davis-Stirling Act, Civ. Code § 4000 et seq.
- **Condominiums vs. cooperatives** (R-PRP-059): condo = fee in unit + tenancy in common in common areas (Civ. Code § 783); co-op = shares + proprietary lease; board approval of transfers generally upheld.

### II.B Easements, profits, and licenses

#### II.B.1 Nature and type
- **Easement:** nonpossessory right to use (affirmative) or to restrict use of (negative) another's land. **Appurtenant** (dominant and servient tenements; runs with the land; preferred construction) vs. **in gross** (personal or commercial). Civ. Code §§ 801–803. R-PRP-060.
- **Negative easements:** traditionally only light, air, support, flow of an artificial stream; created only expressly; modern conservation easements by statute.
- **Profit à prendre:** right to take resources; carries an implied easement of access; may be exclusive or shared.
- **License:** revocable privilege; not an interest in land; no writing required; ends on revocation, death of either party, or conveyance of the land. Exceptions: license coupled with an interest; irrevocable license by estoppel (R-PRP-065).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: 地役权 (servitude) under the PRC Civil Code ≈ easement appurtenant; it must be created by written contract, and registration is needed to bind a good-faith third party (approx.).

#### II.B.2 Methods of creation
| Method | Key requirements | Scope | Ends | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Express grant or reservation | Signed writing; reservation for a stranger allowed in CA | Terms + reasonable development | Terms, release, merger, abandonment, etc. | Civ. Code § 1091; *Willard v. First Church of Christ, Scientist* (1972) 7 Cal.3d 473 (R-PRP-061) |
| Implied from prior use | Common ownership; apparent, continuous prior use at severance; reasonable necessity (strict for reservation in many states) | Prior use | As other easements | Civ. Code § 1104 (R-PRP-062) |
| Necessity | Common ownership; severance; strict necessity at severance | What necessity requires | When necessity ends | Common law (R-PRP-063) |
| Prescription | Open and notorious; continuous; adverse (no permission); statutory period (CA five years; no tax payment unless separately assessed) | Use during the period | Nonuse for the period (CA, Civ. Code § 811) | Civ. Code §§ 1007, 1008, 813; *Gilardi v. Hallam* (1981) 30 Cal.3d 317 [S-757] [S-764] (R-PRP-064) |
| Estoppel / irrevocable license | Permission + substantial, foreseeable reliance | As reliance requires | When reliance protected | *Holbrook v. Taylor*, 532 S.W.2d 763 (Ky. 1976) (R-PRP-065) |
| Dedication | Offer to public + acceptance | Public use | Vacation | Civ. Code § 1009 limits implied dedication by public recreational use [S-764] |

- **CA note:** owners can block prescription by posting statutory "right to pass by permission" signs at entrances or every 200 feet (Civ. Code § 1008) or recording a notice of consent (Civ. Code § 813) [S-764]; California courts disfavor prescriptive easements that exclude the owner [VERIFY V-654].
- **How tested:** Jul 2016 Q2; Feb 2021 Q5 (express written right-of-way); Jul 2024 Q2 (necessity, implication, prescription) [VERIFY V-071].

#### II.B.3 Scope and apportionment
- Express: terms + reasonably foreseeable development (change in degree yes; change in kind no). Implied: prior use or necessity. Prescriptive: the use during the period, without material increase in burden (R-PRP-066).
- **Subdivision of the dominant estate:** each lot may share the easement if the burden does not become unreasonable; heavy multiplication may exceed scope.
- **Benefit to non-dominant land:** forbidden (*Brown v. Voss*, 105 Wash. 2d 366, 715 P.2d 514 (1986)); remedy may be injunction or damages.
- **Maintenance:** the easement holder repairs and maintains (Civ. Code § 845); the servient owner may use its land consistently with the easement.
- **Relocation:** traditional rule bars unilateral relocation; Restatement (Third) of Property: Servitudes § 4.8(3) allows servient owner relocation [VERIFY V-653].
- **Apportionment of profits and easements in gross:** divisible commercial easements in gross must be used "as one stock" (*Miller v. Lutheran Conference & Camp Ass'n*, 331 Pa. 241, 200 A. 646 (1938)).
- **Misuse remedy:** injunction against the excess use; misuse does not terminate the easement.
- **How tested:** Feb 2021 Q5 (subdivision traffic and utility lines over a right-of-way lane) [VERIFY V-071].

#### II.B.4 Transfer
- Appurtenant benefit passes automatically with the dominant land; burden binds a servient successor with notice (R-PRP-067).
- Commercial easements and profits in gross are assignable; personal easements in gross traditionally are not.
- Licenses are not transferable.
- **How tested:** Jul 2024 Q2 (inquiry notice from a visible right of way) [VERIFY V-071].

#### II.B.5 Termination
- Terms; merger (unity of ownership; no revival); release (writing); abandonment (nonuse + intent shown by acts); estoppel; prescription by the servient owner; end of necessity; condemnation; destruction of a servient structure; BFP without notice (R-PRP-068).
- **CA note:** Civ. Code § 811 (merger; destruction; incompatible act; nonuse for five years extinguishes an easement acquired by enjoyment).

### II.C Fixtures (State Bar outline III)
- **Definition and test:** objective intent from annexation, adaptation and removal damage; constructive annexation for items specially fitted (R-PRP-069). Civ. Code § 660 (what is affixed).
- **By relationship:**

| Parties | Rule |
|---|---|
| Vendor–purchaser; mortgagor–mortgagee | Fixtures pass with the land unless excluded in the contract |
| Landlord–tenant | Tenant may remove trade, domestic and ornamental fixtures before the lease ends if removal does not substantially damage the premises (repairing damage); otherwise they stay (Civ. Code § 1019) |
| Life tenant–remainderman | Like tenant but stricter; items annexed are presumed for the inheritance |
| Trespasser or mistaken improver | Annexations belong to the landowner (Civ. Code § 1013); good-faith improver may get equitable relief (Civ. Code § 1013.5; CCP §§ 871.1–871.7) |
| Secured party vs. real-property interests | UCC § 9-334: purchase-money security interest by fixture filing before annexation or within 20 days after has priority over earlier-recorded real-property interests (not a construction mortgage); readily removable office machines and domestic-appliance replacements also protected; secured party who removes must reimburse for physical injury, not loss in value (UCC § 9-604(c)–(d); Cal. Com. Code §§ 9334, 9604) |

### II.D Zoning

#### II.D.1 Types of zoning laws
- **Source and test:** police power; rational relation to health, safety, morals or general welfare (*Village of Euclid v. Ambler Realty Co.*, 272 U.S. 365 (1926)). California cities and counties have constitutional police power (Cal. Const. art. XI, § 7), and zoning must be consistent with the general plan (Gov. Code § 65860). R-PRP-070.
- **Types:** Euclidean (cumulative or exclusive use districts); bulk and density (height, setbacks, lot size, floor-area ratio); aesthetic and architectural review; historic preservation (*Penn Central Transp. Co. v. New York City*, 438 U.S. 104 (1978)); cluster zoning and planned unit developments; growth controls; inclusionary zoning; overlay districts.
- **Limits:** substantive due process and equal protection (*Village of Belle Terre v. Boraas*, 416 U.S. 1 (1974) (unrelated-persons limit valid); *Moore v. City of East Cleveland*, 431 U.S. 494 (1977) (family definition excluding a grandchild invalid); *City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center*, 473 U.S. 432 (1985) (irrational animus)); First Amendment (signs, adult uses, RLUIPA, 42 U.S.C. § 2000cc); Fair Housing Act (group homes); exclusionary zoning under state law (*Southern Burlington County NAACP v. Township of Mount Laurel*, 67 N.J. 151, 336 A.2d 713 (1975)); takings (R-PRP-073; R-CON-077 to R-CON-081).

#### II.D.2 Protection of pre-existing property rights
- **Nonconforming uses** (R-PRP-071): may continue; no substantial expansion or change; lost by abandonment or substantial destruction; amortization valid in many states including California (*City of Los Angeles v. Gage* (1954) 127 Cal.App.2d 442).
- **Vested rights:** valid permit + substantial good-faith work and liabilities (*Avco Community Developers, Inc. v. South Coast Regional Com.* (1976) 17 Cal.3d 785); development agreements and vesting tentative maps (Gov. Code §§ 65864 et seq., 66498.1).
- **Takings overlay:** an exaction must meet *Nollan/Dolan* (and *Sheetz* for legislative conditions [VERIFY V-035]).
- **How tested:** Feb 2014 Q4 (building-permit request; prior rights) [VERIFY V-071].

#### II.D.3 Rezoning and other zoning changes
- **Variance** (area or use): unnecessary hardship unique to the property, not self-created, no harm to neighborhood or plan. **CA note:** use variances prohibited; special circumstances required (Gov. Code § 65906); findings must bridge evidence and decision (*Topanga Assn. for a Scenic Community v. County of Los Angeles* (1974) 11 Cal.3d 506). R-PRP-072.
- **Special exception / conditional use permit:** use allowed if ordinance standards are met.
- **Rezoning (map amendment):** legislative; plan consistency; **spot zoning** invalid; **contract/conditional zoning** split.
- **Exactions and impact fees:** R-PRP-073.
- **Judicial review:** legislative acts get deference; administrative decisions need substantial evidence.

## Rights incident to ownership (State Bar outline VI)

The State Bar's essay outline lists rights that come with ownership itself [S-090] [VERIFY V-024]. They are tested mostly through Torts crossovers (nuisance, trespass) and takings (Feb 2018 Q3) [VERIFY V-071].

- **Right to exclude; trespass:** intentional physical entry is a trespass even without harm; minor good-faith encroachments may be remedied by damages rather than removal (relative hardship). Cross-reference the Torts files and R-REM-020, R-REM-042.
- **Nuisance:** substantial and unreasonable interference with use and enjoyment (Civ. Code § 3479); remedies by damages or injunction (R-REM-024, R-REM-062). Cross-reference Torts.
- **Lateral and subjacent support** (R-PRP-074): strict liability for land in natural state; negligence for structures (unless the land would have subsided anyway); CA excavation duties (Civ. Code § 832).
- **Water** (R-PRP-075): riparian vs. prior appropriation; California dual system subject to reasonable use (Cal. Const. art. X, § 2); groundwater correlative rights; diffuse surface water reasonableness (*Keys v. Romley* (1966) 64 Cal.2d 396); public trust (*National Audubon Society v. Superior Court* (1983) 33 Cal.3d 419).
- **Light, air, view; spite fences; airspace** (R-PRP-076): no natural right (*Fontainebleau Hotel Corp. v. Forty-Five Twenty-Five, Inc.*, 114 So. 2d 357 (Fla. Dist. Ct. App. 1959)); spite fences over ten feet (Civ. Code § 841.4); *Prah v. Maretti*, 108 Wis. 2d 223, 321 N.W.2d 182 (1982) (minority nuisance view for solar access); overflights (*United States v. Causby*, 328 U.S. 256 (1946)).
- **Takings** (R-PRP-073): the owner's constitutional protection against uncompensated takings; California's "taken or damaged" clause (Cal. Const. art. I, § 19).

## III Real estate contracts (State Bar outline VII–IX: conveyancing, land-sale contracts, deeds, recording and security devices)

**Weight:** about one-fifth of the Real Property MBE [S-040]. On California essays, land-sale contract issues appear mainly in Remedies crossovers (specific performance, damages); the State Bar's conveyancing section (VII–IX) also covers deeds, recording and mortgages, taught in IV and V below [S-090] [VERIFY V-024].

### III.A Real estate brokerage
- Broker = agent of the principal (usually the seller); fiduciary duties of loyalty, care, disclosure, obedience, accounting (R-PRP-077).
- **Listing agreements:** open; exclusive agency (no commission if the owner sells); exclusive right to sell (commission whoever sells).
- **Commission earned:** traditional rule: when the broker produces a ready, willing and able buyer on the listing terms; modern minority: only if closing occurs, unless the seller wrongfully prevents it (*Ellsworth Dobbs, Inc. v. Johnson*, 50 N.J. 528, 236 A.2d 843 (1967)) [VERIFY V-669].
- **Duties to buyers:** no misrepresentation; disclose known material defects. **CA note:** licensing (Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 10130, 10136); commission agreements must be in writing (Civ. Code § 1624(a)(4)); visual-inspection duty for 1–4 residential units (Civ. Code § 2079; *Easton v. Strassburger* (1984) 152 Cal.App.3d 90) [S-768]; agency-disclosure and dual-agency rules [VERIFY V-669].

### III.B Creation and construction

#### III.B.1 Statute of frauds and exceptions
- Writing signed by the party to be charged, with essential terms; multiple writings may be combined; electronic signatures valid (R-PRP-078).
- **Exceptions:** part performance (two of: possession, payment, improvements; unequivocally referable); promissory estoppel (some courts); full performance.
- **Equal dignities:** agent's authority to sign must be in writing (Civ. Code § 2309).
- Authority: Civ. Code §§ 1091, 1624(a)(3); cross-reference R-CTR-042, R-CTR-043, R-CTR-046.

#### III.B.2 Essential terms
- Parties, land description, price; courts imply reasonable terms for time and manner of payment and the duty to convey marketable title, but will not supply a missing price (R-PRP-079).
- **Description:** sufficient if land can be identified with reasonable certainty, using extrinsic evidence for latent ambiguity.

#### III.B.3 Time for performance
- Time is not of the essence unless stated, shown by circumstances, or made so by notice; a party may close within a reasonable time after the date and pay delay damages (R-PRP-079).
- **Tender:** concurrent conditions; tender required to put the other party in breach unless repudiation or futility.

#### III.B.4 Remedies for breach
- Buyer: specific performance; benefit-of-the-bargain damages (market value at breach − price) + incidentals; restitution of deposit; vendee's lien; minority English rule for good-faith title failures (R-PRP-081; R-REM-015).
- Seller: specific performance (mutuality); damages (price − market value) + incidentals; retain deposit as valid liquidated damages; vendor's lien (R-REM-016).
- **CA note:** Civ. Code §§ 3306, 3307 (damages measures), 3387 (presumption that money is inadequate, conclusive for a buyer-occupied single-family dwelling); §§ 1675, 1677 (3% residential liquidated damages); *Freedman v. Rector* (1951) 37 Cal.2d 16. Cross-reference R-REM-071, R-REM-073 (abatement).

### III.C Marketability of title
- Implied covenant of marketable title, measured at closing (R-PRP-080).
- **Unmarketable:** gaps in the chain; title by unadjudicated adverse possession (majority); mortgages and liens (unless paid at closing); easements and covenants (unless excepted or, in some courts, visible and beneficial); significant encroachments; existing zoning violations (*Lohmeyer v. Bower*, 170 Kan. 442, 227 P.2d 102 (1951)); outstanding future interests.
- **Marketable despite:** zoning restrictions themselves; minor discrepancies; a mortgage the seller will pay from proceeds.
- **Procedure:** buyer gives notice; seller may cure until closing; installment contract: at final payment.
- **Remedies:** rescission and return of deposit; damages; specific performance with abatement.

### III.D Equitable conversion
- On signing an enforceable contract, buyer = equitable owner; seller = legal title as security (R-PRP-082).
- **Risk of loss:** majority common law: buyer bears casualty loss (with credit for seller's insurance proceeds: *Skelly Oil Co. v. Ashmore*, 365 S.W.2d 582 (Mo. 1963)); UVPRA (California, Civ. Code § 1662): seller bears risk until legal title or possession passes.
- **Death before closing:** buyer's interest passes as real property (heirs/devisees of realty can demand conveyance; the estate pays the price); seller's interest passes as personal property (the right to the price). Ademption issues: a specific devise of land under contract may be adeemed in some states.
- **Creditors and joint tenancy:** a judgment against the seller after signing attaches only to the seller's interest; a contract by joint tenants may sever.

### III.E Options and rights of first refusal
- Option: consideration; writing; strict exercise within time; irrevocable for its term; an option in a lease runs with the leasehold (R-PRP-083).
- ROFR: triggered by owner's decision to sell; match a bona fide offer; notice and reasonable time; third-party buyer with notice takes subject to it.
- RAP: common law applies to options in gross and (majority) ROFRs; not to a tenant's option to purchase; California USRAP excludes most nondonative transfers (Prob. Code § 21225) [S-766].
- Restraint on alienation: ROFR at a fixed below-market price or without time limit may be unreasonable.

### III.F Fitness and suitability
- **Used property:** no implied warranty; liability for misrepresentation, active concealment and (majority) nondisclosure of known material latent defects (*Johnson v. Davis*, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)); "as is" clauses do not bar fraud; psychological stigma (*Stambovsky v. Ackley*, 169 A.D.2d 254, 572 N.Y.S.2d 672 (1991)) (R-PRP-084).
- **New construction:** builder-vendor's implied warranty of habitability and workmanlike construction; extended to later buyers for latent defects in many states (R-PRP-085).
- **CA note:** Transfer Disclosure Statement for 1–4 unit residential sales (Civ. Code § 1102 et seq.); *Lingsch v. Savage* (1963) 213 Cal.App.2d 729 [VERIFY V-668]; *Reed v. King* (1983) 145 Cal.App.3d 261; Civ. Code § 1710.2 (deaths more than three years before the offer) [VERIFY V-656]; broker inspection (Civ. Code § 2079) [S-768]; Right to Repair Act (Civ. Code §§ 895–945.5; *McMillin Albany LLC v. Superior Court* (2018) 4 Cal.5th 241 [VERIFY V-667]); 10-year repose (CCP § 337.15).

### III.G Merger
- On acceptance of the deed, title promises in the contract merge into the deed; buyer is left with deed covenants (R-PRP-086).
- **Exceptions:** collateral promises (repairs, construction, possession, condition), fraud, mistake, express survival clause.

## IV Mortgages/security devices

**Weight:** about one-fifth of the Real Property MBE [S-040]. MBE questions turn on priorities, who is personally liable after transfers, and how foreclosure proceeds are distributed. California essays add the deed of trust, nonjudicial sale and anti-deficiency rules when a lender seeks a deficiency (Feb 2014 Q4 tested sharing in foreclosure proceeds; Feb 2025 Q3 tested a mortgage's priority under the recording act) [VERIFY V-071].

### IV.A Types of security devices

#### IV.A.1 Mortgages

##### IV.A.1.a In general
- A mortgage secures an obligation with an interest in land; the borrower signs a **note** (personal liability) and a **mortgage** or **deed of trust** (security) (R-PRP-087).
- **Deed of trust:** three parties (trustor-borrower, trustee, beneficiary-lender); trustee's **power of sale** permits nonjudicial foreclosure. Deeds of trust dominate in California (Civ. Code §§ 2920, 2924).
- **Formal requirements:** a writing satisfying the statute of frauds; recording for priority.
- **PRC bridge:** see R-PRP-087 (抵押权; registration-based creation in the PRC).

##### IV.A.1.b Purchase money mortgages
- Secures funds used to buy the land, given to the seller or to a third-party lender (R-PRP-090).
- **Priority:** over pre-existing liens against the purchaser (e.g., judgment liens) that attach when title passes, subject to recording (Civ. Code § 2898) [S-770]; seller PMM generally beats third-party PMM; between two third-party PMMs, the recording act decides.
- **CA note:** anti-deficiency protection for seller-financed loans and third-party loans to buy owner-occupied 1–4 unit dwellings (CCP § 580b) [S-777].

##### IV.A.1.c Future advance mortgages
- Secures later advances (lines of credit, construction loans) (R-PRP-091); Civ. Code § 2884.
- **Obligatory** advances take priority from recording; **optional** advances made with actual notice of an intervening lien are subordinate to it (majority); some statutes protect all advances within a stated maximum.

#### IV.A.2 Installment land contracts
- Seller retains title until the last payment; forfeiture clauses (R-PRP-089).
- **Modern protections:** grace or cure periods; restitution of excess payments; waiver by accepting late payments (notice needed to reinstate strict time); treatment as a mortgage requiring foreclosure in some states.
- **CA note:** *Petersen v. Hartell* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 102 (right to redeem by paying the balance after substantial payments, even if the default was willful) and *MacFadden v. Walker* (1971) 5 Cal.3d 809 (restitution of excess) [S-773]; Civ. Code §§ 2985, 3275.
- **Seller's title duty:** marketable title only at final payment (R-PRP-080).

#### IV.A.3 Absolute deeds as security
- Deed absolute on its face + security intent = equitable mortgage; parol evidence admissible (clear and convincing in most states); lender must foreclose; borrower may redeem (R-PRP-088).
- **Factors:** pre-existing debt; inadequate price; grantor stays in possession; repurchase right; borrower's distress.
- **CA note:** Civ. Code §§ 2924 (transfer made only as security is a mortgage), 2925 (defeasance may be proved).
- **Related devices:** sale-leaseback with repurchase option (scrutinized as disguised loans); negative pledge (unsecured covenant not to encumber).

### IV.B Security relationships

#### IV.B.1 Necessity and nature of obligation
- No obligation, no mortgage; the obligation may be the mortgagor's or a third party's, present or future, and need not be a money debt (R-PRP-087).
- Payment or release of the obligation discharges the mortgage (IV.E).
- **Personal liability vs. property liability:** the note creates personal liability; the mortgage creates the property's liability; nonrecourse loans limit the lender to the property.

#### IV.B.2 Theories: title, lien, and intermediate
| Theory | Who holds title before foreclosure? | Mortgagee's right to possession | Effect on joint tenancy (one JT mortgages) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title | Mortgagee (legal title) | Yes, on demand (absent contrary agreement) | Severs |
| Lien (majority; California) | Mortgagor | No, until foreclosure (Civ. Code § 2927) | No severance; survivor takes free (California: *People v. Nogarr*) [S-765] |
| Intermediate | Mortgagor until default; then mortgagee | After default | Usually no severance until default |

#### IV.B.3 Rights and duties prior to foreclosure
- Possession and rents stay with the mortgagor in lien states; assignment-of-rents clauses and receivers give lenders access to rents after default (R-PRP-092). **CA note:** Civ. Code §§ 2927, 2929, 2938 [VERIFY V-671].
- **Mortgagee in possession:** must manage prudently; account for rents; liable for waste it commits; may be liable in tort as an occupier.
- **Mortgagor's duties:** pay, insure (by covenant), pay taxes, avoid waste and impairment of security.
- **Insurance proceeds:** usually applied to the debt or to restoration, depending on the instrument and (in some states) on whether restoration is feasible.

#### IV.B.4 Right to redeem and clogging equity of redemption
- **Equity of redemption:** before sale, pay the full amount due (whole accelerated debt) plus costs; mortgagor, junior lienors and successors may redeem (R-PRP-093).
- **Clogging:** provisions in the original transaction that cut off or restrict redemption (lender's option to buy; deed in escrow on default) are void; later separately bargained arrangements are valid. **CA note:** Civ. Code §§ 2889, 2903; reinstatement until five business days before a trustee's sale (Civ. Code § 2924c).
- **Distinguish** statutory redemption after sale (IV.F.5).

### IV.C Transfers by mortgagor

#### IV.C.1 Assumption and transfer subject to
- "Subject to": no personal liability for the grantee; the land remains liable (R-PRP-094).
- "Assumes": grantee personally liable to the lender (third-party beneficiary) as primary obligor.
- Original mortgagor stays liable (surety) unless released by novation.

#### IV.C.2 Rights and obligations
- After a transfer, the land is the primary fund, the assuming grantee the principal debtor, the original mortgagor a surety (R-PRP-094, R-PRP-095).
- **Lender's options on default:** foreclose; sue the original mortgagor for any deficiency (subject to anti-deficiency rules); sue an assuming grantee.
- **Grantee's rights:** to redeem; to receive surplus; to defend.
- **MBE pattern:**

| Transfer chain | Lender can sue personally |
|---|---|
| M → A (assumes) → B (subject to) | M, A |
| M → A (subject to) → B (assumes) | M; whether the lender can sue B is split: many courts deny it because A, to whom B promised, was never personally liable (broken chain of liability) |
| M → A (subject to) | M only |

#### IV.C.3 Subrogation and suretyship
- Surety (original mortgagor) who pays is subrogated to the lender's rights against the principal (assuming grantee) and the land (R-PRP-095).
- Material modification by lender and grantee without the surety's consent discharges the surety to the extent of prejudice.
- **Equitable subrogation:** a refinancing lender that pays off a senior mortgage, expecting first priority, steps into the senior's priority over intervening liens if they are not prejudiced.
- **Modification of a senior mortgage:** increases in interest or principal are subordinate to existing junior interests [VERIFY V-673].

#### IV.C.4 Restrictions on transfer, including due-on-sale clauses
- Due-on-sale clauses enforceable under the Garn–St Germain Act (12 U.S.C. § 1701j-3), preempting state limits such as *Wellenkamp v. Bank of America* (1978) 21 Cal.3d 943; residential exceptions (spouse/children, death, divorce, inter vivos trust, junior liens, short leases) [VERIFY V-665] (R-PRP-096). *Fidelity Federal Savings & Loan Ass'n v. de la Cuesta*, 458 U.S. 141 (1982).
- Due-on-encumbrance clauses: limited for residential junior liens by the same exceptions.

### IV.D Transfers by mortgagee
- The mortgage follows the note (*Carpenter v. Longan*, 83 U.S. (16 Wall.) 271 (1872)); transfer of the note transfers the mortgage; an assignment of the mortgage without the note is ineffective under the majority view (R-PRP-097).
- **Holder in due course** of a negotiable note takes free of personal defenses (fraud in the inducement, failure of consideration) but not real defenses (forgery, fraud in the factum, incapacity making the note void) (UCC §§ 3-302, 3-305).
- **Payment to the original lender** after transfer: protected if the borrower lacked notice under the Restatement (Third) of Property: Mortgages and 2002 UCC Article 3; not under the traditional negotiable-instrument rule [VERIFY V-673].
- **Recording assignments:** protects the assignee against later purchasers of the mortgage, not against the borrower's payments made without notice.
- **CA note:** a borrower may challenge a void assignment after a nonjudicial sale (*Yvanova v. New Century Mortgage Corp.* (2016) 62 Cal.4th 919).

### IV.E Discharge

#### IV.E.1 Payment
- Payment in full discharges the mortgage; lender must release or reconvey (CA: Civ. Code § 2941) (R-PRP-098).
- **Prepayment:** no common-law right to prepay before maturity absent a clause ("perfect tender in time"); prepayment fees enforceable if reasonable; CA regulates residential prepayment charges (Civ. Code § 2954.9) [VERIFY V-658].
- **Partial releases:** a release clause lets the mortgagor obtain release of parcels on payment of stated amounts (common in development loans).
- **Tender:** a valid tender of the full amount due at maturity discharges the lien in many states even if refused (the debt survives).

#### IV.E.2 Deed in lieu of foreclosure
- Voluntary conveyance after default in satisfaction of the debt; valid if fair and not obtained at the loan's origin (clogging) (R-PRP-098).
- **Risks:** lender takes subject to junior liens (no foreclosure wipeout); merger questions (lender may want the mortgage kept alive to foreclose juniors); possible avoidance in the mortgagor's bankruptcy.
- Debt is usually extinguished unless the parties agree otherwise.

### IV.F Foreclosure

#### IV.F.1 Types
| Type | Process | Deficiency? | Post-sale redemption? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Judicial | Court action; supervised sale | Yes, subject to fair-value and anti-deficiency limits | Where statute provides (CA: 3 months or 1 year, CCP § 729.030) [S-763] |
| Nonjudicial (power of sale) | Trustee's private sale after statutory notices (CA: Civ. Code §§ 2924, 2924f) | CA: barred (CCP § 580d) [S-777] | CA: none |
| Strict foreclosure | Title vests in lender without sale | Varies | Rare (few states) |

- Sale must be conducted fairly and in good faith. A low price alone does not void a regularly conducted sale; a court may set it aside if the price is so grossly inadequate that it shocks the conscience (the Restatement treats gross inadequacy alone as sufficient), or if an inadequate price is coupled with fraud, unfairness or an irregularity. California requires unfairness or irregularity in addition to a low price (R-PRP-099) [VERIFY V-675].

#### IV.F.2 Acceleration
- Acceleration clause lets the lender declare the whole debt due on default; without it, foreclosure only for overdue installments (R-PRP-099).
- **Waiver:** accepting late payments without objection may waive strict timing; reinstate by notice.
- **CA note:** reinstatement right by curing arrears up to five business days before a trustee's sale (Civ. Code § 2924c).

#### IV.F.3 Parties to the proceeding (and priorities)
- **Necessary parties:** mortgagor, current owner, junior lienholders and junior tenants; senior lienholders are not necessary and are unaffected (R-PRP-101).
- **Effect:** foreclosing mortgage and joined junior interests are extinguished; buyer takes subject to senior interests; omitted juniors keep their liens and rights to redeem.
- **Priorities** (R-PRP-100): first in time (Civ. Code § 2897), as adjusted by the recording act (Civ. Code § 1214); purchase-money priority (§ 2898) [S-770]; subordination agreements; future advances; senior modifications (subordinate as to increases); equitable subrogation; fixture filings (UCC § 9-334); property-tax liens (generally first) [VERIFY V-661]; federal tax liens after filing (26 U.S.C. § 6323).

| Priority scenario | Winner |
|---|---|
| Earlier recorded mortgage vs. later mortgage | Earlier |
| Earlier unrecorded mortgage vs. later mortgagee for value without notice who records first (race-notice) | Later |
| Prior judgment lien against buyer vs. purchase-money mortgage given at acquisition | Purchase-money mortgage |
| Senior mortgage modified to raise the rate vs. intervening junior | Junior, as to the increase |
| Refinancing lender paying off senior vs. intervening second lien | Refinancer (equitable subrogation), up to the amount paid off |

#### IV.F.4 Deficiency and surplus
- **Order of distribution:** costs; foreclosing debt; junior interests by priority; surplus to mortgagor (R-PRP-102). Seniors are not paid from proceeds.
- **Deficiency:** personal judgment against the mortgagor (and assuming grantees) for the shortfall, subject to fair-value statutes and anti-deficiency laws.
- **CA note** (R-PRP-104): one-action rule (CCP § 726); no deficiency after nonjudicial sale (§ 580d); purchase-money protection (§ 580b); fair-value limit after judicial sale (§§ 726(b), 580a); protections not waivable in advance (*Palm v. Schilling* (1988) 199 Cal.App.3d 63) [S-763] [S-777]; sold-out junior may sue on its note (*Roseleaf Corp. v. Chierighino* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 35).
- **How tested:** Feb 2014 Q4 (a party's claim to share in foreclosure proceeds) [VERIFY V-071].

#### IV.F.5 Redemption after foreclosure
- **Statutory redemption** (about half the states): fixed period after sale; pay the sale price plus costs and interest; mortgagor usually keeps possession (R-PRP-103).
- **CA note:** judicial sale: one year if a deficiency is possible (proceeds insufficient), three months if proceeds satisfy the debt (CCP § 729.030) [S-763]; none after a trustee's sale.
- **Compare:** equitable redemption ends at the sale (IV.B.4).

## V Titles

**Weight:** about one-fifth of the Real Property MBE [S-040]. Essay favorites: recording acts (Feb 2015 Q2, Jul 2024 Q2, Feb 2025 Q3), deeds and warranty covenants (Oct 2020 Q3, Feb 2025 Q3), adverse possession (Oct 2020 Q3) [VERIFY V-071].

### V.A Adverse possession (State Bar outline V)
- **Elements:** actual; open and notorious; exclusive; hostile (objective majority; good-faith and aggressive-trespasser minorities); continuous for the statutory period (R-PRP-105). *Van Valkenburgh v. Lutz*, 304 N.Y. 95, 106 N.E.2d 28 (1952); *Manillo v. Gorski*, 54 N.J. 378, 255 A.2d 258 (1969).
- **Color of title:** constructive possession of the whole described parcel when part is actually occupied.
- **Tacking** (privity), **disabilities** (existing at entry), **future interests** (no running until possessory), **cotenants** (ouster), **government land** (immune) (R-PRP-107). *Howard v. Kunto*, 3 Wash. App. 393, 477 P.2d 210 (1970).
- **Effect:** new title by operation of law, relating back to entry; not recorded, so unmarketable until quieted (R-PRP-080).
- **CA note** (R-PRP-106): five years; payment of all taxes levied and assessed during the period, timely and proved by tax-collector records; color of title (CCP §§ 322, 323) vs. claim of right with substantial enclosure or usual cultivation or improvement (CCP §§ 324, 325) [S-756]; no adverse possession against public entities (Civ. Code § 1007); boundary-strip claims usually fail on taxes, so consider prescriptive easement (*Gilardi v. Hallam* (1981) 30 Cal.3d 317) [S-757] or agreed boundary (*Bryant v. Blevins* (1994) 9 Cal.4th 47) (R-PRP-108).
- **Agreed boundary, acquiescence, estoppel, encroachment** (R-PRP-108).

| Issue | Common-law / MBE | California |
|---|---|---|
| Period | Statute (often 10–21 years; question gives it) | 5 years (CCP §§ 318, 321) |
| Taxes | Not required (some states require under color of title) | Required: all taxes levied and assessed during the period (CCP § 325) [S-756] |
| Hostility | Objective (majority) | Claim of right or color of title |
| Against government | No | No (Civ. Code § 1007) |
| Disabilities | Toll if existing at entry | CCP § 328 [VERIFY V-666] |

### V.B Transfer by deed

#### V.B.1 Requirements for deed
- Writing; grantor's signature; identified grantor and existing grantee; adequate description; words of present grant; delivery; consideration, seal and witnesses unnecessary; acknowledgment needed for recording (R-PRP-109). Civ. Code §§ 1091, 1092; Gov. Code § 27287.
- **Delivery** (R-PRP-110): present intent to pass title; presumptions; conditional delivery to the grantee (condition ineffective, Civ. Code § 1056); escrows (commercial; death escrows); relation back; acceptance presumed. Civ. Code §§ 1054–1057.
- **Void vs. voidable deeds** (R-PRP-113): forged, undelivered, fraud in the factum → void even against a BFP; fraud in the inducement, undue influence, minority, incapacity → voidable, not against a BFP.
- **Estoppel by deed** (R-PRP-112): after-acquired title inures to the grantee (Civ. Code § 1106).
- **How tested:** Oct 2020 Q3 (deeds within a family; delivery and survivorship) [VERIFY V-071].

#### V.B.2 Types of deeds
| Deed | Covenants | Breach timing | Runs to remote grantees? |
|---|---|---|---|
| General warranty | Seisin, right to convey, against encumbrances (present); warranty, quiet enjoyment, further assurances (future) | Present: at delivery. Future: on actual or constructive eviction | Present: no (majority). Future: yes |
| Special warranty | Same, limited to the grantor's own acts | Same | Same |
| California grant deed | Implied: no prior conveyance by grantor; free of encumbrances done, made or suffered by grantor (Civ. Code § 1113) | At delivery / on eviction | Limited |
| Quitclaim | None | — | — |

- **Damages:** limited to the consideration received by the covenantor (plus interest); encumbrance: removal cost or diminution (R-PRP-111).
- **How tested:** Feb 2025 Q3 (claims under a general warranty deed against the grantor) [VERIFY V-071].

#### V.B.3 Drafting, review, and negotiation of closing documents
- **Closing package:** purchase contract and escrow instructions; title commitment or preliminary report (review exceptions and requirements); survey; deed (type, legal description, grantor capacity and signatures, acknowledgment); note and mortgage or deed of trust; settlement (closing) statement; affidavits (title, nonforeign status), payoff letters and releases; transfer-tax and recording forms.
- **Descriptions:** metes and bounds, government survey, recorded plat, street address (weak); priority: natural monuments → artificial monuments → courses → distances → area; road boundary to center line presumption; patent vs. latent ambiguity (R-PRP-109).
- **Review points an MBE question may test:** a deed whose description cannot be located is void; an exception in a title commitment for a recorded easement means the policy will not cover it (R-PRP-120); the closing statement prorates taxes and rents; a contract term not repeated in the deed may merge (R-PRP-086).
- **Negotiation:** the type of deed, the title exceptions the buyer will accept, risk-of-loss allocation (R-PRP-082), survival of seller representations past closing (R-PRP-086), and liquidated damages (R-PRP-081).
- **CA note:** escrow is the standard closing method; a preliminary report is not a representation of title [VERIFY V-657].

#### V.B.4 Persons authorized to execute documents
- Owners (all co-owners for the whole); agents with written authority (Civ. Code § 2309); corporations through authorized officers (Corp. Code § 313); partnerships and LLCs through authorized members or partners (cross-reference Business Associations); trustees, personal representatives and conservators within powers or court orders; minors and incompetents lack capacity (R-PRP-114).
- **CA note:** spouses' joinder for community real property (Fam. Code § 1102; R-CMP-067); powers of attorney (Prob. Code § 4000 et seq.).

### V.C Transfer by operation of law and by will
- **By will or intestacy:** situs law; ademption, lapse/anti-lapse (R-WIL-063), exoneration (CA: no exoneration; specific devisee takes subject to the mortgage, Prob. Code § 21131) (R-PRP-115).
- **Nonprobate transfers:** joint tenancy survivorship; revocable trust; **CA revocable transfer-on-death deed** (Prob. Code § 5600 et seq.; notarized and signed by two witnesses; timely recording) [S-767] [VERIFY V-660].
- **By operation of law:** adverse possession; eminent domain (R-PRP-073); foreclosure and tax sales; escheat; bankruptcy; judicial decrees (partition, quiet title R-REM-093, ejectment R-REM-042); marriage (community property).

### V.D Title assurance systems

#### V.D.1 Recording acts
| Statute type | Typical language | Who wins: O → A (unrecorded), then O → B (BFP), then A records, then B records | California? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Race | "first recorded" with no notice language | A (recorded first) | No |
| Notice | "without notice" | B (BFP without notice when value paid) | No |
| Race-notice | "in good faith and for value … whose conveyance is first duly recorded" | A (B did not record first) | **Yes** (Civ. Code § 1214) |

- **Common law (no act):** first in time wins.
- **Protected persons:** purchasers and mortgagees for value without notice (not donees, heirs, devisees); California also excludes leases of one year or less from the act and protects a judgment affecting title where notice of action is recorded before the earlier conveyance (Civ. Code § 1214) (R-PRP-116, R-PRP-117).
- **Notice:** actual; record (Civ. Code § 1213: recording is constructive notice); inquiry (possession, references in recorded instruments, visible easements); imputed.
- **Chain of title** (R-PRP-118): grantor-grantee vs. tract index; wild deeds; late-recorded and early-recorded deeds (majority: outside the chain); misindexing (split).
- **Shelter rule; limits of recording** (R-PRP-119): BFP's transferees protected; forged and void deeds remain void; off-record interests (adverse possession, prescriptive and some implied easements, rights of persons in possession) not cut off.
- **Valid between the parties:** an unrecorded instrument binds the parties and those with notice (Civ. Code § 1217).
- **How tested:** Feb 2015 Q2; Jul 2024 Q2 (inquiry notice); Feb 2025 Q3 (race-notice priority of a mortgage over a later deed) [VERIFY V-071].

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: in the PRC, registration generally creates the real right (登记生效); in the US, delivery of the deed passes title and recording only decides priority against later takers (approx.).

#### V.D.2 Title insurance
- Indemnity contract covering defects in record title as of the policy date, up to the policy amount, with a duty to defend; owner's policy does not run to later buyers; lender's policy follows the loan (R-PRP-120).
- **Standard exceptions:** off-record matters (possession, unrecorded easements, survey matters, mechanics' liens), governmental regulation, defects known to the insured, post-policy defects.
- **Liability theory:** contract (majority); some states allow negligence claims for a negligent search; abstractors liable in negligence.
- **Other systems:** attorney title opinions; abstracts; Torrens registration (rare; certificate of title conclusive).
- **CA note:** Ins. Code § 12340 et seq.; preliminary report not a representation (§ 12340.11) [VERIFY V-657].

### V.E Special problems
- **Estoppel by deed** and its recording-act interaction (R-PRP-112, R-PRP-118).
- **Judgment liens:** attach on recording/docketing to the debtor's present and after-acquired land in the county, but only to the debtor's actual interest; purchase-money mortgage priority (R-PRP-121, R-PRP-090). **CA note:** abstract of judgment lien lasts ten years (CCP § 697.310).
- **Tax liens:** property-tax liens senior to private liens [VERIFY V-661]; federal tax liens effective against purchasers and secured creditors after filing (26 U.S.C. §§ 6321, 6323); surplus after a tax sale belongs to the owner (*Tyler v. Hennepin County* (2023)) [VERIFY V-035].
- **Lis pendens:** constructive notice of pending litigation affecting title; later takers bound (CCP § 405.20 et seq.) [VERIFY V-671]; R-REM-074.
- **Forgery and fraud; bona fide purchaser of a voidable title** (R-PRP-113).
- **Mechanics' liens:** statutory liens for labor and materials, often with priority relating back to the start of work; California's mechanics' lien is constitutionally based (Cal. Const. art. XIV, § 3).
