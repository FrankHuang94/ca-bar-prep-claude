# Real Property — essay templates

**Paragraph structure for every issue (IRAC).**
1. **Issue** as a heading (underlined or bold in Examplify).
2. **Rule** in 1–3 sentences from [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md); add "In California, …" in one sentence where it changes the result.
3. **Analysis:** match each element to specific facts ("Here, …"); argue the other side where the facts allow ("However, B will argue …").
4. **Conclusion:** one sentence with "likely" or "probably."

**Time:** about 15 minutes to read and outline, 45 minutes to write. Spend the most words on the issues the facts develop most. Past-question references below are indicative only [VERIFY V-071].

## Template 1 — "What interests do A, B, C and D hold?" (title timeline)
Used in Feb 2015 Q2, Jul 2015 Q2, Oct 2020 Q3, Jul 2024 Q2.

```
I. Timeline (optional 3–5 line summary of transfers)
II. [Year 1] O's conveyance to A and B
    A. Estate created (fee simple / defeasible fee / life estate)        R-PRP-001–007
    B. Future interests (reversion / remainder / executory interest)    R-PRP-008–011
    C. RAP (if contingent interest in a transferee)                      R-PRP-014–016
    D. Concurrent estate: TIC or JT (four unities; language)             R-PRP-019–020
III. [Year 2] A's transfer to C
    A. Validity of the deed (writing, delivery)                         R-PRP-109–110
    B. Effect on the joint tenancy (severance)                           R-PRP-021–022
    C. Recording / notice issues if a later buyer competes               R-PRP-116–117
IV. [Year 3] B's death
    A. Survivorship vs. will / intestacy                                 R-PRP-020–021
V. Conclusion: each party's interest stated in one line
VI. Second call (e.g., accounting and contribution)                     R-PRP-024–025
```
**Tips.** Re-state the state of title after every event. End with a table-like line: "A: none; C: one-third TIC; D: two-thirds TIC."

## Template 2 — Landlord-tenant claims and counterclaims
Used in Feb 2012 Q6, Feb 2019 Q3, Feb 2023 Q3.

```
I. Tenant's claims against Landlord
    A. Type of tenancy and enforceability (SOF)                          R-PRP-027
    B. Failure to deliver possession (English vs. American rule)         R-PRP-030
    C. Breach of quiet enjoyment: actual or constructive eviction        R-PRP-034–035
    D. Breach of the warranty of habitability (residential)              R-PRP-036–037
    E. Remedies: terminate, abate, damages
II. Tenant's claims against third parties (holdover occupant: trespass, ejectment, damages)
III. Landlord's claims / counterclaims against Tenant
    A. Past-due rent (any defense: eviction, habitability)               R-PRP-031
    B. Abandonment and future rent: surrender? duty to mitigate?         R-PRP-033, R-PRP-047
    C. Waste or other covenant breaches                                  R-PRP-032
IV. Liability of assignees / sublessees (if transfers)                   R-PRP-039–041
V. Likely outcome (net amounts if the call asks)
```
**Tips.** For each argument, give both rules where the jurisdiction is unstated (English/American; mitigation/no mitigation) and conclude under the majority. Add the California sentence (Civ. Code § 1951.2; *Green*; § 1942) only where it changes the result.

## Template 3 — Is the restriction enforceable? (covenants and equitable servitudes)
Used in Feb 2021 Q5, Feb 2026 Q1.

```
I. Nature of the restriction (negative covenant on use)                  R-PRP-050
II. Enforceable as a real covenant (damages)?
    Writing → Intent → Touch and concern → Horizontal privity → Vertical privity → Notice
III. Enforceable as an equitable servitude (injunction)?                 R-PRP-052
    Writing (or common scheme) → Intent → Touch and concern → Notice
    A. Common scheme for lots without the restriction                    R-PRP-053
       (California: recorded instrument required — Riley / Citizens)
IV. Who may enforce (original party, successors, other lot owners)       R-PRP-055
V. Does the conduct violate the restriction? (scope)                     R-PRP-055
VI. Defenses                                                             R-PRP-056
    A. Changed conditions  B. Acquiescence / abandonment  C. Laches
    D. Unclean hands  E. Other (release, merger, prescription)
VII. Remedy and conclusion
VIII. Second call: claims between the neighbors (nuisance; easement)
```
**Tips.** Count the violations and the years; name who tolerated them. A defense often bars one plaintiff but not another.

## Template 4 — Easement creation and scope
Used in Jul 2016 Q2, Feb 2021 Q5, Jul 2024 Q2.

```
I. Does X have an easement over Y's land?
    A. Express (writing; grant or reservation; third-party reservation)  R-PRP-061
    B. Implied from prior use                                            R-PRP-062
    C. Necessity                                                         R-PRP-063
    D. Prescription (California: 5 years; § 1008 signs)                  R-PRP-064
    E. Estoppel / irrevocable license                                    R-PRP-065
II. Classification: appurtenant or in gross; profit; license            R-PRP-060
III. Does the easement bind the current servient owner? (notice; recording; inquiry notice)  R-PRP-067, R-PRP-117
IV. Scope: proposed new use (subdivision, utilities, wider road)        R-PRP-066
V. Termination arguments                                                 R-PRP-068
VI. Remedies (injunction, damages)
```
**Tips.** Run every creation method the facts suggest, even if one clearly works; graders reward alternative theories. Scope turns on "degree vs. kind."

## Template 5 — Recording priority and deed covenants
Used in Feb 2025 Q3 (and recording issues in Feb 2015 Q2, Jul 2024 Q2).

```
I. Priority between [Buyer] and [Mortgagee/earlier grantee]
    A. Common law: first in time
    B. Recording act (type from the call or facts; California race-notice)   R-PRP-116
    C. Was the later taker a purchaser for value?                            R-PRP-117
    D. Notice: actual, record (chain of title), inquiry                      R-PRP-117–118
    E. Recorded first? (race-notice)
    F. Shelter rule for later grantees                                       R-PRP-119
II. Claims under the deed
    A. Type of deed (general warranty / special / grant / quitclaim)         R-PRP-111
    B. Present covenants: seisin, right to convey, against encumbrances — breached at delivery
    C. Future covenants: warranty, quiet enjoyment, further assurances — breach on eviction
    D. Damages (limited to price received) and who may sue (remote grantees)
    E. Estoppel by deed if grantor later acquired title                      R-PRP-112
III. Conclusion
```

## Template 6 — Mortgages and foreclosure
Used in Feb 2014 Q4 (claim to share in foreclosure proceeds).

```
I. Nature of each security interest (mortgage, deed of trust, equitable mortgage, installment contract)  R-PRP-087–089
II. Priorities among the liens (time, recording, purchase money, future advances, modification, subrogation)  R-PRP-090–091, R-PRP-100
III. Effect of transfers by the mortgagor (subject to / assumption; due-on-sale)  R-PRP-094–096
IV. Foreclosure
    A. Method and acceleration                          R-PRP-099
    B. Who is wiped out; who survives                  R-PRP-101
    C. Distribution of proceeds; surplus; deficiency   R-PRP-102
    D. Redemption before and after sale                R-PRP-093, R-PRP-103
V. California overlay (one paragraph): one-action, §§ 580b, 580d, fair value   R-PRP-104
VI. Conclusion: who receives what
```
