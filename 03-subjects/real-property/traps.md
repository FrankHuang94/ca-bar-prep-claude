# Real Property — traps

## Estates and future interests
1. **Purpose words are not limits.** "To the church for church purposes" is a fee simple absolute, not a determinable fee (R-PRP-005).
2. **FSD vs. FSSCS.** "So long as" = automatic; "but if … may re-enter" = election. A distractor will say the grantor "automatically" regained title after a condition subsequent (R-PRP-002, R-PRP-003).
3. **Third-party gift over is never a possibility of reverter or right of entry.** It is an executory interest, and the RAP applies (R-PRP-004).
4. **The RAP does not apply to the grantor's interests** (reversion, possibility of reverter, right of entry) (R-PRP-014).
5. **"Likely" is irrelevant under the common-law RAP;** only "possible" matters (fertile octogenarian, unborn widow) (R-PRP-015).
6. **Class gifts are all-or-nothing** under the RAP; one possible late member voids the whole gift (R-PRP-015).
7. **Vested subject to open is subject to the RAP;** indefeasibly vested and vested subject to complete divestment are not (R-PRP-009, R-PRP-014).
8. **Life tenant pays mortgage interest, not principal,** and only up to the income or rental value (R-PRP-007).
9. **California is different for FSDs** (power of termination, 30 years) and **RAP** (90-year wait-and-see); do not forget the CA sentence on an essay, and do not use it on the MBE (R-PRP-005, R-PRP-016).

## Concurrent ownership
10. **A will never severs a joint tenancy** (R-PRP-021).
11. **A conveyance by one of three joint tenants severs only that share;** the other two remain joint tenants with each other (R-PRP-021).
12. **Mortgage severance depends on theory;** the MBE question will tell you lien or title; California is lien theory (R-PRP-022).
13. **No rent from a cotenant in possession** without ouster; a distractor offers "fair rental value" as the majority rule (R-PRP-024).
14. **Tenancy by the entirety does not exist in California;** analyze spouses under CP (R-PRP-023).

## Landlord-tenant
15. **Constructive eviction requires leaving;** a tenant who stays must use habitability (residential) or damages (R-PRP-035, R-PRP-036).
16. **Habitability is residential only** in most states (R-PRP-036).
17. **The American rule on delivery is the minority;** pick the English rule unless the question says otherwise (R-PRP-030).
18. **A sublessee is not liable to the landlord for rent;** an assignee is, but only while it holds the estate (unless it assumed) (R-PRP-039, R-PRP-040).
19. **The original tenant stays liable after assignment** unless the landlord released it (R-PRP-040).
20. **A ban on assignment does not ban subletting,** and vice versa (R-PRP-042).
21. **Landlord's duty to mitigate:** modern majority, but the traditional no-duty rule may still appear as a distractor (R-PRP-033).
22. **Accepting the keys is not automatically a surrender;** look for the landlord's intent (R-PRP-047).

## Covenants, servitudes and easements
23. **Horizontal privity is required only for the burden of a real covenant** (damages); an equitable servitude needs none (R-PRP-050, R-PRP-052).
24. **Notice is required for an equitable servitude,** not for the benefit side (R-PRP-052).
25. **California does not imply a common scheme** without a recorded instrument (R-PRP-053).
26. **Changes outside the subdivision rarely terminate a covenant** (R-PRP-056).
27. **Prescriptive easements do not require exclusive use;** adverse possession does (R-PRP-064, R-PRP-105).
28. **Necessity must exist at severance and ends when necessity ends** (R-PRP-063).
29. **Nonuse alone does not abandon an express easement** (R-PRP-068); but California extinguishes a prescriptive easement after five years of nonuse (Civ. Code § 811).
30. **Misuse of an easement does not terminate it;** the remedy is injunction or damages (R-PRP-066).
31. **A license is revocable** unless coupled with an interest or made irrevocable by reliance (R-PRP-065).

## Contracts and titles
32. **Risk of loss:** majority puts it on the buyer after signing; California (UVPRA) on the seller until title or possession passes (R-PRP-082).
33. **Zoning restrictions do not make title unmarketable;** existing violations do (R-PRP-080).
34. **Marketable title is measured at closing,** not at signing (R-PRP-080).
35. **Merger:** title promises in the contract disappear at closing; collateral promises survive (R-PRP-086).
36. **Present covenants do not run** and are breached at delivery; future covenants run and are breached on eviction (R-PRP-111).
37. **Estoppel by deed does not apply to a quitclaim** (R-PRP-112).
38. **A deed delivered to the grantee with an oral condition passes title absolutely** (R-PRP-110).
39. **Forged deeds are void even for BFPs;** fraudulently induced deeds are voidable and good in a BFP's hands (R-PRP-113).
40. **Donees, heirs and devisees are never protected** by recording acts (R-PRP-117).
41. **Possession by someone other than the seller gives inquiry notice** (R-PRP-117).
42. **Wild deeds give no constructive notice** (R-PRP-118).
43. **The shelter rule protects even a taker with notice** from a BFP (R-PRP-119).
44. **California adverse possession requires tax payment;** boundary-strip claims usually fail (R-PRP-106).

## Mortgages
45. **"Subject to" is not personal liability** (R-PRP-094).
46. **Foreclosure never affects senior interests** (R-PRP-101).
47. **A deed in lieu does not wipe out junior liens** (R-PRP-098).
48. **Equitable redemption (before sale) requires the full accelerated debt;** statutory redemption (after sale) requires the sale price (R-PRP-093, R-PRP-103).
49. **A purchase-money mortgage beats an earlier judgment lien against the buyer** (R-PRP-090).
50. **In California, no deficiency after a trustee's sale,** and none on owner-occupied purchase-money loans (R-PRP-104).
