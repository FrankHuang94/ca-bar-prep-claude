# Real Property — MBE rules

## NCBE's framework
- **25 scored questions** of 175 on each MBE [S-007].
- **Distribution:** about one-fifth of the Real Property questions come from each of categories I–V (ownership; rights in land; real estate contracts; mortgages and security devices; titles) [S-040]. Plan on roughly five questions per category.
- **Governing law:** answer under the generally accepted rule. When a statute matters (a recording act, a perpetuities reform, a mortgage theory, a joint-tenancy statute), the question supplies it or tells you the common law is unmodified. Never apply California law on the MBE.
- **Read the call.** "Who has the better claim?" "What is the most likely result?" and "Which of the following is A's best argument?" ask different things. For "best argument" questions, pick the argument that is legally sound **and** fits the facts, even if it may not win.

## How to read a Real Property question
1. **Draw the timeline** of conveyances (date, grantor → grantee, type of instrument, recorded or not, value paid, notice).
2. **Identify the doctrine** from the call and the last line of facts.
3. **Find the statute** in the facts and classify it (race / notice / race-notice from its words).
4. **Eliminate** answers that state a real rule that does not apply on these facts (the most common distractor).

## Category I — Ownership (about 5 questions)
**Favored rules**
- Classify from the words: "so long as" (FSD + possibility of reverter); "but if … right to re-enter" (FSSCS + right of entry); "but if …, then to B" (executory interest). R-PRP-002 to R-PRP-004.
- Words of purpose ("for church purposes") do not create a defeasible fee. R-PRP-005.
- Vested vs. contingent remainder: the comma test; vested subject to open for classes. R-PRP-009, R-PRP-010.
- RAP: common law unless a statute is given; class gifts all-or-nothing; fertile octogenarian; unborn widow; options in gross. The possibility of reverter and right of entry are exempt. R-PRP-014, R-PRP-015.
- Rule of convenience closes a class when a member can demand possession. R-PRP-013.
- Life tenant pays taxes and mortgage interest (to the extent of income or rental value); remainderman pays principal. R-PRP-007.
- Joint tenancy: conveyance severs; will does not; mortgage severs only in a title-theory state (the question will tell you the theory). R-PRP-021, R-PRP-022.
- Cotenant in possession owes no rent absent ouster; third-party rents are shared. R-PRP-024.
- Landlord-tenant: English rule on delivery of possession (majority); assignee liable on privity of estate only while in possession unless it assumed; original tenant liable on contract; sublessee not liable to landlord. R-PRP-030, R-PRP-039, R-PRP-040.
- Constructive eviction requires vacating; habitability (residential) does not. R-PRP-035, R-PRP-036.
- Majority duty to mitigate. R-PRP-033.
- Landlord tort liability: common-law exceptions (common areas, latent defects, negligent repairs, public use). R-PRP-045.
- Fair Housing Act: advertising ban applies even to exempt owners. R-PRP-048.

**Recurring fact patterns**
- A deed to "A for life, then to A's children who reach 25": classify, close the class, test the RAP.
- A, B and C as joint tenants; A conveys to D; B dies with a will: who owns what? (C and D as tenants in common: C two-thirds, D one-third.)
- T assigns to A, A assigns to B; B defaults: whom can L sue? (T on contract; B on estate; A only if A assumed.)
- Tenant leaves because of a leaking roof in a commercial building: constructive eviction, not habitability.

## Category II — Rights in land (about 5 questions)
**Favored rules**
- Real covenant burden: writing, intent, touch and concern, horizontal privity, vertical privity, notice. Equitable servitude: writing (or common scheme), intent, touch and concern, notice. R-PRP-050, R-PRP-052.
- Common scheme: lots sold without the restriction are bound if a general plan existed and the buyer had notice. R-PRP-053.
- Termination: changed conditions must be radical and within the area; acquiescence; abandonment. R-PRP-056.
- Easement by necessity requires common ownership and strict necessity at severance; ends with necessity. R-PRP-063.
- Easement implied from prior use: apparent, continuous, reasonably necessary. R-PRP-062.
- Prescriptive easement: no exclusivity needed; permission defeats it. R-PRP-064.
- Scope: change in degree OK, change in kind not; cannot serve non-dominant land. R-PRP-066.
- Appurtenant easements pass automatically; commercial easements in gross are assignable. R-PRP-067.
- Abandonment needs nonuse plus an act showing intent. R-PRP-068.
- License becomes irrevocable through substantial, foreseeable reliance. R-PRP-065.
- Fixtures: trade fixtures removable by tenant before the term ends; fixture-filing priority within 20 days. R-PRP-069.
- Zoning: nonconforming uses continue but cannot expand; variances require unique hardship not self-created; spot zoning invalid. R-PRP-070 to R-PRP-072.

**Recurring fact patterns**
- Developer sells 20 lots with restrictions in 19 deeds; the 20th buyer opens a store.
- Owner divides land, leaving a landlocked parcel, then a public road is built.
- Neighbor crosses for the statutory period after being told "go ahead": permission defeats prescription.
- Dominant owner buys an adjoining parcel and uses the easement to reach both.

## Category III — Real estate contracts (about 5 questions)
**Favored rules**
- Statute of frauds; part performance (possession plus payment or improvements). R-PRP-078.
- Time is not of the essence unless stated or made so; reasonable delay allowed. R-PRP-079.
- Implied marketable title at closing; existing zoning violations make title unmarketable, zoning restrictions do not; seller may cure until closing; a mortgage the seller pays off at closing is not a defect. R-PRP-080.
- Equitable conversion: buyer bears risk of loss (majority), with credit for seller's insurance; UVPRA if stated. R-PRP-082.
- Merger: contract title promises merge into the deed; collateral promises survive. R-PRP-086.
- Seller liable for nondisclosure of known latent material defects; "as is" does not protect fraud. R-PRP-084.
- Builder's implied warranty for new homes. R-PRP-085.
- Broker earns commission when producing a ready, willing and able buyer (traditional). R-PRP-077.
- Options must be exercised strictly; ROFR holder may enforce against a buyer with notice. R-PRP-083.

**Recurring fact patterns**
- Fire destroys the house between contract and closing; no statute given: buyer bears risk.
- Buyer's title search shows an old recorded easement: title unmarketable unless the contract excepts it.
- Buyer accepts the deed, then discovers the seller's promise of "good title" was false: sue on the deed covenants, not the contract.

## Category IV — Mortgages (about 5 questions)
**Favored rules**
- Lien theory (majority): mortgagor keeps possession until foreclosure. R-PRP-087.
- Absolute deed + security intent = equitable mortgage. R-PRP-088.
- Purchase-money mortgage beats prior liens against the purchaser. R-PRP-090.
- Obligatory future advances keep priority; optional advances made with actual notice of an intervening lien do not. R-PRP-091.
- "Subject to" = no personal liability; "assume" = personal liability; original mortgagor remains a surety. R-PRP-094.
- Surety discharged to the extent of prejudice by material modification. R-PRP-095.
- Due-on-sale clauses enforceable. R-PRP-096.
- Mortgage follows the note; HDC takes free of personal defenses. R-PRP-097.
- Deed in lieu does not cut off junior liens. R-PRP-098.
- Equity of redemption: pay full accelerated debt before sale; clogging void. R-PRP-093.
- Foreclosure: juniors wiped out if joined; seniors unaffected; omitted juniors survive. R-PRP-101.
- Proceeds: costs, foreclosing debt, juniors in order, then owner. R-PRP-102.
- Statutory redemption: after sale, at the sale price. R-PRP-103.

**Recurring fact patterns**
- Second mortgagee forecloses; first mortgage stays on the land; third mortgagee is paid from surplus.
- Buyer takes "subject to" the mortgage and stops paying; lender sues buyer personally: buyer not liable.
- Modification of the first mortgage raises the interest rate: priority over the second only to the original terms.

## Category V — Titles (about 5 questions)
**Favored rules**
- Adverse possession: actual, open and notorious, exclusive, hostile (objective), continuous; tacking with privity; disabilities at entry only; no running against future interests until possessory. R-PRP-105, R-PRP-107.
- Delivery: present intent; a deed handed over with an oral condition passes title absolutely; a death escrow is valid if the grantor gives up control. R-PRP-110.
- Present covenants breached at delivery and do not run; future covenants breached on eviction and run. R-PRP-111.
- Estoppel by deed: after-acquired title passes to the warranty grantee. R-PRP-112.
- Forged deed void even for a BFP; deed procured by fraud in the inducement voidable but good in a BFP's hands. R-PRP-113.
- Recording-act types; donees are not protected; shelter rule. R-PRP-116, R-PRP-117, R-PRP-119.
- Inquiry notice from possession. R-PRP-117.
- Wild deeds give no constructive notice. R-PRP-118.
- Title insurance does not run with the land to the buyer's grantee. R-PRP-120.
- Judgment lien attaches to after-acquired property; purchase-money mortgage still wins. R-PRP-121.

**Recurring fact patterns**
- O → A (unrecorded, value), O → B (gift), B records: B loses because B is not a purchaser for value, under any statute type.
- O → A (unrecorded), O → B (value, no notice), A records, B records: race-notice → A; notice → B.
- B buys from O while A is living on the land under an unrecorded deed: inquiry notice defeats B.
- Grantor gives a deed to the grantee "to hold until I die": delivery question.

## Distractor patterns to expect
- **Real rule, wrong facts:** e.g., "because the restriction runs with the land" when there is no horizontal privity and the plaintiff seeks damages.
- **Minority rule stated as if it were the rule:** e.g., American rule on delivery of possession; good-faith requirement for adverse possession.
- **Confusing the two redemptions** (equitable before sale vs. statutory after sale).
- **Treating "subject to" as personal liability.**
- **Treating a will as severing a joint tenancy.**
- **Applying the RAP to a possibility of reverter or right of entry.**
- **Saying a recorded deed is "in the chain" when it is a wild deed or recorded late.**
