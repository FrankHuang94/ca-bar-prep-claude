# Real Property — California distinctions

## How to use California law on a Real Property essay

**Default rule.** California Real Property essays are answered under **general principles** (the majority common-law rule) unless the call asks for California law [S-077]. Past Real Property calls have been framed in general terms ("What interests do the parties hold?", "What claims does the tenant have?"), and one named the statute type to apply ("under the race-notice recording act", Feb 2025 Q3) [VERIFY V-071]. So:

1. Write the **majority rule** first, as the main analysis.
2. Where California differs in a way that changes the outcome, add **one sentence**: "In California, …" with the citation. Do not write a separate California analysis unless the call asks for it.
3. Where the call names the law (a recording-act type, "under California law"), apply that law as the main rule.
4. The MBE never tests California law. On the MBE, use the majority rule or the statute quoted in the question.

**Where California follows the general rule (write one rule, no CA sentence needed).** Present estates and future-interest classification; class gifts; the elements of real covenants and equitable servitudes (with § 1468 as California's codification); easements by implication and necessity; scope and termination of easements; fixtures (Civ. Code §§ 660, 1019 restate the common law); assignment vs. sublease and privity; covenants running with leaseholds; constructive eviction; marketable title; equitable conversion (except risk of loss); merger; options; deeds, delivery and estoppel by deed (Civ. Code §§ 1054–1057, 1106); chain-of-title doctrine; shelter rule; title insurance; zoning basics.

## California mortgage and foreclosure rules at a glance

| Step | Rule | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument | Deed of trust with power of sale; lien theory | Civ. Code §§ 2924, 2927 |
| Default and reinstatement | Notice of default; cure arrears until five business days before sale | Civ. Code §§ 2924, 2924c, 2924f |
| Choice of remedy | Nonjudicial sale (no deficiency, no redemption) or judicial foreclosure (deficiency possible, redemption follows) | CCP §§ 580d, 726, 729.030 [S-763] [S-777] |
| Purchase-money loans | No deficiency on seller financing or on a loan to buy an owner-occupied 1–4 unit home | CCP § 580b [S-777] |
| Security first | Creditor must foreclose before suing on the note | CCP § 726 |
| Sold-out junior | May sue on the note (unless § 580b applies) | *Roseleaf Corp. v. Chierighino* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 35 |
| Priority of purchase money | Over other liens created against the purchaser, subject to recording laws | Civ. Code § 2898 [S-770] |
