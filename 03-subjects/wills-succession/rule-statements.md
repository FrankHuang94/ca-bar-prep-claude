# Wills and Succession — rule statements

**How to use this file.** Each block gives a rule you can write verbatim on an essay, then its elements, exceptions, authority and a short example. `build_anki.py` turns the **Rule (write this)** paragraphs into cards. The order follows [`outline.md`](outline.md).

**Governing law.** Wills is **not on the MBE**. California Wills essays are answered under the **California Probate Code** (the call says "Answer according to California law"). The "MBE vs. CA essay" line therefore states only how the rule is used on the essay and, where useful, the common-law or UPC contrast (see [`ca-distinctions.md`](ca-distinctions.md)).

**Abbreviations.** Prob. Code = California Probate Code; CP / QCP / SP = community / quasi-community / separate property; DRR = dependent relative revocation; UPC = Uniform Probate Code.

## I General provisions

### R-WIL-001 — Community and quasi-community property at death; testamentary power
**Rule (write this):** When a married person dies, one-half of the community property belongs to the surviving spouse and one-half to the decedent, and one-half of the decedent's quasi-community property belongs to the surviving spouse. The decedent may dispose by will only of his or her separate property, one-half of the community property and one-half of his or her quasi-community property.

**Elements:** 1. Characterize each asset (CP, QCP, SP). 2. Survivor owns half of each CP asset outright. 3. Survivor owns half of the decedent's QCP (decedent domiciled in California). 4. Will reaches only SP + decedent's half of CP + half of decedent's QCP.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Out-of-state land is not QCP at death (§ 66); equitable election if the will purports to give away the survivor's half (R-CMP-095).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 100, 101, 6101; cross-reference R-CMP-091, R-CMP-092.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; the first step of every CP + Wills crossover.

**Example:** H's will leaves "my house" (CP) to his son. The son takes H's half; W keeps her half as a tenant in common with the son.

### R-WIL-002 — Recapture of quasi-community property
**Rule (write this):** If a decedent domiciled in California transferred quasi-community property to someone other than the surviving spouse without substantial consideration and without the spouse's written consent or joinder, and kept possession, income, a power to revoke or invade, or a survivorship interest, the surviving spouse may require the transferee to restore one-half of the property or its value.

**Elements:** 1. QCP. 2. Transfer to a non-spouse. 3. No substantial consideration. 4. No written consent or joinder. 5. Retained interest or survivorship. 6. Survivor recovers one-half.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Statutory carve-outs for certain insurance, annuity and pension payments (see R-CMP-093).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 102 [S-403]; cross-reference R-CMP-093.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2022 Q5 pattern.

**Example:** H deeds QCP land to "H and daughter D as joint tenants" without W's consent and dies. W can compel D to restore one-half.

### R-WIL-003 — Simultaneous death of spouses: community and quasi-community property
**Rule (write this):** If spouses die and it cannot be established by clear and convincing evidence that one survived the other, one-half of the community and quasi-community property is administered as if it belonged to each spouse and that spouse survived, so each half passes through that spouse's own estate.

**Elements:** 1. Both spouses dead. 2. No clear and convincing evidence of survival. 3. Split CP/QCP in half. 4. Each half passes in that spouse's estate.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A governing instrument that provides otherwise.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 103 [VERIFY V-521].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H and W die in a crash; no proof of who died first. Half the CP passes under H's will, half under W's.

### R-WIL-004 — Surviving spouse's waiver of rights at death
**Rule (write this):** A spouse may waive rights arising at the other spouse's death, including community property, intestate and omitted-spouse rights, by a written waiver signed by the waiving spouse. The waiver is enforceable unless the surviving spouse proves that fair and reasonable disclosure of the decedent's property and obligations was not provided (and not waived after independent advice) or that the surviving spouse was not represented by independent counsel; even then the court may enforce it to the extent it was a fair and reasonable disposition when signed.

**Elements:** 1. Written waiver. 2. Signed by the surviving spouse. 3. Disclosure or independent counsel (§ 143). 4. Otherwise, court discretion (§ 144).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Revocation only by a writing signed by both (§ 146); premarital agreements must also satisfy the Family Code.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 140–147 [S-606] [S-607] [VERIFY V-522].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; defeats an omitted-spouse claim under § 21611(c).

**Example:** Before marrying H, W signs a waiver of "all rights in H's estate" with her own lawyer. On H's death W cannot claim an omitted-spouse share.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: compare renouncing inheritance (放弃继承), which under PRC law is made after the succession opens; California allows a binding advance waiver (approx.).

### R-WIL-005 — Contracts concerning wills
**Rule (write this):** A contract to make a will, not to revoke a will, or to die intestate can be established only by the will's or another instrument's statement of its material terms, an express reference in an instrument plus extrinsic evidence of the terms, a writing signed by the decedent, or clear and convincing evidence of an agreement or promise enforceable in equity. The remedy is against the estate, typically a constructive trust or damages, not invalidation of the later will.

**Elements:** 1. Contract made after Jan 1, 2001. 2. One of the five § 21700(a) methods of proof. 3. Breach (a contrary will). 4. Remedy against the estate.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Joint or mutual wills alone create no presumption of a contract (§ 21700(b)).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21700 [S-627].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Niece cares for Uncle for years on his oral promise to leave her his house; he leaves it to a friend. If she proves the promise by clear and convincing evidence and equity enforces it, a constructive trust is imposed on the house.

### R-WIL-006 — Joint and mutual wills
**Rule (write this):** The execution of a joint will or of mutual (reciprocal) wills does not create a presumption of a contract not to revoke them; a survivor may revoke unless the contract is proved under § 21700.

**Elements:** 1. Joint or mirror wills. 2. No presumption of contract. 3. Contract must be proved independently.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A will that recites the contract's material terms satisfies § 21700(a)(1).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21700(b) [S-627].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H and W sign mirror wills leaving all to each other, then to their children. After H dies, W may make a new will unless the wills or other evidence prove a contract.

### R-WIL-007 — Simultaneous death: general rule
**Rule (write this):** If title to or devolution of property depends on priority of death and it cannot be established by clear and convincing evidence that one person survived the other, each person's property is administered as if that person survived the other.

**Elements:** 1. Two deaths. 2. Devolution depends on who died first. 3. No clear and convincing evidence. 4. Each treated as survivor as to own property.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Specific rules for beneficiaries, joint tenants and insurance (§§ 222–224); a governing instrument.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 220.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; the UPC uses a 120-hour rule for wills, California uses clear-and-convincing proof (120 hours only in intestacy).

**Example:** A and B, each the other's sole devisee, die in a fire. Without proof, A's estate passes as if B predeceased, and vice versa.

### R-WIL-008 — Survival of beneficiaries under an instrument
**Rule (write this):** A transferee who fails to survive the transferor, or a future time the instrument requires, does not take under the instrument, and a beneficiary whose right depends on surviving another is deemed not to have survived unless survival is established by clear and convincing evidence.

**Elements:** 1. Gift conditioned on survival (express or implied). 2. Clear and convincing evidence of survival. 3. Otherwise the gift fails, subject to anti-lapse.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Anti-lapse (R-WIL-063); alternative dispositions.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21109(a) [S-622], 222.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T's will gives $50,000 to her brother B, who dies an hour after T in the same accident with no reliable evidence of the order. B is treated as not surviving; anti-lapse may give the gift to B's issue.

### R-WIL-009 — Joint tenants dying simultaneously
**Rule (write this):** If joint tenants die and it cannot be established by clear and convincing evidence that one survived the other, the property is divided into equal shares, and each share is administered as if that joint tenant survived the others.

**Elements:** 1. Joint tenancy. 2. All tenants dead. 3. No clear and convincing proof of order. 4. Equal shares through each estate.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Contrary instrument.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 223 [S-636].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Brothers A and B hold land in joint tenancy and die together. Half passes through A's estate, half through B's.

### R-WIL-010 — Insured and beneficiary dying simultaneously
**Rule (write this):** If the insured and the beneficiary of a life or accident policy die and it cannot be established by clear and convincing evidence that the beneficiary survived the insured, the proceeds are distributed as if the insured survived the beneficiary.

**Elements:** 1. Policy. 2. Insured and beneficiary both dead. 3. No clear and convincing proof that beneficiary survived. 4. Proceeds go as if beneficiary predeceased (contingent beneficiary or insured's estate).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Policy terms.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 224 [S-636].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H insures his life for W; both die in a crash. The proceeds go to the contingent beneficiary or H's estate, not W's estate.

### R-WIL-011 — Distribution by representation
**Rule (write this):** Under California's default rule of representation, the property is divided into equal shares at the first generation below the ancestor that has a living member, with one share for each living member and one for each deceased member who left living issue; each deceased member's share passes to his or her issue in the same manner. An instrument calling for "per stirpes" divides at the children's generation, and "per capita at each generation" pools the shares of deceased members at each level.

**Elements:** 1. Identify the ancestor. 2. Find the nearest generation with a living member. 3. Count living members plus deceased members with issue. 4. Divide equally; drop shares down.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Instrument choosing § 246 or § 247.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 240, 246, 247 [VERIFY V-523].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; UPC default is per capita at each generation.

**Example:** All three of D's children predeceased; A left two children, C left one, B none. Under § 240 the three grandchildren take one-third each; under § 246 A's two children take one-quarter each and C's child one-half.

### R-WIL-012 — Slayer rule
**Rule (write this):** A person who feloniously and intentionally kills the decedent takes nothing under the decedent's will, trust or intestacy, and the property passes as if the killer had predeceased the decedent; a joint tenancy between them is severed. A final felony conviction is conclusive; otherwise the probate court decides by a preponderance of the evidence.

**Elements:** 1. Killing. 2. Felonious and intentional. 3. Proof: conviction or preponderance in probate. 4. Killer treated as predeceased; JT severed.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Accidental, negligent or justified killings (self-defense); insanity may negate "felonious and intentional."

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 250–254.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** S shoots his father intentionally and is acquitted at trial. The probate court may still find by a preponderance that the killing was felonious and intentional and bar S.

### R-WIL-013 — Elder and dependent-adult abuse forfeiture
**Rule (write this):** A person shown by clear and convincing evidence to be liable for physical abuse, neglect or financial abuse of a decedent who was an elder or dependent adult, and to have acted in bad faith or with recklessness, oppression, fraud or malice, is deemed to have predeceased the decedent to the extent the statute provides; certain criminal convictions have the same effect.

**Elements:** 1. Decedent was an elder or dependent adult. 2. Abuse or neglect. 3. Clear and convincing evidence. 4. Bad faith / recklessness, oppression, fraud or malice. 5. Treated as predeceased.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Statutory exception where the decedent, with capacity, knew of the abuse and later expressed an intent to benefit the abuser [VERIFY V-539].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 259 [S-639].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A caregiver-son drains his elderly mother's accounts. If proven under § 259, he is treated as predeceased and takes nothing from her estate.

### R-WIL-014 — Disclaimers
**Rule (write this):** An heir or beneficiary may disclaim an interest by a signed writing filed within a reasonable time; a disclaimer filed within nine months of the death is conclusively presumed timely, and a later disclaimer is effective only if the disclaimant proves the time was reasonable; the disclaimed interest passes as if the disclaimant had predeceased the decedent.

**Elements:** 1. Writing. 2. Signed. 3. Filed or delivered timely. 4. Not yet accepted. 5. Passes as if disclaimant predeceased.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Acceptance of benefits bars disclaimer.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 275–295 (esp. §§ 279, 282) [S-931] [VERIFY V-517].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; nice-to-know.

**Example:** Daughter disclaims her $100,000 gift; anti-lapse treats her as predeceased, so her children take it.

## II Intestate succession

### R-WIL-015 — Property subject to intestacy
**Rule (write this):** Any part of a decedent's estate not effectively disposed of by will passes by intestate succession, whether because there is no valid will or because part of the will fails. Nonprobate property and the surviving spouse's half of the community property are not part of the intestate estate.

**Elements:** 1. Probate estate. 2. Not effectively disposed of by will. 3. Distribute under §§ 6401–6402.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Barred heirs (slayers, abusers) treated as predeceased.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6400.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; the last stop for every failed gift.

**Example:** T's will gives her car to A and has no residuary clause. Her house and bank account pass by intestacy.

### R-WIL-016 — Surviving spouse's intestate share
**Rule (write this):** When a married person dies intestate, the surviving spouse takes the decedent's one-half of the community property and of the decedent's quasi-community property, and a share of the separate property: all of it if the decedent left no issue, parent, sibling or issue of a deceased sibling; one-half if the decedent left one child or the issue of one deceased child, or no issue but a parent or a parent's issue; and one-third if the decedent left more than one child or the issue of more than one child.

**Elements:** 1. CP → all to spouse. 2. QCP → decedent's half to spouse. 3. SP share: all / one-half / one-third depending on surviving relatives. 4. Rest under § 6402.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Former spouse is not a surviving spouse (§ 78); valid waiver (§ 141).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6401; cross-reference R-CMP-094.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; UPC § 2-102 gives the spouse more of the estate.

**Example:** W dies intestate leaving H and her sister. H takes all CP, W's half of QCP and half of W's SP; the sister takes the other half of the SP.

### R-WIL-017 — Who is a surviving spouse
**Rule (write this):** A person whose marriage to the decedent was dissolved or annulled is not a surviving spouse, nor is a person who obtained or consented to a dissolution not recognized in California or who was a party to a proceeding purporting to end all marital property rights; a spouse in a pending dissolution or a legal separation that did not end the marriage remains a surviving spouse.

**Elements:** 1. Marital status at death. 2. Final dissolution or annulment? 3. § 78 disqualifications.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Putative spouses may take as spouses in quasi-marital property [VERIFY V-524].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 78 [VERIFY V-524].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H dies while his divorce from W is pending. W is still his surviving spouse for intestacy.

### R-WIL-018 — Other heirs' shares
**Rule (write this):** The part of the intestate estate not passing to the surviving spouse passes, in order, to the decedent's issue; if none, to the parents; if none, to the issue of the parents; if none, to the grandparents or their issue; if none, to the issue of a predeceased spouse; if none, to the next of kin; and if none, to the parents of a predeceased spouse or their issue. Each class takes by representation where its members are of unequal degree.

**Elements:** 1. Determine the first class with a survivor. 2. Apply § 240 within the class. 3. 120-hour survival (§ 6403).

**Exceptions / defenses:** § 6402.5 predeceased-spouse property; barred parents (§ 6452).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6402.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Unmarried D dies leaving no issue, her mother and two brothers. The mother takes everything; the brothers take nothing.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC law puts spouse, children and parents together in the first order; California puts parents behind issue, and siblings behind parents (approx.).

### R-WIL-019 — Predeceased spouse's relatives
**Rule (write this):** If the decedent leaves no surviving spouse and no issue, the portion of the estate attributable to a predeceased spouse who died within 15 years (real property) or 5 years (personal property, above the statutory threshold) passes first to the predeceased spouse's issue, then parents, then their issue, before the decedent's own collateral relatives; otherwise the issue of a predeceased spouse take only after the decedent's grandparents and their issue.

**Elements:** 1. No surviving spouse or issue. 2. Predeceased spouse died within 15 years (real) or 5 years (personal). 3. Property attributable to that spouse. 4. Passes to that spouse's relatives.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Personal-property value and title-record threshold [VERIFY V-508].

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 6402(e), (g), 6402.5 [S-632].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; nice-to-know.

**Example:** W inherited H's house when he died 10 years ago; W dies intestate with no issue. H's children by a prior marriage take the house ahead of W's cousins.

### R-WIL-020 — 120-hour survival rule in intestacy
**Rule (write this):** For intestate succession, a person who fails to survive the decedent by 120 hours, proven by clear and convincing evidence, is deemed to have predeceased the decedent, unless applying the rule would cause the estate to escheat.

**Elements:** 1. Intestate succession. 2. Heir's survival under 120 hours or unproven. 3. Heir deemed predeceased. 4. Not if escheat would result.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Does not govern wills (use §§ 220–224 and the will's terms).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6403.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; UPC applies 120 hours to wills too.

**Example:** D dies intestate; her only child dies two days later. The child is treated as predeceased, so D's estate passes to the child's issue or D's other heirs.

### R-WIL-021 — Escheat
**Rule (write this):** If no one is entitled to take under the intestacy statutes, the intestate estate escheats to the State of California.

**Elements:** 1. No heir in any § 6402 class. 2. Escheat.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The 120-hour rule yields if it would cause escheat.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6404.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** D dies intestate with no traceable relatives or predeceased spouse's relatives. The estate goes to the State.

### R-WIL-022 — Relatives of the half blood
**Rule (write this):** Relatives of the half blood inherit the same share they would inherit if they were of the whole blood, subject to the adoption rules of § 6451.

**Elements:** 1. Half-blood relationship. 2. Same share as whole blood.

**Exceptions / defenses:** § 6451 limits for natural relatives of an adoptee.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6406.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** D leaves a full brother and a half-sister and no closer relatives. Each takes one-half.

### R-WIL-023 — Afterborn and posthumously conceived heirs
**Rule (write this):** Relatives conceived before the decedent's death but born afterward inherit as if born in the decedent's lifetime; a child conceived after the decedent's death using the decedent's genetic material is treated as born in the decedent's lifetime only if the statutory requirements of written consent, notice and timely conception are met.

**Elements:** 1. In utero at death → inherits. 2. Posthumous conception → § 249.5 requirements.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Failure of § 249.5 conditions.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 6407, 249.5 [VERIFY V-509].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; nice-to-know.

**Example:** H dies while W is pregnant; the child born four months later inherits from H.

### R-WIL-024 — Advancements
**Rule (write this):** A lifetime gift to an heir is treated as an advancement against the heir's intestate share only if the decedent declared so in a contemporaneous writing or the heir acknowledged it in writing; the gift is valued when the heir received it or at death, whichever is first, unless the writing fixes a value, and it is not charged against the issue of an advancee who predeceased unless the writing so provides.

**Elements:** 1. Intestacy. 2. Lifetime gift to heir. 3. Contemporaneous writing by decedent or written acknowledgment by heir. 4. Valuation. 5. Hotchpot.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No writing → no advancement.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6409.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Mother gave Son $50,000 with a note saying "advance on your inheritance" and then died intestate with $250,000 and two children. Add back $50,000 (total $300,000); each child's share is $150,000; Son receives $100,000 and Daughter $150,000.

### R-WIL-025 — Debts owed to the decedent
**Rule (write this):** A debt an heir owes the decedent is charged against that heir's intestate share, but not against the share of the issue of a debtor-heir who failed to survive the decedent.

**Elements:** 1. Debt owed by heir. 2. Offset against share. 3. Not against debtor's issue.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Forgiven debts.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6410.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Son owes his father $20,000. In intestacy, $20,000 is deducted from Son's share.

### R-WIL-026 — Parent and child: natural parents
**Rule (write this):** For intestate succession, a parent-child relationship exists between a person and his or her natural parents regardless of the parents' marital status, and natural parentage may be established under the Uniform Parentage Act or as the Probate Code otherwise allows.

**Elements:** 1. Natural parentage. 2. Marital status irrelevant. 3. Proof under UPA presumptions, court order or § 6453 evidence.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Adoption severance (§ 6451); parent barred (§ 6452).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 6450(a), 6453 [VERIFY V-511].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A child born to unmarried parents inherits from her father whose paternity is established.

### R-WIL-027 — Adopted children
**Rule (write this):** An adopted person is the child of the adopting parents for intestate succession. Adoption severs the relationship with a natural parent unless the natural parent and the child lived together as parent and child at any time (or the parent died before birth while married to or cohabiting with the other parent) and the adoption was by the spouse of a natural parent or after a natural parent's death; even then, the natural parent's side (other than whole-blood siblings and their issue) does not inherit from the adoptee.

**Elements:** 1. Adoption → full child of adopters. 2. Severance from natural parents unless both § 6451(a) conditions. 3. One-way inheritance limit (§ 6451(b)).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Stepparent adoption after divorce or death preserves inheritance from the noncustodial natural parent.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 6450(b), 6451 [VERIFY V-526].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2018 Q5 (adopted son).

**Example:** C lived with her father F until age 5; after F died, her stepfather adopted her. C can still inherit from F's parents.

### R-WIL-028 — When a parent cannot inherit from a child
**Rule (write this):** A parent does not inherit from or through a child if the parent's parental rights were terminated and not reestablished, the parent did not acknowledge the child, or the parent left the child during minority without support or communication for at least seven consecutive years continuing to the end of minority with intent to abandon; the barred parent is treated as having predeceased the child.

**Elements:** 1. Termination; or 2. Non-acknowledgment; or 3. Seven-year abandonment through minority. 4. Parent deemed predeceased.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Failure to support or communicate is only presumptive evidence of intent to abandon.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6452 [S-625].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Father left at the child's birth and never paid or called; the child dies at 25 intestate. Father is treated as predeceased; the mother takes.

### R-WIL-029 — Stepchildren and foster children
**Rule (write this):** A stepchild or foster child inherits from or through a stepparent or foster parent only if the relationship began during the child's minority and continued throughout their joint lifetimes, and clear and convincing evidence shows the stepparent or foster parent would have adopted the child but for a legal barrier that persisted until death.

**Elements:** 1. Relationship began during minority. 2. Continued through joint lifetimes. 3. Would have adopted. 4. Legal barrier (persisting until death). 5. Clear and convincing evidence.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A barrier that ended at majority (natural parent's refusal to consent) fails; stepchildren may still take as issue of a predeceased spouse (§ 6402(e)) or via equitable adoption.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6454; *Estate of Joseph* (1998) 17 Cal.4th 203 [S-610].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2013 Q5 (stepson).

**Example:** Stepfather raised S from age 3; S's father refused consent to adoption, but S turned 18 and stepfather lived 20 more years without adopting. No persisting legal barrier, so S cannot inherit under § 6454.

### R-WIL-030 — Equitable adoption
**Rule (write this):** A child may inherit from a person who agreed to adopt but never completed the adoption if clear and convincing evidence shows an intent to adopt, such as an express or implied agreement, and conduct consistent with that intent; a close and caring relationship alone is not enough.

**Elements:** 1. Agreement or clear intent to adopt. 2. Conduct consistent with adoption (treating as own child, holding out). 3. Clear and convincing evidence.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Operates for the child's benefit; the would-be parent does not inherit from the child.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6455; *Estate of Ford* (2004) 32 Cal.4th 160 [S-624].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A couple took in a foster child, told everyone they were "adopting" him, and signed papers they never filed. The child may claim by equitable adoption.

### R-WIL-031 — Small-estate procedures
**Rule (write this):** Personal property may be collected by small-estate affidavit without probate if the estate, net of excluded property, does not exceed $208,850 for deaths on or after April 1, 2025, and a primary residence worth up to $750,000 may pass by a simplified court petition.

**Elements:** 1. Death on or after Apr 1, 2025. 2. Estate value at or under $208,850 (affidavit) or residence at or under $750,000 (§ 13151 petition). 3. Waiting period and procedure.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Property passing to a surviving spouse uses the spousal property petition (§§ 13500, 13650).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 13100, 13151 [S-128].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; low essay yield, possible PT fact.

**Example:** D dies in 2026 with a $150,000 bank account and no real property. Her heir collects it by affidavit after the statutory waiting period.

## III Validity of wills

### R-WIL-032 — Who may make a will
**Rule (write this):** Any individual who is 18 years of age or older and of sound mind may make a will; a conservator may make a will for a conservatee only if authorized by a court order under Probate Code section 2580.

**Elements:** 1. Age 18+. 2. Sound mind (§ 6100.5). 3. Conservator → court order.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A competent conservatee may revoke or replace a conservator-made will.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6100 [S-642].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A 17-year-old's handwritten will is invalid even if otherwise perfect.

### R-WIL-033 — Testamentary capacity
**Rule (write this):** A testator lacks capacity if, at the time of making the will, he or she cannot understand the nature of the testamentary act, understand and recollect the nature and situation of his or her property, or remember and understand his or her relations to living descendants, spouse and parents and to those whose interests are affected by the will. Capacity is measured at execution, and a will made in a lucid interval is valid.

**Elements:** 1. Nature of the act. 2. Nature and situation of property. 3. Natural objects of bounty. 4. At the time of execution.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Lucid interval; old age, illness or eccentricity alone are not incapacity; contestant bears the burden (§ 8252).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6100.5(a)(1).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; capacity is a lower standard than contractual capacity [common law].

**Example:** T, with early dementia, signs on a "good day," describes his home and stocks, and names his three children. He has capacity.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC test turns on the testator's legal capacity status (民事行为能力); California uses a functional three-part test at the moment of signing (approx.).

### R-WIL-034 — Insane delusion
**Rule (write this):** A testator also lacks capacity if he or she suffers from a mental health disorder with symptoms including delusions or hallucinations, and those delusions or hallucinations cause the testator to dispose of property in a way he or she would not otherwise have done; only the gifts caused by the delusion fail.

**Elements:** 1. Mental health disorder. 2. Delusion or hallucination (false belief persisting against all evidence). 3. Causation: the delusion produced the disposition.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A mistaken belief with some factual basis is not a delusion; no causation → will stands.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6100.5(a)(2).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T, hallucinating that his daughter poisons his food, disinherits her. The gift away from her fails if the delusion caused it.

### R-WIL-035 — Burden of proof in will contests
**Rule (write this):** In a will contest, the proponent has the burden of proving due execution, and the contestant has the burden of proving lack of testamentary intent or capacity, undue influence, fraud, duress, menace, mistake or revocation; presumptions such as the undue-influence presumption, § 6112 and § 21380 can shift that burden.

**Elements:** 1. Proponent: due execution. 2. Contestant: invalidating grounds. 3. Burden-shifting presumptions.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Statutory presumptions (§§ 6112(c), 21380).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 8252 [VERIFY V-525].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Heirs claiming T was senile must prove incapacity; the proponent only proves the will was properly signed and witnessed.

### R-WIL-036 — Conservatees and conservators
**Rule (write this):** A conservatorship does not take away the conservatee's right to make a will; capacity is judged under § 6100.5 at execution. A conservator may make or change a will for the conservatee only under a court order of substituted judgment, signs it for the conservatee, and a competent conservatee may later revoke it.

**Elements:** 1. Conservatee's own will: § 6100.5 at execution. 2. Conservator's will: § 2580 order + § 6110(b)(3) signature. 3. Conservatee may revoke.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Court-approved instruments are exempt from the § 21380 presumption (§ 21382).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 1871(c) [S-641], 6100 [S-642], 2580, 6110(b)(3).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2015 Q6, Feb 2019 Q1 patterns.

**Example:** T is under conservatorship but, during a stable period, signs a holographic codicil. The codicil is valid if she met § 6100.5 that day.

### R-WIL-037 — Property that may be disposed of by will
**Rule (write this):** A will may dispose of the testator's separate property, one-half of the community property and one-half of the testator's quasi-community property, and it passes all such property owned at death, including property acquired after the will was executed.

**Elements:** 1. SP. 2. Half CP. 3. Half own QCP. 4. After-acquired property included.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Nonprobate property passes by its own terms; powers of appointment (§§ 641–642).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 6101, 21105.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T's 2015 will leaves "all my property" to his niece; T buys a cabin in 2020. The cabin (SP) passes to the niece.

### R-WIL-038 — Effect of duress, menace, fraud or undue influence
**Rule (write this):** The execution or revocation of a will or any part of a will is ineffective to the extent it was procured by duress, menace, fraud or undue influence; the untainted provisions remain valid if they can stand alone.

**Elements:** 1. Wrongful procurement. 2. Causation. 3. Only the procured parts fail.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Severability; contestant's burden of proof.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6104.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Nurse N unduly influences T to add a $200,000 gift to N. Only N's gift fails; the rest of the will stands.

### R-WIL-039 — Undue influence defined
**Rule (write this):** Undue influence is pressure brought to bear directly on the testamentary act that overcomes the testator's free will and substitutes the influencer's wishes; under Probate Code section 86 it also means excessive persuasion that overcomes free will and results in inequity, judged by the victim's vulnerability, the influencer's apparent authority, the tactics used and the equity of the result.

**Elements:** 1. Susceptible testator. 2. Opportunity. 3. Disposition to influence (tactics). 4. Result reflecting the influence (inequity).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Persuasion, affection, kindness or requests are not undue influence.

**Authority:** *Rice v. Clark* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 89, 96 [S-616]; Prob. Code § 86 [S-630]; Welf. & Inst. Code § 15610.70 [S-631].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A daughter isolates her frail father, controls his phone and mail, and drives him to her own lawyer. Those tactics plus his vulnerability support undue influence.

### R-WIL-040 — Common-law presumption of undue influence
**Rule (write this):** A presumption of undue influence arises when the contestant shows a confidential relationship between the testator and a beneficiary, the beneficiary's active participation in procuring the will, and an undue benefit to that beneficiary; the presumption shifts the burden of proof to the proponent to show the will was not the product of undue influence.

**Elements:** 1. Confidential relationship. 2. Active participation in procurement. 3. Undue benefit. 4. Burden shifts.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Proponent rebuts with evidence of independent advice, the testator's long-held intent, natural objects of bounty.

**Authority:** *Rice v. Clark* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 89, 96–97 [S-616].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2019 Q1 pattern.

**Example:** T's financial adviser selects the lawyer, attends the meeting and receives half the estate, cutting out T's children. The presumption arises.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC law voids a will made under coercion or fraud (受胁迫、欺骗); California adds a judge-made burden-shifting presumption for confidential relationships (approx.).

### R-WIL-041 — Fraud
**Rule (write this):** A will or provision is invalid to the extent it was procured by fraud: fraud in the execution, where the testator is deceived about the nature or contents of the document, or fraud in the inducement, where a person knowingly makes a false statement intending to influence the disposition and the testator relies on it. Fraud that prevents a will or revocation is remedied by a constructive trust.

**Elements:** 1. False representation. 2. Knowledge of falsity. 3. Intent to influence the will. 4. Testator's reliance (but-for causation).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Innocent misstatements are not fraud (but see mistake).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6104; common law; *Beckwith v. Dahl* (2012) 205 Cal.App.4th 1039 (tort alternative) [VERIFY V-529].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Son tells his mother his sister has died, so the mother leaves everything to him. The sister may prove fraud in the inducement.

### R-WIL-042 — Duress and menace
**Rule (write this):** A will or provision procured by duress, meaning physical force or threats that coerce the testamentary act, or by menace, meaning a threat of injury to the person, property or character of the testator or a family member, is invalid to the extent so procured.

**Elements:** 1. Threat or force. 2. Coerced the testamentary act. 3. Causation.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Lawful threats to do what one has a right to do may not qualify [common law].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6104; compare Civ. Code §§ 1569–1570.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A nephew threatens to stop T's medication unless T signs a new will. The will is invalid.

### R-WIL-043 — Testamentary intent and conditional wills
**Rule (write this):** A document is a will only if the testator intended that document itself to make a revocable disposition effective at death; a will made conditional by its own terms is given effect according to whether the condition occurs, but courts read ambiguous language as stating the motive for the will rather than a condition.

**Elements:** 1. Present testamentary intent. 2. Condition in the will's terms? 3. Motive vs condition. 4. Occurrence of condition.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Extrinsic evidence admissible on intent (§ 6111.5).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 6105 [S-613], 6111.5; common law [VERIFY V-540].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** "Since I am going into surgery, I leave everything to my sister." T survives surgery and dies years later. Read as motive; the will is effective.

### R-WIL-044 — Intent controls; extrinsic evidence and ambiguity
**Rule (write this):** The testator's intention as expressed in the will controls, and extrinsic evidence is admissible to determine whether the will's language is reasonably susceptible to more than one meaning and, if so, to resolve the ambiguity, whether the ambiguity appears on the face of the will or arises when the will is applied to the facts.

**Elements:** 1. Plain meaning. 2. Provisional review of extrinsic evidence. 3. Reasonably susceptible? 4. Resolve with evidence.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Extrinsic evidence cannot add a gift the words cannot bear (but see reformation, R-WIL-046).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21102, 6111.5; *Estate of Russell* (1968) 69 Cal.2d 200.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2025 Q2 (coin album and medals).

**Example:** "My coin collection to Frank." Evidence that T kept military medals in the coin album and called them part of his "collection" is admissible; the court decides whether the medals pass.

### R-WIL-045 — Constructional preferences
**Rule (write this):** Every expression in a will is given some effect, a construction that avoids intestacy or failure of a gift is preferred, all parts are read together as a consistent whole, and words take their ordinary meaning unless another meaning is clearly intended.

**Elements:** 1. Give effect to every word. 2. Avoid intestacy. 3. Read as a whole. 4. Ordinary meaning.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Technical words need not take technical meaning in a lay-drafted will.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21120, 21121, 21122.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A holographic will says "my estate to my nephews" and later "my house to Ann." Read together, Ann takes the house and the nephews the rest.

### R-WIL-046 — Mistake and reformation
**Rule (write this):** An unambiguous will may be reformed to conform to the testator's intent if clear and convincing evidence establishes that the will contains a mistake in the expression of the testator's intent at the time it was drafted and establishes the testator's actual specific intent at that time.

**Elements:** 1. Mistake in expression (drafting error). 2. Actual specific intent at drafting. 3. Both by clear and convincing evidence.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Mistake in the inducement (wrong belief about facts) generally gives no relief unless a statute applies (e.g., § 21622).

**Authority:** *Estate of Duke* (2015) 61 Cal.4th 871.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; the UPC § 2-805 and Restatement (Third) of Property take the same approach.

**Example:** The lawyer typed "my nephew John" when T's notes and instructions show "my niece Joan." Clear and convincing evidence allows reformation.

## IV Execution of wills

### R-WIL-047 — Attested will formalities
**Rule (write this):** An attested will must be in writing, signed by the testator (or in the testator's name by another in the testator's presence and at the testator's direction, or by a conservator under court order), and signed during the testator's lifetime by at least two witnesses who, being present at the same time, witnessed the testator's signing or acknowledgment of the signature or will, and who understand that the instrument is the testator's will.

**Elements:** 1. Writing. 2. Testator's signature (or proxy/conservator). 3. Two witnesses. 4. Present at the same time for signing or acknowledgment. 5. Witnesses understand it is a will. 6. Witnesses sign during testator's life.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Harmless error for witnessing defects (§ 6110(c)(2)); holographic alternative (§ 6111); foreign execution (§ 6113).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6110.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; California does not require witnesses to sign in the testator's presence or publication.

**Example:** T signs in front of A and B together, tells them it is her will, and they sign in the next room. Valid.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: like the PRC attested forms (代书遗嘱/打印遗嘱) requiring two witnesses, but California needs the two witnesses present at the same time and nothing more (approx.).

### R-WIL-048 — Harmless error for witnessing defects
**Rule (write this):** A will not executed in compliance with the witnessing requirements is treated as properly executed if the proponent establishes by clear and convincing evidence that, at the time the testator signed the will, the testator intended it to be his or her will. The doctrine cures only witnessing defects, not the requirements of a writing and the testator's signature.

**Elements:** 1. Writing signed by testator. 2. Witnessing defect. 3. Clear and convincing evidence. 4. Intent at signing that it be the will.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No cure for an unsigned document.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6110(c)(2); *Estate of Stoker* (2011) [S-620] [VERIFY V-507].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; UPC § 2-503 is broader (any formality).

**Example:** T signs a typed will with only one witness, emails her lawyer "my new will is signed," and tells friends it is her will. Likely valid under § 6110(c)(2).

### R-WIL-049 — Witnesses and interested witnesses
**Rule (write this):** Any person generally competent to be a witness may witness a will, and a will is not invalid because an interested witness signed it. But unless two other disinterested witnesses also signed, a gift to a subscribing witness raises a presumption that the witness procured it by duress, menace, fraud or undue influence; if not rebutted, the witness takes only as much as he or she would receive if the will were not established.

**Elements:** 1. Witness is a beneficiary. 2. Fewer than two other disinterested witnesses. 3. Presumption affecting the burden of proof. 4. Unrebutted → capped at intestate share.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Gifts to the witness solely as fiduciary (executor, trustee) are not covered.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6112.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; common-law purge statutes void the gift automatically.

**Example:** T's will gives $100,000 to her son S, who witnessed with one friend. S must rebut the presumption or take only his intestate share.

### R-WIL-050 — Holographic wills
**Rule (write this):** A will that does not comply with the attested-will rules is valid as a holographic will, whether or not witnessed, if the signature and the material provisions are in the testator's handwriting; a statement of testamentary intent may appear in a commercially printed form.

**Elements:** 1. Testator's handwritten signature. 2. Material provisions (who takes what) in testator's handwriting. 3. Testamentary intent (handwritten or printed form).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Typed or printed non-material words are surplusage; witnesses unnecessary.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6111(a), (c); *Estate of Black* (1982) 30 Cal.3d 880.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; many states do not recognize holographs.

**Example:** T fills in a store-bought form "I give my house to my son Ben and everything else to my daughter Ann" in her own hand and signs. Valid holograph.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: like the PRC holographic will (自书遗嘱), handwritten and signed; but California does not require a date for validity (approx.).

### R-WIL-051 — Holographic will dating
**Rule (write this):** A holographic will need not be dated, but if it is undated or incompletely dated it is invalid to the extent it is inconsistent with another will unless its execution is shown to be after that will, and it is invalid if the testator lacked capacity at any time during which it might have been executed.

**Elements:** 1. No date or incomplete date. 2. Inconsistent will? → prove which is later. 3. Period of incapacity in the possible window? → invalid.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Extrinsic evidence of the date (§ 6111.5).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6111(b).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2022 Q5 (undated holograph).

**Example:** An undated holograph leaves all to Bob; a dated 2020 typed will leaves all to Carol. Unless evidence shows the holograph came after 2020, it fails.

### R-WIL-052 — Extrinsic evidence on whether a document is a will
**Rule (write this):** Extrinsic evidence is admissible to determine whether a document constitutes a will under the attested-will or holographic-will statutes, and to determine the meaning of a will or part of a will if its meaning is unclear.

**Elements:** 1. Question of testamentary character or meaning. 2. Admit statements, conduct, drafting history.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Evidence cannot supply missing formalities except via § 6110(c)(2).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6111.5.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A signed handwritten letter says "Sis, you get the house." T's statements that "my letter to Sis is my will" are admissible on testamentary intent.

### R-WIL-053 — Choice of law for execution
**Rule (write this):** A written will is validly executed if its execution complies with the law at the time of execution of the place where it was executed, the law of the place where the testator was domiciled, had a place of abode or was a national at execution or at death, or California law.

**Elements:** 1. Identify each candidate law. 2. Test execution under any one. 3. Valid if any is satisfied.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Applies to execution only; substantive validity and construction follow other rules (CP/QCP characterization; § 21103 designation).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6113.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2019 Q1 and Jul 2021 Q5 patterns.

**Example:** T signs a will with one witness in State X, where one witness suffices, then moves to California. The will is valid in California.

### R-WIL-054 — California statutory will
**Rule (write this):** The Probate Code's fill-in California Statutory Will is valid if completed and executed with two witnesses as the statute requires; a defectively executed statutory will may still be valid as an ordinary attested will, under harmless error, or as a holograph if its handwritten parts suffice.

**Elements:** 1. Statutory form. 2. Testator's signature. 3. Two witnesses signing in the testator's presence (§ 6221). 4. Fallbacks: §§ 6110, 6110(c)(2), 6111.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Notarization does not replace witnesses.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 6200–6248 (form at § 6240; § 6221) [S-634] [VERIFY V-513].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2021 Q5 (self-made form will).

**Example:** T fills in the statutory form by hand and signs with no witnesses. It fails as a statutory or attested will, but the handwritten names and gifts plus her signature may make it a holograph.

### R-WIL-055 — Handwritten changes to a typed will
**Rule (write this):** Handwritten changes added to an executed typed will are not part of the attested will; they take effect only if they qualify on their own as a holographic codicil, with the material provisions and a signature in the testator's handwriting. A crossing-out may still be a partial revocation by physical act, subject to dependent relative revocation.

**Elements:** 1. Change made after execution. 2. Holographic codicil? (material provisions + signature). 3. If not, partial revocation by act? 4. DRR.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Changes by someone other than the testator have no effect.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 6111, 6120(b); common law [VERIFY V-532].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2013 Q5, Feb 2025 Q2.

**Example:** T writes "and $5,000 to my nurse Joy — T.R." in the margin of his typed will. If "T.R." was intended as his signature, it is a valid holographic codicil.

## V Component parts of will

### R-WIL-056 — Integration
**Rule (write this):** All papers present at the time of execution that the testator intended to be part of the will are integrated into it, as shown by physical connection or by the internal coherence of the text.

**Elements:** 1. Pages present at execution. 2. Intended as part of the will. 3. Proof by physical connection or internal sense.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A page substituted after execution is not integrated.

**Authority:** Common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A three-page will is stapled and the sentences run across pages. All three pages are integrated even though only page 3 bears signatures.

### R-WIL-057 — Codicils and republication by codicil
**Rule (write this):** A codicil is a will that must be executed with attested or holographic formalities; a valid codicil republishes the will it amends, so the will is treated as executed on the codicil's date, which can defeat an omitted-spouse or omitted-child claim and can validate a defectively executed earlier will that the codicil identifies.

**Elements:** 1. Codicil validly executed. 2. Refers to the will. 3. Will deemed re-executed on codicil date. 4. Consequences: omitted heirs, incorporation, validation.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Republication not applied where it would defeat the testator's intent; revoking a codicil does not revoke the will.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 88; common law [VERIFY V-519].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2018 Q5 (2001 will, 2006 codicil).

**Example:** T's 2010 will omits W; T marries W in 2015 and signs a codicil in 2017 changing the executor. W is not an omitted spouse because the will was republished after the marriage.

### R-WIL-058 — Incorporation by reference
**Rule (write this):** A writing in existence when the will is executed may be incorporated by reference if the will's language shows the intent to incorporate it and describes it sufficiently to permit its identification.

**Elements:** 1. Writing existed at execution (or at a republishing codicil). 2. Will shows intent to incorporate. 3. Sufficient description.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Writings made later fail unless § 6132 applies or a codicil republishes.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6130.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T's will says "I give my jewelry as listed in my letter to my sister dated May 1, 2024." The letter, already written, is incorporated.

### R-WIL-059 — Writing disposing of tangible personal property
**Rule (write this):** A will may refer to a separate writing that disposes of tangible personal property not otherwise specifically given, if the will is unrevoked, the writing is dated and is in the testator's handwriting or signed by the testator, and it describes items and recipients with reasonable certainty; the writing may be made before or after the will, but the total value may not exceed $25,000 and no item may exceed $5,000.

**Elements:** 1. Unrevoked will refers to the writing. 2. Dated; handwritten or signed. 3. Reasonable certainty. 4. Tangible personal property only (not money or business property). 5. Value caps.

**Exceptions / defenses:** If writings conflict, the most recent controls.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6132 [S-601] [VERIFY V-500].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; the UPC § 2-513 analogue has no dollar caps.

**Example:** T's will refers to "my list." After signing the will T writes and signs a dated list leaving her $3,000 piano to her niece. Valid.

### R-WIL-060 — Acts of independent significance
**Rule (write this):** A will may dispose of property by reference to acts and events that have significance apart from their effect on the will, whether they occur before or after execution or before or after death, including the execution or revocation of another person's will.

**Elements:** 1. Reference to an act or event. 2. Independent, non-testamentary significance. 3. Timing irrelevant.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A list or note whose only purpose is to dispose of property lacks independent significance.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6131.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2018 Q5 (gift to employees).

**Example:** "$5,000 to each person employed by my company at my death." Employment decisions have business significance, so the gift is valid.

### R-WIL-061 — Pour-over devise to a trust
**Rule (write this):** A will may devise property to the trustee of a trust identified in the will whose terms are set out in a written instrument executed before, concurrently with, or within 60 days after the will, regardless of whether the trust is funded, amendable or revocable; the property is administered under the trust as amended before or after death, and revocation of the trust before the testator's death causes the devise to lapse unless the will provides otherwise.

**Elements:** 1. Trust identified in the will. 2. Written trust instrument timed as required. 3. Devise valid even if unfunded or revocable. 4. Amendments apply. 5. Revoked trust → lapse.

**Exceptions / defenses:** If § 6300 fails, incorporation by reference may still save the terms.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6300 (as amended by AB 309, eff. 2018) [S-611] [S-612].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Wills + Trusts crossovers.

**Example:** T's will leaves the residue "to the trustee of the T Family Trust dated today." T amends the trust in 2025. The residue is administered under the amended trust.

## V.2 Changes occurring after execution

### R-WIL-062 — After-acquired property; survival and lapse
**Rule (write this):** A will passes all property the testator owns at death, including property acquired after execution, and a beneficiary who fails to survive the testator, or a future time the will requires, does not take; at common law the gift lapses, and a gift to a person already dead at execution is void.

**Elements:** 1. Will speaks at death. 2. Beneficiary survived? (clear and convincing). 3. If not, gift fails unless anti-lapse applies.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Anti-lapse (R-WIL-063); alternative gifts.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21105, 21109 [S-622].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T's will leaves $10,000 to her friend Fay, who dies first. The gift lapses and falls into the residue.

### R-WIL-063 — Anti-lapse
**Rule (write this):** If a beneficiary who is kindred of the testator, or kindred of the testator's surviving, deceased or former spouse, is dead when the will is executed or fails to survive the testator, the beneficiary's issue take in his or her place by representation, unless the will expresses a contrary intention or a substitute disposition. A class member is covered unless the member died before execution and the testator knew it.

**Elements:** 1. Gift fails for death. 2. Beneficiary is kindred of testator or of a spouse/former spouse. 3. Beneficiary left issue. 4. No contrary intent or substitute gift. 5. Issue take by § 240.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Spouses and friends are not kindred; survivorship conditions defeat anti-lapse.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21110.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; UPC § 2-603 differs on survivorship words.

**Example:** T leaves $20,000 to her brother B, who predeceases leaving a daughter D. D takes the $20,000.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC subrogation (代位继承) lets a predeceased child's descendants take the child's statutory share; California's § 21110 also protects will beneficiaries who are blood relatives (approx.).

### R-WIL-064 — Contrary intent defeats anti-lapse
**Rule (write this):** The issue of a deceased beneficiary do not take if the will expresses a contrary intention or a substitute disposition, and a requirement that the beneficiary survive the testator, survive for a stated period, or survive to a time related to probate or administration is a contrary intention.

**Elements:** 1. Survivorship condition or alternative gift. 2. Anti-lapse displaced. 3. § 21111 governs the failed gift.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Ambiguous language construed by intent (§ 21102).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21110(b).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; the UPC treats survivorship words alone as insufficient.

**Example:** "$20,000 to my brother B if he survives me." B predeceases leaving a daughter. The gift fails; the daughter takes nothing under anti-lapse.

### R-WIL-065 — Failed gifts
**Rule (write this):** If a gift fails and anti-lapse does not apply, the property passes under any alternative disposition in the will; otherwise a non-residuary gift falls into the residue, and a failed residuary gift, or any gift where there is no residue, passes by intestacy. If a residuary gift to two or more persons fails as to one, that share passes to the other residuary takers in proportion to their interests.

**Elements:** 1. Gift fails. 2. Alternative disposition? 3. Residue? 4. Residuary share to co-residuary takers. 5. Otherwise intestacy.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Contrary intent in the instrument.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21111.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; rejects the common-law "no residue of a residue" rule.

**Example:** Residue "to my friends A and B equally." A predeceases (not kindred). B takes the whole residue.

### R-WIL-066 — Class gifts
**Rule (write this):** A gift to a group described collectively, whose membership can change, is a class gift; members who survive the testator share the gift, a predeceased member's share passes to his or her issue if anti-lapse applies, and at common law the class closes when any member is entitled to distribution.

**Elements:** 1. Group description and intent to benefit the group. 2. Identify members at distribution. 3. Anti-lapse for predeceased members (unless death before execution known). 4. Class closing.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A gift to named individuals ("to my nieces Amy and Beth") is not a class gift.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21110(a); common law.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** "$30,000 to my nephews." Of three nephews, one predeceases leaving a son. Anti-lapse gives the son his father's one-third share.

### R-WIL-067 — Class-gift terms: adoptees, half-bloods and stepchildren
**Rule (write this):** Half-bloods, adopted persons, persons born out of wedlock, stepchildren, foster children and their issue are included in class-gift and relationship terms according to the intestacy rules; but in a transfer by someone other than the adoptive parent, a person adopted by the adoptive parent counts as that parent's child only if he or she lived while a minor as a regular member of the adopting parent's household.

**Elements:** 1. Class term ("children", "grandchildren", "issue"). 2. Apply intestacy definitions (§§ 6450–6455). 3. Third-party transferor → minor-household test for adoptees.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Contrary intent in the instrument.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21115 [S-626].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Grandmother leaves money "to my grandchildren." Her son adopted his 40-year-old business partner. The adoptee is not a grandchild for this gift.

### R-WIL-068 — Classification of gifts
**Rule (write this):** A specific gift is a transfer of specifically identifiable property; a general gift is a transfer from the general assets; a demonstrative gift is a general gift payable primarily from a specified fund or property; and a residuary gift is whatever remains after specific and general gifts are satisfied. Courts prefer to classify a doubtful gift as general or demonstrative to avoid ademption.

**Elements:** 1. Specific. 2. General (incl. general pecuniary; annuity). 3. Demonstrative. 4. Residuary.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Classification follows intent; preference against ademption.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21117; *Estate of Worthy* (1988) 205 Cal.App.3d 760 [S-644].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** "$10,000 from my Chase account to Ann" is demonstrative; if the account is closed, Ann is paid from general assets.

### R-WIL-069 — Ademption by extinction: California approach
**Rule (write this):** A specific gift is adeemed if the specific property is not in the estate at death, but California does not favor ademption: a change in the form of the property does not adeem the gift absent proof the testator intended it to fail, statutory exceptions preserve the beneficiary's rights, and only where the testator disposed of the property and it cannot be traced is the gift adeemed regardless of intent.

**Elements:** 1. Specific gift. 2. Property missing at death. 3. Traceable change of form? 4. Statutory exceptions (§§ 21132–21134). 5. Intent.

**Exceptions / defenses:** General and demonstrative gifts do not adeem.

**Authority:** *Estate of Mason* (1965) 62 Cal.2d 213 [S-615]; Prob. Code § 21139 [S-614] [VERIFY V-534].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; majority identity theory adeems whenever the item is gone.

**Example:** T leaves "my Main Street house" to Ann, sells it, and deposits the proceeds in a separate account still identifiable at death. Ann can argue no ademption under *Mason* (traceable change in form).

### R-WIL-070 — Statutory nonademption: what follows a specific gift
**Rule (write this):** The recipient of a specific gift takes the property to the extent it is in the estate at death plus any unpaid balance of the purchase price owed from the testator's sale of it, any unpaid condemnation award, any unpaid fire or casualty insurance proceeds or other recovery for injury to it, and property obtained by foreclosure on a specifically given secured obligation.

**Elements:** 1. Specific gift. 2. Item sold, condemned, damaged or foreclosed. 3. Amount unpaid at death passes to the beneficiary.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Amounts already paid to the testator before death are not covered (unless traceable under *Mason* or § 21134).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21133 [S-629].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T leaves her house to Ben, then sells it on a promissory note; $200,000 is still owed at death. Ben takes the $200,000 balance.

### R-WIL-071 — Change in form of securities
**Rule (write this):** A specific gift of securities includes additional securities of the same entity owned at death because of action initiated by the entity, such as stock splits, stock dividends, mergers and reorganizations, but not securities the testator acquired by exercising purchase rights or options.

**Elements:** 1. Specific gift of securities. 2. Change initiated by issuer. 3. Beneficiary takes resulting securities.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Testator-initiated purchases are excluded.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21132 [VERIFY V-512].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T leaves "my 100 shares of Acme" to Carl. Acme splits 2-for-1. Carl takes 200 shares.

### R-WIL-072 — Ademption by satisfaction
**Rule (write this):** A lifetime gift to a beneficiary satisfies a gift under the will, in whole or part, only if the will provides for deduction, the testator declared so in a contemporaneous writing, the beneficiary acknowledged it in writing, or the lifetime gift was the same property specifically given; the gift is valued at the earlier of the beneficiary's possession and the testator's death unless the writing fixes a value.

**Elements:** 1. Lifetime gift to a beneficiary. 2. One of four conditions. 3. Valuation. 4. Deduct.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No writing → no satisfaction (except same specific property).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21135 [S-637]; *Sachs v. Sachs* (2020) [S-638].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; parallels § 6409 advancements.

**Example:** T's will gives Ann $50,000. T later gives Ann $20,000 with a signed note "toward your inheritance." Ann takes $30,000 at death.

### R-WIL-073 — Nonexoneration of specific gifts
**Rule (write this):** A specific gift passes subject to any mortgage, deed of trust or other lien existing at death, without a right to have the estate pay it off, regardless of a general directive in the will to pay debts.

**Elements:** 1. Specific gift of encumbered property. 2. Beneficiary takes subject to lien. 3. General "pay my debts" clause insufficient.

**Exceptions / defenses:** An express exoneration direction (then § 21404 governs).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21131 [S-629].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; the old common-law rule favored exoneration.

**Example:** T leaves her condo (with a $300,000 mortgage) to Dan and says "pay my just debts." Dan takes the condo subject to the mortgage.

### R-WIL-074 — Sale or encumbrance by conservator, agent or trustee
**Rule (write this):** If specifically given property is sold after the will is executed by the testator's conservator, by an agent under a durable power of attorney for an incapacitated principal, or by a trustee for an incapacitated settlor, the beneficiary receives a general pecuniary gift equal to the net sale price, unreduced by the payoff of any encumbrance the fiduciary placed on the property; if the fiduciary encumbers it, the beneficiary takes the property plus a pecuniary gift equal to the unpaid encumbrance. The rule does not apply if the incapacity ends and the testator survives that by a year.

**Elements:** 1. Specific gift. 2. Sale, encumbrance, condemnation or casualty while incapacitated. 3. By conservator, agent or trustee. 4. General pecuniary gift equal to net price or proceeds. 5. Recovery-plus-one-year exception.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Conservatorship terminated and testator survived one year [VERIFY V-502]; reduced by any § 21133 right.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21134 [S-602] [S-603]; *Estate of Mason* (1965) 62 Cal.2d 213 [S-615].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2015 Q6 (conservatorship).

**Example:** T's will gives her house to Greg. Her conservator sells it for a net $500,000 to pay for her care and spends the money. Greg takes a $500,000 general pecuniary gift (which abates as a general gift if the estate is short).

### R-WIL-075 — Order of abatement
**Rule (write this):** When the estate is insufficient to pay all debts, expenses and gifts, shares abate in this order unless the will or the testator's plan requires otherwise: property not disposed of by the will, residuary gifts, general gifts to non-relatives, general gifts to relatives, specific gifts to non-relatives, and specific gifts to relatives; gifts within a class abate pro rata.

**Elements:** 1. Shortfall. 2. Classify each gift. 3. Relative? (would take in intestacy). 4. Abate by the six-step order. 5. Pro rata within class.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Contrary intent or plan (§§ 21400–21401).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21402 [S-604], 21403.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; common law did not prefer relatives.

**Example:** After debts $60,000 remains for general gifts of $50,000 to niece N and $30,000 to a charity. The charity abates first and takes $10,000; N takes $50,000.

### R-WIL-076 — Abatement: demonstrative gifts and contrary intent
**Rule (write this):** A demonstrative gift abates as a specific gift to the extent the designated fund is available and as a general gift for any balance, abatement applies without preference between real and personal property, and the statutory order yields where the will provides otherwise or where it would defeat the testator's plan or the purpose of a gift.

**Elements:** 1. Demonstrative → split treatment. 2. No real/personal priority. 3. Intent or plan override.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Omitted spouse and child shares are funded under §§ 21612, 21623.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21400–21403 [VERIFY V-503] [VERIFY V-504].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** "$20,000 to Ann from my credit-union account," which holds $15,000. Ann's gift is specific as to $15,000 and general as to $5,000.

## VI Revocation and revival of wills

### R-WIL-077 — Revocation by subsequent will
**Rule (write this):** A will or any part of it is revoked by a later valid will that revokes it expressly or by inconsistency; without an express clause, the later will revokes the earlier one only to the extent of the inconsistency, unless it disposes of the entire estate.

**Elements:** 1. Later instrument is a valid will (attested or holographic). 2. Express revocation, or 3. Inconsistency (partial or total).

**Exceptions / defenses:** Later instrument procured by fraud or undue influence (§ 6104) does not revoke.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6120(a).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T's 2018 typed will leaves her car to A; a 2023 valid holograph leaves her car to B and says nothing else. The car goes to B; the rest of the 2018 will stands.

### R-WIL-078 — Revocation by physical act
**Rule (write this):** A will or any part of it is revoked by being burned, torn, canceled, obliterated or destroyed, with the intent and for the purpose of revoking it, by the testator or by another person in the testator's presence and at the testator's direction.

**Elements:** 1. Revocatory act on the will. 2. Intent to revoke at the time of the act. 3. By testator, or by another in testator's presence and at direction.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Accidental destruction; destruction by another outside the testator's presence; act procured by fraud or undue influence (§ 6104).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6120(b).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T phones her lawyer and says "tear up my will"; the lawyer does so at his office. Not a revocation (not in T's presence); the will can be proved by a copy.

### R-WIL-079 — Partial revocation and interlineations
**Rule (write this):** California permits revocation of part of a will by a physical act, such as striking a gift with intent to revoke it, but a new gift written in after execution is effective only if it is separately executed as a will or qualifies as a holographic codicil.

**Elements:** 1. Act on part of the will. 2. Intent to revoke that part. 3. Added words: separate execution or holograph. 4. Consider DRR.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Alterations by a beneficiary are ineffective.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6120(b); *Estate of Uhl* (1969) 1 Cal.App.3d 138 [S-640].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2025 Q2.

**Example:** T draws a line through "$5,000 to my cousin" and initials it. The cousin's gift is revoked and the $5,000 falls into the residue.

### R-WIL-080 — Duplicate originals
**Rule (write this):** If a will was executed in duplicate, a revocatory act performed on one duplicate with intent to revoke revokes the will.

**Elements:** 1. Duplicate originals. 2. Act on one with intent. 3. Whole will revoked.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Act without intent.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6121.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T keeps one signed original and her lawyer the other. T burns hers intending to revoke. The lawyer's copy cannot be probated.

### R-WIL-081 — Lost or missing will presumption
**Rule (write this):** If a will was last in the testator's possession, the testator was competent until death, and neither the will nor a duplicate original can be found after death, it is presumed that the testator destroyed it with intent to revoke; the presumption affects only the burden of producing evidence and can be overcome by evidence that the testator did not destroy it, after which the will may be proved by a copy.

**Elements:** 1. Last in testator's possession. 2. Competent until death. 3. Original and duplicate not found. 4. Presumption of revocation (burden of producing evidence). 5. Rebuttal; proof by copy.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Access by hostile heirs; recent statements affirming the will.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6124 [S-600]; *Estate of Trikha* (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 791 [S-643]; mutilated wills: common law [VERIFY V-501].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T's will, kept in her desk, is missing at death, but her son (cut out of the will) had her keys. Evidence of his access rebuts the presumption.

### R-WIL-082 — Revocation by dissolution or annulment
**Rule (write this):** Unless the will expressly provides otherwise, dissolution or annulment of the testator's marriage after the will is executed revokes every gift and power of appointment to the former spouse and any nomination of the former spouse as executor, trustee, conservator or guardian; the will is read as if the former spouse predeceased, and remarriage to the former spouse revives those provisions. Legal separation does not revoke.

**Elements:** 1. Will executed before dissolution. 2. Final dissolution or annulment. 3. Gifts/appointments to former spouse revoked. 4. Treat as predeceased. 5. Revival on remarriage.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Express contrary provision; legal separation; gifts to the former spouse's relatives are not revoked.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6122 (§ 6122.1 for domestic partnerships).

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; UPC § 2-804 also reaches the former spouse's relatives.

**Example:** H's will leaves everything to W, alternate to his sister. H and W divorce; H dies without changing the will. The sister takes.

### R-WIL-083 — Nonprobate transfers to a former spouse
**Rule (write this):** A nonprobate transfer to a former spouse in an instrument executed before or during the marriage fails if, at death, the former spouse is not the surviving spouse because of dissolution or annulment, unless the transfer was irrevocable, clear and convincing evidence shows the transferor intended to preserve it, or a court order required it; the instrument is read as if the former spouse predeceased.

**Elements:** 1. Nonprobate transfer (POD, TOD, trust, beneficiary designation). 2. Executed before or during marriage. 3. Dissolution or annulment. 4. No exception. 5. Former spouse treated as predeceased.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Irrevocable transfer; clear and convincing intent to preserve; court order; ERISA preemption.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 5040 [S-605]; § 5042 (joint tenancy) [VERIFY V-516].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's POD account names W; they divorce and H dies. The account passes as if W predeceased, to the contingent payee or H's estate.

### R-WIL-084 — Revival
**Rule (write this):** If a later will that revoked an earlier will is itself revoked by physical act, the earlier will stays revoked unless the circumstances of the revocation or the testator's contemporaneous or later declarations show the testator intended the earlier will to take effect; if the later will is revoked by a third will, the earlier will is revived only to the extent the terms of the third will show that intent.

**Elements:** 1. Will 1 revoked by will 2. 2. Will 2 revoked. 3. Method: physical act (circumstances and declarations) or third will (its terms only). 4. Intent to revive.

**Exceptions / defenses:** If no revival, consider DRR.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 6123.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; differs from the old English automatic-revival rule.

**Example:** T's 2020 will revoked her 2015 will. In 2024 T tears up the 2020 will, saying "now my 2015 will is back in force." The 2015 will is revived.

### R-WIL-085 — Dependent relative revocation
**Rule (write this):** If a testator revokes a will or gift on the mistaken assumption that another disposition will be effective, and would not have revoked it but for that mistake, the revocation is disregarded and the revoked will or gift is given effect, provided that result is closer to the testator's intent than the result without the doctrine.

**Elements:** 1. Revocation. 2. Mistaken belief that a substitute disposition is valid (or about revival or a recited fact). 3. Revocation conditional on that belief. 4. Disregarding it better serves intent.

**Exceptions / defenses:** No DRR if the testator would have revoked regardless.

**Authority:** *Estate of Kaufman* (1945) 25 Cal.2d 854 [S-619]; *Estate of Uhl* (1969) 1 Cal.App.3d 138 [S-640].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T crosses out "$10,000 to Natalie," writes "$20,000" and does not sign. The $20,000 fails, but DRR restores the $10,000 because T plainly preferred Natalie to have something.

## Family protection: omitted spouses and children

### R-WIL-086 — Omitted spouse: share
**Rule (write this):** A surviving spouse who married the decedent after the execution of all the decedent's testamentary instruments and who is not provided for in them receives the decedent's one-half of the community property, the decedent's one-half of the quasi-community property, and a share of the separate property equal to the intestate share, but not more than one-half of the separate property.

**Elements:** 1. Marriage after all testamentary instruments (will, codicils, revocable trust). 2. No provision for the spouse. 3. No § 21611 exception. 4. Share: CP half + QCP half + SP intestate share capped at one-half.

**Exceptions / defenses:** § 21611 exceptions (R-WIL-087).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21610; cross-reference R-CMP-097.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2013 Q5, Feb 2018 Q5, Jul 2021 Q5.

**Example:** H's 2015 will leaves all to his brother; H marries W in 2020 and dies leaving SP of $600,000 and no issue or parents. W takes H's half of the CP and $300,000 of SP.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC law needs no omitted-spouse rule because the spouse is always a first-order heir unless the will excludes her; California presumes the testator forgot a later spouse (approx.).

### R-WIL-087 — Omitted spouse: exceptions
**Rule (write this):** The omitted spouse takes nothing if the failure to provide was intentional and that intent appears in the testamentary instruments, if the decedent provided for the spouse outside the instruments with intent that the transfer be in lieu of a testamentary provision, as shown by statements, the amount of the transfer or other evidence, or if the spouse validly waived the right to share in the estate.

**Elements:** 1. Intent on the face of the instruments. 2. Transfer in lieu outside the instruments. 3. Valid waiver.

**Exceptions / defenses:** A general disinheritance clause that does not mention the spouse or a future marriage is not enough.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21611; *Estate of Shannon* (1990) 224 Cal.App.3d 1148 [S-618].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H's will says "I intentionally make no provision for anyone I may later marry." W, married later, takes no omitted-spouse share (she keeps her own half of the CP).

### R-WIL-088 — Satisfying an omitted spouse's or child's share
**Rule (write this):** An omitted spouse's or child's share is taken first from estate property not disposed of by the decedent's will or trust and, if that is not enough, from all beneficiaries of the testamentary instruments in proportion to the value each receives, measured at death, unless the court orders otherwise to preserve the decedent's plan.

**Elements:** 1. Undisposed property first. 2. Then pro rata from beneficiaries. 3. Values at death. 4. Court adjustment.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Court may vary the order to carry out the decedent's intent [VERIFY V-536].

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21612, 21623.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** The omitted spouse's $300,000 share is taken pro rata from the brother (sole beneficiary) because nothing passed by intestacy.

### R-WIL-089 — Testamentary instruments include revocable trusts
**Rule (write this):** For the omitted spouse and omitted child statutes, the decedent's testamentary instruments include both the will and any revocable trust, and the estate includes both the probate estate and property in a revocable trust that becomes irrevocable at death.

**Elements:** 1. Will and revocable trust both count. 2. Marriage or birth must follow all of them. 3. Share computed on probate plus trust property.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Nonprobate assets passing by beneficiary designation (e.g., IRAs) are not included [VERIFY V-514].

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21601; *Reich v. Reich* (2024) [S-623].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2012 Q1 (child omitted from a trust).

**Example:** T's 2010 trust and pour-over will omit his 2015 spouse. Her share is computed on probate and trust assets together.

### R-WIL-090 — Omitted child: share
**Rule (write this):** A child born or adopted after the execution of all the decedent's testamentary instruments and not provided for in them receives a share equal to what the child would have received if the decedent had died without any testamentary instrument.

**Elements:** 1. Born or adopted after all instruments. 2. Not provided for. 3. No § 21621 exception. 4. Intestate share computed on the whole estate.

**Exceptions / defenses:** § 21621 (R-WIL-091); children alive at execution are not omitted (except § 21622).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21620.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Feb 2018 Q5, Jul 2021 Q5.

**Example:** W's will leaves all to her daughter D; son S is born later; W dies unmarried. S takes one-half of the estate (his intestate share).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Civil Code reserves shares for heirs unable to work and for a fetus; California's omitted-child share protects any after-born child regardless of need (approx.).

### R-WIL-091 — Omitted child: exceptions
**Rule (write this):** The omitted child takes nothing if the failure to provide was intentional and appears in the testamentary instruments, if the decedent had one or more children and left substantially all the estate to the omitted child's other parent, or if the decedent provided for the child outside the instruments with intent that the transfer be in lieu of a testamentary provision.

**Elements:** 1. Intent on face. 2. Substantially all to other parent (and decedent had a child at execution). 3. Transfer in lieu.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The "other parent" exception requires that the other parent be the omitted child's parent.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21621.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** H, who has a son, leaves everything to his wife W; their daughter is born later. The daughter takes nothing; H relied on W to provide for her.

### R-WIL-092 — Child believed dead or unknown
**Rule (write this):** If, when executing all testamentary instruments in effect at death, the decedent failed to provide for a living child solely because the decedent believed the child dead or did not know of the child's birth, the child receives an intestate share.

**Elements:** 1. Child alive at execution. 2. Omission solely because of belief in death or ignorance of birth. 3. Intestate share.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Omission for other reasons (estrangement) gives no claim.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21622.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T never knew a former girlfriend gave birth to his son. The son, discovered after T's death, takes an intestate share.

## IX No-contest clauses

### R-WIL-093 — No-contest clauses: when enforced
**Rule (write this):** A no-contest clause is enforced only against a direct contest brought without probable cause, and, if the clause expressly so provides, against a challenge claiming the transferred property was not the transferor's or the filing of a creditor's claim; probable cause exists if the known facts would cause a reasonable person to believe there is a reasonable likelihood the relief will be granted after further investigation or discovery.

**Elements:** 1. Clause in a protected instrument. 2. Pleading is a direct contest (or an expressly covered pleading). 3. No probable cause. 4. Forfeiture.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Strict construction; probable cause defeats enforcement.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21311 [S-608]; *Donkin v. Donkin* (2013) 58 Cal.4th 412 [S-609].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** Son contests his father's will for undue influence with credible witness statements; he loses. Because he had probable cause, the no-contest clause does not forfeit his gift.

### R-WIL-094 — No-contest definitions: direct contest and protected instrument
**Rule (write this):** A direct contest is a pleading alleging the invalidity of a protected instrument or its terms for forgery, lack of due execution, lack of capacity, menace, duress, fraud or undue influence, revocation, or disqualification of a beneficiary; a protected instrument is the instrument containing the clause and any existing instrument the clause expressly identifies.

**Elements:** 1. Pleading. 2. One of the listed grounds. 3. Protected instrument.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Petitions to construe the instrument or challenge a fiduciary's conduct are not direct contests.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21310 [VERIFY V-506].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A beneficiary's petition to interpret an ambiguous gift is not a direct contest and cannot trigger the clause.

## Presumptively disqualified donees (outline III.C.5)

### R-WIL-095 — Presumptively disqualified donees
**Rule (write this):** A donative transfer to the person who drafted the instrument, to a fiduciary who transcribed it or caused it to be transcribed, to a care custodian of a dependent adult when the instrument was executed during or within 90 days of the care, or to certain relatives, cohabitants, employees or law-firm colleagues of those persons is presumed to be the product of fraud or undue influence. The presumption is rebuttable only by clear and convincing evidence and is conclusive for the drafter and the drafter's associates, and an unsuccessful beneficiary pays the costs and attorney's fees.

**Elements:** 1. Donative transfer. 2. Disqualified category. 3. Presumption affects burden of proof. 4. Rebuttal by clear and convincing evidence (none for drafter). 5. Fee shifting.

**Exceptions / defenses:** § 21382 exceptions and § 21384 certificate (R-WIL-096).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21380 [S-645] [VERIFY V-505]; *Rice v. Clark* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 89 [S-616].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; PR crossover (lawyer drafting a gift to himself).

**Example:** Lawyer L drafts a will leaving himself $50,000 from an unrelated client. The gift is conclusively presumed invalid.

### R-WIL-096 — Exceptions and certificate of independent review
**Rule (write this):** The presumption does not apply to transfers to a relative within the fourth degree or the transferor's cohabitant, instruments drafted by such a person, instruments approved by court order, gifts to public entities and qualified charities, small gifts of $5,000 or less from estates at or above the small-estate amount, or instruments executed outside California by a nonresident; nor does it apply if an independent attorney counsels the transferor alone and signs a certificate of independent review.

**Elements:** 1. Relationship or cohabitant. 2. Court-approved instrument. 3. Charity/public entity. 4. $5,000 rule. 5. Out-of-state. 6. Independent-review certificate.

**Exceptions / defenses:** The drafting attorney cannot certify his or her own instrument.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21382 [S-621], 21384 [S-635].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T's will gives $40,000 to her paid caregiver; T first meets alone with an independent lawyer, who signs a § 21384 certificate. No presumption.

### R-WIL-097 — Care custodian and dependent adult
**Rule (write this):** A care custodian is a person who provides health or social services to a dependent adult, but an unpaid helper is excluded if he or she had a personal relationship with the dependent adult at least 90 days before providing services, at least six months before the death, and before any hospice admission; a dependent adult is generally a person 65 or older who could not provide for personal needs or, because of deficits in mental function, had difficulty managing finances or resisting undue influence.

**Elements:** 1. Services to a dependent adult. 2. Paid, or unpaid without a qualifying prior personal relationship. 3. Instrument executed during or within 90 days of services.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Prior personal relationship for unpaid helpers.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21362 [S-633], 21366 [VERIFY V-505]; *Bernard v. Foley* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 794 [S-617].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** A neighbor of 20 years who cared for T without pay in T's last year is not a care custodian.

### R-WIL-098 — Effect of disqualification
**Rule (write this):** If a donative transfer fails under the disqualified-donee statute, the instrument operates as if the beneficiary had predeceased the transferor without spouse, domestic partner or issue, so the gift passes to alternate takers or the residue and anti-lapse cannot save it for the beneficiary's family.

**Elements:** 1. Unrebutted presumption. 2. Beneficiary treated as predeceased without issue. 3. Gift passes under § 21111.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Applies only to the tainted transfer.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 21386 [S-646].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** The caregiver's $40,000 gift fails; it falls into the residue rather than going to the caregiver's children.

## X Will substitutes and nonprobate transfers

### R-WIL-099 — Nonprobate transfers valid without will formalities
**Rule (write this):** A provision for a transfer on death in an insurance policy, account agreement, pension or retirement plan, trust, deed, marital property agreement or similar written instrument is not invalid because it does not comply with the formalities for executing a will.

**Elements:** 1. Written instrument of the listed type. 2. Transfer effective at death. 3. No will formalities needed.

**Exceptions / defenses:** CP limits (§§ 5020–5021); former-spouse rule (§ 5040).

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 5000.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T names her niece as POD payee on a bank account. The niece takes the account outside probate even though the designation was never witnessed.

### R-WIL-100 — Joint tenancy and survivorship property
**Rule (write this):** Property held in joint tenancy passes to the surviving joint tenant by right of survivorship and is not subject to the decedent's will or intestacy, except that a slayer's or former spouse's joint tenancy is severed and CP held in joint-tenancy form may still be community property between spouses.

**Elements:** 1. Valid joint tenancy. 2. Survivor takes outside probate. 3. Severance exceptions.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Slayer (§ 251); former spouse (§ 5042); CP/Brace (R-CMP-014); QCP recapture (§ 102).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 251, 5042 [VERIFY V-516]; common law of joint tenancy.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Jul 2022 Q5 (JT deed of QCP).

**Example:** T and her brother own a cabin as joint tenants; T's will leaves "all my real property" to her son. The brother takes the cabin.

### R-WIL-101 — Multiple-party accounts, POD and Totten trust accounts
**Rule (write this):** During the parties' lives a joint account belongs to them in proportion to their net contributions, and at death the remaining balance belongs to the surviving party, the POD payee or the Totten trust beneficiary, unless clear and convincing evidence shows a different intent.

**Elements:** 1. Type of account. 2. Lifetime ownership by net contributions. 3. Survivorship at death. 4. Clear and convincing evidence of contrary intent.

**Exceptions / defenses:** CP rules for married parties; convenience accounts.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 5301, 5302 [VERIFY V-538].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** "Mom in trust for Ben" savings account: Ben takes the balance at Mom's death, though Mom could withdraw it all during life.

### R-WIL-102 — Revocable trusts as will substitutes
**Rule (write this):** Property in a funded revocable trust passes under the trust's terms at the settlor's death without probate, but the trust is treated like a will for many purposes: the omitted spouse and omitted child statutes, the disqualified-donee presumption, anti-lapse and other Division 11 rules apply to it.

**Elements:** 1. Valid revocable trust. 2. Property funded or poured over. 3. Will-like rules apply.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Trust validity, revocation and modification follow Trusts rules.

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 21101, 21601, 21380; *Haggerty v. Thornton* (2024) 15 Cal.5th 729 [S-129] [VERIFY V-033].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; Wills + Trusts crossovers.

**Example:** T's trust leaves her house to her niece; T marries W after signing the trust. W can claim an omitted-spouse share from trust property.

### R-WIL-103 — Revocable transfer-on-death deed
**Rule (write this):** An owner with testamentary capacity may transfer California real property at death by a revocable transfer-on-death deed that is signed, dated, acknowledged before a notary, signed by two witnesses for deeds executed on or after January 1, 2022, and recorded within 60 days; the deed has no effect during the owner's life and is revocable.

**Elements:** 1. Capacity. 2. Signed, dated, notarized. 3. Two witnesses (post-2022). 4. Recorded within 60 days. 5. Revocable; effective at death.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Former-spouse rule (§ 5040).

**Authority:** Prob. Code §§ 5600 et seq. (§§ 5624, 5626) [S-628] [VERIFY V-520].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; nice-to-know.

**Example:** T records a TOD deed naming her son, then leaves "my house" to her daughter by will. The son takes the house; the will cannot override the recorded deed.

### R-WIL-104 — A will cannot override a nonprobate designation
**Rule (write this):** Property that passes by a nonprobate designation, such as life insurance, a retirement account or a POD account, goes to the named beneficiary under the contract, and a later will cannot change that designation unless the contract permits change by will.

**Elements:** 1. Nonprobate asset. 2. Designation per contract. 3. Will ineffective to change it.

**Exceptions / defenses:** CP half of the surviving spouse (§§ 5020–5021); former-spouse rule; federal preemption for ERISA and federal programs.

**Authority:** Prob. Code § 5000; common law; cross-reference R-CMP-096, R-CMP-101.

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE.

**Example:** T's life policy names her ex-boyfriend; her will says "all my insurance to my sister." The ex-boyfriend takes, subject to any CP claim of a spouse.

### R-WIL-105 — Intentional interference with expected inheritance
**Rule (write this):** A plaintiff who had a reasonable expectation of an inheritance may sue a defendant who, by independently tortious conduct such as fraud or duress, intentionally interfered with it, but only where the plaintiff has no adequate remedy in probate.

**Elements:** 1. Expectancy. 2. Defendant's knowledge. 3. Independently tortious conduct. 4. Reasonable certainty the inheritance would have been received. 5. Damages. 6. No adequate probate remedy.

**Exceptions / defenses:** Available probate remedy (will contest) bars the tort.

**Authority:** *Beckwith v. Dahl* (2012) 205 Cal.App.4th 1039 [VERIFY V-529].

**MBE vs. CA essay:** not on the MBE; nice-to-know.

**Example:** A brother lies to stop their dying mother from signing a new will in his sister's favor. The sister may sue in tort if probate gives her no remedy.
