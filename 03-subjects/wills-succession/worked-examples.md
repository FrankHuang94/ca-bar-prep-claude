# Wills and Succession — worked examples

Each pattern is original. Read the facts, decide the answer, then check the analysis (rule → application → conclusion). Rule IDs point to [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md).

---

### 1. The fill-in form will
**Facts.** Tara buys a printed will form at a stationery store. She writes in her own hand "my house to my niece Nora; everything else to the Red Cross," signs at the bottom, and asks no one to witness it.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* A will is a valid holograph if the signature and material provisions are in the testator's handwriting; testamentary intent may appear in a commercially printed form (R-WIL-050). *Application:* The material provisions (who takes what) and the signature are handwritten; the printed words "Last Will and Testament" supply intent under § 6111(c). Lack of witnesses is irrelevant. *Conclusion:* Valid holographic will; Nora takes the house and the Red Cross the residue.

### 2. One witness, strong evidence
**Facts.** Sam signs a typed will in his lawyer's office. Only the receptionist witnesses; the second witness was out sick. That evening Sam emails his daughter: "Signed my new will today — finally done."

**Analysis.** *Rule:* A witnessing defect is cured if clear and convincing evidence shows the testator, when signing, intended the document as his will (R-WIL-048). *Application:* Sam signed; the defect is only the missing witness; the lawyer's file and his same-day email show intent. *Conclusion:* Likely valid under § 6110(c)(2).

### 3. Witnesses in sequence
**Facts.** Uma signs her will in front of witness A. An hour later witness B arrives; Uma shows B her signature and says "this is my will." A and B both sign.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Two witnesses, **present at the same time**, must witness the signing or the acknowledgment (R-WIL-047). *Application:* A and B were never present together. *Conclusion:* § 6110(c)(1) fails, but the will can be saved under § 6110(c)(2) if clear and convincing evidence shows Uma's intent at signing (R-WIL-048) — likely here.

### 4. The beneficiary-witness
**Facts.** Victor's will leaves $100,000 to his caretaker-friend Fay and the residue to his son. Fay and one neighbor are the only witnesses.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* A gift to a subscribing witness, without two other disinterested witnesses, raises a presumption of duress, menace, fraud or undue influence; if not rebutted, the witness takes no more than her intestate share (R-WIL-049). *Application:* Only one other witness. Fay must rebut; as a non-relative her intestate share is zero. If Fay was Victor's care custodian (paid, or unpaid without a qualifying prior personal relationship) and Victor was a dependent adult, § 21380 adds a second presumption (R-WIL-095, R-WIL-097). *Conclusion:* Unless Fay rebuts, she takes nothing; the will otherwise stands.

### 5. Signed in Nevada
**Facts.** Wen, domiciled in Nevada, signs a will with one witness, valid under the law where she signs. She later moves to California and dies here.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* A will is validly executed if it complies with the law of the place of execution at the time of execution (R-WIL-053). *Application:* Assume the place of execution accepted one witness. *Conclusion:* Valid in California under § 6113, even though § 6110 would require two.

### 6. Undated holograph and a typed will
**Facts.** Xia's 2019 typed will leaves everything to her brother. After her death, an undated handwritten, signed note is found: "All to my friend Lin." Xia had dementia from 2022 on.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* An undated holograph is invalid if inconsistent with another will unless shown to be later, or if the testator lacked capacity at any time it might have been executed (R-WIL-051). *Application:* The note conflicts with the 2019 will; Lin must prove it was written after 2019 and at a time Xia had capacity. Evidence (paper, references to events, witnesses) is admissible (R-WIL-052). *Conclusion:* Lin likely fails unless she can date the note to 2019–2022.

### 7. Capacity on a good day
**Facts.** Yusuf, 88, with fluctuating dementia, signs a new will on a morning when his doctor notes he was oriented, discussed his rental properties, and named his three children and why he favored one.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Capacity requires understanding the act, the property and family relations at execution; a lucid interval suffices (R-WIL-033). *Application:* The medical note and his discussion cover all three elements on the day of signing; the contestants bear the burden (R-WIL-035). *Conclusion:* Capacity likely established.

### 8. The adviser who arranged everything
**Facts.** Zora, frail and isolated, relies on her financial adviser Pete. Pete picks the lawyer, drives Zora there, stays in the room, and receives 80% of the estate; her prior will left everything to her nieces.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Confidential relationship + active procurement + undue benefit raises a presumption of undue influence, shifting the burden to the proponent (R-WIL-040); § 15610.70 factors guide the analysis (R-WIL-039). *Application:* Pete is a fiduciary-like adviser; he procured the will; the gift departs from Zora's plan. Vulnerability, authority, tactics (presence, control) and inequity all point one way. *Conclusion:* Presumption arises; Pete likely cannot rebut; his gift fails (§ 6104) and the nieces take under the residue or prior will via DRR.

### 9. The lawyer's own gift
**Facts.** Attorney Ann drafts a will for an unrelated client leaving Ann's son $25,000.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* A gift to a person related within the third degree to the drafter is presumptively disqualified, and for the drafter and her relatives the presumption is conclusive (R-WIL-095). *Application:* Son is related in the first degree to the drafter; no § 21382 exception applies (gift exceeds $5,000; not the client's relative). *Conclusion:* The gift fails; it passes as if the son predeceased without issue (R-WIL-098). PR crossover: Rule 1.8(c) issues.

### 10. "My papers in the blue folder"
**Facts.** Ben's will says "I give my jewelry as described in the papers in my blue folder." At death the folder holds a list dated two years before the will and another dated after it.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Incorporation by reference requires a writing in existence at execution, intent and identification (R-WIL-058); a § 6132 writing may postdate the will but is limited to tangible personal property within the value caps (R-WIL-059). *Application:* The earlier list is incorporated. The later list may work under § 6132 if dated and signed or handwritten and the jewelry items are each $5,000 or less and total $25,000 or less. *Conclusion:* The earlier list controls unless the later list satisfies § 6132, in which case the most recent writing controls for items within the caps.

### 11. The pour-over after a trust revocation
**Facts.** Cal's will leaves the residue to "the trustee of the Cal Trust." Cal revokes the trust in 2024 and dies in 2025 without changing the will.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Unless the will provides otherwise, revocation of the trust before the testator's death causes the pour-over to lapse (R-WIL-061). *Application:* The trust was revoked before death; nothing in the will says otherwise. *Conclusion:* The residuary gift lapses; with no other residuary taker, the residue passes by intestacy (R-WIL-065).

### 12. Torn will, unsigned replacement
**Facts.** Dana tears up her 2015 will, telling her friend "I've written my new will." The new will, found later, is typed and unsigned. Without a will, Dana's estate goes to her estranged cousin; both wills benefit her best friend.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Physical-act revocation requires act + intent (R-WIL-078); DRR disregards a revocation based on the mistaken belief that a new will is valid if that better serves intent (R-WIL-085). *Application:* The new will fails (unsigned; harmless error cannot cure a missing signature, R-WIL-048). Dana revoked only because she thought the new will replaced it; both wills favor the friend. *Conclusion:* DRR revives the 2015 will; the friend takes.

### 13. The altered legacy
**Facts.** Ed's typed attested will gives "$5,000 to my nephew Nick." Later Ed draws a line through "$5,000," writes "$15,000" above it, and does not sign or initial the change.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Handwritten changes take effect only as a holographic codicil (R-WIL-055); a crossing-out with intent can be a partial revocation (R-WIL-079), subject to DRR (R-WIL-085). *Application:* "$15,000" lacks a signature, so the increase fails. The strike-out shows intent to revoke, but only as part of increasing the gift. *Conclusion:* Under DRR the revocation is disregarded; Nick takes $5,000.

### 14. The missing will
**Facts.** Fran kept her will in her bedroom desk. After her death it cannot be found. Her son Gus, disinherited by the will, lived with her and had access to the desk. A photocopy exists.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* A will last in the testator's possession that cannot be found is presumed revoked; the presumption affects only the burden of producing evidence (R-WIL-081). *Application:* The presumption arises, but Gus's access and motive, plus evidence that Fran recently reaffirmed the will, rebut it. *Conclusion:* The trier of fact may admit the will by the photocopy.

### 15. Divorce and the old will
**Facts.** Hal's will leaves everything to his wife Iris, alternate to his sister June, and names Iris executor. Hal and Iris divorce; Hal dies without a new will. His 401(k) and a POD bank account still name Iris.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Dissolution revokes gifts and nominations to the former spouse; the will reads as if she predeceased (R-WIL-082). Nonprobate transfers to a former spouse fail under § 5040, subject to exceptions, while ERISA plans follow federal law (R-WIL-083). *Application:* June takes under the will and an alternate executor serves; the POD account passes as if Iris predeceased. The 401(k), an ERISA plan, follows the plan documents under federal preemption. *Conclusion:* June takes the probate estate and likely the POD account; Iris may take the 401(k).

### 16. Revoking the revoker
**Facts.** Kim's 2018 will expressly revoked her 2012 will. In 2023 Kim burns the 2018 will and tells her lawyer, "I want the 2012 will back." She dies in 2024.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* When the revoking will is revoked by act, the earlier will is revived if circumstances or the testator's contemporaneous or later declarations show intent (R-WIL-084). *Application:* Kim's statement to her lawyer is a contemporaneous declaration. *Conclusion:* The 2012 will is revived.

### 17. The friend who died first
**Facts.** Leo's will: "$20,000 to my friend Max; $20,000 to my sister Nan; residue to my children." Max and Nan both die before Leo; each leaves a child.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Anti-lapse substitutes the issue of a predeceased beneficiary who is kindred of the testator (R-WIL-063); otherwise the gift falls to the residue (R-WIL-065). *Application:* Nan is kindred; Max is not. *Conclusion:* Nan's child takes $20,000; Max's gift falls into the residue for Leo's children.

### 18. "If she survives me"
**Facts.** "I leave $50,000 to my daughter Ola if she survives me." Ola dies before the testator, leaving a son.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* A survivorship condition is a contrary intention that displaces anti-lapse (R-WIL-064). *Conclusion:* The gift fails and falls into the residue; Ola's son takes only if he is a residuary taker.

### 19. The house sold by the conservator
**Facts.** Pia's will gives "my home" to her grandson Quinn and the residue to a charity. Pia's conservator sells the home for a net $600,000 to pay for her care; Pia never recovers and dies two years later with $700,000.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* If a conservator sells specifically given property, the beneficiary takes a general pecuniary gift equal to the net sale price (R-WIL-074). *Application:* The conservatorship never terminated, so the recovery exception does not apply. Quinn's gift becomes a $600,000 general pecuniary gift. *Conclusion:* Quinn takes $600,000; the charity takes the $100,000 residue. If the estate were short, Quinn's gift would abate as a general gift to a relative (R-WIL-075).

### 20. Stock split and a sold car
**Facts.** Rae's will gives "my 100 shares of Delta Corp" to Sid and "my Toyota" to Tia. Delta splits 3-for-1; Rae trades the Toyota for a Honda.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Securities from issuer action follow the specific gift (R-WIL-071). A specific gift is adeemed if the item is gone and cannot be traced, but California may avoid ademption for a change in form absent intent (R-WIL-069). *Application:* Sid takes 300 shares. Tia argues the Honda is the Toyota in a new form; the estate argues a new car is a new item. *Conclusion:* Sid takes 300 shares; Tia's claim is arguable under *Mason* but a trade-in for a different car is likely treated as ademption.

### 21. Not enough money
**Facts.** After debts, Una's estate is $90,000 plus a painting worth $30,000. Will: painting to friend Val; $60,000 to niece Wes; $40,000 to the Opera; residue to her son Xavi.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* Abatement order: residue, general to non-relatives, general to relatives, specific to non-relatives, specific to relatives (R-WIL-075). *Application:* $90,000 cash against $100,000 of general gifts; residue (Xavi) takes nothing. The Opera (non-relative) abates by $10,000. *Conclusion:* Val gets the painting; Wes $60,000; the Opera $30,000; Xavi nothing.

### 22. Married after the will
**Facts.** Yan's 2016 will leaves everything to his brother. He marries Zoe in 2021 and dies in 2026 leaving CP of $500,000, SP of $400,000, no issue, and his mother. The will says nothing about future spouses.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* An omitted spouse takes the decedent's half of CP and QCP plus the intestate SP share capped at one-half (R-WIL-086), unless an exception applies (R-WIL-087). *Application:* Marriage followed all instruments; no intent on the face; no facts of a transfer in lieu or waiver. With a surviving parent, the intestate SP share is one-half. *Conclusion:* Zoe keeps her $250,000 CP half, takes Yan's $250,000 CP half and $200,000 SP; the brother takes the remaining $200,000 SP.

### 23. Child born after the will
**Facts.** Abe's will leaves everything to his wife Bea, the mother of his son Cy. Their daughter Di is born later. Abe dies.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* An after-born child takes an intestate share (R-WIL-090) unless the decedent had a child and left substantially all to the omitted child's other parent (R-WIL-091). *Application:* Abe had Cy at execution and left everything to Bea, Di's mother. *Conclusion:* Di takes nothing as an omitted child.

### 24. The stepson
**Facts.** Dee raised her stepson Eli from age 4; Eli's father refused to consent to adoption. Eli turned 18 in 2005; Dee never adopted him and died intestate in 2026 with no spouse or issue; her sister survives.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* A stepchild inherits only if clear and convincing evidence shows the stepparent would have adopted but for a legal barrier that persisted until death (R-WIL-029). *Application:* The barrier (the father's refusal) ended at Eli's majority; Dee could have adopted him as an adult. Equitable adoption needs an intent to adopt (R-WIL-030); evidence is thin. *Conclusion:* Eli likely takes nothing; Dee's sister takes (R-WIL-018).

### 25. The no-contest clause
**Facts.** Fay's will contains a no-contest clause. Her son Gil, left $10,000, files a contest alleging undue influence, supported by declarations from two nurses who saw his sister isolating Fay. He loses.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* A no-contest clause is enforced only against a direct contest brought without probable cause (R-WIL-093). *Application:* Undue influence is a direct-contest ground; the nurses' declarations gave Gil a reasonable belief in a reasonable likelihood of success. *Conclusion:* Probable cause existed; Gil keeps his $10,000.

### 26. A drafting mistake
**Facts.** Hana's lawyer's notes and her signed instruction letter say "residue to my alma mater"; the typed will gives the residue to "my alma mater's athletic booster club," a separate entity.

**Analysis.** *Rule:* An unambiguous will may be reformed on clear and convincing evidence of a mistake in expression and of the testator's actual specific intent (R-WIL-046). *Application:* The instruction letter and notes show both. *Conclusion:* The court may reform the residuary clause under *Estate of Duke*.
