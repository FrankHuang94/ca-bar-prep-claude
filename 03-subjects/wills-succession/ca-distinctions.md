# Wills and Succession — California distinctions

**Why this file is different.** Wills is **not on the MBE**, and every Wills essay in the archive is answered **under California law** [S-079]. There is no "general rule" to write first. This file instead contrasts the **California Probate Code** with the **common law** (what your casebook calls the traditional rule) and the **Uniform Probate Code (UPC)** (the modern reform model many states use). Your Trusts & Estates course will teach the UPC and majority rules; on the bar, write the **California** rule.

**The one-line essay rule.** Apply the Probate Code section; mention the common-law rule only when California adopted it (integration, republication, DRR, the undue-influence presumption) or when the facts involve another state's law (choice of law for execution, § 6113; out-of-state land at death).

## Comparison table

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Witnesses' presence | Many states require witnesses to sign in the testator's presence (and sometimes each other's) | Two witnesses **present at the same time** for the signing or acknowledgment; they need not sign in the testator's presence; sign during the testator's life | Prob. Code § 6110(c)(1) | "Both witnesses were present when T signed and understood it was her will, so § 6110 is met" (R-WIL-047) |
| Harmless error | UPC § 2-503: clear and convincing evidence can excuse **any** formality | Clear and convincing evidence cures **only witnessing defects**; writing and testator's signature still required | § 6110(c)(2) | "§ 6110(c)(2) cannot save an unsigned will" (R-WIL-048) |
| Interested witness | Purge statutes void the witness's gift beyond the intestate share | Will valid; **rebuttable presumption** of duress, menace, fraud or undue influence unless two disinterested witnesses; if unrebutted, gift capped at intestate share | § 6112 | Raise and apply the presumption (R-WIL-049) |
| Holographic wills | Not recognized in about half the states; UPC requires "material portions" in handwriting | Recognized; **signature and material provisions** in handwriting; printed-form intent allowed; **no date required** (date matters only for inconsistency or incapacity) | § 6111; *Estate of Black* | Test handwriting of signature + who/what (R-WIL-050, R-WIL-051) |
| Choice of law for execution | Most states have similar savings statutes | Valid if it complies with law of place of execution, domicile/abode/nationality (execution or death), or California | § 6113 | "Valid in California because valid where executed" (R-WIL-053) |
| Tangible personal property list | UPC § 2-513: separate writing, no dollar caps | Allowed, dated and handwritten or signed; **$25,000 total, $5,000 per item** | § 6132 [S-601] | Check the caps (R-WIL-059) |

| Revival | English rule: automatic revival; strict anti-revival in some states; UPC intent-based | **Intent-based**: physical-act revocation of will 2 → circumstances and declarations; revocation by will 3 → terms of will 3 only | § 6123 | Apply the right evidence for the method (R-WIL-084) |

## Community property overlay at death

The biggest structural difference from the UPC states is that California **characterizes before it distributes**. Every distribution problem in a married decedent's estate runs on two tracks:

1. **What the survivor already owns:** half of all CP and half of the decedent's QCP (§§ 100, 101) — never part of the estate.
2. **What the decedent's will or intestacy controls:** SP, the decedent's half of CP, and half of the decedent's QCP (§ 6101).

Out-of-state real property is **not** QCP at death and passes under the situs state's law (§ 66) [S-404]. See [`../community-property/outline.md`](../community-property/outline.md) VII (R-CMP-091 to R-CMP-099).

## Where California follows the common law

These doctrines have no Probate Code section; cite them as common law (California applies them):

- **Integration** (R-WIL-056) and **republication by codicil** (R-WIL-057).
- **Dependent relative revocation** (R-WIL-085; *Estate of Kaufman* (1945) 25 Cal.2d 854 [S-619]).
- **Undue-influence presumption** (R-WIL-040; *Rice v. Clark* [S-616]).
- **Fraud in the execution and in the inducement** (R-WIL-041).
- **Class-gift closing** (R-WIL-066).

## Trusts overlap (cross-reference by topic)

Revocable trusts are will substitutes. The omitted-spouse and omitted-child statutes (§ 21601), the disqualified-donee presumption (§ 21380), anti-lapse and abatement (Division 11) apply to trusts as well as wills. Pour-overs (§ 6300) link the two. Trust creation, revocation, modification (*Haggerty v. Thornton*, [`../../00-exam-intel/recent-changes.md`](../../00-exam-intel/recent-changes.md) §7) and trustee duties are in the Trusts subject ([`../trusts/README.md`](../trusts/README.md)).
