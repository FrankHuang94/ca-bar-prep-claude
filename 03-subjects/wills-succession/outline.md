# Wills and Succession — comprehensive outline

**How to use this outline.** This is the complete reference for California Wills and Succession. It follows the State Bar's essay outline for the subject, which is keyed to sections of the **California Probate Code** [S-094]. Each heading below carries the outline's topic name, so every coverage-matrix row maps to a heading here. The essay-ready rule for each point is in [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md) (cited as R-WIL-###). Points where California differs from the common law or the Uniform Probate Code (UPC) are collected in [`ca-distinctions.md`](ca-distinctions.md).

**Governing law.** Wills is **not on the MBE**. Every California Wills essay in the archive is answered **under California law**; calls usually say "Answer according to California law" [S-079]. So the rules below are the Probate Code rules, with common-law doctrine where the Code is silent (integration, republication, dependent relative revocation, the undue-influence presumption).

**Abbreviations.** Prob. Code = California Probate Code; CP = community property; QCP = quasi-community property; SP = separate property; DRR = dependent relative revocation; UPC = Uniform Probate Code (for contrast only). "Issue" means lineal descendants of all generations. "Transferor" and "transferee" are the Division 11 words for testator and beneficiary; the rules apply to wills, trusts and other instruments.

**Placement note.** Phase 0 search summaries of the State Bar outline disagreed on where "rules of interpretation" sit (at III.E, or with V). A later summary lists "Rules of Interpretation of Instruments, Sections 21105, 21109, 21110" with the sub-topics "changes occurring after execution" and anti-lapse, and lists integration and republication by codicil under component parts [S-094]. This outline therefore teaches **general interpretation** at III.E and **changes after execution and anti-lapse** in Part V.2, and maps both matrix rows.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the Probate Code plays the role that the succession book (继承编) of the PRC Civil Code plays, but California adds a large body of judge-made doctrine around it, and California has no "necessary share" (必留份) for dependent heirs (approx.).

## I General provisions

The first part of the State Bar outline collects rules that apply whether or not there is a will: what a married decedent owns at death, contracts about rights at death, survival, and how shares are divided among a group.

### I.A Part 1 ... Effect of Death of Married Person on Community and Quasi-Community Property, Sections 100-103.

**Do not re-learn this here.** Community Property owns the detailed rules; use the cross-references below and write them in the Wills essay as the **first step** whenever the decedent was married.

- **CP at death (§ 100).** One-half of the CP belongs to the surviving spouse and one-half to the decedent. Each spouse owns one-half of **each** CP asset (item theory). See R-CMP-091 and R-WIL-001.
- **QCP at death (§ 101).** If the decedent was domiciled in California, one-half of the **decedent's** QCP belongs to the surviving spouse. The survivor's own QCP is not split. At death, QCP covers personal property wherever located and **California** real property; land in another state passes under the situs state's law (§ 66) [S-404]. See R-CMP-092.
- **Recapture (§ 102).** The surviving spouse may require a transferee to restore one-half of QCP the decedent gave away without substantial consideration and without the survivor's written consent or joinder, where the decedent kept possession, income, a power to revoke or invade, or a survivorship interest [S-403]. See R-CMP-093 and R-WIL-002.
- **Simultaneous death of spouses (§ 103).** If it cannot be shown by clear and convincing evidence that one spouse survived the other, one-half of the CP and QCP is administered as if it belonged to each spouse and that spouse survived (each half passes in that spouse's estate). R-WIL-003 [VERIFY V-521].
- **Testamentary power (§ 6101).** The decedent may dispose by will of SP, one-half of the CP and one-half of the decedent's QCP (III.B).
- **Equitable election.** If a will purports to give away the survivor's half and also benefits the survivor, the survivor must elect. See R-CMP-095.
- **Nonprobate transfers of CP** without the other spouse's written consent are ineffective as to the non-consenting spouse's half (§§ 5020–5022) [S-420]. See R-CMP-096.

**How tested.** Feb 2018 Q5, Feb 2019 Q1, Jul 2022 Q5 and Jul 2026 Q2 (CP + Wills crossovers) all began by characterizing assets at death [VERIFY V-071].

### I.B Contractual arrangements relating to rights at death (Prob. Code §§ 140–147; § 21700)

#### I.B.1 Surviving spouse's waiver of rights (§§ 140–147)

- **What can be waived (§ 141).** A spouse may waive, wholly or in part, rights arising at the other spouse's death: rights to CP and QCP, the intestate share, the omitted-spouse share, a probate homestead, family allowance and exempt-property set-aside, the right to be appointed administrator, and similar rights. A waiver of "all rights" (or similar words) waives all of them (§ 145).
- **Form (§ 142).** The waiver must be **in writing and signed** by the surviving spouse. It may be made before or during marriage.
- **Enforceability (§ 143).** A written waiver is **enforceable unless the surviving spouse proves either**: (1) **fair and reasonable disclosure** of the decedent's property and financial obligations was not provided before signing (unless the survivor waived disclosure after advice of independent counsel); **or** (2) the survivor was **not represented by independent counsel** when signing. Fam. Code § 721(b) (the fiduciary presumption between spouses) does not apply to a waiver enforceable under § 143 [S-606].
- **Court's alternative (§ 144).** Even if § 143 is not satisfied, the court enforces the waiver if it determines **either** (1) the waiver made a **fair and reasonable disposition** of the survivor's rights at the time of signing, **or** (2) the survivor had, or reasonably should have had, **adequate knowledge** of the decedent's property and financial obligations **and** the decedent did not violate the Fam. Code § 721(b) fiduciary duty. But if enforcement would be **unconscionable** under the circumstances when enforcement is sought, the court may refuse to enforce it, enforce it in part, or limit it (§ 144(b)). On an essay, run § 144 whenever § 143 fails [S-607] [S-1060].
- **Revocation (§ 146).** A waiver may be altered or revoked only by a later writing signed by both spouses (or as the waiver itself allows).
- **Premarital agreements.** A waiver of death rights is often inside a premarital agreement; the agreement must also satisfy the Family Code (R-CMP-018 to R-CMP-021).
- R-WIL-004.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: compare a spouse's renunciation of inheritance (放弃继承) under the PRC Civil Code, except that California lets the waiver be signed long before death, subject to disclosure/counsel protections (approx.).

#### I.B.2 Contracts to make a will, not to revoke, or to die intestate (§ 21700)

- **Proof requirements (§ 21700(a)).** A contract made after the statute took effect (Jan 1, 2001) to make a will or other instrument, not to revoke one, or to die intestate can be established **only** by: (1) provisions of a will or other instrument stating the contract's material provisions; (2) an express reference in a will or other instrument to the contract, plus extrinsic evidence of its terms; (3) a writing **signed by the decedent** evidencing the contract; (4) **clear and convincing evidence** of an agreement between the decedent and the claimant, or a promise by the decedent to the claimant, that is **enforceable in equity**; or (5) clear and convincing evidence of such an agreement or promise made to another person for the claimant's benefit [S-627].
- **Remedy.** The contract does not stop the testator from making a new will; the will is probated and the promisee's remedy is against the estate (damages, quantum meruit, or a **constructive trust** over the property promised) [common law].
- R-WIL-005.

#### I.B.3 Joint and mutual wills

- A **joint will** is one instrument signed as the will of two people; **mutual (reciprocal) wills** are separate wills with mirror provisions. Executing either **does not create a presumption** of a contract not to revoke (§ 21700(b)) [S-627]. The contract must be proved by one of the § 21700(a) methods. R-WIL-006.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: a contract to leave property at death resembles the PRC legacy-support agreement (遗赠扶养协议), which takes priority over a will; in California the promisee's remedy is a claim against the estate, not automatic priority (approx.).

### I.C Simultaneous death, Probate Code Sections 220, 222-224 (and the 120-hour rule)

- **Standard of proof.** Survival must be shown by **clear and convincing evidence**. If it cannot be, the person is treated as **not** having survived for the purpose in question.
- **General rule (§ 220).** If title or devolution depends on priority of death and survival cannot be established by clear and convincing evidence, each person's property is administered as if that person had survived the other. R-WIL-007.
- **Beneficiaries (§ 222).** If a beneficiary's right depends on surviving another person and survival is not established by clear and convincing evidence, the beneficiary is deemed **not** to have survived; where alternative beneficiaries each must survive the others, the property is divided into equal shares. Under Division 11, a transferee who fails to survive the transferor "does not take under the instrument" (§ 21109(a)) [S-622]. R-WIL-008.
- **Joint tenants (§ 223).** If two joint tenants die and survival cannot be established, the property is divided into equal shares and each share is administered as if that joint tenant survived the other; the same pro rata method applies to more than two joint tenants [S-636]. R-WIL-009.
- **Life insurance (§ 224).** If the insured and the beneficiary die and the beneficiary's survival of the insured cannot be shown by clear and convincing evidence, the proceeds are distributed as if the insured survived the beneficiary (so the contingent beneficiary or the insured's estate takes) [S-636]. Exception: if the policy is CP or QCP of the insured and the spouse and there is no alternative beneficiary except the insured's estate, the proceeds are distributed as CP under § 103 [VERIFY V-916]. R-WIL-010.
- **Intestate heirs: 120 hours (§ 6403).** For **intestate succession only**, an heir must survive the decedent by **120 hours**, proven by clear and convincing evidence; otherwise the heir is deemed to have predeceased. The rule does not apply if it would cause the estate to escheat. Wills are governed by §§ 220–224 and the will's own survival clause, not by the 120-hour rule. R-WIL-020.
- **Instrument controls.** All of these default rules yield to a governing instrument that deals with simultaneous death or requires survival for a stated period.

### I.D Distribution among heirs or beneficiaries (§§ 240–247)

- **California's default "representation" (§ 240).** Divide the property into equal shares at the **first generation below the ancestor that has a living member**: one share for each living member of that generation and one for each deceased member who left living issue; each deceased member's share is divided among his or her issue the same way. Intestacy uses § 240 (§§ 6402, 6402.5) and so does any instrument that calls for § 240.
- **Strict per stirpes (§ 246).** Where an instrument says "per stirpes", "by right of representation" or "by representation" without more, divide at the **children's generation** even if all children are dead [VERIFY V-523].
- **Per capita at each generation (§ 247).** Used only where an instrument calls for it: shares of deceased members at each generation are pooled and divided equally among the next generation.
- **Worked numbers.** D's children A, B and C all predeceased D. A left two children (A1, A2); B left none; C left one (C1). Under § 240 the first generation with a living member is the grandchildren: A1, A2 and C1 take **1/3 each**. Under § 246 the division is at the children's level: A's line takes 1/2 (A1 and A2 1/4 each) and C1 takes 1/2. R-WIL-011.

### I.E Persons barred from taking; disclaimers

- **Slayer rule (§§ 250–254).** A person who **feloniously and intentionally kills** the decedent takes nothing under the decedent's will, trust or intestacy, and the property passes **as if the killer predeceased** the decedent; a joint tenancy is severed so the killer's share does not grow by survivorship (§ 251). A final felony conviction is conclusive; without one, the court decides on a **preponderance of the evidence** whether the killing was felonious and intentional (§ 254). R-WIL-012.
- **Elder and dependent-adult abuse (§ 259).** A person proven by **clear and convincing evidence** to be liable for physical abuse, neglect or financial abuse of the decedent (an elder or dependent adult), and to have acted in bad faith or with recklessness, oppression, fraud or malice, is deemed to have **predeceased** the decedent to the extent stated in the statute; a conviction under Pen. Code § 236 or § 368 has the same effect [S-639]. The civil-liability route also appears to require that the decedent was substantially unable to manage his or her finances or resist fraud or undue influence from the time of the abuse until death [VERIFY V-915]. R-WIL-013.
- **Disclaimer (§§ 275–295).** A beneficiary or heir may refuse an interest by a **signed writing filed within a reasonable time**; a disclaimer within **nine months** of the death is conclusively presumed timely, and a later disclaimer is effective only if the disclaimant proves the time was reasonable (§ 279) [S-931]. The disclaimed interest passes as if the disclaimant **predeceased** the decedent (§ 282). Disclaiming does not let the disclaimant steer the property. R-WIL-014.

## II Intestate succession

### Part 2. Intestate Succession, Sections 6400-6402 (statutory framework at a glance)

| Step | Rule | Section |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What passes by intestacy: any part of the estate not effectively disposed of by will | § 6400 |
| 2 | Surviving spouse or registered domestic partner: CP, QCP, SP share | § 6401 |
| 3 | The rest of the SP (or everything if no spouse): issue → parents → issue of parents → grandparents and their issue → issue of a predeceased spouse → next of kin → parents of a predeceased spouse and their issue | § 6402 |
| 4 | Special rule for property from a predeceased spouse | § 6402.5 |
| 5 | Survival 120 hours; escheat; half-blood; afterborn; advancements | §§ 6403–6410 |
| 6 | Who counts as a child or parent | §§ 6450–6455 |

### II.A Property subject to intestacy (§ 6400)

- **Rule.** Any part of the decedent's estate **not effectively disposed of by will** passes to the heirs under Part 2 (§ 6400). Intestacy applies to the whole estate if there is no valid will, and to the **residue of a partial intestacy** where a will fails in part (e.g., a lapsed residuary gift with no alternative taker, § 21111(a)(3)).
- **What is not in the intestate estate.** Nonprobate property (joint tenancy, POD and Totten accounts, life insurance with a living beneficiary, trust assets, TOD deeds) passes by its own terms (Part X). The **survivor's half of CP** is the survivor's own property, not part of the estate.
- **Bars.** Slayers and abusers are treated as predeceased (I.E). R-WIL-015.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: like statutory succession (法定继承) under the PRC Civil Code, intestacy applies to whatever a will does not validly cover; but California ranks heirs very differently (approx.).

### II.B Surviving spouse's share (§ 6401)

- **CP:** the decedent's one-half of the CP passes to the surviving spouse, who therefore ends up with **all** the CP (§ 6401(a)).
- **QCP:** the decedent's one-half of the decedent's QCP passes to the surviving spouse (§ 6401(b)).
- **SP (§ 6401(c)):**
  - **all** of it if the decedent left no issue, parent, sibling or issue of a deceased sibling;
  - **one-half** if the decedent left **one child** or the issue of one deceased child, **or** left no issue but a **parent** or parents or their issue (siblings, nieces, nephews);
  - **one-third** if the decedent left **more than one child**, one child and the issue of one or more deceased children, or issue of two or more deceased children.
- **Registered domestic partners** take as spouses. Cross-reference: R-CMP-094 (same rule from the CP side). R-WIL-016.
- **Who is a surviving spouse (§ 78).** Not a person whose marriage to the decedent was **dissolved or annulled**, nor a person who obtained or consented to a dissolution not recognized in California, nor a person who was party to a valid proceeding concluded by an order purporting to terminate all marital property rights (§ 78(d)). A spouse in a **pending** dissolution is still a surviving spouse for intestacy. A **legally separated** spouse is still a surviving spouse **only if** the separation judgment did not terminate all marital property rights: in *Estate of Lahey* (1999) 76 Cal.App.4th 1056, a legal-separation judgment that resolved the spouses' property rights barred the wife from inheriting as a surviving spouse [S-1061]. (Contrast § 6122(d): legal separation never revokes will provisions for the spouse.) R-WIL-017.
- **Putative spouse.** A putative spouse (good-faith belief in a valid marriage) is generally treated as a surviving spouse for intestate succession to quasi-marital property (Fam. Code § 2251 concept; see R-CMP-006) [VERIFY V-910].

**Worked numbers.** W dies intestate. CP $600,000; W's SP $300,000. W leaves husband H and two children. H keeps his $300,000 half of CP and takes W's $300,000 half (all CP to H) plus 1/3 of W's SP ($100,000). The two children split $200,000.

### II.C Other heirs' shares (§ 6402)

The part of the estate not passing to the surviving spouse (all of it if there is no spouse) passes in this order; each tier takes only if **no one survives in a higher tier**:

1. **Issue** of the decedent, equally if all of the same degree, otherwise by representation under § 240 (§ 6402(a)).
2. **Parent or parents**, equally (§ 6402(b)) — subject to § 6452 (a parent who abandoned or did not acknowledge the child is barred; II.E).
3. **Issue of the parents** (brothers, sisters, nieces, nephews), by § 240 (§ 6402(c)).
4. **Grandparents** equally, or if none survive, their issue (aunts, uncles, cousins), by § 240 (§ 6402(d)).
5. **Issue of a predeceased spouse** (stepchildren and their issue) (§ 6402(e)).
6. **Next of kin** in equal degree, preferring those claiming through the nearest ancestor (§ 6402(f)).
7. **Parents of a predeceased spouse**, or their issue (§ 6402(g)).
8. **Escheat** to the State (§ 6404; II.G).

- **Predeceased-spouse property (§ 6402.5).** If the decedent leaves **no surviving spouse and no issue**, property attributable to a spouse who died **within 15 years** (real property) or **within 5 years** (personal property, subject to a value or title-record threshold) before the decedent passes first to the predeceased spouse's relatives (issue, then parents, then the parents' issue), ahead of the decedent's own parents, siblings and other kin, who take it under § 6402 only if none of those relatives survives [S-632] [VERIFY V-508]. Real property sold before death becomes personal property for this rule (Estate of Ochoa (2019), per summary [S-632] [VERIFY V-508]). R-WIL-019.
- **Double relationship.** A relative related through two lines takes only one share, the larger (§ 6413) [VERIFY V-510].
- **Afterborn heirs (§ 6407).** Relatives **conceived before** the decedent's death but born after take as if born in the decedent's lifetime. A child **conceived after** death with the decedent's genetic material is treated as born in the decedent's lifetime only if § 249.5's requirements (a signed writing, timely notice, conception within the statutory period) are met [VERIFY V-509]. R-WIL-023.
- **Advancements (§ 6409).** A lifetime gift to an heir counts against that heir's intestate share **only if** (1) the decedent declared in a **contemporaneous writing** that it was an advancement or was to be deducted, or (2) the **heir acknowledged in writing** that it was. Value is fixed at the earlier of when the heir got possession or the decedent's death, unless the writing states a value. If the advancee predeceases, the advancement is **not** charged against the advancee's issue unless the writing says so. R-WIL-024.
- **Debts owed to the decedent (§ 6410).** A debt an heir owes the decedent is charged against that heir's share, but not against the issue of a debtor who predeceased. R-WIL-025.
- **Aliens (§ 6411).** Alienage does not disqualify an heir.
- R-WIL-018.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: under the PRC Civil Code, parents share in the first order with the spouse and children; in California, parents take only if the decedent left **no issue**, and the spouse's share depends on which relatives survive (approx.).

### II.D Relatives of the halfblood (§ 6406)

- **Rule.** Relatives of the half blood inherit **the same share** they would inherit if they were of the whole blood (§ 6406), except as § 6451 provides for adoptees. A half-sister shares equally with a full brother. R-WIL-022.
- **Contrast.** Some common-law states gave half-bloods half shares; California does not.

### II.E Adopted children (and the parent-child relationship generally)

- **Natural parents (§ 6450(a)).** A parent-child relationship exists between a person and his or her natural parents **regardless of the parents' marital status**. Parentage may be shown under the Uniform Parentage Act presumptions or a court order, and in some cases by clear and convincing evidence (§ 6453) [VERIFY V-511]. R-WIL-026.
- **Adoption (§ 6450(b)).** An adopted person is the child of the adopting parent(s) for all intestacy purposes, inheriting **from and through** them.
- **Severance from natural parents (§ 6451(a)).** Adoption **severs** the relationship with a natural parent **unless both**: (1) the natural parent and the adoptee **lived together at any time as parent and child** (or the natural parent was married to or cohabiting with the other natural parent when the child was conceived and died before the birth); **and** (2) the adoption was **by the spouse of either natural parent** or **after the death of either natural parent**. If both are met, the adoptee can still inherit from and through that natural parent (typical case: stepparent adoption after a divorce, or adoption after a parent's death).
- **One-way street (§ 6451(b)).** Even then, the natural parent and that parent's relatives (other than a **whole-blood** sibling of the adoptee and that sibling's issue) **do not inherit from** the adoptee through that relationship [S-1062], unless the adoption was by the natural parent's spouse or surviving spouse [VERIFY V-917]. R-WIL-027.
- **Parent barred from inheriting from a child (§ 6452).** A parent does not inherit from or through a child if: (1) parental rights were **terminated** and not judicially reestablished; (2) the parent **did not acknowledge** the child; or (3) the parent **left the child during minority without an effort to provide support, or without communication, for at least seven consecutive years**, continuing to the end of minority, with intent to abandon (the failure is presumptive evidence of that intent). The barred parent is treated as having **predeceased** the child (§ 6452(b)) [S-625]. R-WIL-028.
- **Stepchildren and foster children (§ 6454).** A stepchild or foster child (and the child's issue) inherits from or through a stepparent or foster parent **only if both**: (a) the relationship **began during the child's minority** and **continued throughout their joint lifetimes**; **and** (b) it is established by **clear and convincing evidence** that the stepparent or foster parent **would have adopted the child but for a legal barrier**. The barrier must **persist until death**, not merely exist while the child was a minor (*Estate of Joseph* (1998) 17 Cal.4th 203) [S-610]. Because adults may be adopted, the typical barrier (a natural parent's refusal to consent) usually ends at majority, so most stepchildren fail § 6454 and inherit, if at all, only under § 6402(e) as issue of a predeceased spouse. R-WIL-029.
- **Equitable adoption (§ 6455).** The doctrine survives. It requires an **intent to adopt** (an express or implied agreement to adopt, which may be oral) and conduct consistent with it, proved by **clear and convincing evidence**; a close, loving relationship alone is not enough (*Estate of Ford* (2004) 32 Cal.4th 160) [S-624]. Equitable adoption lets the child inherit **from** the would-be parent, not the reverse. R-WIL-030.
- **Wills and trusts.** Whether an adoptee, stepchild, foster child or half-blood relative is within a class gift ("to my grandchildren") is answered by § 21115 (V.2.B).

**How tested.** Jul 2013 Q5 (stepson's rights) and Feb 2018 Q5 (adopted son and a later-born child) [VERIFY V-071].

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Civil Code lets a stepchild who was raised and supported by the stepparent (有扶养关系的继子女) inherit as a child; California demands either adoption, a § 6454 legal-barrier case, or equitable adoption (approx.).

### II.F Method of distribution (representation)

- **Intestacy uses § 240** (I.D): divide at the **first generation with a living member**; each deceased member of that generation who left issue gets a share that drops to his or her issue.
- **Instruments** may choose § 246 (strict per stirpes) or § 247 (per capita at each generation); a will that says "per stirpes" or "by right of representation" without more gets § 246 [VERIFY V-523].
- **Step-by-step on an essay.** (1) Identify the tier under § 6402. (2) Find the nearest generation with a living member. (3) Count living members plus deceased members with living issue. (4) Divide. (5) Drop deceased members' shares down the line. R-WIL-011.

### II.G Escheat (§ 6404)

- **Rule.** If there is no taker under Part 2, the intestate estate **escheats** to the State (§ 6404). Because § 6402 reaches next of kin of any degree and the predeceased spouse's relatives, escheat is rare.
- **Escheat-avoidance rules.** The 120-hour rule does not apply if applying it would cause escheat (§ 6403). R-WIL-021.

### II.H Small estates and probate shortcuts (recent change — apply)

- **Small-estate affidavit (§ 13100).** Personal property may be collected without probate by affidavit when the gross estate, net of excluded property, does not exceed **$208,850** for deaths on or after **April 1, 2025** (adjusted every three years; next adjustment April 1, 2028, so this figure governs a July 2027 exam) [S-128].
- **Primary residence (§ 13151).** A decedent's **primary residence** worth up to **$750,000** may pass by a simplified court petition (AB 2016) [S-128].
- **Spousal property petition.** Property passing to a surviving spouse may be confirmed without administration (§§ 13500, 13650) — see R-CMP-098. R-WIL-031.

| § 6100.5 | Test for mental incompetence to make a will (capacity; insane delusion) | III.A |
| § 6101 | Property that may be disposed of by will (SP, one-half CP, one-half own QCP) | III.B |
| § 6102 | A will may dispose of property to individuals, entities and governments | III.B |
| § 6104 | Execution or revocation procured by duress, menace, fraud or undue influence is ineffective to that extent | III.C |
| § 6105 | A will made conditional by its own terms is given effect according to the condition | III.D |

### III.A Testamentary capacity (§§ 6100, 6100.5)

- **Who may make a will (§ 6100(a)).** An individual **18 or older** who is of **sound mind**. R-WIL-032.
- **Capacity test (§ 6100.5(a)(1)).** An individual lacks capacity if, **at the time of making the will**, he or she does not have sufficient mental capacity to be able to:
  1. **understand the nature of the testamentary act**;
  2. **understand and recollect the nature and situation of his or her property**; or
  3. **remember and understand his or her relations** to living descendants, spouse and parents, and to those whose interests are affected by the will.
- **Insane delusion (§ 6100.5(a)(2)).** An individual also lacks capacity if he or she suffers from a **mental health disorder with symptoms including delusions or hallucinations**, and those delusions or hallucinations **result in a devise** the individual would not otherwise have made. Only the gifts **caused** by the delusion fail. R-WIL-034.
- **Timing.** Capacity is measured at the moment of execution. A will made during a **lucid interval** is valid [common law]. Old age, illness, eccentricity, forgetfulness or physical weakness alone do not show incapacity [common law].
- **Low threshold.** Testamentary capacity requires **less** than capacity to contract; it asks only whether the testator understood these three things at execution [common law]. R-WIL-033.
- **Burden (§ 8252).** The **proponent** must prove due execution; the **contestant** bears the burden of proving lack of testamentary intent or capacity, undue influence, fraud, duress, mistake or revocation [S-1064]. Testators are presumed competent [common law]. R-WIL-035.
- **Conservatees (§ 1871(c); § 6100).** A conservatorship does **not** remove the right to make a will: nothing in the conservatorship article denies a conservatee "the right to make a will" [S-641]. Whether a conservatee had capacity is decided under § 6100.5 at execution; the conservatorship is evidence, not proof, of incapacity. A **conservator** may make a will for the conservatee only under a **court order (§ 2580, substituted judgment)**, signed by the conservator (§ 6110(b)(3)), and a competent conservatee may later revoke or replace it (§ 6100) [S-642]. R-WIL-036. See also the conservatorship row at V.2.E.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Civil Code voids a will made by a person without or with limited capacity for civil conduct (无/限制民事行为能力人); California instead asks the three-part functional question at the moment of signing (approx.).

### III.B Property which may be disposed of by will (§ 6101)

- **Rule (§ 6101).** A will may dispose of: (a) the testator's **separate property**; (b) the **one-half of the CP** that belongs to the testator under § 100; and (c) the **one-half of the testator's QCP** under § 101. The survivor's half is not the testator's to give (I.A). R-WIL-037.
- **After-acquired property (§ 21105).** A will passes all property the testator owns **at death**, including property acquired after the will was signed (except as §§ 641–642 provide for powers of appointment) [S-094].
- **Nonprobate property** (joint tenancy, POD accounts, insurance, trust assets) does not pass by will; a residuary clause does not reach it (R-WIL-104).
- **Permissible takers (§ 6102).** Individuals, corporations, unincorporated associations, local governments, states, the United States and foreign governments [VERIFY V-527]. A gift to an animal fails because an animal cannot take (*Estate of Russell* (1968) 69 Cal.2d 200); a gift for an animal's care can be made through a pet trust (Trusts outline).

### III.C Effect of duress, menace, fraud, or undue influence (§ 6104)

- **Statutory rule (§ 6104).** The execution or revocation of a will **or a part of a will** is **ineffective to the extent** it was procured by duress, menace, fraud or undue influence. Only the tainted provisions fail; the rest of the will stands if severable. R-WIL-038.

#### III.C.1 Undue influence

- **Definition.** Pressure that **overcomes the testator's free will** and substitutes the influencer's wishes, operating **directly on the testamentary act**; mere opportunity, affection, kindness or a request is not enough (*Rice v. Clark* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 89, 96) [S-616]. Since 2014, **Prob. Code § 86** gives "undue influence" the meaning in **Welf. & Inst. Code § 15610.70** ("excessive persuasion that causes another person to act or refrain from acting by overcoming that person's free will and results in inequity"), which **supplements** rather than replaces the common law [S-630] [S-631]. R-WIL-039.
- **§ 15610.70 factors** to weigh: (1) the **vulnerability** of the victim (illness, disability, age, cognitive impairment, emotional distress, isolation, dependency); (2) the influencer's **apparent authority** (fiduciary, family, care provider, professional); (3) the **actions or tactics** used (controlling necessaries, medication, interactions or information; secrecy; haste; intimidation); and (4) the **equity of the result** (departure from prior plans, relationship of the parties, value of the gift) [S-631].
- **Common-law presumption.** A presumption of undue influence arises when the contestant shows: (1) a **confidential relationship** between the testator and the beneficiary; (2) the beneficiary's **active participation in procuring** the will (arranging the lawyer, choosing provisions, being present at drafting, controlling access); and (3) an **undue benefit** to the beneficiary (*Rice v. Clark* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 89, 96–97) [S-616]. The presumption **shifts the burden of proof** to the proponent to show the will was not the product of undue influence. R-WIL-040.
- **Interested witness presumption (§ 6112(c))** and **statutory disqualified-donee presumption (§ 21380)** are separate, statutory routes: see IV.D and III.C.5.
- **Spousal transactions.** A lifetime transfer between spouses that advantages one spouse triggers the Family Code undue-influence presumption (R-CMP-016); a will is not an interspousal transaction in that sense [VERIFY V-528].

#### III.C.2 Fraud

- **Fraud in the execution (factum).** The testator is deceived about the **nature or contents** of the document signed (e.g., told it is a power of attorney). The affected provisions are invalid [common law].
- **Fraud in the inducement.** A beneficiary or other person **knowingly misrepresents a fact**, **intending** to influence the testator's disposition, and the testator **relies** on it in making the gift (the "but for" causation). The provisions induced by the fraud are invalid [common law].
- **Fraud preventing a will or a revocation.** Where fraud stops the testator from making or revoking a will, the will itself is valid but courts impose a **constructive trust** on the wrongdoer's gain [common law].
- **Tort remedy.** California recognizes a tort of intentional interference with an expected inheritance where no adequate probate remedy exists (*Beckwith v. Dahl* (2012) 205 Cal.App.4th 1039) [S-1063]. R-WIL-041.

#### III.C.3 Duress and menace

- **Duress** is physical force or threats that coerce the testamentary act; **menace** is a threat of injury to the person, property or character of the testator or a family member (compare Civ. Code §§ 1569–1570). Either invalidates the affected provisions under § 6104. R-WIL-042.

#### III.C.4 Undue influence and fraud: how to write it

1. State § 6104 and the definition.
2. Run the presumption (confidential relationship; active participation; undue benefit).
3. Weigh the § 15610.70 factors with the facts (vulnerability, authority, tactics, inequity).
4. Note § 6112 (interested witness) and § 21380 (drafter, fiduciary-transcriber, care custodian) if the facts fit.
5. Conclude which provisions fail and who takes instead (residue, intestacy, prior will via DRR).

#### III.C.5 Presumptive disqualification of certain donees (Prob. Code §§ 21360–21392)

**Author-added topic (SUP-006).** This statutory presumption is tested whenever a lawyer, caregiver or fiduciary benefits.

- **Who is presumptively disqualified (§ 21380(a)).** A provision making a donative transfer to any of the following is **presumed to be the product of fraud or undue influence**:
  1. the person who **drafted** the instrument;
  2. a person who **transcribed** the instrument or **caused it to be transcribed** and who was in a **fiduciary relationship** with the transferor (e.g., a conservator, trustee or agent under a power of attorney) when it was transcribed (*Rice v. Clark* read "causes to be transcribed" narrowly, as requiring direct participation in the transcription) [S-616];
  3. a **care custodian** of a transferor who is a **dependent adult**, if the instrument was executed while the care custodian provided services or within **90 days** before or after that period;
  4. a care custodian who began a **marriage, cohabitation or domestic partnership** with the dependent adult while providing services or within **90 days** after services were last provided, if the transfer occurred or the instrument was executed **less than six months** after that relationship began (added by AB 328 (2019)) [S-1052];
  5. a person related by blood or affinity **within the third degree** to a person in the first categories;
  6. a **cohabitant or employee** of such a person;
  7. a **partner, shareholder or employee of a law firm** in which the drafter or transcriber has an ownership interest [S-645].
- **Rebuttal (§ 21380(b)).** The presumption affects the **burden of proof** and is rebutted only by **clear and convincing evidence** that the transfer was not the product of fraud or undue influence. For the **drafter** and persons related to or associated with the drafter, the presumption is **conclusive** (§ 21380(c)) [S-936]. A beneficiary who fails to rebut it bears **all costs, including reasonable attorney's fees** [S-645].
- **Exceptions (§ 21382).** The presumption does not apply to: a transfer to a person related to the transferor **within the fourth degree**, or the transferor's **cohabitant** (but since AB 328 (2019) this exception does not shelter a care custodian who married or began cohabiting with the dependent adult within the paragraph 4 window [S-1052]); an instrument **drafted or transcribed** by such a relative or cohabitant; an instrument **approved by court order** under the substituted-judgment statute (§ 2580); transfers to **public entities** and qualifying tax-exempt organizations such as **§ 501(c)(3)** charities; a transfer worth **$5,000 or less** if the transferor's estate is at least the § 13100 small-estate amount; and an instrument executed **outside California** by a non-resident [S-621]. R-WIL-096.
- **Certificate of independent review (§ 21384).** The presumption does not apply if an **independent attorney** counsels the transferor **outside the presence of** heirs and proposed beneficiaries about the nature and consequences of the transfer, attempts to determine whether it is the product of fraud or undue influence, and signs a **certificate of independent review**. The drafting attorney generally cannot certify [S-635].
- **Care custodian (§ 21362).** A person who provides **health or social services** to a dependent adult. An **unpaid** helper is not a care custodian if he or she had a **personal relationship** with the dependent adult at least **90 days before** providing services, at least **six months before** the death, and before any hospice admission [S-633]. The Legislature added this exception after *Bernard v. Foley* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 794 held that unpaid friends who provided care could be care custodians under the former statute [S-617]. **Dependent adult (§ 21366)** means a person who, **when the instrument was executed**, was 65 or older, or 18 or older, and could not provide properly for personal needs (health, food, clothing, shelter) or, because of deficits in mental function, had difficulty managing finances or resisting fraud or undue influence [S-1053] (for adults under 65 the mental-function prong may require "substantial" difficulty [VERIFY V-913]). R-WIL-097.
- **Effect (§ 21386).** If the transfer fails, the instrument operates **as if the beneficiary had predeceased the transferor without spouse, domestic partner or issue** — so anti-lapse cannot save the gift for the beneficiary's children [S-646]. R-WIL-098.
- **Scope.** The current part applies to instruments that became irrevocable on or after **January 1, 2011** (§ 21392(a)); former §§ 21350–21356 govern older instruments [S-1054]. R-WIL-095.
- **How tested.** Feb 2019 Q1 (codicil benefiting conservator Sid) [VERIFY V-071].

### III.D Testamentary intent and conditional wills (§ 6105)

- **Testamentary intent.** The testator must intend **this document** to make a revocable disposition effective at death. A letter or note that merely describes future plans is not a will; extrinsic evidence is admissible on the question (§ 6111.5) [common law; § 6111.5].
- **Conditional wills (§ 6105).** A will whose validity is made conditional **by its own terms** is admitted or refused according to whether the condition occurs [S-613]. Courts prefer to read "if I die on this trip" as stating the **motive** for making the will rather than a condition, unless the language clearly makes the will's operation depend on the event [common law] [VERIFY V-540]. R-WIL-043.
- **Sham wills.** A will executed as a joke or without intent to make a disposition is not a will [common law].

- **Scope.** Division 11 applies to wills, trusts, deeds and other instruments (§ 21101). Its rules are **defaults**: the transferor's intention, as expressed in the instrument, controls (§ 21102(a)).

#### III.E.1 Intent controls; plain meaning and extrinsic evidence

- **Intent (§ 21102).** The intention of the transferor **as expressed in the instrument** controls; the construction rules apply only where the instrument does not show the intention; nothing limits the use of extrinsic evidence where otherwise authorized (§ 21102(c)). R-WIL-044.
- **Extrinsic evidence (§ 6111.5).** Extrinsic evidence is admissible "to determine the meaning of a will or a portion of a will if the meaning is unclear."
- **Ambiguity.** The court may consider extrinsic evidence **provisionally** to decide whether the language is reasonably susceptible to more than one meaning (a **latent** ambiguity), and if so, to resolve it; the old rule barring extrinsic evidence for "patent" ambiguities no longer controls (*Estate of Russell* (1968) 69 Cal.2d 200 — "to Roxy Russell", the testator's dog) [common law as stated in Russell].
- **Exam pattern.** Feb 2025 Q2: does "my coin collection" include medals kept in the coin album? Admit evidence of how the testator described and stored the items; resolve the latent ambiguity; one selected answer concluded the medals probably did not pass [VERIFY V-071].

#### III.E.2 Constructional preferences (§§ 21120–21122)

- Give every expression **some effect**; prefer a construction that **avoids intestacy** or failure of a gift (§ 21120).
- Read **all parts together** as a consistent whole (§ 21121).
- Give words their **ordinary and grammatical meaning**; technical words are not necessarily given a technical meaning if the instrument was drawn by a transferor unfamiliar with that sense (§ 21122). R-WIL-045.

#### III.E.3 Effect of later events (cross-reference)

- What happens when a beneficiary dies, property is sold, or the estate is too small is taught in **Part V.2 (Changes occurring after execution)**: lapse and anti-lapse (§§ 21109–21111), class gifts (§ 21115), classification (§ 21117), ademption (§§ 21131–21139), abatement (§§ 21400–21403).

#### III.E.4 Mistake and reformation of wills

**Author-added topic (SUP-004).**

- **Traditional rule.** Courts would not reform an **unambiguous** will for mistake; extrinsic evidence could only resolve ambiguity (*Estate of Barnes* (1965), disapproved).
- **California rule (*Estate of Duke* (2015) 61 Cal.4th 871).** An unambiguous will **may be reformed** to conform to the testator's intent if **clear and convincing evidence** establishes (1) that the will contains a **mistake in the expression** of the testator's intent at the time it was drafted, and (2) the testator's **actual specific intent** at that time. In *Duke* the will provided for the testator's wife and for charities if they died "at the same time"; the wife predeceased, and the charities were allowed to prove the testator's intent to benefit them rather than his heirs. R-WIL-046.
- **Mistake in the inducement** (the testator acted on a wrong belief about facts, e.g., that a child was dead) generally gives no relief unless a statute applies (§ 21622, omitted child believed dead) or the mistake and the intended disposition appear on the face of the will [common law].
- **Mistake in execution** (spouses sign each other's mirror wills) — courts may correct by reformation under *Duke* principles where the evidence is clear and convincing [VERIFY V-530].
- **Essay use.** When a will's words produce an obviously unintended result (a missing gift-over, a wrong name, a typo in an amount), state *Duke*, the clear-and-convincing standard, and both required showings.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC courts interpret wills by the testator's true intent (真实意思) much as *Duke* now allows; the difference is California's explicit clear-and-convincing evidence requirement (approx.).

### IV.A–E Witnessed wills; holographic wills; extrinsic evidence; witnesses; choice of law as to execution

**This is the most-tested Wills topic** (Jul 2013 Q5, Feb 2019 Q1, Jul 2021 Q5, Jul 2022 Q5; also Oct 2020 Q3) [VERIFY V-071]. On every Wills essay, start by testing the will's formal validity.

#### IV.A Witnessed (attested) wills (§ 6110)

- **Writing (§ 6110(a)).** The will must be in writing.
- **Signature (§ 6110(b)).** Signed (1) by the **testator**; (2) in the testator's name by **another person in the testator's presence and by the testator's direction**; or (3) by a **conservator** under a § 2580 court order. A mark, initials or a nickname suffices if intended as a signature [common law]. California has no rule that the signature must be at the end [VERIFY V-531].
- **Witnesses (§ 6110(c)(1)).** Signed **during the testator's lifetime** by **at least two persons**, each of whom:
  - (A) **being present at the same time**, witnessed **either** the testator's signing **or** the testator's **acknowledgment** of the signature or of the will; **and**
  - (B) **understands that the instrument is the testator's will**.
- **What California does not require.** No requirement that the witnesses sign **in the testator's presence** or in each other's presence; no requirement that the testator "publish" (declare) the will in words; no attestation clause (though one creates a presumption of due execution); no notarization. A notary's acknowledgment does **not** substitute for a witness [common law; S-634]. R-WIL-047.

#### IV.A.1 Harmless error for defective witnessing (§ 6110(c)(2))

- **Rule.** If the will was **not** executed in compliance with the witnessing requirements of § 6110(c)(1), it is treated as properly executed if the proponent establishes by **clear and convincing evidence** that, **at the time the testator signed the will**, the testator **intended the will to constitute his or her will** (effective Jan 1, 2009).
- **Limits.** The cure reaches **only witnessing defects** (no witnesses, one witness, witnesses not present together, witness signing after death). It does **not** excuse the requirements of a **writing** or the **testator's signature**. A typed, unsigned document cannot be saved by § 6110(c)(2) [statutory text].
- **Example case.** *Estate of Stoker* (2011) upheld an unwitnessed will dictated by the decedent and signed by him, on clear and convincing evidence of intent, and affirmed the finding that he revoked earlier documents [S-620] [VERIFY V-507]. R-WIL-048.
- **Exam pattern.** Jul 2021 Q5: a downloaded "self-made form will" — test § 6110 (how many witnesses, present together?), then § 6110(c)(2), then § 6111 (holographic), then § 6113 (where executed) [VERIFY V-071].

#### IV.B Holographic wills (§ 6111)

- **Rule (§ 6111(a)).** A will that does not comply with § 6110 is valid as a holographic will, **whether or not witnessed**, if **the signature and the material provisions are in the testator's handwriting**.
- **Material provisions** = **who** takes **what** (beneficiaries and the property given). Printed or typed words that are not material are disregarded as surplusage if the handwritten words make sense alone (*Estate of Black* (1982) 30 Cal.3d 880, holographic will written partly on preprinted stationery upheld).
- **Printed forms (§ 6111(c)).** A statement of **testamentary intent** may be in the testator's handwriting **or** part of a **commercially printed form will**. So a fill-in form is a valid holograph if the testator **handwrote the dispositive terms and signed** it.
- **Signature placement.** The signature may appear anywhere (including in the opening "I, Mary Smith, declare...") if the testator intended it as a signature [common law] [VERIFY V-531].
- **Witnesses unnecessary.** Extra witness signatures do no harm. R-WIL-050.

#### IV.B.1 Holographic will dating (§ 6111(b))

- **No date required** for validity. But if the holographic will is **undated** or the date is incomplete, it is **invalid if**: (1) it is **inconsistent with another will** unless the time of its execution is shown to be **after** the other will; or (2) it is established that the testator **lacked capacity at any time during which the will might have been executed**, unless it is shown that the will was executed at a time when the testator **had** capacity (§ 6111(b)(2)). R-WIL-051.
- **Exam pattern.** Jul 2022 Q5: an undated, unwitnessed handwritten will — valid holograph if signature and material provisions are handwritten; the missing date matters only if there is an inconsistent will or a period of incapacity [VERIFY V-071].

#### IV.B.2 Handwritten changes to a typed will

- A handwritten interlineation on a typed attested will is **not** part of the attested will (it was added after execution and was not witnessed). It is effective **only if** it qualifies on its own as a **holographic codicil**: the **material provisions** of the change and a **signature** (which may be a new signature or initials intended as a signature, with the testator's handwriting supplying context) are in the testator's handwriting [common law + § 6111] [VERIFY V-532].
- If the change fails as a holograph, ask (1) whether the crossing-out was a **partial revocation by physical act** (VI.B) and (2) whether **DRR** disregards that revocation (VI.F). R-WIL-055.
- **Exam pattern.** Jul 2013 Q5 (handwritten changes after remarriage); Feb 2025 Q2 ($10,000 changed to $20,000) [VERIFY V-071].

#### IV.C Extrinsic evidence (§ 6111.5)

- **Rule.** Extrinsic evidence is admissible to determine (1) **whether a document constitutes a will** under § 6110 or § 6111, and (2) the **meaning** of a will or a portion of it if the meaning is unclear. So the testator's statements, drafting notes and conduct are admissible on testamentary intent, a holograph's date or the meaning of a term. R-WIL-052.

#### IV.D Witnesses (§ 6112)

- **Competence (§ 6112(a)).** Any person **generally competent to be a witness** may witness a will.
- **Interested witness (§ 6112(b)–(c)).** A will is **not invalid** because an interested witness signed it. But unless there are **at least two other disinterested subscribing witnesses**, a devise to a subscribing witness creates a **presumption** that the witness procured the devise by **duress, menace, fraud or undue influence**. The presumption **affects the burden of proof**. It does not apply to a devise to the witness **solely in a fiduciary capacity** (e.g., as executor or trustee).
- **Effect if not rebutted (§ 6112(d)).** The interested witness takes **only so much of the gift as does not exceed** the share he or she would receive if the will were not established (typically the intestate share). R-WIL-049.
- **Contrast.** Common-law "purging" statutes voided the gift automatically; California only presumes wrongdoing.

#### IV.E Choice of law as to execution (§ 6113)

- **Rule.** A written will is validly executed if its execution complies with **any** of: (a) the law **at the time of execution** of the **place where the will is executed**; (b) the law of the place where, **at the time of execution or at the time of death**, the testator is **domiciled, has a place of abode, or is a national**; or (c) **California law**.
- **Exam pattern.** Feb 2019 Q1: a will with one witness, valid under State X law where executed — valid in California under § 6113(a) even though it fails § 6110 [VERIFY V-071]. Jul 2021 Q5: a form will completed while the testator lived in another state. R-WIL-053.

#### IV.F California statutory will (§§ 6200–6248)

- The Probate Code provides a fill-in **California Statutory Will** form (§ 6240). It must be executed with **two witnesses**, and § 6221 requires each witness to sign **in the testator's presence**; witnesses need no longer sign in each other's presence (AB 1986, 2010) [S-634] [VERIFY V-513]. A statutory will that is defectively executed may still be valid as an ordinary attested will (§ 6110, including § 6110(c)(2)) or as a holograph if the handwritten parts suffice. R-WIL-054.

## V Component parts of will

### V.A Integration

- **Rule.** All **papers present at the time of execution** that the testator **intended to be part of the will** are integrated into it. Intent is shown by **physical connection** (stapled, bound) or by **internal coherence** (sentences running from page to page, consistent numbering) [common law]. R-WIL-056.
- **Problem cases.** A page substituted after execution is not integrated; the original page (if it can be proved) controls, and the substituted page is effective only if it independently qualifies (e.g., as a holograph).

### V.B Codicils and republication by codicil

- **Requirements of a codicil.** A codicil is a "will" (§ 88) and must be executed with the formalities of an **attested** will (§ 6110) or a **holographic** will (§ 6111). A holographic codicil may amend an attested will and vice versa [common law; § 88].
- **Republication.** A validly executed codicil **republishes** the will it refers to: the will is treated as **re-executed on the codicil's date** unless that would defeat the testator's intent [common law] [VERIFY V-519]. Consequences:
  - an **omitted spouse or child** is not "omitted" if the codicil was executed **after** the marriage or birth (VII–VIII);
  - a writing in existence on the codicil date can be **incorporated by reference** (V.C);
  - a will invalid for a **formal defect** may be validated by a later valid codicil that identifies it (republication by codicil / incorporation by reference) [common law].
- **Revocation interplay.** Revoking a will revokes its codicils; revoking a codicil alone does not revoke the will [common law]. R-WIL-057.
- **Exam pattern.** Feb 2018 Q5 (2001 will, 2006 codicil, later marriage and children) [VERIFY V-071].

### V.C Incorporation by reference (§ 6130)

- **Rule.** A writing **in existence when the will is executed** may be incorporated by reference if the **language of the will manifests this intent** and **describes the writing sufficiently to permit its identification** (§ 6130). R-WIL-058.
- **Elements.** (1) Existence at execution (or at republication by codicil); (2) intent to incorporate shown by the will; (3) sufficient description.
- **Trap.** A writing created **after** the will cannot be incorporated, unless a later codicil republishes the will after the writing exists, or § 6132 applies.
- **Holographs.** A holographic will may incorporate a typed document; the typed matter does not destroy the holograph's validity because it is incorporated, not part of the handwritten will [common law] [VERIFY V-532].

### V.D Writing disposing of tangible personal property (§ 6132)

- **Rule.** A will may refer to a writing that directs disposition of **tangible personal property** not otherwise specifically disposed of by the will (excluding money and property used primarily in a trade or business). The writing is effective if: (1) an **unrevoked will refers to** it; (2) it is **dated** and either **in the testator's handwriting or signed by the testator**; and (3) it describes the items and recipients with **reasonable certainty** [S-601] [VERIFY V-500].
- **Timing.** The writing may be prepared **before or after** the will (an exception to § 6130's existence requirement).
- **Caps.** Total value disposed of by the writing may not exceed **$25,000**, and any item worth **more than $5,000** may not pass by it [S-601].
- **Conflicts.** If writings are inconsistent, the **most recent** controls [S-601]. R-WIL-059.

### V.E Facts (acts) of independent significance (§ 6131)

- **Rule.** A will may dispose of property **by reference to acts and events that have significance apart from their effect on the will's dispositions**, whether they occur before or after execution or before or after the testator's death. The execution or revocation of **another person's will** is such an event (§ 6131). R-WIL-060.
- **Examples.** "My car to the person who is my housekeeper at my death" (employment has independent significance); "the contents of my desk drawer" (a closer case: does the drawer have a non-testamentary function?).
- **Contrast.** An unattested list naming who gets what has **no** independent significance; it must qualify under § 6130 or § 6132.

### V.F Pour-over devises to trusts (§ 6300)

- **Rule (current text, AB 309, eff. Jan 1, 2018).** A will may devise property to the **trustee of a trust** that is **identified in the will** and whose terms are in a **written instrument executed before, concurrently with, or within 60 days after** the will (or in the valid will of a person who predeceased the testator), **regardless of the existence, size or character of the trust corpus** [S-611] [S-612].
- **Amendments.** The devise is valid even though the trust is **amendable or revocable**, and the property is administered under the trust **as amended before or after the testator's death** (and before or after the will).
- **Not a testamentary trust.** Unless the will provides otherwise, the property becomes part of the existing trust, not a separate testamentary trust.
- **Revocation of the trust.** Unless the will provides otherwise, revocation or termination of the trust **before the testator's death** causes the devise to **lapse** (applied in *Estate of Coleman* (2005), per summary) [S-611] [VERIFY V-533]. R-WIL-061.
- **Trusts crossover.** Validity, revocation and modification of the receiving revocable trust are Trusts topics (including *Haggerty v. Thornton* (2024) 15 Cal.5th 729 on modification by the statutory revocation method [S-129]); see [`../trusts/README.md`](../trusts/README.md).

## V.2 Changes occurring after execution (Division 11 rules of interpretation, continued)

Under the State Bar outline these rules sit with "Rules of Interpretation of Instruments, Sections 21105, 21109, 21110" [S-094]. They answer: **the will is valid, but something changed** — a beneficiary died, property was sold, or the estate cannot pay every gift.

### V.2.A Lapse, Anti-lapse provisions, and failed gifts (§§ 21109–21111)

- **After-acquired property (§ 21105).** The will speaks at death and passes property acquired after execution (III.B).
- **Survival requirement (§ 21109(a)).** A transferee who **fails to survive** the transferor (or until a future time the instrument requires) **does not take** under the instrument [S-622]. At common law the gift **lapses**; a gift to someone already dead at execution is **void**. R-WIL-062.
- **Anti-lapse (§ 21110(a)).** If a transferee is **dead when the instrument is executed**, or **fails or is treated as failing to survive** the transferor (or a required future time), the **issue** of the deceased transferee **take in the transferee's place** in the manner provided in § 240.
- **Who is protected (§ 21110(c)).** "Transferee" means a person who is **kindred of the transferor** or **kindred of a surviving, deceased or former spouse** of the transferor. A **spouse** is **not** kindred, so a gift to a spouse who predeceases lapses; a gift to a **friend** lapses. A stepchild is kindred of the transferor's spouse, so the stepchild's issue are protected.
- **Class gifts (§ 21110(a)).** A member of a class is a transferee for anti-lapse purposes **unless** the member died **before** the instrument was executed **and the transferor knew it**. R-WIL-063.
- **Contrary intent (§ 21110(b)).** Issue do **not** take if the instrument expresses a **contrary intention** or a **substitute disposition**. A requirement that the transferee **survive the transferor** (e.g., "to A if A survives me") or survive for a stated period, or survive to a time related to probate or administration, **is** a contrary intention. R-WIL-064.
- **Failed gifts (§ 21111).** If a gift fails for any reason (lapse not cured by § 21110, disclaimer, slayer, disqualification) and no anti-lapse substitute takes:
  1. it passes under the instrument's **alternative disposition**, if any;
  2. otherwise a **non-residuary** gift falls into the **residue**;
  3. if there is no residue, or the failed gift **is** residuary, it passes to the **estate** (intestacy).
  - **Residuary and future-interest gifts to two or more persons (§ 21111(b)).** If one share fails and no alternative is provided, it passes to the **other residuary (or future-interest) takers** in proportion to their interests. California rejects the common-law "no residue of a residue" rule. R-WIL-065.
- **Order of analysis.** (1) Did the beneficiary survive (clear and convincing evidence; § 21109)? (2) Is there a contrary intent or substitute gift? (3) Is the beneficiary kindred (§ 21110(c))? Did he or she leave issue? (4) If anti-lapse fails, apply § 21111.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: under the PRC Civil Code, a legacy to a beneficiary who predeceases the testator falls back into statutory succession, and only statutory heirs benefit from representation (代位继承); California's § 21110 is a broader "substitute gift" that protects kindred beneficiaries under a will (approx.).

### V.2.B Class gifts and class-gift terms (§ 21115)

- **Class gift.** A gift to a group described collectively ("to my children", "to my nephews and nieces") whose number may change. If a member predeceases, the survivors share the gift unless anti-lapse substitutes the member's issue (§ 21110(a)) [common law + § 21110].
- **Class closing.** At common law a class closes when a member is entitled to distribution (rule of convenience); members born or adopted later are excluded, except children in gestation [common law].
- **Who is in the class (§ 21115(a)).** Half-bloods, adopted persons, persons born out of wedlock, stepchildren, foster children and their issue are included in class gift and relationship terms **according to the rules for intestate succession** (so stepchildren and foster children are included only if § 6454 is satisfied) [S-626].
- **Adoptee of someone else (§ 21115(b)).** In construing a transfer by a transferor who is **not** the adoptive parent, a person adopted by the adoptive parent is not that parent's child unless the person **lived while a minor** as a **regular member of the adopting parent's household** (or of certain relatives' households) [S-626]. Adult adoptees who never lived in the household as minors are excluded (*Lavado v. DeLoreto*, per summary [S-626]). R-WIL-067, R-WIL-066.
- **Exam pattern.** Feb 2018 Q5: a gift to "company employees" — class gift and acts of independent significance [VERIFY V-071].

### V.2.C Classification of testamentary gifts (§ 21117)

**Author-added topic (SUP-001).**

- **Specific gift (§ 21117(a)):** a transfer of **specifically identifiable property** ("my 2019 Tesla", "my Apple shares").
- **General gift (§ 21117(b)):** a transfer from the **general assets** that does not give specific property ("$10,000 to Ann").
- **Demonstrative gift (§ 21117(c)):** a general gift that **specifies the fund or property** from which it is **primarily** to be paid ("$10,000 from my Chase savings account").
- **General pecuniary gift (§ 21117(d)):** a pecuniary (cash) gift that is a general gift. **Annuity (§ 21117(e)):** a general pecuniary gift payable periodically.
- **Residuary gift (§ 21117(f)):** property remaining after all specific and general gifts are satisfied.
- **Why it matters.** Classification drives **ademption** (only specific gifts adeem) and **abatement** (residue abates first, specific last). Courts prefer to classify a doubtful gift as **general or demonstrative** to avoid ademption (*Estate of Worthy* (1988) 205 Cal.App.3d 760) [S-644]. R-WIL-068.

### V.2.D Ademption (by extinction; by satisfaction)

**Author-added topic (SUP-002).**

#### V.2.D.1 Ademption by extinction — the California approach

- **Rule.** A **specific gift** fails (is adeemed) if the specific property is **not in the estate at death**, unless a statute or the testator's intent saves it.
- **Identity vs intent.** The traditional **identity** theory looks only at whether the item is in the estate. California is **intent-sensitive**: a **change in form** of the property does not adeem the gift **absent proof that the testator intended the gift to fail**, but a gift **is adeemed regardless of intent** where the testator disposed of the property and it **cannot be traced** into other property in the estate (*Estate of Mason* (1965) 62 Cal.2d 213) [S-615] [VERIFY V-534].
- **§ 21139.** The statutory nonademption rules (§§ 21133–21135) **are not exhaustive**, and nothing in them is intended to **increase the incidence of ademption** under California law [S-614]. R-WIL-069.
- **Essay approach.** (1) Classify the gift. (2) If specific, is the item (or traceable replacement) in the estate? (3) Statutory savings: §§ 21132, 21133, 21134. (4) Intent and tracing under *Mason*. (5) Conclude.

#### V.2.D.2 Statutory nonademption: what follows a specific gift (§ 21133)

- The recipient of a specific gift takes the property **to the extent it is in the estate at death**, **plus**: (a) any **balance of the purchase price** (with any security) owed by a buyer at death from the testator's sale of the property; (b) any **unpaid eminent domain award** for its taking; (c) any **unpaid fire or casualty insurance proceeds** or other recovery for injury to it; and (d) property obtained by **foreclosure** (or in lieu) on a specifically given secured obligation [S-629]. R-WIL-070.

#### V.2.D.3 Change in form of securities (§ 21132)

- If the testator owned the described securities when the will was executed, a specific gift of securities includes **additional securities** owned at death that came from owning them: securities of the same entity from action **initiated by the entity** (stock splits, stock dividends), securities from a **merger, consolidation or reorganization**, and securities acquired through a **plan of reinvestment**. **Cash distributions** received before death are **not** part of the gift [S-1058]. Securities bought by exercising purchase options are believed to be excluded, as under the UPC model [VERIFY V-911]. R-WIL-071.

#### V.2.D.4 Ademption by satisfaction (§ 21135)

- **Rule.** A **lifetime gift** to a beneficiary counts as satisfaction of an at-death gift, in whole or part, **only if**: (1) the **instrument** provides for deduction; (2) the transferor declares in a **contemporaneous writing** that the gift satisfies the at-death gift or is to be deducted; (3) the **transferee acknowledges in writing** that it does; or (4) the lifetime gift is **the same property** that is the subject of a specific gift [S-637].
- **Valuation.** As of the earlier of the transferee's possession or enjoyment and the transferor's death, unless the contemporaneous writing states a value, which is then conclusive.
- **Predeceasing transferee.** If the transferee fails to survive, the gift is still treated as a satisfaction (binding substitute takers) unless the writing provides otherwise [VERIFY V-535].
- **Writing.** No particular form or signature is required; a handwritten ledger of distributions kept with the decedent's papers sufficed in *Sachs v. Sachs* (2020) [S-638]. R-WIL-072.
- **Parallel.** § 6409 (advancements) is the intestacy version (II.C).

#### V.2.D.5 Exoneration (§ 21131)

- A specific gift passes **subject to any mortgage, deed of trust or other lien** existing at death, **without right of exoneration**, regardless of a general directive in the will to pay debts [S-629]. If the will expressly requires exoneration, other specific gifts do not abate to pay it (§ 21404). R-WIL-073.

### V.2.E Conservatorship effects on testamentary gifts (conservator's acts; ademption/abatement interplay)

**Past-exam row (Feb 2015 Q6).** The Phase 3 index tied Feb 2015 Q6 (a will leaving property in trust for grandchildren with remainder to a zoo, followed by a conservatorship) to the effect of the conservatorship on the estate plan; the exact conservator's acts were not captured [VERIFY V-071].

- **Sale by a conservator, agent or trustee (§ 21134).** If, after the instrument is executed, **specifically given property** is **sold** by a **conservator**, by an **agent under a durable power of attorney** for an incapacitated principal, or by a **trustee acting for an incapacitated settlor**, the transferee of the specific gift has the right to a **general pecuniary gift equal to the net sale price**, **unreduced** by the payoff of any encumbrance the conservator, agent or trustee placed on the property after execution [S-602] [S-603].
  - **Encumbrance.** If such a fiduciary **encumbers** the property, the transferee takes the property plus a general pecuniary gift equal to the unpaid encumbrance [S-603].
  - **Condemnation and casualty.** If an eminent domain award, insurance proceeds or recovery for the property is paid to the conservator, agent or trustee, the transferee has a general pecuniary gift equal to the award or proceeds, likewise unreduced [S-602].
  - **Recovery exception (conservators only).** As to a conservator's acts, the rule does not apply if, after the sale or loss, the **conservatorship is terminated** and the transferor **survives the termination by one year** (the testator had a chance to change the will) (§ 21134(c)) [S-1050]. The statute states this exception only for conservators, not for agents or trustees.
  - **Offset.** The transferee's § 21134 right is reduced by any right the transferee has under § 21133 [S-1051].
  - **Trusts.** The rule extends to trustees acting for an incapacitated settlor; *Brown v. Labow* (2007) had applied § 21134 to trusts by inference before the 2012 amendment (AB 1985) made it express [S-603].
  - **Background.** *Estate of Mason* (1965) 62 Cal.2d 213 had already held that a guardian's sale during an incompetency from which the testator never recovered did not adeem the gift [S-615]. R-WIL-074.
- **Abatement interplay.** Because § 21134 converts the specific gift into a **general pecuniary gift**, it abates with **general** gifts, not specific gifts, if the estate is short (§ 21402) [statutory inference from §§ 21117(b), (d), 21402].
- **Conservatee's own will.** A conservatee keeps the right to make a will (§ 1871(c)); capacity is tested at execution under § 6100.5 (III.A) [S-641].
- **Conservator's will for the conservatee.** Only with a **§ 2580 substituted-judgment order**; the conservator signs (§ 6110(b)(3)); a competent conservatee may revoke it (§ 6100) [S-642]. A court-approved instrument is exempt from the § 21380 presumption (§ 21382) [S-621].
- **Gifts to the conservator.** A conservator who drafts, transcribes or causes the transcription of an instrument benefiting himself is a presumptively disqualified donee (§ 21380(a)(2)); the common-law undue-influence presumption also fits (confidential relationship; active participation; undue benefit) — Feb 2019 Q1 [VERIFY V-071].

### V.2.F Abatement

**Author-added topic (SUP-003).**

- **When.** The estate's assets are insufficient to pay debts, expenses and all gifts.
- **Order (§ 21402(a)).** Unless the instrument or the transferor's plan requires otherwise, shares abate in this order:
  1. property **not disposed of** by the instrument (intestate property);
  2. **residuary** gifts;
  3. **general** gifts to persons **other than** the transferor's **relatives**;
  4. **general** gifts to the transferor's **relatives**;
  5. **specific** gifts to persons **other than** relatives;
  6. **specific** gifts to **relatives** [S-604].
- **Relative (§ 21402(b))** means a person who would take under the intestacy statutes (§§ 6401, 6402) [S-604].
- **Pro rata within a class (§ 21403(a)).** Gifts in the same class abate proportionally.
- **Demonstrative gifts.** Treated as **specific** to the extent paid from the designated fund or property and as **general** for any balance (§ 21403(b)) [S-1055].
- **No real/personal priority.** Abatement is believed to apply without any priority between real and personal property (§ 21401) [VERIFY V-912].
- **Contrary intent.** If the instrument provides for abatement, or the transferor's plan or the purpose of a gift would be defeated by the statutory order, shares abate as necessary to give effect to that instrument, plan or purpose (§ 21400) [S-1055]. R-WIL-075, R-WIL-076.
- **Omitted spouse/child shares** are funded under their own rules (§§ 21612, 21623): first from property not disposed of, then pro rata from all beneficiaries (VII–VIII).

**Worked numbers.** Estate (after debts) $100,000. Will: "My painting (worth $40,000) to my friend F; $50,000 to my niece N; $30,000 to the Red Cross; residue to my son S." Residue is zero (S takes nothing). General gifts total $80,000 but only $60,000 is left after the painting. The Red Cross (non-relative, general) abates first: it takes $10,000 ($30,000 reduced by $20,000). N (relative, general) takes $50,000. F keeps the painting.

## VI Revocation and revival of wills

### VI.0 Revocation, Sections 6120, 6121, 6123 (statutory map)

| Section | Rule |
|---|---|
| § 6120 | Revocation by a subsequent will (express or inconsistent) or by physical act with intent, by the testator or another in the testator's presence and at the testator's direction |
| § 6121 | Physical act on one duplicate revokes the will |
| § 6122 | Dissolution or annulment revokes provisions for the former spouse |
| § 6123 | Revival of a revoked will |
| § 6124 | Presumption of revocation when a will last in the testator's possession cannot be found |

**How tested.** Jul 2012 Q5 (revocation and revival), Feb 2025 Q2 (altered bequest) [VERIFY V-071].

### VI.A Revocation by subsequent will (§ 6120(a))

- **Rule.** A will or any part of it is revoked by a **subsequent will** that revokes the prior will or part **expressly** or **by inconsistency**. The later instrument must itself be a **valid will** (attested or holographic). R-WIL-077.
- **Express revocation.** "I revoke all prior wills and codicils."
- **Inconsistency.** Without a revocation clause, a later will revokes the earlier one only **to the extent of the inconsistency**; a later will that disposes of the **entire estate** revokes the earlier will entirely. Read both together where possible [common law].
- **Codicil.** A codicil revokes only inconsistent provisions.

### VI.B Revocation by physical act (§ 6120(b))

- **Rule.** A will or any part of it is revoked by being **burned, torn, canceled, obliterated or destroyed**, **with the intent and for the purpose of revoking it**, by **(1) the testator** or **(2) another person in the testator's presence and by the testator's direction**. R-WIL-078.
- **Both act and intent.** An act without intent (accidental burning) does not revoke; intent without an act (telling a friend "my will is void") does not revoke.
- **Proxy revocation.** Destruction by another person **outside the testator's presence** is ineffective even if the testator asked for it; the will (if provable) stands [statutory text].
- **Cancellation.** Words such as "VOID" or "revoked" written across the text, or lines drawn through provisions, with intent to revoke, are cancellation (*Estate of Uhl* (1969) 1 Cal.App.3d 138: cancellation and interlineation must be done with present intent to revoke) [S-640].
- **Partial revocation.** California allows **partial revocation by physical act** ("a will or any part thereof") [S-640]. R-WIL-079.
- **Duplicates (§ 6121).** If a will was executed in **duplicate**, a revocatory act on **one** duplicate revokes the will. R-WIL-080.
- **Lost or missing wills (§ 6124).** If the will was **last in the testator's possession**, the testator was **competent until death**, and **neither the will nor a duplicate original can be found** after death, it is **presumed** the testator **destroyed it with intent to revoke**. This is a presumption **affecting the burden of producing evidence**: once the proponent offers evidence that the testator did not destroy it (e.g., a hostile heir had access; the testator recently reaffirmed the will), the presumption disappears and the trier of fact decides (*Estate of Trikha* (2013) 219 Cal.App.4th 791) [S-600] [S-643]. If the presumption is overcome, a lost will may be **proved by a copy** and testimony of its contents. The statute speaks only of a will that **cannot be found**. Where a will that stayed in the testator's possession is found **torn or mutilated** among the testator's papers after death, case law infers that the **testator** made the marks (*Estate of Holmes*, 88 Cal.App.2d 360) [S-1065]; whether the testator **intended to revoke** is then decided on all the evidence (the circumstances of the act and the testator's statements). R-WIL-081.

### VI.C Interlineations and partial revocation (essay pattern)

1. **Who made the change?** Only the testator, or another in the testator's presence at the testator's direction, can revoke by act. Changes by a beneficiary are ineffective.
2. **Was the crossing-out a partial revocation?** Act + intent → the struck gift is revoked.
3. **Is the new handwritten text a valid holographic codicil?** Material provisions + signature in the testator's hand (IV.B.2). If not, the new gift fails.
4. **Apply DRR (VI.F).** If the testator revoked the old gift **only because** he believed the new gift would be valid, disregard the revocation and give the old gift.
5. **Example (Feb 2025 Q2 pattern).** T strikes "$10,000" and writes "$20,000" for niece N, unsigned. The new $20,000 gift fails (no signature). The strike was a partial revocation, but DRR restores the $10,000 because T plainly preferred $10,000 to nothing [S-640] [VERIFY V-071].

### VI.D Revocation by operation of law: dissolution or annulment (§ 6122)

- **Rule (§ 6122(a)).** Unless the will **expressly provides otherwise**, if after executing a will the testator's marriage is **dissolved or annulled**, the dissolution or annulment **revokes**: (1) any **disposition or appointment of property** to the **former spouse**; (2) any provision conferring a **power of appointment** on the former spouse; and (3) any **nomination of the former spouse as executor, trustee, conservator or guardian**.
- **Effect (§ 6122(c)).** The will is read **as if the former spouse failed to survive** the testator, so the property passes to the alternate taker or the residue (anti-lapse does not help the former spouse, who is not kindred).
- **Remarriage** to the former spouse **revives** the revoked provisions (§ 6122(b)).
- **Legal separation** that does not end the marriage is **not** a dissolution (§ 6122(d)).
- **Only the former spouse.** The statute reaches gifts to the **former spouse**; gifts to the former spouse's relatives are not revoked by § 6122 [statutory text].
- **No other change of circumstances** revokes a will (§ 6122(e)); marriage and birth of children are handled by the omitted spouse and child statutes (VII–VIII). Registered domestic partnerships: § 6122.1. R-WIL-082.
- **Nonprobate transfers (§ 5040).** A **nonprobate transfer** to a former spouse (POD designation, TOD deed, revocable trust gift) in an instrument executed before or during the marriage **fails** if, at death, the former spouse is not the surviving spouse because of dissolution or annulment — unless (1) the transfer was **not revocable** by the transferor at death, (2) there is **clear and convincing evidence** the transferor intended to preserve it, or (3) a **court order** required it to be maintained. The instrument is treated as if the former spouse **failed to survive** [S-605]. A **joint tenancy** with the former spouse created before or during the marriage is **severed** at death as to the decedent's interest on similar terms (unless it was not severable by the decedent at death, or clear and convincing evidence shows intent to preserve it); CP with right of survivorship is treated the same (§ 5042) [S-1059]. **ERISA** plan designations follow federal law (*Egelhoff*; R-CMP-101). R-WIL-083.

### VI.E Revival (§ 6123)

- **Revoking the revoking will by physical act (§ 6123(a)).** If a second will that would have revoked the first (wholly or partly) is itself **revoked by physical act**, the first will **stays revoked unless** it is **evident from the circumstances** of the revocation of the second will, or from the testator's **contemporaneous or subsequent declarations**, that the testator **intended the first will to take effect** as executed.
- **Revoking by a third will (§ 6123(b)).** If the second will is revoked by a **third will**, the first will stays revoked **except to the extent** the **terms of the third will** show the testator intended the first will to take effect.
- **Contrast.** The old English rule revived the first will automatically; strict anti-revival states never revive without re-execution. California's rule is **intent-based**, with different evidence allowed for each method. R-WIL-084.
- **If no revival:** intestacy for the affected property, unless **DRR** applies.

### VI.F Dependent relative revocation

- **Rule.** If a testator revokes a will (or a gift) **on the mistaken assumption** that another disposition will be effective, and the testator **would not have revoked** but for that mistake, the revocation is **disregarded** and the revoked will (or gift) is given effect, provided that result is **closer to the testator's intent than the alternative** (usually intestacy). California applies the doctrine (*Estate of Kaufman* (1945) 25 Cal.2d 854: an earlier will revoked only to give effect to a later one, on the supposition the later will is effective, remains effective to the extent the later one fails) [S-619]. R-WIL-085.
- **Elements.** (1) A revocation (by act or instrument). (2) A mistake of law or fact about another disposition (the new will or interlineation is invalid; a fact recited is false). (3) The revocation was **conditional** on that mistaken belief. (4) Applying DRR comes **closer** to the testator's intent than the result without it.
- **Typical uses.** (a) T tears up will 1 intending to execute will 2, which turns out invalid; (b) T crosses out a gift and writes in a larger one without signing (VI.C); (c) T revokes will 2 believing that will 1 will revive, but § 6123 does not revive it.
- **Limits.** DRR does not apply if the testator would have revoked anyway, or if giving effect to the old disposition is further from intent than intestacy. Courts have applied DRR to ineffective interlineations changing relatives' shares (*Estate of Uhl* (1969) 1 Cal.App.3d 138) [S-640].

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Civil Code treats a later inconsistent will as revoking an earlier one and allows revocation by the testator's acts; it has no named equivalent of DRR, which is a common-law salvage doctrine based on presumed intent (approx.).

## Family Protection: Omitted Spouses and Children (Omitted Spouses, Sections 21610-21612; Omitted Children, Sections 21620-21623)

The State Bar outline lists these rules "for decedents dying on or after January 1, 1998" [S-094]. Shared definitions:

- **Testamentary instruments (§ 21601(a))** means the decedent's **will or revocable trust**. So a spouse or child can be "omitted" from a **trust-based plan** (Feb 2012 Q1: child not provided for in a revocable trust) [VERIFY V-071].
- **Estate (§ 21601(b))** includes the probate estate **and** property in any revocable trust that becomes irrevocable at death. Nonprobate assets such as IRAs passing by beneficiary designation are **not** included (*Reich v. Reich* (Cal. Ct. App. 2024, certified for publication): the court affirmed orders refusing to include IRA proceeds in computing the omitted spouse's share) [S-623]. R-WIL-089.
- **Timing.** The question is whether the marriage or birth/adoption came **after the execution of all** testamentary instruments. A **codicil** or trust amendment executed after the marriage or birth defeats the claim (republication; V.B).
- **Satisfying the share (§§ 21612, 21623).** The share is taken **first from estate property not disposed of** by will or trust; if that is not enough, **pro rata from all beneficiaries** of the testamentary instruments in proportion to the values they receive (values as of death); the court may vary the order where needed to carry out the decedent's plan [VERIFY V-536]. R-WIL-088.

## VII Omitted spouses

### VII.A Share of a spouse married after execution (§ 21610)

- **Rule.** If the decedent **fails to provide** in a testamentary instrument for a surviving spouse who **married the decedent after the execution of all** the decedent's testamentary instruments, the omitted spouse receives:
  - (a) the **one-half of the CP** that belongs to the decedent (§ 100);
  - (b) the **one-half of the QCP** that belongs to the decedent (§ 101); and
  - (c) a share of the decedent's **SP equal to the intestate share** (§ 6401(c)), **but not more than one-half** of the SP. R-WIL-086.
- **"Fails to provide."** Any provision for the person in the instrument (even one made before marriage, e.g., to a fiancée by name) means the spouse was provided for [common law reading] [VERIFY V-537].
- **Worked numbers.** H's 2015 will leaves everything to his brother. H marries W in 2020 and dies in 2026 with CP of $400,000 and SP of $600,000, no issue, parents dead, brother alive. W keeps her $200,000 CP half and takes H's $200,000 CP half. Her intestate SP share with a surviving sibling would be one-half ($300,000), and the cap is one-half, so she takes $300,000. The brother takes the remaining $300,000 of SP.

### VII.B Circumstances where the spouse takes no share (§ 21611)

The spouse takes **nothing** under § 21610 if any of these is established:

1. **Intentional omission on the face of the instruments.** The failure to provide was **intentional** and the intention **appears from the testamentary instruments**. A **general disinheritance clause** that does not mention the spouse or a future marriage is **not enough** (*Estate of Shannon* (1990) 224 Cal.App.3d 1148, decided under the predecessor statute) [S-618].
2. **Provision outside the will in lieu.** The decedent provided for the spouse by **transfer outside** the testamentary instruments (life insurance, joint tenancy, POD account) **and** the intent that the transfer be **in lieu** of a testamentary provision is shown by the decedent's statements, the **amount** of the transfer, or other evidence.
3. **Valid waiver.** The spouse made a **valid agreement waiving** the right to share in the estate (§§ 140–147; premarital agreement) (I.B.1).
4. **Care-custodian spouse (AB 328, 2019).** The spouse was the decedent's **care custodian**, the decedent was a **dependent adult**, and the decedent **died within six months** of the marriage, unless the spouse proves by **clear and convincing evidence** that the marriage was not the product of fraud or undue influence (§ 21611(d)) [S-1052]. R-WIL-087.

### VII.C Manner of satisfying the share (§ 21612)

- First from property **not disposed of** by the testamentary instruments; then **pro rata** from the beneficiaries; court may adjust to preserve the plan (see the shared rules above). R-WIL-088.
- **Exam pattern.** Jul 2013 Q5, Feb 2018 Q5, Jul 2021 Q5 (later spouses) [VERIFY V-071]. Cross-reference R-CMP-097.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC law has no "omitted spouse" rule because a spouse is always a first-order statutory heir and a will simply overrides that; California presumes the testator forgot the new spouse and gives a statutory share unless a § 21611 exception applies (approx.).

## VIII Omitted children

### VIII.A Share of a child born or adopted after execution (§ 21620)

- **Rule (§ 21620).** If the decedent fails to provide in a testamentary instrument for a **child born or adopted after the execution of all** the decedent's testamentary instruments, the omitted child receives a share **equal in value to what the child would have received if the decedent had died without any testamentary instrument** (the intestate share). R-WIL-090.
- **Children alive at execution** are **not** omitted children (the testator is presumed to have meant to leave them out), except under § 21622.
- **Compute from the whole estate as if intestate.** If the decedent left a spouse, the spouse would take all the CP in intestacy, so the omitted child's share usually comes only from **SP** (one-third or one-half split among children).
- **Worked numbers.** W's 2016 will leaves everything to her husband H and her daughter D equally; son S is born in 2019; W dies in 2026 with CP $600,000 and SP $300,000. In intestacy H would take all CP and 1/3 of SP; D and S would split 2/3 of SP. S's omitted-child share = 1/3 of SP = $100,000. But see § 21621(b) below: if substantially all went to S's mother, S takes nothing.

### VIII.B Denial of share (§ 21621)

The child takes **nothing** under § 21620 if any of these is established:

1. **Intentional omission on the face of the instruments** — the failure to provide was intentional and appears from the testamentary instruments ("I intentionally make no provision for any child born after this will").
2. **Substantially all to the other parent.** The decedent had **one or more children** when the instrument was executed and **devised or otherwise directed the disposition of substantially all the estate to the parent of the omitted child**.
3. **Provision outside the will in lieu.** The decedent provided for the child by **transfer outside** the instruments, with intent that it be in lieu of a testamentary provision, shown by statements, the amount of the transfer, or other evidence. R-WIL-091.

### VIII.C Child believed dead or unknown (§ 21622)

- If, when **all** testamentary instruments in effect at death were executed, the decedent **failed to provide for a living child solely because** the decedent **believed the child to be dead** or **was unaware of the child's birth**, the child receives the **intestate share**. This is the one case where a child alive at execution can claim. R-WIL-092.

### VIII.D Manner of satisfying the share (§ 21623)

- Same method as § 21612: first undisposed property, then pro rata from beneficiaries; the court may order otherwise to carry out the plan. R-WIL-088.
- **Exam pattern.** Feb 2012 Q1 (child omitted from a revocable trust), Feb 2018 Q5 (later-born child), Jul 2021 Q5 (daughter and son) [VERIFY V-071].
- **Adopted and stepchildren.** An adopted child is a child (§ 6450(b)); adoption after the will makes an omitted child. A stepchild is not the decedent's child unless adopted or § 6454 applies (II.E).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC Civil Code requires a will to reserve a necessary share (必留份) for an heir who lacks the ability to work and has no income, and reserves a share for a fetus (胎儿); California's omitted-child rule is narrower in one way (only after-born or believed-dead children) and broader in another (no need to show dependency) (approx.).

## IX No-contest clauses

**Author-added topic (SUP-005).** Prob. Code §§ 21310–21315.

- **Definitions (§ 21310).** A **no-contest clause** penalizes a beneficiary for filing a pleading. A **direct contest** alleges the invalidity of a **protected instrument** or its terms on grounds of: (1) **forgery**; (2) **lack of due execution**; (3) **lack of capacity**; (4) **menace, duress, fraud or undue influence**; (5) **revocation** (§ 6120 for wills, § 15401 for trusts, or another statutory or instrument method); or (6) **disqualification** of a beneficiary under § 6112, § 21350 or § 21380. A **protected instrument** is the instrument containing the clause and any instrument in existence when it was executed that the clause **expressly identifies**, individually or as part of an identifiable class [S-1056]. R-WIL-094.
- **When enforced (§ 21311(a)).** A no-contest clause is enforced **only** against:
  1. a **direct contest brought without probable cause**;
  2. a pleading challenging a transfer on the ground that the property **was not the transferor's** at the time of transfer — **only if the clause expressly** so provides;
  3. the filing of a **creditor's claim** or an action on it — **only if the clause expressly** so provides [S-608].
- **Probable cause (§ 21311(b)).** Exists if, at filing, the facts known to the contestant would cause a reasonable person to believe there is a **reasonable likelihood** the requested relief will be granted after an opportunity for further investigation or discovery [S-608].
- **Strict construction.** No-contest clauses are construed strictly (§ 21312) and the common law governs where the statute does not apply (§ 21313) [VERIFY V-919].
- **Applicability.** The current law (eff. Jan 1, 2010) applies to any instrument, whenever executed, that became irrevocable on or after Jan 1, 2001 (§ 21315) [S-1056]; *Donkin v. Donkin* (2013) 58 Cal.4th 412 applied the current law to a trust that became irrevocable after that date [S-609] [S-1057].
- **Essay use.** (1) Is the pleading a "contest" (a direct contest)? (2) Is the instrument protected? (3) Probable cause? (4) Result: if enforced, the contestant forfeits gifts under the instrument (they pass as the instrument directs, often as if the contestant predeceased). R-WIL-093.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC law has no direct statutory analogue to no-contest clauses; a will may impose conditions (附义务的遗嘱), but forfeiture for litigation is a common-law device (approx.).

## X Will substitutes and nonprobate transfers

**Author-added topic (SUP-007).** Much of a modern estate passes **outside the will**; the will and intestacy reach only the probate estate.

- **Validity without will formalities (§ 5000(a)).** A provision for a **nonprobate transfer on death** in an insurance policy, employment contract, bond, mortgage, promissory note, security, account agreement, pension or retirement plan, **trust**, conveyance, deed of gift, **marital property agreement** or similar written instrument **is not invalid** because it does not comply with the requirements for executing a will. R-WIL-099.
- **Joint tenancy.** Passes to the surviving joint tenant by **right of survivorship**, outside probate; the will cannot reach it. Simultaneous death: § 223 (I.C). Slayer: severed (§ 251). Former spouse: § 5042. Married couples: joint-tenancy property bought with CP funds is presumptively CP for characterization (*In re Brace* (2020) 9 Cal.5th 903), but *Brace* left the **right of survivorship at death** undisturbed: the surviving spouse takes the whole asset and the decedent's will cannot pass half, unless the spouses validly transmuted it in writing (Fam. Code § 852) into CP held without survivorship (R-CMP-014). R-WIL-100.
- **Multiple-party accounts (§§ 5301–5302).** During life, a joint account belongs to the parties in proportion to their **net contributions** unless clear and convincing evidence shows a different intent. At death, sums remaining belong to the **surviving party** (joint account), to the **POD payee** (after all parties die), or to the **beneficiary of a Totten trust account** ("A, in trust for B"), unless there is clear and convincing evidence of a different intent [VERIFY V-538]. A Totten trust is revocable during the depositor's life and needs no will formalities. R-WIL-101.
- **Revocable trusts.** A revocable living trust funded during life (or by pour-over, § 6300) passes trust property under the trust terms without probate. Trust creation, revocation and modification are Trusts topics (*Haggerty v. Thornton*, recent-changes §7) [S-129]. Omitted-spouse and omitted-child rules apply to revocable trusts (§ 21601), and so does the § 21380 presumption. R-WIL-102.
- **Revocable transfer-on-death (TOD) deed (§§ 5600 et seq.).** A transferor with testamentary capacity may designate a beneficiary of California real property by a TOD deed that is **signed, dated, acknowledged before a notary**, signed by **two witnesses** (for deeds executed on or after Jan 1, 2022; § 5624) and **recorded within 60 days** (§ 5626). It is revocable and has no effect until death [S-628] [S-767] [VERIFY V-520]. R-WIL-103.
- **Life insurance and retirement accounts.** Pass to the named **beneficiary** by contract. A will **cannot change** a beneficiary designation unless the contract allows it; a residuary clause does not reach these assets [common law]. CP rules may give the surviving spouse half (§§ 5020–5021; R-CMP-096); ERISA and federal programs follow federal law (R-CMP-101, R-CMP-102). R-WIL-104.
- **Former spouse.** Nonprobate transfers to a former spouse fail at death under § 5040 (VI.D).
- **Essay use.** In an "estate" essay, first list what passes **outside probate** (joint tenancy, POD, insurance, trust), then apply the will and intestacy to what is left; then check whether CP rules or the omitted-spouse/child rules reach the nonprobate property.

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: PRC practice also lets insurance proceeds with a named beneficiary pass outside the estate (保险金不属于遗产 when a beneficiary is named); California extends the same idea to POD accounts, TOD deeds and revocable trusts (approx.).

## XI Tort and equitable remedies around inheritance (nice-to-know)

- **Intentional interference with expected inheritance** (*Beckwith v. Dahl* (2012) 205 Cal.App.4th 1039) [S-1063]: available only where the plaintiff lacks an adequate probate remedy (in *Beckwith* the decedent died intestate and the plaintiff lacked standing in probate). Elements: expectancy; reasonable certainty it would have been realized but for the interference; intent; independently tortious conduct directed at the testator; damages. Check for later Court of Appeal decisions narrowing the tort [VERIFY V-914]. R-WIL-105.
- **Constructive trust** for fraud preventing a will or revocation, for breach of a contract to make a will (§ 21700), and for a slayer's gain (III.C.2, I.B.2, I.E).
