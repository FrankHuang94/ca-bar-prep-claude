# Wills and Succession — traps

Wills is essay-only, so these are **essay traps**: mistakes that cost points in California answers, and rules students confuse. Rule IDs point to [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md).

## Property and characterization

1. **Distributing the survivor's half.** The surviving spouse's half of CP (and half of the decedent's QCP) is never in the estate. A gift of "my house" (CP) passes only the decedent's half (R-WIL-001).
2. **Treating out-of-state land as QCP at death.** At death QCP includes only California real property; land in State X passes under State X law (§ 66; R-CMP-092). At divorce the rule differs.
3. **Forgetting the survivor's own QCP is not split.** Only the **decedent's** QCP is halved (§ 101).
4. **Writing "elective share."** California has none; protection comes from CP, QCP recapture and the omitted-spouse statute (R-WIL-002, R-WIL-086).
5. **Letting the will reach nonprobate assets.** A residuary clause does not reach joint tenancy, POD accounts or insurance (R-WIL-104).

## Execution

6. **Requiring witnesses to sign in the testator's presence.** California requires only that the two witnesses be present **at the same time** for the signing or acknowledgment (R-WIL-047). (The statutory will form is different: § 6221.)
7. **Using harmless error to save an unsigned will.** § 6110(c)(2) cures **witnessing** defects only (R-WIL-048).
8. **Calling a typed, signed, unwitnessed will "holographic."** The material provisions must be handwritten (R-WIL-050).
9. **Requiring a date on a holograph.** A date matters only if there is an inconsistent will or possible incapacity (R-WIL-051).
10. **Voiding an interested witness's gift automatically.** California only presumes wrongdoing, and the gift is cut to the intestate share only if the presumption is not rebutted (R-WIL-049).
11. **Ignoring § 6113.** A will invalid under § 6110 may be valid because it complied with the law where it was signed (R-WIL-053).
12. **Treating notarization as a witness.** A notary's acknowledgment does not satisfy § 6110.

## Validity attacks

13. **Equating a conservatorship with incapacity.** A conservatee keeps the right to make a will; capacity is tested at execution (R-WIL-036).
14. **Stopping at the common-law presumption.** Also run § 21380 (drafter, fiduciary-transcriber, care custodian) and the § 6112 interested-witness presumption (R-WIL-040, R-WIL-095).
15. **Invalidating the whole will.** Undue influence, fraud and duress void only the tainted provisions (§ 6104; R-WIL-038).
16. **Forgetting fee-shifting and the conclusive presumption for drafters** under § 21380 (R-WIL-095).
17. **Saving a disqualified donee's gift with anti-lapse.** § 21386 treats the donee as predeceased **without issue** (R-WIL-098).

## Contents and changes

18. **Incorporating a document written after the will.** § 6130 requires existence at execution (or at a republishing codicil); only § 6132 allows later writings, and only for tangible personal property within the caps (R-WIL-058, R-WIL-059).
19. **Forgetting republication.** A codicil after the marriage or birth defeats the omitted-spouse or omitted-child claim (R-WIL-057).
20. **Applying the old pour-over rule.** Since 2018, the trust instrument may be executed up to **60 days after** the will (R-WIL-061).

## Revocation

21. **Accepting a proxy destruction outside the testator's presence.** It does not revoke (R-WIL-078).
22. **Giving effect to an unsigned increase.** The new amount fails; then ask partial revocation and DRR (R-WIL-079, R-WIL-085).
23. **Automatic revival.** California revival depends on intent, proved differently for act vs. third-will revocation (R-WIL-084).
24. **Overstating § 6124.** It covers a will that **cannot be found**; it is a presumption affecting only the burden of producing evidence (R-WIL-081).
25. **Extending § 6122 to the ex-spouse's relatives.** It revokes only provisions for the former spouse; and legal separation does not trigger it (R-WIL-082). But for **intestacy**, a legal-separation judgment that ended all marital property rights **does** stop the spouse from being a surviving spouse (§ 78(d); *Estate of Lahey*; R-WIL-017).
26. **Forgetting nonprobate transfers to an ex-spouse** (§ 5040) and ERISA preemption (R-WIL-083).

## Distribution

27. **Applying anti-lapse to a friend or spouse.** Only kindred of the testator or of a spouse/former spouse are covered (R-WIL-063).
28. **Applying anti-lapse despite "if he survives me."** California treats survivorship words as a contrary intention (R-WIL-064) — the UPC does not.
29. **Sending a lapsed residuary share to intestacy.** It goes to the other residuary takers (§ 21111(b); R-WIL-065).
30. **Using the 120-hour rule for a will.** It applies only in intestacy; under a will, survival needs clear and convincing evidence (R-WIL-007, R-WIL-020).
31. **Adeeming a demonstrative or general gift.** Only specific gifts adeem (R-WIL-068, R-WIL-069).
32. **Missing § 21134.** A conservator's, agent's or trustee's sale of specifically given property gives the beneficiary the net sale price; for a conservator's sale, not if the conservatorship ended and the testator survived the termination by a year (R-WIL-074).
33. **Abating by the common-law order.** California prefers relatives within the general and specific classes and has no real/personal priority (R-WIL-075).
34. **Exonerating a mortgage because the will says "pay my debts."** § 21131 says no (R-WIL-073).

## Omitted heirs and intestacy

35. **Giving the omitted spouse all the SP.** The SP share is the intestate share **capped at one-half** (R-WIL-086).
36. **Treating a general disinheritance clause as intent to omit a later spouse.** It must appear from the instruments; a generic "I disinherit anyone not named" is not enough (*Shannon*; R-WIL-087). And do not forget the AB 328 bar on a care-custodian spouse whose dependent-adult spouse died within six months of the marriage (§ 21611).
37. **Computing the omitted child's share from SP only without explaining why.** Compute the full intestate share: with a surviving spouse, the spouse takes all CP, so the child's share usually comes from SP (R-WIL-090).
38. **Missing "substantially all to the other parent."** It defeats the omitted child (R-WIL-091).
39. **Letting a stepchild inherit because of a close relationship.** § 6454 needs a legal barrier lasting until death; equitable adoption needs intent to adopt (R-WIL-029, R-WIL-030).
40. **Giving parents a share when the decedent left issue.** In California, parents take only if there is no issue (R-WIL-018).
