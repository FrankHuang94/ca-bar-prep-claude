# Professional Responsibility — primer

## 1. What this subject is
Professional Responsibility (PR, "legal ethics") is the law governing lawyers. It answers one question: **what does a lawyer owe, and to whom?**
- The main duties run to **clients**: loyalty, confidentiality, competence, communication, honest fees, care of money.
- Others run to **courts** (candor), **opponents and third parties** (fairness, truthfulness), and **the profession** (no dishonesty; reporting misconduct).

On the California bar, PR is the **single most predictable essay**: 30 of the last 31 exams. Almost every call says "Answer according to California **and** ABA authorities." Your job is to spot each lawyer's violations and, for each one, compare the two rule sets.

## 2. The three sources you cite

| Source | What it is | How to cite |
|---|---|---|
| **California Rules of Professional Conduct** (CRPC) | Court-approved rules, effective Nov. 1, 2018, numbered like the ABA rules (1.1, 1.7 …) but often different in content | "rule 1.7(b)" |
| **State Bar Act** (Bus. & Prof. Code § 6000 et seq.) | Statutes on attorney duties, fees, UPL, discipline | "B&P § 6068(e)" |
| **ABA Model Rules** (MR) | National model, adopted with changes by most states; not binding in California but the comparison on every essay and the whole of the MPRE | "MR 1.6(b)(2)" |

Case law fills gaps, for example:
- disqualification (*Flatt*, *SpeeDee Oil*);
- fee splits (*Chambers v. Kay*);
- out-of-state practice (*Birbrower*).

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: think of the CRPC as a far more detailed 律师执业行为规范 with statutory backing (律师法), enforced by the State Bar and the California Supreme Court (approx.).

## 3. Map of the subject (follow the State Bar outline)
```
I   Client-lawyer relationship
      formation · scope/authority · competence/diligence/communication
      CONFIDENTIALITY · CONFLICTS (LOYALTY) · fees · trust account · withdrawal
II  Counselor            candid advice; no assisting crime/fraud; organization as client
III Advocate             merit · candor to court · fairness to opponent · witnesses
                         publicity · lawyer-witness · prosecutors · threats (CA 3.10)
IV  Third persons        truthfulness · represented / unrepresented persons · inadvertent docs
V   Firms                supervisors/subordinates · nonlawyers · fee sharing · UPL/MJP
VI  Public service       pro bono · appointments
VII Advertising          misleading ads · paying for referrals · solicitation
VIII Integrity           bar admission · duty to report (8.3) · misconduct · self-reporting
```
The two big boxes are **confidentiality** and **conflicts**. Every essay touches at least one.

## 4. Key vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Informed consent** | Client agrees after the lawyer explains the material risks and alternatives |
| **Informed written consent** (CA) | The explanation **and** the consent are both in writing — stricter than the ABA's "informed consent, confirmed in writing" |
| **Confidential information** | Everything relating to the representation, from any source — much broader than privileged communications |
| **Privilege** | Evidence rule: the client can refuse to let confidential lawyer-client communications be compelled in court |
| **Direct adversity** | Acting against a current client (even in an unrelated matter) |
| **Material limitation** | A significant risk that other duties or the lawyer's own interests will dull the representation |
| **Substantially related** | A new matter that shares facts or issues with an old one, so the old client's secrets could be used |
| **Imputation** | One lawyer's conflict spreads to the whole firm |
| **Screen** | Walling off a tainted lawyer so the firm can act |
| **Trust account** | Separate bank account for client money; mixing it with the lawyer's own money is **commingling** |
| **Unconscionable fee** (CA) / **unreasonable fee** (ABA) | The fee standards |
| **UPL / MJP** | Unauthorized practice of law / multijurisdictional practice |
| **Moral turpitude** | Dishonest or corrupt conduct; grounds for discipline in California even outside practice (B&P § 6106) |

## 5. How the pieces connect
Read the facts by asking four questions for **each lawyer**:
1. **Who is the client?** The organization or its officer? The child or the parent who pays? The insured or the insurer? Everything else depends on this.
2. **Is the lawyer loyal?** Any other client, former client, relationship or personal interest pulling the other way → conflicts analysis (rules 1.7–1.10, 1.8.x).
3. **Is the lawyer keeping secrets — and keeping the client informed?** Confidentiality (rule 1.6, § 6068(e)) and communication (rule 1.4) are two sides of the same relationship.
4. **Is the lawyer honest with everyone?** With the court (3.3), the opponent (3.4), third parties (4.1), and in advertising (7.1). And is the lawyer **competent, diligent and fair about money** (1.1, 1.3, 1.5, 1.15)?

Then: **how does it end?** Withdrawal (1.16), file return, refund. **Who else is responsible?** Supervisors (5.1), the firm (imputation), and other lawyers who must report (8.3).

## 6. The ideas that matter most
1. **California confidentiality is nearly absolute.**
   - Section 6068(e) allows only one exception: preventing a **criminal act** likely to cause **death or substantial bodily harm**.
   - The ABA adds financial fraud, self-defense, court orders and more; it also lets candor to the court and corporate "reporting out" override confidentiality.
   - This is the most-tested difference.
2. **Conflicts: identify, classify, consent.**
   - Classify the conflict: direct adversity or material limitation; current or former client.
   - Ask whether it can be consented to.
   - Get the right consent: California requires **informed written consent**, and rule 1.7(c) requires **written disclosure** of close relationships even without a real risk.
3. **Specific transactions have specific rules.**
   - Rule 1.8.x: business deals with clients, gifts, loans, third-party payors, sex with clients, malpractice releases.
   - California and the ABA differ on loans (CA allows post-retention loans) and liability limits (CA bans them outright).
4. **Fees: California wants writings.**
   - B&P § 6148: written agreements above $1,000.
   - § 6147: contingency fees.
   - Rule 1.5.1: referral-fee splits need written client consent but no proportionality.
   - No contingent fees in criminal defense or divorce.
5. **Client money is sacred.**
   - Trust account; no commingling.
   - California's 14-day notice and 45-day distribution standards.
   - Disputed portions stay in trust.
6. **Candor beats advocacy — except where California confidentiality wins.**
   - No false statements or false evidence.
   - Remedial measures for perjury: the ABA ends in disclosure; California ends at withdrawal.
7. **California has rules the ABA lacks.**
   - Rule 3.10: no threatening criminal, administrative or disciplinary charges.
   - Rule 5.3.1: employing disbarred lawyers.
   - Rule 1.4.2: malpractice-insurance disclosure.
   - B&P § 6068(o): self-reporting.
   - Rule 8.3 (2023): reporting on "credible evidence".
8. **Write it the California way.**
   - Lawyer by lawyer, duty by duty.
   - ABA rule, California rule, application, conclusion — with "likely" and both sides argued.

## 7. What this means for you
- Your PRC training gives you the instinct that lawyers owe the court and the state real duties. US ethics is more **client-centred**, especially in California, where even a client's past fraud must stay secret.
- Expect the conflicts rules to feel very fine-grained compared with the PRC ban on representing both parties in one case (双方代理). They reach unrelated matters, former clients, family relationships with opposing counsel and the lawyer's own job hunt.
- Practise by writing one PR essay every week from January; it is the one essay you will almost certainly see.
