# Professional Responsibility — essay templates

**Timing (60 minutes).** About 15 minutes to read and outline (run [`issue-checklist.md`](issue-checklist.md) per lawyer), 40 minutes to write, 5 to check. A typical passing PR answer covers 5–8 issues.

**Paragraph pattern for every issue (the "CA/ABA IRAC"):**
> **[Duty — specific rule]**
> *ABA:* [one-sentence rule, MR cite].
> *California:* "California's rule is the same" **or** [California rule, CRPC/B&P cite, and how it differs].
> *Application:* [facts → each element; argue the other side where facts cut both ways].
> *Conclusion:* "[Lawyer] likely violated both rules" / "violated the ABA rule but not California's" (or the reverse).

Selected answers organize by the call: named lawyers → lawyer headings; listed matters → item headings; numbered questions → numbered headings (`00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §5). The patterns below are drawn from the PR calls in `06-past-exams/index.csv`.

## Template 1 — "What ethical violations has [Lawyer] committed? Answer according to California and ABA authorities."
Used in: Jul. 2013 Q1, Feb. 2016 Q3, Feb. 2017 Q5, Jul. 2018 Q3, Feb. 2019 Q5, Jul. 2022 Q3, Feb. 2023 Q4, Feb. 2025 Q5.

```
Introduction (2 lines): CA lawyers are governed by the CRPC and the State Bar Act;
  the ABA Model Rules are persuasive. Both are discussed below.  (R-PRF-001)

[Lawyer] — follow the chronology of the facts:
  1. Formation / who is the client                         (R-PRF-002, R-PRF-055)
  2. Duty of loyalty — each conflict as its own heading     (R-PRF-021 to R-PRF-037)
     a. [e.g., Third-party payment of fees]
     b. [e.g., Business transaction with client]
     c. [e.g., Sexual relationship]
  3. Duty of confidentiality                                (R-PRF-013 to R-PRF-015)
  4. Competence / diligence                                 (R-PRF-008, R-PRF-009)
  5. Communication                                          (R-PRF-010, R-PRF-011)
  6. Fees (amount; writing; contingency; split)             (R-PRF-041 to R-PRF-046)
  7. Safekeeping                                            (R-PRF-047 to R-PRF-049)
  8. Withdrawal / termination                               (R-PRF-050 to R-PRF-053)
  9. Any advocate / third-party / advertising issue
Conclusion: one line listing the violations under each rule set.
```
**Tip:** chronological order makes it hard to miss an issue — follow the facts paragraph by paragraph.

```

   - Contingent fee in criminal case (R-PRF-044) — ABA / CA / application / conclusion
   - Written fee agreement § 6148 (R-PRF-042)
   - Business transaction / conflict (R-PRF-026) if the fee is property

   - Witness compensation (R-PRF-063); request not to cooperate (R-PRF-064)
```

```
1. Who is the client? The organization, acting through its board (R-PRF-055)
   - Duties to constituents; Upjohn-style warning (R-PRF-074)
   - Dual representation of an officer? Rule 1.7 + consent by another official (R-PRF-024)
2. Conflicts: constituent's interests vs. the organization's (R-PRF-021, R-PRF-023)
3. Constituent wrongdoing:
   a. Report up (both) (R-PRF-055)
   b. Report out — ABA MR 1.13(c) yes; California rule 1.13(c) + § 6068(e) no (R-PRF-056)
   c. Confidentiality exceptions — ABA 1.6(b)(2)–(3); CA none for fraud (R-PRF-015)
   d. Withdrawal / resignation (R-PRF-050, R-PRF-051)
4. Lawyer's own conduct: harassment of subordinates (CA 8.4.1 / MR 8.4(g)), crimes,
   self-reporting (§ 6068(o)), moral turpitude (§ 6106) (R-PRF-093 to R-PRF-095)
```

## Template 6 — Evidence crossover (privilege vs. confidentiality)
Used in: Jul. 2017 Q2, Feb. 2022 Q4.

```
PART 1 — Evidence (as the call specifies: CEC or FRE)
  1. Attorney-client privilege: elements; holder; exceptions (Evid. Code §§ 952, 954, 956, 956.5, 958) (R-PRF-017)
  2. Work product (CCP § 2018.030; FRCP 26(b)(3)) (R-PRF-018)
  3. Physical evidence — location/condition after removal (Meredith) (R-PRF-020)
PART 2 — Ethics
  1. Duty of confidentiality is broader than privilege (R-PRF-016)
  2. Did the lawyer reveal more than required? Disclosure only after asserting the privilege and a court order
  3. Handling evidence: rule 3.4(a); Penal Code § 135 (R-PRF-020)
  4. Withdrawal (R-PRF-050 to R-PRF-052)
```
**Tip:** never merge the two. Privilege answers "can the court compel it?"; confidentiality answers "may the lawyer volunteer it?".

## Model paragraph (illustration of the CA/ABA IRAC)
> **Duty of Confidentiality — disclosure to the Attorney General**
> *ABA:* Under MR 1.6(b)(2)–(3) a lawyer may reveal information to the extent reasonably necessary to prevent, mitigate or rectify substantial financial injury from a client's crime or fraud in which the lawyer's services were used. Under MR 1.13(c), if the highest authority fails to act on a clear violation reasonably certain to injure the organization substantially, the lawyer may report out.
> *California:* Business and Professions Code § 6068(e)(1) requires a lawyer to maintain client confidences "at every peril", and rule 1.6 permits disclosure only to prevent a criminal act likely to result in death or substantial bodily harm. Rule 1.13(c) forbids revealing § 6068(e) information when reporting up.
> *Application:* Laura reported the loan fraud to the CEO and the board, which did nothing. The fraud threatens financial harm to borrowers and the company, not death or bodily harm. Under the ABA, her limited disclosure to the Attorney General was likely permitted. California has no financial-fraud exception and no reporting out.
> *Conclusion:* Laura's disclosure was likely proper under the ABA rules but violated California rule 1.13(c), rule 1.6 and § 6068(e). Her proper course in California was to continue urging the board and to withdraw.
