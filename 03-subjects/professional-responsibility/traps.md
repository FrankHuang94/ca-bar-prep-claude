# Professional Responsibility — traps

Rule IDs point to [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md). PR is not on the MBE; the "distractor" traps below are the ones the **MPRE** sets, and the essay traps are the ones California graders punish.

## A. Structure traps (essay)
1. **Answering by rule instead of by lawyer.** The grader expects each lawyer (and co-counsel, the partner, opposing counsel) separately, then each duty. Missing the second lawyer (July 2024 "Len"; Feb. 2026 out-of-state attorney) loses a whole section. (R-PRF-001)
2. **Giving only one rule set.** "California and ABA authorities" means both rules on every issue — even if just "California's rule is the same". (R-PRF-001)
3. **One conclusion at the end.** Conclude each issue, under each rule set when they differ. (`00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §5)
4. **Writing "the lawyer breached the duty of loyalty" without naming the rule.** Name the type: direct adversity, material limitation, 1.8.x, former client, imputation. (R-PRF-021)

## B. California-vs-ABA confusions
5. **Importing ABA confidentiality exceptions into California.** No financial-fraud, no rule-based self-defense, no reporting out, no candor override. Only a criminal act → death/SBH (§ 6068(e)(2)). (R-PRF-014, R-PRF-015)
6. **Forgetting that the California death/SBH exception requires a *criminal act*,** plus dissuasion and telling the client where reasonable. An accident threat (a defective product) doesn't fit California's text. (R-PRF-014)
7. **"Confirmed in writing" vs. "informed written consent".** California needs the client's written consent after written disclosure; a lawyer's confirming email is not enough. (R-PRF-021)
8. **Missing rule 1.7(c).** California requires written *disclosure* of relationships even when there is no significant risk — the ABA does not. (R-PRF-022)
9. **Screening a lateral who worked on the matter.** It works under the ABA; it does not work in California if he substantially participated. (R-PRF-037)
10. **Fee splits.** ABA requires proportionality or joint responsibility. California does **not**, but requires a written lawyer-lawyer agreement and the client's written consent after written disclosure. Do not say "referral fees are banned" in California. (R-PRF-046)
11. **Loans to clients.** Prohibited under the ABA; permitted in California after retention with a written promise to repay **and** rule 1.7/1.8.1 compliance. (R-PRF-028)
12. **Prospective malpractice limits.** ABA: allowed if the client is independently represented. California: never. (R-PRF-031)
13. **Lawyer-witness exception.** ABA: substantial hardship. California: client's informed written consent — and for a prosecutor, consent of the head of the office or a designee (not "unavailable to prosecutors"). Don't swap them. (R-PRF-069)
14. **Threatening criminal charges.** California rule 3.10 bans it; the ABA has no rule (Formal Op. 92-363). Don't say "both prohibit". (R-PRF-071)
15. **Duty to report (rule 8.3).** California has had a duty since Aug. 1, 2023 — older selected answers that say "California has no reporting duty" are **out of date**. Trigger: CA "credible evidence" of listed conduct; ABA "knows" of any serious violation. (R-PRF-091)
16. **Closing argument.** California rule 3.4(g) **does** bar personal opinion on the guilt or innocence of an accused (and personal knowledge). It does **not** list credibility, justness of cause or civil culpability. The Feb. 2024 selected-answer claim that California has no bar at all is wrong as to guilt. (R-PRF-066) [S-1255]
17. **Client file.** No retaining lien in California — return the file even if unpaid. (R-PRF-053)
18. **Permissive withdrawal "repugnant" ground.** ABA only; California has no such ground. (R-PRF-051)
19. **Diminished capacity.** California has no rule 1.14; don't apply ABA protective disclosures to a California lawyer. (R-PRF-054)

## C. Concept confusions
20. **Confidentiality ≠ privilege.** Privilege is evidence law (communications, compelled testimony); confidentiality covers everything, everywhere. In Evidence crossovers use separate headings. (R-PRF-016)
21. **"It's public, so I can tell."** California confidentiality covers publicly available but embarrassing or detrimental information. (R-PRF-013)
22. **Former client vs. current client.** A former client's conflict needs a substantial relationship; a current client's needs none (*Flatt*). Firing a client to convert it to "former" is the hot-potato trap. (R-PRF-025, R-PRF-034)
23. **Romance with a former client.** Not barred by rule 1.8.10, but a romance that starts mid-representation is barred, and a lingering relationship can still be a 1.7(b) conflict. (R-PRF-032)
24. **Unconscionable vs. unreasonable.** California's standard is unconscionability; don't just apply the ABA reasonableness factors. (R-PRF-041)
25. **Contingency in criminal or divorce cases includes non-cash rewards** (tickets, a share of property). (R-PRF-044)
26. **"Nonrefundable retainer" that is really an advance fee.** Only a true retainer (availability only) can be nonrefundable. (R-PRF-045)
27. **Section 6148 is not "any fee over $1,000".** It is triggered when total expense is *reasonably foreseeable* to exceed $1,000. It has exceptions (corporate client, emergency, prior same services, written waiver), and noncompliance makes the agreement *voidable*, not void. (R-PRF-042)
28. **Commingling defenses.** "I paid it back" and "it was only overnight" are not defenses. (R-PRF-047)
29. **Settlement authority.** The lawyer cannot settle without the client's authority even if the offer is excellent. (R-PRF-005)
30. **Subordinate lawyer defense.** It applies only to an *arguable* question resolved *reasonably* by the supervisor. (R-PRF-077)
31. **Represented organization.** Rule 4.2 does not cover every employee — only managers, plus employees asked about their own act or omission that may bind or be imputed to the organization (CA), or constituents who supervise, direct or regularly consult with the organization's lawyer, can obligate it, or whose act or omission may be imputed (ABA cmt. [7]). The old ABA "admission" category was deleted in 2002. Former employees are usually fair game. (R-PRF-073)
32. **Inadvertent documents.** California requires the lawyer to stop reading; the ABA only to notify. (R-PRF-075)
33. **Organization as client.** Advising the CEO personally when the client is the company. Give an Upjohn-style warning when interests diverge. (R-PRF-055)
34. **Self-reporting (§ 6068(o)).** Three suits **within 12 months**, not three suits ever. A first misdemeanor DUI is generally not self-reportable under (o)(5). (R-PRF-094) [S-1250]
35. **Moral turpitude.** It is not limited to crimes or to conduct in practice (B&P § 6106). (R-PRF-093)

## D. MPRE distractor patterns (ABA only)
36. A choice saying the lawyer "must" disclose under MR 1.6(b) — **all** 1.6(b) exceptions are permissive.
37. A choice saying a lawyer may represent both sides in the same litigation with consent — **never** (MR 1.7(b)(3)).
38. A choice saying a lawyer "must withdraw" whenever a client refuses to pay — that is **permissive**, after warning.
39. A choice saying a contingent fee needs only oral agreement — MR 1.5(c) requires a **writing signed by the client**.
40. A choice relying on California rules (3.10, 1.7(c), § 6068(e)) — the MPRE tests **only** ABA law.
