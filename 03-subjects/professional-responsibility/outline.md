# Professional Responsibility — outline

**Scope.** This outline follows the State Bar's essay-outline structure for Professional Responsibility (I–VIII, modelled on the ABA Model Rules chapters) [S-082], with dedicated sections for the most-tested duties: **confidentiality** (I.D) and **loyalty/conflicts** (I.E). Every topic states the ABA rule, the California rule, and whether they differ. Rule IDs point to [`rule-statements.md`](rule-statements.md); the side-by-side table is in [`ca-distinctions.md`](ca-distinctions.md).

**Governing law on the essay.** California essays test the California Rules of Professional Conduct (CRPC, effective Nov. 1, 2018, as amended), the State Bar Act (Bus. & Prof. Code § 6000 et seq.) and California case law; since at least 2013 nearly every PR call has said "Answer according to California and ABA authorities" (`06-past-exams/index.csv`). The ABA Model Rules (MR) are the comparison column — and the whole content of the MPRE (see [X. MPRE notes](#x-mpre-notes)).

**How PR is tested.**
- 30 of 31 essay administrations (Feb. 2012 – Jul. 2026); every exam since July 2015 (`06-past-exams/frequency-analysis.md`).
- Most-tagged issues: conflicts/loyalty (12×), advocate duties (8×), confidentiality (8×), CA-vs-ABA comparison (8×), competence/communication (6×), fees (6×).
- Common crossovers: PR + Business Associations, PR + Evidence (privilege vs. confidentiality), PR + Community Property, PR + Contracts, PR + Criminal Procedure (plea bargains, prosecutors).
- Answer **actor by actor, then duty by duty** (`00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md` §5).

**Key definitions (CRPC rule 1.0.1; MR 1.0).**

| Term | California | ABA |
|---|---|---|
| Informed consent | Agreement after the lawyer communicates and explains the relevant circumstances and the material risks, including actual and reasonably foreseeable adverse consequences (rule 1.0.1(e)) | Agreement after adequate information and explanation about material risks and reasonably available alternatives (MR 1.0(e)) |
| Informed **written** consent / confirmed in writing | Both the communication and the consent in writing (rule 1.0.1(e-1)) [S-556] | Oral consent confirmed promptly by a writing from the lawyer (MR 1.0(b)) |
| Knowingly / knows | Actual knowledge; may be inferred from circumstances | Same |
| Reasonably believes / should know | Objective standard of a reasonably prudent and competent lawyer | Same |
| Screened | Timely isolation of a lawyer from a matter with procedures adequate to protect information and prevent communication (rule 1.0.1(k)) | Same (MR 1.0(k)) |
| Firm / law firm | Includes lawyers in a legal services organization or the legal department of a corporation or other organization (rule 1.0.1(c)) [S-1260] | Same (MR 1.0(c)) |
| Writing | Includes electronic communications and recordings; "signed" includes electronic signature | Same |

## I. Client-lawyer relationship (scope; competence; confidentiality; conflicts; fees; safekeeping; declining and terminating)

### I.A Formation of the relationship, identifying the client, prospective clients
- **Formation** — express agreement or implied from conduct when a person reasonably relies on the lawyer for legal advice; no fee or writing needed (R-PRF-002). Watch for "accidental clients": the nonprofit's employee, a family member who pays, the other spouse.
- **Who is the client?** Organization vs. constituents (rule 1.13 / MR 1.13; R-PRF-055); insurer vs. insured (R-PRF-039); payor vs. client (rule 1.8.6; R-PRF-029); fiduciary vs. beneficiaries.
- **Prospective clients** (rule 1.18 / MR 1.18; R-PRF-003):
  - Confidentiality applies even if no engagement follows.
  - Disqualification: CA — information **material to the matter**; ABA — information **significantly harmful**.
  - Cure: both clients' informed written consent, or (if intake was limited) timely screen + no fee share + written notice [S-556].
- **Declining representation** — a lawyer may decline for any non-discriminatory reason (CA rule 8.4.1 forbids unlawful discrimination in refusing representation; R-PRF-095). ABA 2023: duty to inquire into and assess each representation (MR 1.16(a)) [S-125] [S-126] (R-PRF-050).

### I.B Scope of representation and allocation of authority
**Allocation of authority** (rule 1.2(a); MR 1.2(a); R-PRF-005).

| Client decides | Lawyer decides (after consultation) |
|---|---|
| Objectives; whether to settle; plea; jury waiver; whether to testify; whether to appeal | Means: legal tactics, witnesses, motions, scheduling, procedural matters |

- A lawyer who settles, dismisses or stipulates away a substantial right without authority violates rule 1.2(a) and B&P § 6104 (appearing without authority); *Blanton v. Womancare, Inc.* (1985) 38 Cal.3d 396.
- **Limited scope** (rule 1.2(b) / MR 1.2(c)): reasonable + informed consent; California limited-scope court forms (Cal. Rules of Court, rules 3.35–3.37, 5.425) (R-PRF-006).
- **Assisting crime, fraud or violation of law** (rule 1.2.1 / MR 1.2(d); R-PRF-007): California is broader — "a violation of any law, rule, or ruling of a tribunal". Lawyer may explain consequences and test a law in good faith. If the client expects improper help, the lawyer must explain the limits (rule 1.4(a)(4); MR 1.4(a)(5)).
- **Representation is not endorsement** of the client's views (MR 1.2(b)).
- **Tested:** Feb. 2013 Q2, Feb. 2020 Q2, July 2024 Q3 (settling without client notice).

### I.C Competence, diligence and communication
**Competence** (rule 1.1 / MR 1.1; R-PRF-008).
- ABA: knowledge, skill, thoroughness and preparation reasonably necessary.
- California: discipline only for intentional, reckless, grossly negligent or repeated failure; "competence" includes mental, emotional and physical ability [S-566].
- Cure routes (both): associate or consult a competent lawyer; acquire the skill before it is needed; refer. Emergency assistance allowed.
- Technology: comment [1] to rule 1.1 (eff. Mar. 22, 2021) and MR 1.1 cmt. [8] — keep abreast of the benefits and risks of relevant technology [S-566]. Generative AI: State Bar Practical Guidance (2023) and ABA Formal Op. 512 (2024); AI-specific CRPC amendments (rules 1.1, 1.4, 1.6, 3.3, 5.1, 5.3) were **pending, not adopted**, as of Nov. 2026 [S-122] [VERIFY V-031].

**Diligence** (rule 1.3 / MR 1.3; R-PRF-009) — commitment and dedication; no neglect, disregard or undue delay; California uses the same intentional/reckless/gross-negligence/repeated threshold.

**Communication** (rule 1.4 / MR 1.4; B&P § 6068(m); R-PRF-010).
- Promptly inform of decisions requiring informed consent.
- Reasonably consult about means.
- Keep reasonably informed of significant developments; promptly comply with reasonable requests.
- Explain enough for informed decisions.
- Advise of limits on what the lawyer may do.
- California extras: may delay transmitting information that would likely cause the client to react with imminent harm (rule 1.4(c)); duties yield to protective orders (rule 1.4(d)).

**Settlement and plea offers** (rule 1.4.1; B&P § 6103.5; MR 1.4 cmt. [2]; R-PRF-011): every written civil settlement offer and every plea offer, promptly; *Missouri v. Frye*, 566 U.S. 134 (2012).

**Malpractice insurance disclosure** (rule 1.4.2; R-PRF-012) — California only: written notice at engagement if uninsured, or within 30 days of a lapse. Exceptions (rule 1.4.2(c)): representation known at engagement not to exceed four hours (comply if it later does); government lawyers and in-house counsel advising the employer; emergencies; notice already given [S-1262].

**Tested:** Jul. 2012 Q2, Feb. 2013 Q2, Jul. 2013 Q1, Feb. 2016 Q3, Jul. 2018 Q3, Feb. 2020 Q2.

### I.D Duty of confidentiality vs. attorney-client privilege and work product; exceptions
**The duty** (B&P § 6068(e)(1); rule 1.6; MR 1.6; R-PRF-013).
- California: "maintain inviolate the confidence, and at every peril to himself or herself … preserve the secrets, of his or her client".
- Scope: everything relating to the representation, from any source, privileged or not, including embarrassing or detrimental information; continues after the representation and after death.
- Safeguards: reasonable precautions against inadvertent disclosure (MR 1.6(c); State Bar Formal Op. 2020-203 on data breaches): shared offices, printers, receptionists, email, cloud services, AI tools.
- Use vs. disclosure: using information to the client's disadvantage is separately barred (rule 1.8.2, 1.9(c); MR 1.8(b), 1.9(c); R-PRF-019).

**Exceptions compared.**

| Exception | ABA (MR 1.6(b)) | California (§ 6068(e)(2); rule 1.6(b)) |
|---|---|---|
| Informed consent; implied authorization | Yes | Consent yes; implied authorization through comments |
| Prevent death or substantial bodily harm | Reasonably certain death/SBH, from any cause (b)(1) | Only to prevent a **criminal act** likely to cause death/SBH; must first try to dissuade and tell the client, if reasonable; disclose no more than necessary (rule 1.6(c)–(d)) |
| Prevent client crime/fraud causing financial injury (lawyer's services used) | Yes (b)(2) | **No** |
| Prevent, mitigate, rectify such financial injury | Yes (b)(3) | **No** |
| Lawyer obtains ethics advice | Yes (b)(4) | Not in the rule (consulting ethics counsel is generally treated as consistent with confidentiality) [VERIFY V-472] |
| Lawyer's claim or defense (fee suit, malpractice, discipline) | Yes (b)(5) | Not in the rule; privilege exception Evid. Code § 958 (breach of duty) [VERIFY V-472] |
| Comply with law or court order | Yes (b)(6) | Court-ordered disclosure after asserting privilege is not a violation |
| Conflict checking on lateral moves | Yes (b)(7) | Not express |
| Candor to tribunal overrides | Yes (MR 3.3(c)) | **No** — rule 3.3 remedial measures stop at § 6068(e) |
| Organization reporting out | Yes (MR 1.13(c)) | **No** (rule 1.13(c)) |

- **California's exception is permissive**: failure to disclose is never a violation (rule 1.6(e)) (R-PRF-014).
- **ABA exceptions** (R-PRF-015) are all permissive; disclosure must be no more than reasonably necessary.

**Attorney-client privilege** (Evid. Code §§ 952, 954, 955; FRE 501; R-PRF-017).

| | Duty of confidentiality | Attorney-client privilege | Work product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source | Ethics rules + § 6068(e) | Evidence Code / federal common law | CCP § 2018.030; FRCP 26(b)(3) |
| Protects | All information relating to the representation | Confidential lawyer-client communications for legal advice | Lawyer's preparation materials |
| When it applies | Always (voluntary disclosure) | When disclosure is compelled (testimony, discovery) | Discovery |
| Holder | Client (lawyer must protect) | Client; lawyer must claim it (Evid. Code § 955) | Attorney (CA); party (federal) |
| Main exceptions | CA: death/SBH crime; ABA: 1.6(b) list | Crime-fraud (§ 956), prevent death/SBH (§ 956.5), breach of duty (§ 958), joint clients (§ 962) | CA: opinion work product absolute; other qualified (unfair prejudice/injustice) |

- *Costco Wholesale Corp. v. Superior Court* (2009) 47 Cal.4th 725: the privilege covers the entire communication, including facts in it, and a court may not order disclosure to rule on the claim.
- *Upjohn Co. v. United States*, 449 U.S. 383 (1981): federal privilege covers communications by corporate employees to counsel.
- *Swidler & Berlin v. United States*, 524 U.S. 399 (1998): privilege survives death.
- **Work product** (CCP § 2018.030; *Coito v. Superior Court* (2012) 54 Cal.4th 480; FRCP 26(b)(3); *Hickman v. Taylor*, 329 U.S. 495 (1947); R-PRF-018).

**Physical evidence** (rule 3.4(a); Penal Code § 135; *People v. Meredith* (1981) 29 Cal.3d 682; R-PRF-020): a lawyer may not conceal or destroy it; if he takes possession, he may examine it for a reasonable time and must then turn it over; if he moves it, the privilege no longer covers its original location or condition. California case law describes turning it over to the court or prosecution [VERIFY V-462].

**Tested:** Jul. 2013 Q1, Jul. 2017 Q2 (Evidence crossover), Jul. 2018 Q3, Feb. 2020 Q2, Jul. 2021 Q2 (shared office), Feb. 2022 Q4 (bloody pants), Jul. 2023 Q3 (general counsel to Attorney General), Feb. 2025 Q5 (payor-parent).

### I.E Duty of loyalty: conflicts of interest (current clients, former clients, imputation, screening)
**Approach for every conflict:**
1. Identify every client, former client, prospective client and third person.
2. Classify the conflict.
3. Is it consentable?
4. What consent or disclosure is required (CA vs. ABA)?
5. Imputation to the firm; screening.
6. Consequences: discipline, disqualification, fee forfeiture.

#### I.E.1 Current-client conflicts (rule 1.7 / MR 1.7)
- **Direct adversity** — suing or negotiating against a current client, even in an unrelated matter; cross-examining a current client (R-PRF-021).
- **Material limitation** — significant risk that responsibilities to another client, former client or third person, or the lawyer's own interests, will materially limit the representation (R-PRF-021, R-PRF-023).
- **Consentability (both):** reasonable belief the lawyer can represent each competently and diligently; not prohibited by law; no claim by one client against another in the same litigation (rule 1.7(d); MR 1.7(b)).
- **Consent:** CA informed **written** consent from each affected client; ABA informed consent, confirmed in writing.
- **California written disclosure without consent (rule 1.7(c); R-PRF-022)** — the lawyer (or, to his knowledge, a firm colleague) has a legal, business, financial, professional or personal relationship with or responsibility to a party or witness; or opposing counsel is the lawyer's spouse, parent, child or sibling, lives with him, is his or the firm's client, or has an intimate personal relationship with him. No ABA counterpart (see MR 1.7 cmt. [11]).
- **"Matter"** — broadly defined in rule 1.7(e): any proceeding, application, contract, transaction, claim, investigation or similar action focused on specific persons.
- **Joint representation** (R-PRF-024): co-plaintiffs, co-defendants, buyer and seller, spouses, entity and constituent. Disclose information-sharing, loss of privilege between joint clients (Evid. Code § 962), and the possibility of withdrawal from all. Criminal co-defendants: Sixth Amendment (*Holloway*; *Cuyler v. Sullivan*; *Wheat*).
- **Personal-interest conflicts** (R-PRF-023):
  - job negotiations with an opposing firm (MR 1.7 cmt. [10]) (Jul. 2019 Q4);
  - membership in or funding of an advocacy group hostile to the client's position (Feb. 2023 Q4);
  - a relationship with opposing counsel (Oct. 2020 Q1);
  - romance with the client (Feb. 2021 Q4, Feb. 2025 Q5);
  - the lawyer's own potential malpractice exposure.
- **Hot potato and advance waivers** (R-PRF-025): *Flatt v. Superior Court* (1994) 9 Cal.4th 275; *Truck Ins. Exchange v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Co.* (1992) 6 Cal.App.4th 1050 [VERIFY V-465]; *Sheppard, Mullin, Richter & Hampton, LLP v. J-M Manufacturing Co.* (2018) 6 Cal.5th 59 (undisclosed known conflict voids consent and the engagement agreement).
- **Insurance defense / Cumis** (Civ. Code § 2860; R-PRF-039).

#### I.E.3 Former-client conflicts (rule 1.9 / MR 1.9)
- Same or **substantially related** matter + **materially adverse** + no informed written consent (CA) / informed consent confirmed in writing (ABA) (R-PRF-034).
- Substantial relationship: similarities of facts, legal issues, and the lawyer's involvement (*H.F. Ahmanson & Co. v. Salomon Brothers, Inc.* (1991) 229 Cal.App.3d 1445). In California, where the lawyer had a direct and personal relationship with the former client, access to confidential information is presumed (*Jessen v. Hartford Casualty Ins. Co.* (2003) 111 Cal.App.4th 698).
- Lawyers who move: disqualified only if they actually acquired material confidential information about the former firm's client (rule 1.9(b); R-PRF-035).
- Former-client information: no use to the client's disadvantage unless generally known; no revelation (rule 1.9(c)).

#### I.E.4 Imputation and screening (rule 1.10 / MR 1.10)
- General rule: one lawyer's 1.7/1.9 conflict is imputed to every lawyer in the "firm" (R-PRF-036). Office-sharers who hold themselves out as a firm are treated as one (Jul. 2021 Q2). An "of counsel" lawyer's conflict is imputed (*People ex rel. Dept. of Corporations v. SpeeDee Oil Change Systems, Inc.* (1999) 20 Cal.4th 1135).
- Exceptions: personal-interest conflicts without significant risk to colleagues; lateral screening; client consent.
- **Screening laterals** (R-PRF-037) [S-556]:

| | ABA MR 1.10(a)(2) | California rule 1.10(a)(2) |
|---|---|---|
| Who can be screened | Any lateral with a 1.9 conflict | Only if the lawyer **did not substantially participate** in the same or substantially related matter |
| Requirements | Timely screen; no fee share; written notice; certifications of compliance | Timely screen; no fee share; prompt written notice to the former client |
| Case law | — | *Kirk v. First American Title Ins. Co.* (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 776 (screen can rebut presumption in some cases) |

- After a lawyer leaves: firm free unless the matter is substantially related and a remaining lawyer has material information (rule 1.10(b)).

#### I.E.5 Government lawyers, judges and neutrals (rules 1.11–1.12 / MR 1.11–1.12)
- Former government lawyer: no private representation in a matter in which he participated personally and substantially, unless the agency consents (CA: informed written consent); firm screen + notice to agency (R-PRF-038).
- Current government lawyer: no participation in matters he handled in private practice without consent; no negotiating employment with parties or their lawyers in matters he is handling.
- Former judges, arbitrators, mediators and law clerks: personally barred; firm may screen; law clerk may negotiate employment after notifying the judge.

#### I.E.6 Organization as client (rule 1.13 / MR 1.13)
- Client = the entity acting through authorized constituents (R-PRF-055).
- **Report up** when a constituent's violation of a duty to the organization, or a violation of law imputable to it, is likely to cause substantial injury.
- **Report out** (R-PRF-056):
  - ABA: allowed after the highest authority fails to act, if the violation is clear and substantial injury is reasonably certain. Not allowed when the lawyer was hired to investigate or defend the violation.
  - California: **no** — rule 1.13(c) forbids revealing § 6068(e) information [S-551]. The lawyer may continue to urge action, and may resign or withdraw under rule 1.16.
- **Identity warning:** when the organization's interests are adverse to a constituent's, explain whom the lawyer represents.
- **Dual representation** is allowed under rule 1.7; consent comes from a different authorized official.
- In-house counsel: *General Dynamics Corp. v. Superior Court* (1994) 7 Cal.4th 1164.
- **Tested:** Feb. 2020 Q2 (nonprofit and employee), Jul. 2023 Q3 (general counsel), Jul. 2026 Q1 (in-house).

#### I.E.7 Consequences of conflicts
- **Discipline.**
- **Disqualification** (court's inherent power, CCP § 128(a)(5)):
  - concurrent adverse representation → near-automatic (*Flatt*);
  - former-client conflicts → substantial-relationship test plus balancing (*SpeeDee Oil*) (R-PRF-040).
- **Fee consequences:** unenforceable agreement and fee forfeiture (*Sheppard Mullin*) (R-PRF-098).
- **Malpractice / breach of fiduciary duty** (R-PRF-097, R-PRF-098).

### I.F Fees (fee agreements; unconscionable fees; contingent fees; referral fees)
**Standard** (R-PRF-041): ABA — not **unreasonable** (MR 1.5(a), eight factors). California — not **unconscionable or illegal** (rule 1.5(a)–(b)), judged at formation with factors including fraud/overreaching, nondisclosure, disproportion, relative sophistication and informed consent. The California threshold is higher, but disclosure failures count heavily.

**Written agreements** (R-PRF-042):
- ABA — communicate the basis, preferably in writing (MR 1.5(b)).
- California — **B&P § 6148** requires a signed written agreement when total expense foreseeably exceeds **$1,000**: basis of compensation, nature of services, responsibilities of each side, and a copy to the client.
- Exceptions: emergency; same kind of services previously rendered and paid; client's written waiver; corporate client.
- Noncompliance: the agreement is voidable by the client, and the lawyer gets a reasonable fee.

**Contingent fees** (R-PRF-043, R-PRF-044):
- ABA (MR 1.5(c)): writing signed by the client; method and percentages; expense treatment; closing statement.
- California (**B&P § 6147**): signed by both; copy to the client; rate; how costs affect the fee and the recovery; compensation for related matters; statement that the fee is "not set by law and is negotiable" (unless § 6146 applies).
- Consequence in California: voidable at the client's option; the lawyer receives a reasonable fee that never exceeds the contract amount [S-560].
- Medical-malpractice caps: § 6146. Workers' compensation is excluded; claims between merchants fall under § 6147.5 [S-560].
- **Prohibited** (rule 1.5(c); MR 1.5(d)): contingent fees in criminal defense, and family-law fees contingent on obtaining a dissolution or nullity (ABA: divorce) or on the amount of spousal or child support, "or property settlement in lieu thereof" [VERIFY V-1010]. A fee tied to an ordinary division of community property is not expressly covered by the text; argue the rule's policy and the lawyer's personal stake (R-PRF-044).

**Retainers, advance fees and flat fees** (R-PRF-045):
- True retainer: paid only for availability; may be "nonrefundable" only with written agreement after disclosure (rule 1.5(d)).
- Flat fee: permitted for specified services (rule 1.5(e)). In California it may go to the operating account after written disclosures, signed by the client if over $1,000 (rule 1.15(b)).
- Labeling a flat fee "nonrefundable" violates rule 1.5(d) according to State Bar Formal Op. 2026-210 [S-559] [VERIFY V-467].
- Unearned fees must be refunded on termination (rule 1.16(e)(2); MR 1.16(d)).

**Fee division between lawyers** (R-PRF-046):

| | ABA MR 1.5(e) | California rule 1.5.1 |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Proportion to services **or** joint responsibility | None required — pure referral fees allowed |
| Client | Agrees to the arrangement including each share, confirmed in writing | **Written consent** after **full written disclosure** of the division, the lawyers' identities and terms — at the time or as soon as reasonably practicable |
| Lawyers | — | **Written agreement** between the lawyers |
| Total | Reasonable | Not increased by reason of the division (and not unconscionable) |
| Enforcement | — | Noncompliant split unenforceable (*Chambers v. Kay* (2002) 29 Cal.4th 142); quantum meruit possible (*Huskinson & Brown v. Wolf* (2004) 32 Cal.4th 453) |

**Fee disputes:** Mandatory Fee Arbitration Act notice before suing (B&P §§ 6200–6206); a discharged lawyer recovers in quantum meruit when the contingency occurs (*Fracasse v. Brent* (1972) 6 Cal.3d 784) (R-PRF-099).

**Tested:** Jul. 2013 Q1, Jul. 2018 Q3, Feb. 2020 Q2 (no written agreement), Jul. 2022 Q3 (fee collected before work; release), Feb. 2024 Q3 (season tickets contingent on acquittal), Jul. 2024 Q3 (fee split).

### I.G Safekeeping funds and property of clients and other persons
Rules 1.15 / MR 1.15 [S-553]; R-PRF-047 to R-PRF-049.

**Trust account and commingling.**
- Client and third-party funds, including advance fees and costs, go in an identifiable trust account in California. Another jurisdiction is allowed only with the client's written consent and a substantial relationship.
- Do not commingle the lawyer's own money, except enough to pay bank charges.
- Mixed funds go into trust; withdraw the lawyer's share at the earliest reasonable time after it becomes fixed.
- IOLTA accounts for nominal or short-term funds (B&P § 6211).

**Notice, records and distribution (California, since Jan. 1, 2023).**
- Notice of receipt within **14 days**, absent good cause.
- Complete records, kept for **five years**.
- Prompt written accounting.
- Distribute undisputed funds promptly. A rebuttable presumption of violation arises after **45 days** from when the funds become undisputed [S-553] [VERIFY V-469].
- Comply with any audit order.

**Disputed funds and liens.**
- The disputed portion stays in trust until the dispute is resolved; pay the undisputed portion now.
- Protect a known third-party lien (for example, a medical provider's lien).

**Other points.**
- Securities and property: label and keep in safekeeping.
- Misappropriation is usually moral turpitude (B&P § 6106); intent to repay is no defense.
- **CTAPP:** annual trust-account registration, self-assessment and certification (State Bar rule 2.5; Cal. Rules of Court, rule 9.8.5) [S-123].

**ABA comparison.** Account in the state where the office is situated; records kept five years; "promptly" instead of fixed day counts; advance fees withdrawn as earned (MR 1.15(c)).

**Tested:** Feb. 2016 Q3, Feb. 2025 retest Q4.

### I.H Declining and terminating representation
**Mandatory** (rule 1.16(a) / MR 1.16(a); R-PRF-050).

| California | ABA |
|---|---|
| Client is bringing an action or defense without probable cause to harass or maliciously injure | — |
| Representation will result in violation of the rules or State Bar Act | Will result in violation of the rules or other law |
| Mental or physical condition renders it unreasonably difficult to carry out the representation effectively | Condition materially impairs ability |
| Client discharges the lawyer | Same |
| — | (2023) Inquiry and assessment duty (cmts. [1]–[2]); client or prospective client seeks to use or persists in using the lawyer's services to commit or further a crime or fraud despite the lawyer's warning (MR 1.16(a)(4)) [S-125] [S-1263] [S-1264] |

**Permissive** (rule 1.16(b) / MR 1.16(b); R-PRF-051). California grounds (paraphrased) [VERIFY V-457]:
1. Unwarranted claim or defense.
2. Client seeks to pursue, or has used the lawyer for, a criminal or fraudulent course.
3. Client insists the lawyer pursue a criminal or fraudulent course.
4. Client makes effective representation unreasonably difficult.
5. Client breaches a material term, including fees, after reasonable warning.
6. Client knowingly and freely assents.
7. Inability to work with co-counsel.
8. Lawyer's condition makes representation difficult.
9. Continuing is likely to result in a violation.
10. Good cause before a tribunal.

ABA-only grounds: withdrawal without material adverse effect on the client; client insists on action the lawyer finds **repugnant** or fundamentally disagrees with; unreasonable financial burden.

**Procedure** (rule 1.16(c)–(e); MR 1.16(c)–(d); R-PRF-052, R-PRF-053):
- Court permission where required; continue if ordered.
- Take reasonable steps to avoid prejudice: notice and time to find counsel.
- Release all client materials on request **whether or not paid**. California has no retaining lien; the ABA allows retention as other law permits.
- Refund unearned fees (except a true retainer).
- In a motion to withdraw, say only that "professional considerations" require it; do not reveal confidences.

**Tested:** Oct. 2020 Q1, Feb. 2022 Q4, Feb. 2025 retest Q4.

## II. Counselor (lawyer as advisor)
- **Advisor** (rule 2.1 / MR 2.1; R-PRF-057) [S-558]:
  - independent professional judgment and candid advice;
  - may refer to moral, economic, social and political factors (ABA rule text; California comment [2]);
  - generally no duty to investigate the client's affairs or to volunteer advice the client does not want (California comment [1]).
- **Limits of advice:** may not counsel or assist crime, fraud or (CA) violation of law (rule 1.2.1; R-PRF-007); may discuss consequences.
- **Third-party neutral** (rule 2.4 / MR 2.4): a lawyer serving as mediator or arbitrator must tell unrepresented parties that he does not represent them. California rule 2.4.1 covers temporary judges, referees and court-appointed arbitrators. ABA MR 2.3 (evaluations for third persons) is not a separate California rule; use rules 1.6 and 1.7.
- **Counseling an organization:** report up (R-PRF-055); in California, no reporting out (R-PRF-056).
- **Tested:** Feb. 2023 Q4 (advising a client to withhold information while the lawyer supports an activist group), Jul. 2023 Q3.

## III. Advocate (candor to the tribunal; fairness to opposing party and counsel)

### III.A Meritorious claims and delay
- **Meritorious claims** (rule 3.1 / MR 3.1; B&P § 6068(c); R-PRF-058):
  - ABA: no frivolous claims (good-faith argument to change the law is fine).
  - California: no action, defense or appeal without probable cause **and** to harass or maliciously injure; no claim unwarranted by law or a good-faith argument.
  - A criminal defense lawyer may put the prosecution to its proof.
  - Parallel sanctions: FRCP 11; CCP § 128.7.
- **Delay** (rule 3.2 / MR 3.2; R-PRF-059):
  - ABA: duty to expedite.
  - California: no means whose only substantial purpose is delay or needless expense.
  - B&P § 6128(b): willful delay of a client's suit for the lawyer's own gain is a misdemeanor.

### III.B Candor toward the tribunal
Rule 3.3 / MR 3.3; B&P § 6068(d); R-PRF-060, R-PRF-061.
- No knowing false statement of fact or law; correct earlier false material statements.
- Disclose **directly adverse controlling authority** not disclosed by the opponent. Do not misquote authority. Verify AI-generated citations.
- **Ex parte proceedings:** disclose all material facts, adverse or not.
- **False evidence:**
  - Never offer evidence known to be false.
  - If material false evidence has been offered: remonstrate → seek withdrawal → ABA: disclose to the tribunal if necessary, even if confidential (MR 3.3(c)). California: remedial measures **short of** disclosure prohibited by § 6068(e) and rule 1.6.
  - The lawyer may refuse evidence he reasonably believes false, except the criminal defendant's testimony.
- **Criminal defendant's perjury:** *Nix v. Whiteside*, 475 U.S. 157 (1986) (threatening disclosure did not violate the Sixth Amendment). California: a rule 3.3 comment permits the narrative approach (*People v. Johnson* (1998) 62 Cal.App.4th 608) after the lawyer has tried to dissuade the client and has sought permission to withdraw [S-557] [S-1265].
- **Crimes and frauds related to the proceeding** (juror bribery, witness tampering): reasonable remedial measures. ABA includes disclosure; California only to the extent § 6068(e) and rule 1.6 permit (rule 3.3(b)).
- **Duration:** until the proceeding concludes, including appeal (rule 3.3(c); MR 3.3(c)).
- **Tested:** Feb. 2018 Q1 (expert testimony he should have known was false), Feb. 2024 Q3.

### III.C Fairness to opposing party and counsel
Rule 3.4 / MR 3.4 [S-550]; R-PRF-062 to R-PRF-066.

| Conduct | ABA MR 3.4 | California rule 3.4 |
|---|---|---|
| Obstruct access to evidence; alter, destroy, conceal | (a) | (a) — includes "a witness" |
| Suppress evidence there is a legal duty to produce | (a), (d) | (b) |
| Falsify evidence; assist false testimony; prohibited inducements | (b) | (c) |
| Pay a witness contingent on content or outcome | (b) and cmt. [3] | (d): only expenses, lost-time compensation, reasonable expert fee |
| Cause a witness to hide or leave the jurisdiction | (a) | (e) |
| Knowingly disobey tribunal rules (open-refusal exception) | (c) | (f) |
| Frivolous discovery / failure to comply | (d) | No express clause (Civil Discovery Act) |
| Ask a non-client not to give information voluntarily | (f) (relative/employee/agent exception) | **No counterpart** |
| Trial: allude to unsupported matters | (e) | Not in rule 3.4 |
| Trial: personal knowledge of facts | (e) | (g) |
| Trial: personal opinion | (e): justness of cause, witness credibility, civil culpability, guilt/innocence | (g): **guilt or innocence of an accused only** [S-550] [S-1255] |

**V-058 finding.** California's rule 3.4(g) does prohibit stating a personal opinion as to the guilt or innocence of an accused. It omits the ABA's other three categories: the justness of a cause, the credibility of a witness, and the culpability of a civil litigant. A statement that "California has no express bar" is therefore accurate only for those three categories (R-PRF-066) [S-550] [S-1255].
- **Physical evidence** (R-PRF-020).
- **Tested:** Feb. 2014 Q1, Jul. 2014 Q3 (discovery extension; physician's note), Jul. 2019 Q4 (withheld document), Feb. 2024 Q3 (witness payments; closing argument).

### III.D Impartiality and decorum of the tribunal
Rule 3.5 / MR 3.5 [VERIFY V-455]; R-PRF-067.
- No improper influence on, or gifts to, judges or court staff.
- No ex parte communication with a judge on the merits. Permitted: in open court; with consent or presence of all counsel; in writing copied to all counsel; ex parte matters.
- No contact with jurors or venire members during the case.
- After discharge: no contact that the law or a court order forbids, that the juror has declined, or that involves misrepresentation, coercion, duress or harassment.
- ABA: no conduct intended to disrupt a tribunal (MR 3.5(d)).

### III.E Trial publicity
Rule 3.6 / MR 3.6; R-PRF-068.
- Test: substantial likelihood of materially prejudicing an adjudicative proceeding.
- Safe harbors (claim, public record, investigation status, scheduling, warnings; arrest facts in criminal cases) and a right of reply.
- *Gentile v. State Bar of Nevada*, 501 U.S. 1030 (1991).
- Prosecutors must also restrain associates' statements (rule 3.8; MR 3.8(f)).

### III.F Lawyer as witness
Rule 3.7 / MR 3.7; R-PRF-069.
- No advocate at trial if likely a necessary witness.
- Exceptions (both): uncontested issue; nature and value of legal services.
- ABA-only exception: substantial hardship to the client.
- California-only exception: **client's informed written consent**. For a prosecutor or other government lawyer, the consent must come from the head of the office or a designee (rule 3.7(a)(3)); the court may still disqualify (cmt. [3]) [S-1253].
- Other firm lawyers may try the case unless rules 1.7/1.9 bar them.

### III.G Special responsibilities of a prosecutor
Rule 3.8 / MR 3.8 [VERIFY V-454]; R-PRF-070.
- Probable cause.
- Advise the accused of the right to counsel.
- No waiver of pretrial rights from an unrepresented accused.
- **Disclosure of exculpatory and mitigating information.** Broader than *Brady v. Maryland*, 373 U.S. 83 (1963): no materiality threshold. California adds "reasonably should know" (rule 3.8(d)); see also Penal Code § 1054.1.
- Limits on subpoenaing lawyers about clients (MR 3.8(e); California counterpart unconfirmed — V-454).
- Publicity restraint.
- Post-conviction duties on new, credible and material evidence of innocence (CA rule 3.8(f)–(g); MR 3.8(g)–(h)); a good-faith judgment that the duties are not triggered is not a violation (CA cmt. [9]) [S-1261].
- **Tested with Criminal Procedure:** Jul. 2025 Q5 (plea process).

### III.H Threatening criminal, administrative or disciplinary charges
Rule 3.10; R-PRF-071.
- **California only:** a lawyer may not threaten criminal, administrative or disciplinary charges to gain advantage in a civil dispute [VERIFY V-458].
- ABA: no rule. ABA Formal Op. 92-363 permits a threat if the matters are related, both are warranted, and there is no extortion.
- Extortion: Penal Code §§ 518–519; *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299.
- **Tested:** Oct. 2020 Q1 (using the opponent's affair), Feb. 2021 Q4 (threat to report a lawyer).

## IV. Transactions with persons other than clients
- **Truthfulness** (rule 4.1 / MR 4.1; B&P §§ 6106, 6128(a); R-PRF-072):
  - No knowing false statement of material fact or law to third persons.
  - Disclose when necessary to avoid assisting client fraud — unless confidentiality forbids it. California forbids it more often (no fraud exception), so the lawyer's route is withdrawal.
  - Negotiation puffery about price or settlement intentions is not a material fact.
- **Represented persons** (rule 4.2 / MR 4.2; R-PRF-073):
  - No communication about the subject of the representation without counsel's consent, unless authorized by law or court order.
  - Organizations: California covers current officers, directors, partners and managing agents, and any current employee or other constituent when the communication concerns that person's own act or omission in the matter that may be binding on or imputed to the organization (rule 4.2(b)). The ABA (MR 4.2 cmt. [7]) covers a constituent who supervises, directs or regularly consults with the organization's lawyer about the matter, has authority to obligate the organization in the matter, or whose act or omission in the matter may be imputed to it. The comment's former "statement may constitute an admission" category was deleted in 2002.
  - Parties may talk directly to each other, and a lawyer may advise a client about such communications (MR 4.2 cmt. [4]).
- **Unrepresented persons** (rule 4.3 / MR 4.3; R-PRF-074):
  - Do not imply disinterest; correct misunderstandings.
  - No legal advice to a person whose interests may conflict, other than to get counsel.
  - California 4.3(b): do not seek information the person may not reveal without breaching a duty to another.
- **Inadvertent writings and third-party rights** (rule 4.4 / MR 4.4; R-PRF-075):
  - California: stop reading and notify (*Rico v. Mitsubishi Motors Corp.* (2007) 42 Cal.4th 807; *State Comp. Ins. Fund v. WPS, Inc.* (1999) 70 Cal.App.4th 644).
  - ABA: notify only (MR 4.4(b)); MR 4.4(a) bars tactics whose only purpose is to embarrass, delay or burden, and evidence-gathering that violates legal rights.
  - California analogues: B&P § 6068(f) (advance no fact prejudicial to a party's or witness's honor or reputation unless the cause requires) [VERIFY V-950]; rule 3.10.
- **Tested:** Jul. 2012 Q2, Oct. 2020 Q1 (settlement leverage).

## V. Law firms and associations

### V.A Supervisory and subordinate lawyers
- **Managers and supervisors** (rule 5.1 / MR 5.1; R-PRF-076):
  - Managers: reasonable measures to assure compliance.
  - Supervisors: reasonable efforts.
  - Responsible for a violation the lawyer orders or ratifies, or fails to remedy while it can still be avoided or mitigated.
- **Subordinates** (rule 5.2 / MR 5.2; R-PRF-077): bound by the rules; protected only when following a supervisor's **reasonable** resolution of an **arguable** question.
- **Tested:** Jul. 2019 Q4.

### V.B Nonlawyer assistants
- Rule 5.3 / MR 5.3; R-PRF-078. Reasonable efforts so that nonlawyers act compatibly with the lawyer's duties. This covers receptionists, paralegals, investigators, cloud and AI vendors.
- **Tested:** Feb. 2019 Q5, Jul. 2021 Q2.

### V.C Disbarred, suspended, resigned or inactive lawyers (California rule 5.3.1)
Rule 5.3.1 [S-563]; R-PRF-079.

| Prohibited for the ineligible person | Permitted |
|---|---|
| Legal advice to the client | Research, data gathering, drafting pleadings and briefs |
| Appearances in any proceeding or before any judicial officer | Communicating with clients and third parties on scheduling, billing, updates, receipt of documents |
| Appearing as the client's representative at a deposition or other discovery | Accompanying an active lawyer at a deposition for clerical assistance only |
| Negotiating for the client | — |
| Handling client funds | — |
| Any other practice of law | — |

**Notice.** Written notice to the State Bar and to each affected client, before or when the work begins, and to the State Bar on termination.

### V.D Fee sharing with nonlawyers, ownership and independence
Rule 5.4 / MR 5.4; R-PRF-080.
- No fee sharing with nonlawyers, with exceptions: a deceased lawyer's estate; purchase of a practice; nonlawyer employee compensation or retirement plans; court-awarded fees shared with a nonprofit that employed, retained or recommended the lawyer (CA adds "or facilitated employment"); certified lawyer referral service fees (CA); and (CA rule 5.4(a)(6), eff. Mar. 22, 2021) settlement fees shared with a 501(c)(3) nonprofit under a written fee-division agreement [S-1258] [S-1259] [VERIFY V-951].
- No partnership with nonlawyers for law practice.
- No nonlawyer ownership, directorship or control.
- No interference by payors (rule 5.4(c)).
- **AB 931 (2025):** until Jan. 1, 2030, no fee sharing with out-of-state alternative business structures, with exceptions [S-124] [VERIFY V-031].
- Paying a nonlawyer for referrals also implicates rule 7.2(b) and B&P § 6152 (runners and cappers).
- **Tested:** Feb. 2021 Q4.

### V.E Restrictions on the right to practice; sale of a practice
- **Restrictions** (rule 5.6 / MR 5.6; R-PRF-081): no non-competes for lawyers, except retirement benefits; no settlement terms restricting practice (CA rule 5.6(a)(2); MR 5.6(b)). California rule 5.6(b) also bars agreements that preclude reporting a rule violation (no ABA counterpart) [S-1254]. *Howard v. Babcock* (1993) 6 Cal.4th 409 allows a reasonable cost on competing departing partners.
- **Sale of a practice** (rule 1.17 / MR 1.17): sale of all or substantially all of a practice or area of practice; written notice to clients; fees not increased because of the sale.

### V.F Unauthorized practice of law and multijurisdictional practice
**UPL** (B&P §§ 6125–6126; rule 5.5(a) / MR 5.5(a); R-PRF-082).
- Only active California licensees may practice law in California; UPL is a crime.
- A lawyer may not assist UPL: an unsupervised paralegal giving advice; a nonlawyer partner; an ineligible lawyer (rule 5.3.1).

**Multijurisdictional practice** (rule 5.5(b); Cal. Rules of Court, rules 9.40–9.48 [S-562]; R-PRF-083).
- An out-of-state lawyer may not maintain a resident office or a systematic and continuous presence in California, or hold out as admitted, unless authorized.
- California authorizations:

| Rule of Court | Who |
|---|---|
| 9.40 | Pro hac vice: one pending case; associate active California counsel; not a California resident or regularly employed or engaged in substantial activities here |
| 9.43 | Out-of-state arbitration counsel |
| 9.44 | Registered foreign legal consultants |
| 9.45 | Registered legal services attorneys |
| 9.46 | Registered in-house counsel (single employer) |
| 9.47 | Temporary practice as part of litigation; amended eff. Oct. 1, 2025 [VERIFY V-463] |
| 9.48 | Nonlitigating attorneys temporarily in California |

- *Birbrower, Montalbano, Condon & Frank, P.C. v. Superior Court* (1998) 17 Cal.4th 119: New York lawyers who advised a California client in California practiced law here without a license; no fees for that work. Physical presence is not required; sufficient contact with the client or "virtual" practice can count.
- **ABA MR 5.5(c)–(d):** temporary practice is allowed if:
  - in association with a local lawyer;
  - related to a pending or potential proceeding (lawyer authorized or expects to be);
  - related to arbitration, mediation or ADR arising from home practice; or
  - otherwise reasonably related to home-state practice.
  In-house counsel and federally authorized practice are also allowed. ABA Formal Op. 495 (2020) covers remote work.

**Tested:** Feb. 2019 Q5, Feb. 2026 Q3.

R-PRF-084.

## VII. Information about legal services (advertising; solicitation)
- **False or misleading communications** (rule 7.1 / MR 7.1; B&P §§ 6157–6159.2 [S-555]; R-PRF-085):
  - material misrepresentation, or an omission that makes the communication misleading;
  - California Board standards create presumptions: guarantees and predictions, testimonials without disclaimers, dramatizations without disclosure, "no fee unless we win" without disclosing cost liability;
  - *Bates v. State Bar of Arizona*, 433 U.S. 350 (1977).
- **Advertising and paying for recommendations** (rule 7.2 / MR 7.2; B&P §§ 6152, 6155; R-PRF-086):
  - no payment for recommendations, except advertising costs, legal services plans or qualified referral services, purchase of a practice, and non-exclusive disclosed reciprocal referrals;
  - gifts: California — a gift not given under any understanding about future referrals; ABA — nominal thank-you gifts only;
  - name and address of a responsible lawyer [S-555].
- **Solicitation** (rule 7.3 / MR 7.3; R-PRF-087):
  - Live person-to-person contact (in person, live telephone, real-time electronic) for pecuniary gain is banned.
  - Exceptions: the person is a lawyer, or has a family, close personal or prior professional relationship with the lawyer. The ABA adds persons who routinely use the type of legal services for business purposes; California has no such exception [S-1257].
  - No solicitation at all after a refusal, or with coercion, duress or harassment ("intrusion" in California).
  - California "Advertisement" label on targeted written, recorded or electronic solicitations.
  - **New California rule 7.3(f), eff. June 1, 2026:** no solicitation of a respondent in an ex parte DVRO proceeding before service and proof of service, unless a current or former client [S-120].
  - Constitutional cases: *Ohralik*, 436 U.S. 447 (1978); *In re Primus*, 436 U.S. 412 (1978); *Shapero*, 486 U.S. 466 (1988); *Florida Bar v. Went For It*, 515 U.S. 618 (1995).
- **Specialization; firm names** (rules 7.4, 7.5; MR 7.1–7.2; R-PRF-088): "certified specialist" only with recognized certification; no misleading trade names or implied partnerships.
- **Tested:** Feb. 2012 Q5, Feb. 2021 Q4 (chiropractor referrals and gifts), Jul. 2021 Q2 (holding out).

## VIII. Maintaining the integrity of the profession

### VIII.A Bar admission and disciplinary matters
- Rule 8.1 / MR 8.1; B&P § 6068(i); R-PRF-089. No false statements or misleading omissions in admission or discipline matters; respond to lawful demands; cooperate.
- Rule 8.1.1: comply with the conditions of discipline and agreements in lieu of discipline.

### VIII.B Judicial and legal officials
- Rule 8.2 / MR 8.2; R-PRF-090. No knowingly false or reckless statements about the qualifications or integrity of judges or candidates.
- The Supreme Court denied the State Bar's proposed amendments to rules 8.2 and 8.4 in April 2026 [S-121].

### VIII.C Duty to report misconduct (California rule 8.3)
R-PRF-091 [S-118] [S-119] [S-570].

| | ABA MR 8.3 | California rule 8.3 (eff. Aug. 1, 2023) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Lawyer **knows** another lawyer committed a violation | Lawyer knows of **credible evidence** |
| Conduct covered | Any rule violation raising a substantial question as to honesty, trustworthiness or fitness | Criminal act (as defined by California law); dishonesty, fraud, deceit; reckless or intentional misrepresentation; misappropriation of funds or property — raising a substantial question as to honesty, trustworthiness or fitness |
| Report to | Appropriate professional authority | State Bar or a tribunal with jurisdiction; without undue delay (8.3(a)) [S-119] |
| Other violations | — | May, but need not, report other violations of the rules or State Bar Act (8.3(b)) [S-1256] |

**Interplay with other rules.**
- Threatening to report another lawyer to gain advantage in a civil dispute violates California rule 3.10 (Feb. 2021 Q4).
- The 8.3 duty is satisfied by reporting; it is not a bargaining chip.

**Tested:** Feb. 2021 Q4, Jul. 2024 Q3.

### VIII.D Misconduct, moral turpitude and criminal conduct
**Rule 8.4 / MR 8.4** (R-PRF-092):
- violating the rules or assisting another to;
- criminal acts reflecting adversely on fitness;
- dishonesty, fraud, deceit — California adds "reckless or intentional misrepresentation";
- conduct prejudicial to the administration of justice;
- implying improper influence;
- assisting judicial misconduct.

**State Bar Act** (R-PRF-093):
- B&P § 6106: any act of moral turpitude, dishonesty or corruption, in or out of practice, crime or not.
- B&P §§ 6101–6102: conviction-based discipline.
- *In re Kelley* (1990) 52 Cal.3d 487: a DUI is not per se moral turpitude, but repeated DUIs while on probation supported discipline as "other misconduct warranting discipline" [S-565].

**Tested:** Jul. 2026 Q1 (in-house lawyer's misdemeanor DUI; relationship with a subordinate).

### VIII.E Self-reporting duties (B&P § 6068(o))
R-PRF-094 [S-552] [S-1250] [S-1251]. Within **30 days** of knowledge, report in writing to the State Bar:
1. Three or more malpractice or wrongful-conduct suits in 12 months.
2. Civil judgment for fraud, misrepresentation, breach of fiduciary duty or gross negligence in a professional capacity.
3. Judicial sanctions (not for discovery, and not under $1,000).
4. Felony indictment or information.
5. Conviction (guilty verdict, or guilty or no-contest plea) of a felony, or of a misdemeanor committed in the practice of law, with a client victim, or a necessary element of which involves improper attorney conduct including dishonesty or other moral turpitude; attempts, conspiracies and solicitations included [S-568] [S-1250].
6. Discipline by another licensing agency.
7. Reversal of a judgment based in whole or in part on the lawyer's misconduct, grossly incompetent representation or willful misrepresentation [S-1251].

Failure to report is a separate ground for discipline. **Tested:** Feb. 2026 Q3, Jul. 2026 Q1.

### VIII.F Discrimination, harassment and retaliation (rule 8.4.1 vs. ABA 8.4(g))
R-PRF-095 [S-1256] [S-1260].
- California never adopted ABA MR 8.4(g) [S-082].
- **California rule 8.4.1** prohibits **unlawful** discrimination, harassment and retaliation in representing clients, in accepting or terminating representation, and in law firm operations — which include a corporate legal department, a "law firm" under rule 1.0.1(c) [S-1260].
- **ABA 8.4(g)** covers conduct the lawyer knows or reasonably should know is harassment or discrimination in conduct related to the practice of law.

### VIII.G Disciplinary authority, choice of law and civility
- **Choice of law** (rule 8.5 / MR 8.5; R-PRF-096): the tribunal's rules for litigation conduct; otherwise the rules where the conduct occurred or has its predominant effect.
- **Civility oath** (Cal. Rules of Court, rules 9.4, 9.7; annual reaffirmation since Oct. 1, 2025) [S-561]. Incivility was **not** made a stand-alone ground for discipline (R-PRF-100).

## IX. Civil liability and remedies
- **Legal malpractice** (R-PRF-097):
  - elements: duty, breach, causation (case within a case), damages;
  - criminal malpractice requires actual innocence (*Wiley v. County of San Diego* (1998) 19 Cal.4th 532);
  - limitations: CCP § 340.6 (1 year from discovery / 4 years from the act, whichever first, with tolling).
- **Breach of fiduciary duty, fee forfeiture, unenforceable agreements** (R-PRF-098): *Sheppard Mullin* (2018); *Huskinson & Brown* (2004); Restatement (Third) of the Law Governing Lawyers § 37.
- **Disqualification** (R-PRF-040).
- **Fee arbitration** (R-PRF-099).
- **Rules as evidence.** Rule violations are not themselves causes of action but may evidence the standard of care (CRPC rule 1.0 cmt.; MR Scope [20]).
- **Criminal exposure:**
  - UPL (B&P § 6126);
  - deceit or collusion (B&P § 6128(a));
  - destroying evidence (Penal Code § 135);
  - extortion (Penal Code §§ 518–519);
  - runners and cappers (B&P § 6152).

## X. MPRE notes
**The test.** The user sits the MPRE on Mar. 23–24, 2027; California requires a scaled score of **86** [S-065] [S-066]. The MPRE has 60 multiple-choice questions, 50 scored and 10 unscored pretest items, with no penalty for wrong answers [S-567].

**What it covers.**
- ABA Model Rules.
- ABA Model Code of Judicial Conduct.
- Controlling constitutional decisions.
- Generally accepted principles of the law governing lawyers.
