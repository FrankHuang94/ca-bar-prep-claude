# Top rules: California essay-only subjects

**What this is.** 12–16 rules per subject, weighted by how often California tests each issue. Weighting draws on [`frequency-analysis.md`](../06-past-exams/frequency-analysis.md), [`index.csv`](../06-past-exams/index.csv) and each subject's `essay-templates.md`. Closely linked rule IDs share a row.

**How to use it.**
- Write the statement column from memory, then check it against the full block under the same ID in `rule-statements.md`. That file controls if the two ever differ.
- None of these subjects is on the MBE.

**Frequency data.**
- Essay counts are subject-level.
- Issue counts ("tagged in N") are index-level and indicative only [VERIFY V-070] [VERIFY V-071].

**Format by subject.**
- **PR:** each row shows California vs. ABA.
- **CP:** each row shows the presumption, who must rebut it, and how.
- **Wills and Trusts:** each row shows the Probate Code section.

Π = plaintiff; Δ = defendant.

## 1. Professional Responsibility (PRF)

> **How it is tested**
> - **Frequency:** labelled on 30 of 31 administrations (20/20 since Jul 2017), alone or crossed with BA, Evidence, CP, Contracts or Criminal. Tagged: conflicts 12; advocate duties 8; confidentiality 8; CA vs. ABA 8; competence and communication 6; fees 6.
> - **The call** says "California and ABA authorities." State the California rule, then the ABA rule, and conclude separately where they diverge (R-PRF-001).
> - **Numbers:** $1,000 written-fee threshold; 14-day notice and 45-day distribution of client funds; § 6068(e) "at every peril."

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/professional-responsibility/rule-statements.md) · [`ca-distinctions.md`](../03-subjects/professional-responsibility/ca-distinctions.md)

| # | Rule | California (write this) | ABA difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-PRF-021** Current-client conflicts | No representation directly adverse to a current client, or with a significant risk of material limitation from duties to others or the lawyer's own interests, unless the lawyer reasonably believes he can represent each client competently, it is lawful, no client sues another in the same case, and each gives **informed written consent** (rule 1.7). | Same test; **informed consent, confirmed in writing**, may be the lawyer's writing (MR 1.7). |
| 2 | **R-PRF-022** Written disclosure (1.7(c)) | Even without material-limitation risk, give **written disclosure** of a relationship with a party or witness, or a close relationship with opposing counsel (spouse, parent, child, sibling, cohabitant, client, intimate). | No stand-alone duty; treated as a possible personal-interest conflict (MR 1.7 cmt. [11]). |
| 3 | **R-PRF-026** Business transactions | A business deal or adverse pecuniary interest requires fair, reasonable terms fully disclosed in understandable writing; written advice to seek independent counsel with time to do so; and the client's informed written consent to the terms and the lawyer's role (rule 1.8.1). | Same three elements (MR 1.8(a)); ABA permits fee liens authorized by law, while CA treats a charging lien as an adverse interest (*Fletcher v. Davis*). |
| 4 | **R-PRF-034** Former clients | No representation materially adverse to a former client in the same or a substantially related matter (same transaction or dispute, or a substantial risk that confidential information normally obtained would materially advance the new client) without informed written consent (rule 1.9). | Same test; consent confirmed in writing. |
| 5 | **R-PRF-036, R-PRF-037** Imputation; screening | One lawyer's 1.7 or 1.9 conflict is imputed to the firm unless it rests on a personal interest posing no significant risk to the others. A lateral can be screened only if he **did not substantially participate**, is timely screened, takes no fee share, and the former client gets written notice (rule 1.10). | Screening works regardless of participation, with notice and certifications (MR 1.10(a)(2)). |
| 6 | **R-PRF-013** Confidentiality | "Maintain inviolate the confidence, and at every peril … preserve the secrets" of the client (B&P § 6068(e)(1)); reveal only with informed consent or under the one exception (rule 1.6). | Broader exceptions; impliedly authorized disclosures; express duty to guard against unauthorized disclosure (MR 1.6(c)). |
| 7 | **R-PRF-014, R-PRF-015** Exceptions | **May** reveal what is reasonably necessary to prevent a **criminal act** likely to cause death or substantial bodily harm; first try to dissuade and tell the client, if reasonable (§ 6068(e)(2)). No financial-fraud exception and no rule-based self-defense exception [VERIFY V-472]. | Seven permissive exceptions: reasonably certain death or SBH from any source; client crime or fraud causing substantial financial injury through the lawyer's services (prevent, mitigate, rectify); ethics advice; lawyer-client disputes; law or court order; conflict checks (MR 1.6(b)). |
| 8 | **R-PRF-016** Privilege vs. confidentiality | Confidentiality covers all representation information from any source, everywhere; privilege covers only confidential lawyer-client communications for legal advice when disclosure is compelled; work product covers the lawyer's preparation (Evid. Code §§ 952–955). | Same distinction (MR 1.6 cmt. [3]). |
| 9 | **R-PRF-008, R-PRF-010, R-PRF-005** Competence; communication; authority | Discipline for intentional, reckless, grossly negligent or repeated incompetence; competence includes mental, emotional and physical ability (rule 1.1). Keep clients informed and answer status requests (§ 6068(m)). Client decides objectives, settlement, plea, jury waiver and testifying (rule 1.2). | Simple competence standard (MR 1.1); communication and authority are largely the same. |
| 10 | **R-PRF-041, R-PRF-043, R-PRF-044** Fees | No **unconscionable** or illegal fee, judged when made (overreaching, nondisclosure, value, sophistication, consent). A contingency agreement must be signed and state the rates, costs, and that the fee "is not set by law and is negotiable," or it is voidable (B&P § 6147). No contingency for criminal defense, or on securing a divorce or the amount of support or property. | "**Unreasonable**" standard, a lower bar (MR 1.5(a)); written contingency terms but no negotiability statement (MR 1.5(c)). |
| 11 | **R-PRF-042, R-PRF-046** Fee writing; splitting | Foreseeable expense over **$1,000** needs a signed written agreement, else voidable (B&P § 6148). Splitting with another firm needs a written lawyer agreement, the client's written consent after full written disclosure, and no increased total; pure referral fees are allowed (rule 1.5.1). | Basis "preferably" in writing (MR 1.5(b)); division must be proportional to work or with joint responsibility (MR 1.5(e)). |
| 12 | **R-PRF-047, R-PRF-048** Trust account | Client funds, including advances, go in a California trust account with no commingling; notify receipt within 14 days, account, and distribute undisputed funds (presumed violation after 45 days) [VERIFY V-469] (rule 1.15). Misappropriation is moral turpitude (§ 6106). | Same core; "promptly," no fixed day counts (MR 1.15). |
| 13 | **R-PRF-050, R-PRF-051** Withdrawal | **Must** withdraw if continuing violates the rules, the lawyer's condition makes representation unreasonably difficult, the lawyer is fired, or the client pursues litigation without probable cause to harass or maliciously injure. **May** withdraw on listed grounds (unwarranted claims, client crime or fraud, fee breach after warning, consent) [VERIFY V-457]. Get court permission where required. | Since 2023, duty to inquire and withdraw if the client persists in using the lawyer for crime or fraud [VERIFY V-470]; may withdraw when no material adverse effect results. |
| 14 | **R-PRF-060, R-PRF-061** Candor | No knowing false statements to a tribunal; correct them; disclose directly adverse controlling authority (rule 3.3; § 6068(d)). If false evidence is offered, remonstrate, then seek to withdraw, but do not disclose confidences (§ 6068(e)) [VERIFY V-461]. | Candor overrides confidentiality: disclose to the tribunal if necessary (MR 3.3(c)). |
| 15 | **R-PRF-062, R-PRF-071** Fairness; threats | Do not obstruct, destroy, conceal or suppress evidence, or assist false testimony (rule 3.4). Do not threaten criminal, administrative or disciplinary charges for civil advantage (rule 3.10) [VERIFY V-458]. | Same fairness duties; no threat rule, so related, warranted threats short of extortion are allowed (ABA Op. 92-363). |
| 16 | **R-PRF-055, R-PRF-056** Organization client | The organization is the client; if a constituent's conduct threatens substantial injury, report up to the highest authority. **May not report out** confidential information; urge, then resign (rule 1.13). | May report out if the highest authority fails to address a clear violation reasonably certain to cause substantial injury, unless retained to investigate or defend it (MR 1.13(c)). |

**Also drill (must-know tier):** R-PRF-011, R-PRF-017, R-PRF-023, R-PRF-029, R-PRF-031, R-PRF-032, R-PRF-053, R-PRF-063, R-PRF-066, R-PRF-073, R-PRF-076, R-PRF-077, R-PRF-082, R-PRF-083, R-PRF-086, R-PRF-087, R-PRF-091, R-PRF-094.

## 2. Community Property (CMP)

> **How it is tested**
> - **Frequency:** 17 essays on 17/31 administrations (11/20 since Jul 2017). Tagged: presumptions 5; management and fiduciary duty 4; QCP 4; SP, characterization, agreements 3 each; debts, separation, credit 2 each. Pairs with Wills (4).
> - **The call** says "according to California law."
> - **Method per asset:** source and time → presumption → who rebuts, how → agreement or transmutation → apportionment or reimbursement → equal division (R-CMP-008, R-CMP-075).

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/community-property/rule-statements.md) · [`essay-templates.md`](../03-subjects/community-property/essay-templates.md)

| # | Rule | Write this | Presumption / tracing logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-CMP-001, R-CMP-002** CP and SP | Property acquired during marriage while domiciled in California is CP; each spouse owns an equal half (Fam. Code § 760). SP: owned before marriage, gifts and inheritances, rents and profits of SP, post-separation earnings (§§ 770–771). | Classify by **source and time**, not by title or manager. |
| 2 | **R-CMP-003, R-CMP-091, R-CMP-092** QCP; death | QCP: acquired while domiciled elsewhere, would be CP here (§ 125); divided as CP at dissolution if both live in California [VERIFY V-305]. At death the survivor keeps half the CP and half the decedent's QCP (Prob. Code §§ 100–101). | "Non-CP State X" means QCP. At death, out-of-state land is not QCP (§ 66). |
| 3 | **R-CMP-011** General presumption | Property acquired during marriage is presumed CP; the SP claimant must rebut, generally by a preponderance. | Burden on the **SP claimant**: trace (row 8) or prove pre-marriage, gift, or post-separation timing. |
| 4 | **R-CMP-012** Title | Title in one spouse's name does not make property SP; property bought with CP stays CP absent valid transmutation (*Valli*). | Interspousal title change is a transmutation question (row 6). |
| 5 | **R-CMP-013, R-CMP-042** Joint title; § 2640 | At dissolution, property acquired in joint form is presumed CP, rebuttable only by a clear title statement or written agreement (§ 2581). Traced SP contributions to acquisition (down payment, improvements, principal) are reimbursed without interest, capped at net value, unless waived in writing (§ 2640). | Joint title → CP → § 2640 credit, which excludes interest, taxes, insurance and maintenance. Not at death or against creditors. |
| 6 | **R-CMP-022, R-CMP-023, R-CMP-016** Transmutation | Since 1985 a transmutation requires a writing with an **express declaration** changing character, made or accepted by the adversely affected spouse, apparent on its face (§ 852(a); *MacDonald*). An advantaging interspousal deal is presumed procured by undue influence (§ 721(b)). | No writing, no transmutation (*Benson*). Personal gifts not substantial in value are excepted (§ 852(c)). Advantaged spouse must prove free, informed consent. |
| 7 | **R-CMP-018, R-CMP-019** Premarital agreements | Written, signed, no consideration needed (§ 1611). Involuntary unless: independent counsel or written waiver, seven days between final draft and signing, written explanation if unrepresented, and no duress, fraud, undue influence or incapacity (§ 1615(c)) [VERIFY V-300]. | Challenger's burden; walk each factor. No undue-influence presumption premaritally (*Bonds*). |
| 8 | **R-CMP-028 to R-CMP-031** Tracing | Commingling does not change character, but untraceable SP becomes CP. Trace by **direct tracing** (SP funds available and intended at purchase; *Mix*) or **exhaustion** (CP funds already spent on family expenses; *See*). | Family expenses are presumed paid with CP; SP used for them is a gift. Recapitulation fails. |
| 9 | **R-CMP-033 to R-CMP-036** SP business | Community labor increasing an SP business is apportioned. **Pereira**: SP gets capital plus fair return; rest CP (effort-driven growth). **Van Camp**: CP gets reasonable salary minus amounts received; rest SP (capital-driven growth). | Compute both; court picks what achieves substantial justice. |
| 10 | **R-CMP-037, R-CMP-038** Moore/Marsden | CP principal payments on an SP home's purchase loan earn a pro tanto share: principal paid + (principal ÷ price) × marital appreciation; interest, taxes and insurance excluded. Premarital appreciation stays SP. | Show the arithmetic. |
| 11 | **R-CMP-048, R-CMP-049** Pensions | Pensions are CP to the extent earned during marriage before separation, vested or not; time rule: marital months ÷ total months × each payment (*Brown*). | Education is not property; § 2641 reimbursement instead (R-CMP-046). |
| 12 | **R-CMP-058** Credit acquisitions | Loan proceeds are SP only if the lender intended and relied primarily on the borrower's SP (*Gudelj*). | Presumed CP; ask what the lender relied on. |
| 13 | **R-CMP-063, R-CMP-064, R-CMP-067** Management | Spouses manage CP as fiduciaries with full disclosure (§§ 721(b), 1100(e)). No gift of CP personalty without written consent (voidable entirely in life, half after death) [VERIFY V-309]. Both spouses must join to sell, encumber or lease CP realty over a year; a lone transfer is voidable, but if title stood in the transferor alone, a good-faith purchaser is presumed valid and suit must come within one year of recording (§ 1102) [VERIFY V-316]. | Remedies: § 1101 (R-CMP-068): 50% of undisclosed assets, 100% for fraud or malice. |
| 14 | **R-CMP-070, R-CMP-071, R-CMP-073, R-CMP-074** Separation | Separation requires expressed intent to end the marriage plus consistent conduct (§ 70). Post-separation earnings are SP (§ 771). SP payments of community debts earn *Epstein* credits [VERIFY V-321]; exclusive use earns *Watts* charges. | Fix the separation date first. |
| 15 | **R-CMP-084, R-CMP-086, R-CMP-089** Debts | The community estate pays either spouse's pre-separation debts (§ 910); SP pays only its owner's debts plus necessaries (§ 913). At dissolution, marital debts split equally; post-separation debts go to the incurring spouse (§§ 2620–2627). | Creditor reach first, then allocation between spouses. |

## 3. Wills and Succession (WIL)

> **How it is tested**
> - **Frequency:** 13 essays on 13/31 administrations (7/20 since Jul 2017). Tagged: execution (attested and holographic) 4; omitted children 3; omitted spouses 3; revocation and revival, adoption, CP/QCP at death 2 each.
> - **The call** says "according to California law." Pairs with Trusts (6) and CP (4).
> - **Order for "What rights do A, B and C have?":** property at death → validity (execution, capacity, undue influence) → contents → revocation → construction (lapse, ademption, abatement) → omitted heirs → intestacy for anything left.

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/wills-succession/rule-statements.md) · [`essay-templates.md`](../03-subjects/wills-succession/essay-templates.md)

| # | Rule · Prob. Code § | Write this | Trap / contrast |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-WIL-001, R-WIL-002** · §§ 100–102, 6101 | At death, half the CP and half the decedent's QCP belong to the survivor; the decedent may will only SP, half the CP and half the QCP. The survivor may recapture half of QCP the decedent gave away without substantial consideration or the survivor's written consent, if the decedent kept possession, income, a power to revoke, or survivorship. | Out-of-state land is not QCP at death (§ 66). A will giving away the survivor's half raises equitable election. |
| 2 | **R-WIL-016, R-WIL-018** · §§ 6401, 6402 | The intestate spouse takes the decedent's half of CP and QCP plus SP: all if no issue, parent, sibling or sibling's issue; ½ if one child (or issue of one), or no issue but a parent or parent's issue; ⅓ if more than one child. The rest passes to issue, then parents, then parents' issue, then grandparents or their issue. | A former spouse is not a surviving spouse (§ 78). The UPC gives the spouse more. |
| 3 | **R-WIL-011** · § 240 | By representation (the default): divide at the first generation with a living member, one share per living member and per deceased member with living issue; deceased members' shares pass down the same way. | "Per stirpes" in an instrument divides at the children's generation (§ 246) [VERIFY V-523]. |
| 4 | **R-WIL-027, R-WIL-029** · §§ 6450–6451, 6454 | An adoptee inherits from the adopting parents. Adoption cuts off a natural parent unless they lived together as parent and child and the adoption was by a stepparent or after a natural parent's death [VERIFY V-526]. A stepchild or foster child inherits only if the relationship began in minority, lasted for joint lifetimes, and clear and convincing evidence shows the parent would have adopted but for a legal barrier lasting until death. | A barrier that ended at majority fails (*Joseph*). |
| 5 | **R-WIL-047, R-WIL-049** · §§ 6110, 6112 | An attested will is a writing signed by the testator (or by another in the testator's presence at the testator's direction) and signed during the testator's life by two witnesses, present together, who saw the signing or acknowledgment and understand it is the will. A gift to an interested witness is presumed procured by fraud or undue influence unless two other disinterested witnesses signed; if unrebutted, the witness takes only what it would get without the will. | No publication and no witnessing in the testator's presence required. Common-law purge statutes void the gift outright. |
| 6 | **R-WIL-048** · § 6110(c)(2) | A will with a witnessing defect is valid if clear and convincing evidence shows the testator, when signing, intended it as the will. | Cures witnessing defects only, not a missing writing or testator's signature. UPC § 2-503 is broader. |
| 7 | **R-WIL-050, R-WIL-051** · § 6111 | A will is a valid holograph if the signature and material provisions are in the testator's handwriting; no witnesses are needed, and printed intent language is fine. If undated, it is invalid to the extent inconsistent with another will unless shown to be later, and invalid if the testator lacked capacity at any possible time of execution. | Typed non-material words are surplusage (*Black*). Handwritten additions to a typed will work only as a holographic codicil (R-WIL-079). |
| 8 | **R-WIL-033, R-WIL-039, R-WIL-040** · §§ 6100.5, 6104, 86 | **Capacity** at execution: understand the nature of the act, the property, and relations to family and those affected. **Undue influence** overcomes free will; it is presumed from a confidential relationship, the beneficiary's active procurement, and undue benefit, shifting the burden to the proponent (*Rice v. Clark*). Only the tainted parts fail (§ 6104). | Age, illness or eccentricity alone is not incapacity; a lucid interval suffices. |
| 9 | **R-WIL-095** · § 21380 | A gift to the drafter, a fiduciary who transcribed the instrument, or a care custodian of a dependent adult (instrument made during or within 90 days of care), or to certain of their relatives, cohabitants, employees or law-firm colleagues, is presumed the product of fraud or undue influence. It is rebuttable only by clear and convincing evidence, and conclusive for the drafter and associates [VERIFY V-505]. | PR crossover: a lawyer drafts a gift to himself. Exceptions and certificate: R-WIL-096. |
| 10 | **R-WIL-058, R-WIL-060, R-WIL-061** · §§ 6130, 6131, 6300 | **Incorporation by reference:** a writing existing at execution, intended to be incorporated and described enough to identify. **Independent significance:** gifts may turn on acts with significance apart from the will. **Pour-over:** a gift to a trust identified in the will and set out in a writing made before, with, or within 60 days after the will is valid even if the trust is unfunded or amendable. | A later-made list fails incorporation. If the trust is revoked before death, the pour-over lapses. |
| 11 | **R-WIL-062 to R-WIL-065** · §§ 21109–21111 | A beneficiary who fails to survive takes nothing. **Anti-lapse:** if the beneficiary was kindred of the testator or of the testator's surviving, deceased or former spouse, the beneficiary's issue take by representation, unless the will states a contrary intent; a survivorship requirement is contrary intent. A failed gift falls to the residue; a failed residuary share goes to the other residuary takers. | Spouses and friends are not kindred. The UPC treats survivorship words alone as insufficient. |
| 12 | **R-WIL-068, R-WIL-069, R-WIL-075** · §§ 21117, 21139, 21402 | Classify gifts as specific, general, demonstrative or residuary; doubtful gifts are treated as general. A specific gift adeems if the item is gone, but California looks to intent: a change in form does not adeem, and only a disposed-of, untraceable item adeems regardless of intent (*Mason*) [VERIFY V-534]. **Abatement:** intestate property, residue, general to non-relatives, general to relatives, specific to non-relatives, specific to relatives. | The majority identity theory adeems whenever the item is gone. |
| 13 | **R-WIL-077, R-WIL-078, R-WIL-082** · §§ 6120, 6122 | A will is revoked by a later will (expressly or by inconsistency), or by burning, tearing, canceling, obliterating or destroying with intent, by the testator or another in the testator's presence at the testator's direction. Divorce or annulment revokes gifts and nominations to the former spouse unless the will says otherwise. | Legal separation does not revoke. Gifts to the ex-spouse's relatives survive. |
| 14 | **R-WIL-084, R-WIL-085** · § 6123 | Revoking a later will by physical act does not revive the earlier will unless circumstances or the testator's declarations show that intent. **DRR:** a revocation based on a mistaken belief that another disposition would work is disregarded if the testator would not otherwise have revoked. | No automatic revival. Try DRR when revival fails. |
| 15 | **R-WIL-086, R-WIL-087** · §§ 21610, 21611 | A spouse married after all testamentary instruments and not provided for takes the decedent's half of CP and QCP plus an intestate share of SP, capped at half the SP. Exceptions: intentional omission appearing in the instruments, a transfer in lieu, or a valid waiver. | A general disinheritance clause that does not mention the spouse or a future marriage is not enough (*Shannon*). |
| 16 | **R-WIL-090 to R-WIL-092** · §§ 21620–21622 | A child born or adopted after all testamentary instruments and not provided for takes an intestate share. Exceptions: intentional omission appearing in the instruments; the decedent had other children and left substantially all to the omitted child's other parent; or provision outside the will in lieu. A child omitted only because the decedent believed it dead or unknown also takes an intestate share. | Children alive at execution are not omitted (except § 21622); estrangement gives no claim. |

## 4. Trusts (TRS)

> **How it is tested**
> - **Frequency:** 7 essays on 7/31 administrations (3/20 since Jul 2017), the least frequent labelled subject but Wills' most common partner (6). Tagged: modification and termination, trustee liability, charitable trusts and cy pres, trustee duties 2 each.
> - **The call** says "according to California law."
> - **Order:** creation → revocation or amendment → duties breached, one by one → remedies and who is liable → modification or termination → creditors.

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/trusts/rule-statements.md) · [`essay-templates.md`](../03-subjects/trusts/essay-templates.md)

| # | Rule · Prob. Code § | Write this | Trap / contrast |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-TRS-001, R-TRS-002** · §§ 15200, 15208 | A trust is a fiduciary relationship in which a trustee holds title to property for beneficiaries. A settlor creates it by declaration, lifetime transfer, will, exercise of a power of appointment, or enforceable promise; no consideration is needed. | A sole trustee cannot be sole beneficiary. A trust does not fail for want of a trustee (R-TRS-007). |
| 2 | **R-TRS-004, R-TRS-005, R-TRS-018** · §§ 15201, 15202, 15205 | Elements: present intent to impose enforceable duties (precatory "wish" or "hope" usually fails); existing, identifiable property (not an expectancy); and ascertainable beneficiaries (a definite class suffices, "my friends" does not) [VERIFY V-589]. | A settlor-trustee's signed schedule listing an asset can make it trust property without a deed (*Heggstad*) [VERIFY V-583]. |
| 3 | **R-TRS-010, R-WIL-061** · § 6300 | A pour-over to a trust in a writing made before, with, or within 60 days after the will is valid even if the trust is unfunded or amendable; the property follows the trust as amended. | Traditional rule: the trust must exist by the will's execution. |
| 4 | **R-TRS-011, R-TRS-012, R-TRS-013** · §§ 15400, 15401 | A California trust is revocable unless expressly irrevocable [VERIFY V-551]. Revoke by the trust's method or by a signed writing (not a will) delivered to the trustee in the settlor's lifetime, unless the trust's method is explicitly exclusive. A later will does not revoke unless the trust allows it. | Common law: irrevocable unless the power is reserved. Under the UTC, a will expressly referring to the trust may revoke it if the method is not exclusive; argue both sides [VERIFY V-594]. |
| 5 | **R-TRS-014** · § 15402 | A settlor may modify a revocable trust by the revocation procedure, including a delivered signed writing, unless the trust's modification method is explicitly exclusive (*Haggerty v. Thornton* (2024)). | A recent change: older strict-compliance cases are disapproved. |
| 6 | **R-TRS-019, R-TRS-021** · § 17200 | A charitable trust serves a public purpose (poverty, education, religion, health, government, community), needs no ascertainable beneficiary, and is enforced by the Attorney General. If its purpose becomes impossible, impracticable or illegal and the settlor had general charitable intent, cy pres redirects it to the nearest purpose; otherwise a resulting trust follows. | A gift over on failure shows specific intent. The Restatement and UTC presume general intent; California requires it [VERIFY V-559]. |
| 7 | **R-TRS-024, R-TRS-025** · §§ 15403, 15404 | The settlor and all beneficiaries may modify or terminate even an irrevocable trust. All beneficiaries alone may petition, but if a material purpose remains (spendthrift, support, delayed distribution), the court must find the reason for change outweighs it [VERIFY V-555]. | Majority *Claflin* rule: a material purpose is an absolute bar. Write both. |
| 8 | **R-TRS-026** · § 15409 | The court may modify or terminate a trust if circumstances the settlor did not know or anticipate mean continuing would defeat or substantially impair its purposes; a spendthrift clause is a factor only [VERIFY V-555]. | A foreseen change, or a wish for a better return, is not enough. |
| 9 | **R-TRS-036, R-TRS-038, R-TRS-040** · § 21111; Civ. Code §§ 2223–2224 | A failed express trust produces a resulting trust for the settlor or the estate. A constructive trust forces a wrongdoer who gained property by fraud, mistake, undue influence or breach of trust to convey it. Failed testamentary trust property falls into the residue. | A constructive trust is a remedy, not an express trust. Cy pres comes before a resulting trust. |
| 10 | **R-TRS-041, R-TRS-046** · §§ 15300–15301, 15306.5, 15307 | A spendthrift clause bars transfer of a beneficiary's interest before payment. In California, a general creditor may still reach up to 25% of each payment (after support needs), and separately any amount beyond what is needed for education and support [VERIFY V-565]. | General law: a valid spendthrift clause bars general creditors entirely. |
| 11 | **R-TRS-047, R-TRS-048, R-TRS-092, R-TRS-093** · §§ 15303, 15304, 18200, 19001 | A settlor's creditors reach the most the trustee could pay the settlor from a self-settled trust [VERIFY V-567], and all revocable-trust property during life and, after death, to the extent the estate is inadequate [VERIFY V-568]. Creditors cannot compel discretionary payments. | No self-settled asset-protection trusts in California. |
| 12 | **R-TRS-052, R-TRS-053, R-TRS-055** · §§ 16002, 16004 | A trustee must act solely in the beneficiaries' interest. Self-dealing (buying trust property, selling to the trust, borrowing trust funds) is a breach regardless of fairness or good faith, unless the instrument, informed consent or a court authorizes it. Lesser conflicts also breach. | "No further inquiry": a fair price is no defense. |
| 13 | **R-TRS-051, R-TRS-057, R-TRS-058** · §§ 16040, 16047–16051 | Use the care, skill and caution of a prudent person in a like capacity. **Prudent investor:** judge decisions by the whole portfolio and strategy, diversify unless imprudent, review inherited assets promptly, no hindsight [VERIFY V-571]. | The old prudent-man rule judged each investment alone. Unpaid trustees are held to the same standard. |
| 14 | **R-TRS-056, R-TRS-063, R-TRS-068, R-TRS-069** · §§ 16003, 16062, 16080, 16081 | Treat income and remainder beneficiaries impartially. Account at least annually to current beneficiaries [VERIFY V-593]. Exercise discretion reasonably and in good faith; even "absolute" discretion bars bad faith, and a trustee-beneficiary may self-distribute only for health, education, support or maintenance [VERIFY V-576]. | No accounts are owed while the trust is revocable. |
| 15 | **R-TRS-078, R-TRS-079, R-TRS-081, R-TRS-083** · §§ 16400–16440 | For breach, a court may compel, enjoin, surcharge, remove, reduce pay, set aside acts, or impose a constructive trust or lien. The trustee is liable for whichever is appropriate: loss with interest, profit made, or profit the trust would have earned; for self-dealing, beneficiaries may rescind or take the profit. Co-trustees are liable if they participated, concealed, or failed to seek redress [VERIFY V-579]. | A BFP takes free (R-TRS-095). |

## 5. Remedies (REM)

> **How it is tested**
> - **Frequency:** 17 essays on 16/31 administrations (11/20 since Jul 2017), often a "what remedies?" call on Contracts (4), Torts (3), Con Law or BA. Tagged: equitable remedies 8; damages 5; basic principles 4; constructive trusts and liens, defenses, TRO/PI 3 each.
> - **Skeleton:** legal remedies → restitution → equitable remedies with defenses → best remedy (R-REM-006). If the call names remedies, answer only those, in order.
> - **California sentences to know:** § 3294, § 3387, *IT Corp.*, § 3343, § 1692.

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/remedies/rule-statements.md) · [`essay-templates.md`](../03-subjects/remedies/essay-templates.md)

| # | Rule | Write this | CA essay note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-REM-001, R-REM-002** Classification; adequacy | Remedies are compensatory, restitutionary, coercive or declaratory. Legal remedies issue as of right; equitable remedies are discretionary and need an inadequate legal remedy and a favorable balance of equities. | Begin every injunction or SP analysis by explaining why damages fail. No double recovery (R-REM-005). |
| 2 | **R-REM-007 to R-REM-011** Damages limits | Compensatory damages restore Π's position; each item must be caused by Δ, foreseeable (contract: at formation; tort: all proximately caused detriment), reasonably certain, and unavoidable by mitigation. | Civ. Code § 3300 (contract) vs. § 3333 (tort). Δ bears the mitigation burden. |
| 3 | **R-REM-015** Land-sale damages | When a seller refuses to convey, the buyer gets market value at breach minus price, plus incidental and consequential losses and payments made. | § 3306 gives benefit of the bargain without a good-faith limit [VERIFY V-355]. |
| 4 | **R-REM-019, R-REM-021** Property torts | Harm to land: diminution or restoration cost, usually the lesser, plus loss of use. Conversion: value at conversion plus interest. | Compare replevin and restitution; § 3336 alternatives [VERIFY V-357]. |
| 5 | **R-REM-023** Fraud damages | A defrauded party recovers out-of-pocket loss or (majority) benefit of the bargain, plus consequential and, if intentional, punitive damages. | § 3343 limits property-sale fraud to out-of-pocket plus additional damages, except possibly against fiduciaries [VERIFY V-351]. |
| 6 | **R-REM-026 to R-REM-031** Punitive damages | Available for malicious or reckless torts, never for breach of contract alone; due process weighs reprehensibility, ratio and comparable penalties (*Gore*, *State Farm*). | § 3294: clear and convincing evidence of oppression, fraud or malice [VERIFY V-369]. |
| 7 | **R-REM-035, R-REM-036** Restitution | Restitution measures Δ's unjust gain: an innocent recipient pays reasonable value; a conscious wrongdoer disgorges all profit [VERIFY V-350]. | Whether unjust enrichment is a standalone claim is disputed [VERIFY V-361]. |
| 8 | **R-REM-041** Replevin | Replevin recovers specific personal property plus detention damages; a prejudgment writ requires notice, hearing and usually a bond (CCP § 511.010 et seq.). | If the chattel is gone, use conversion damages or a constructive trust. |
| 9 | **R-REM-043** Rescission | Rescission unwinds a contract voidable for fraud, misrepresentation, mistake, duress, undue influence or incapacity, or materially breached; each side restores what it received. | Rescind by prompt notice and restoring (or offering to restore) benefits, then sue; § 1692 allows consequential damages. |
| 10 | **R-REM-044 to R-REM-046** Constructive trust; lien | A constructive trust makes Δ convey wrongfully acquired property, with appreciation and priority. An equitable lien secures Π's claim and allows a deficiency [VERIFY V-350]. | Rising asset: trust. Falling asset: lien. |
| 11 | **R-REM-047 to R-REM-049** Tracing; BFP | Π follows property through exchanges; in commingled accounts, the lowest intermediate balance caps recovery. BFPs take free; donees do not [VERIFY V-350]. | Write the tracing chain and a balance table. |
| 12 | **R-REM-055 to R-REM-060** Permanent injunction | Π shows an inadequate legal remedy, a protectable interest, feasible enforcement, a favorable hardship balance and public interest, and no defense. | A willful Δ gets no balancing. |
| 13 | **R-REM-065 to R-REM-067** TRO; preliminary injunction | Federal TROs need immediate irreparable harm; PIs need likely success, irreparable harm, balance of equities and public interest (*Winter*). | *IT Corp.* sliding scale: likely success plus relative interim harm [VERIFY V-353]. |
| 14 | **R-REM-070 to R-REM-072, R-REM-064** Specific performance | SP requires a definite contract, Π's readiness, an inadequate legal remedy, feasibility and no defense. Land and unique goods qualify; personal services get at most a negative injunction. | § 3387 presumes land unique; conclusive for an owner-occupied single-family home. § 3423(e) for services [VERIFY V-352]. |
| 15 | **R-REM-075, R-REM-081, R-REM-082, R-REM-086** Reformation; defenses | Reformation rewrites a writing to match the actual agreement where mutual mistake, fraud or a known unilateral mistake caused the error; proof by clear and convincing evidence, and the SOF and parol evidence rule do not bar it (Civ. Code § 3399). Defenses: laches (unreasonable delay plus prejudice), unclean hands (same transaction), undue hardship. | California applies unclean hands to some legal claims [VERIFY V-370]. |

## 6. Business Associations (BUS)

> **How it is tested**
> - **Frequency:** 14 essays on 14/31 administrations (8/20 since Jul 2017); crossovers with PR (2) and Remedies (1). Tagged: general partnerships 4, director duties 4; veil piercing, formation, agency 3 each; derivative suits 2.
> - **Approach:** general law (RUPA, MBCA, Delaware, the Restatement of Agency), then "In California, …"
> - **Liability-chain calls:** entity first, then each owner or agent.

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/business-associations/rule-statements.md) · [`essay-templates.md`](../03-subjects/business-associations/essay-templates.md)

| # | Rule | Write this | General law vs. California |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-BUS-001, R-BUS-002** Agency | Agency arises when a principal manifests assent that an agent act for it under its control, and the agent consents; no writing or consideration is needed. Employee status turns on the right to control the manner and means of work. | Gratuitous agents still owe fiduciary duties [VERIFY V-701]. |
| 2 | **R-BUS-008, R-BUS-009, R-BUS-010** Authority | Actual authority comes from the principal's manifestations to the agent; apparent authority from manifestations to the third party. Ratification with knowledge binds from the start. | California calls apparent authority "ostensible" (Civ. Code § 2317) [VERIFY V-702]. |
| 3 | **R-BUS-012** Agent's liability | An agent for a disclosed principal is not a party; for an unidentified or undisclosed principal, the agent is also liable. | Signing without naming the principal risks personal liability. |
| 4 | **R-BUS-014, R-BUS-015, R-BUS-016** Respondeat superior | Employers are liable for employee torts within the scope of employment; detours stay within it, frolics do not; intentional torts count only if they serve the employer or the job made them foreseeable. | California asks whether the risk was engendered by the employment (*Lisa M.*). |
| 5 | **R-BUS-021, R-BUS-022, R-BUS-023** Partnership formation | Two or more persons carrying on as co-owners a business for profit form a partnership, whether or not intended (Corp. Code § 16202). Profit-sharing presumes partnership unless it is wages, rent, interest or similar. | Partnership by estoppel binds those who represent themselves as partners (§ 16308). |
| 6 | **R-BUS-025, R-BUS-027** Partner authority | Each partner is an agent; acts apparently in the ordinary course bind the partnership unless the third party knew of the lack of authority (§ 16301). | Secret limits do not bind outsiders. |
| 7 | **R-BUS-028, R-BUS-029** Partner liability | All partners are jointly and severally liable, but creditors must first exhaust partnership assets unless liability is independent (§§ 16306–16307). | An LLP shields partners (R-BUS-045). |
| 8 | **R-BUS-034, R-BUS-035** Partner duties | Loyalty: account for benefits, no adverse dealing, no competition (§ 16404(b)). Care: avoid gross negligence, recklessness, intentional misconduct, and knowing violations of law. | *Meinhard*: "punctilio of an honor." |
| 9 | **R-BUS-047, R-BUS-048** Veil piercing | Courts pierce when unity of interest erases separateness and respecting the entity would promote injustice; factors include undercapitalization, commingling and ignored formalities. | Tort creditors pierce more easily. California weighs undercapitalization heavily (*Minton*). |
| 10 | **R-BUS-051, R-BUS-052, R-BUS-053** Defective incorporation | De jure existence begins on filing. A de facto corporation needs a statute, a good-faith attempt and use of corporate powers; estoppel can bar creditors who dealt with it as a corporation. | MBCA § 2.04 imposes liability on those who knew there was no incorporation [VERIFY V-713]. |
| 11 | **R-BUS-054, R-BUS-055** Promoters | Promoters are liable on pre-incorporation contracts even after adoption, absent a novation; the corporation is bound only from adoption [VERIFY V-714]. | Adoption is not ratification: there is no relation back. |
| 12 | **R-BUS-061, R-BUS-062** Care; BJR | Directors must act in good faith with ordinary prudence and reasonable inquiry; the BJR presumes informed, disinterested, good-faith decisions unless rebutted. | California codifies the standard in Corp. Code § 309. |
| 13 | **R-BUS-064, R-BUS-065, R-BUS-066** Loyalty | Conflicted fiduciaries lose the BJR and must show fairness unless disinterested directors or shareholders approve after disclosure. | California § 310 also requires board-approved deals to be just and reasonable [VERIFY V-726]. |
| 14 | **R-BUS-067** Corporate opportunity | A fiduciary may not take an opportunity in the corporation's line of business or expectancy without first offering it with full disclosure. | California treats it as a fact question (*Kelegian*) [VERIFY V-725]. |
| 15 | **R-BUS-081 to R-BUS-084** Derivative suits | Derivative suits redress corporate injury; plaintiffs need contemporaneous and continuous ownership and must make demand or plead futility. | Corp. Code § 800 allows waiver of contemporaneous ownership and a bond up to $50,000 [VERIFY V-707]. |
| 16 | **R-BUS-088 to R-BUS-092** Securities | Rule 10b-5 requires material misstatement, scienter, purchase or sale, reliance, loss and loss causation. Insiders must disclose or abstain; tippees are liable if the tipper breached for personal benefit. § 16(b) recaptures short-swing profits. | Federal law applies the same way on every essay. |
