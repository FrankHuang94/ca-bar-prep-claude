# Top rules: MBE subjects

**What this is.** About 15 rules per MBE subject, chosen from each subject's must-know tier and weighted toward NCBE's heaviest categories. Closely linked rule IDs share a row.

**How to use it.**
- Write the **Write this** column from memory, then check it against the full block under the same ID in the subject's `rule-statements.md`. That file controls if the two ever differ.
- **MBE** marks the distractor or the default rule for multiple choice. **CA** marks the extra sentence a California essay grader expects.
- Π = plaintiff; Δ = defendant.

**Frequency data.**
- MBE shares are NCBE's approximate fractions [S-040]. Per-category counts are estimates (`../05-mbe/subject-weighting.md`).
- Essay counts are subject-level, from [`frequency-analysis.md`](../06-past-exams/frequency-analysis.md).
- Issue counts ("tagged in N") are index-level and indicative only [VERIFY V-070] [VERIFY V-071].

## 1. Contracts (CTR)

> **How it is tested**
> - **MBE:** I Formation + IV Performance/breach ≈ ½ of 25 (~6–7 each); II, V ~3–4 each; III, VI ~2–3 each; UCC ≈ ¼ of items [S-040].
> - **CA essay:** 17 essays, 16/31 administrations (10/20 since Jul 2017). Tagged: expectation damages 7, breach 5, governing law 5.
> - **Approach:** pairs with Remedies (4 crossovers). Lead with UCC vs. common law; give each party a damages heading.

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/contracts/rule-statements.md) · [`mbe-rules.md`](../03-subjects/contracts/mbe-rules.md)

| # | Rule | Write this | MBE / CA note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-CTR-001** Governing law | UCC Art. 2 governs sales of goods (movable when identified); common law governs services, land, employment, loans. A mixed contract follows its predominant purpose. | **CA:** always the first heading. |
| 2 | **R-CTR-007, R-CTR-009** Revocation; firm offer | An offer is revocable until accepted, even if promised open; revocation is effective on receipt. Irrevocable only as an option, a merchant's signed written firm offer (≤ 3 months, no consideration; § 2-205), begun unilateral performance, or relied-on offer (R-CTR-011). | **MBE:** non-merchant or unsigned "firm offer" is revocable. |
| 3 | **R-CTR-013** § 2-207 | For goods, a definite, seasonable acceptance with added terms is still an acceptance unless expressly conditional on assent. Between merchants, additional terms join the contract unless the offer limits acceptance, they materially alter it, or the offeror objects. | **MBE:** mirror image is common law only (R-CTR-012). Merchant + non-merchant: added terms stay proposals. |
| 4 | **R-CTR-015** Mailbox rule | Acceptance by reasonable means is effective on dispatch, even if lost (codified in Civ. Code § 1583). Revocation, rejection, counteroffer and option acceptances are effective on receipt. | **MBE:** rejection sent before acceptance: first to arrive wins. Email is unsettled [VERIFY V-114]. |
| 5 | **R-CTR-022, R-CTR-027** Consideration; estoppel | Consideration is a bargained-for performance or return promise; detriment suffices; adequacy is not weighed. Without it, a promise the promisor should reasonably expect to induce action or forbearance, and that does, binds if injustice can be avoided only by enforcement, often for reliance damages (R2d § 90). | **MBE:** past consideration is not bargained for (R-CTR-026). **CA:** argue estoppel as the fallback. |
| 6 | **R-CTR-028, R-CTR-029** Modification | Common law: needs new consideration unless fair after unanticipated events or relied on. UCC: good-faith modification needs no consideration, but a signed no-oral-modification clause and the SOF still apply; a failed modification can still operate as a waiver, retractable by reasonable notice unless the other party has materially relied on it (§ 2-209(4)–(5)). | **CA:** Civ. Code § 1698: modify a written contract by writing, executed oral agreement, or (unless the contract expressly bars it) oral agreement with new consideration. |
| 7 | **R-CTR-042, R-CTR-047** Statute of frauds | Writing needed for: marriage consideration, contracts not performable within a year of making, land (leases > 1 year), executor's personal promise, goods ≥ $500, suretyship. UCC writing binds only up to the quantity stated; merchant confirmation binds unless objected to in 10 days. | **MBE:** lifetime contracts are outside. **CA:** § 1624(a) adds broker agreements and promises not performable in the promisor's lifetime. |
| 8 | **R-CTR-049, R-CTR-052** Parol evidence | A final writing bars prior or contemporaneous agreements that contradict it; a complete integration also bars consistent additions. The judge decides integration. | **MBE:** later agreements, fraud, conditions and ambiguity are never barred (R-CTR-050). **CA:** *PG&E* reasonable-susceptibility test; fraud evidence even if contradictory (*Riverisland*). |
| 9 | **R-CTR-062, R-CTR-064** Conditions | An express condition must be strictly satisfied; doubtful language reads as a promise. Excuse: waiver, estoppel, wrongful prevention, repudiation, impracticability of a non-material condition, or disproportionate forfeiture. Waiving a condition that is a material part of the exchange needs consideration; a waiver of a future condition can be retracted by notice received in time, unless the other party has materially changed position (R2d § 84). | **MBE:** substantial performance never satisfies an express condition. **CA:** forfeiture relief, Civ. Code § 3275. |
| 10 | **R-CTR-066, R-CTR-065** Material breach | A material breach (loss of a substantial part of the expected benefit) lets the victim suspend and, if uncured, terminate and sue. A minor breach gives damages only; keep performing. A substantially performing party recovers, less damages. | **MBE:** terminating for a minor breach is itself material breach. No substantial performance for goods. |
| 11 | **R-CTR-068, R-CTR-070** Repudiation; assurance | An unequivocal pre-performance refusal lets the victim sue at once, wait a commercially reasonable time, or urge retraction, but not run up damages. Reasonable insecurity justifies a demand for adequate assurance; none within a reasonable time (≤ 30 days, § 2-609) is repudiation. | **MBE:** "I may have trouble delivering" is not repudiation; demand assurance. |
| 12 | **R-CTR-072, R-CTR-073** Perfect tender; cure | If goods or tender fail in any respect, the buyer may reject all, accept all, or accept commercial units, with timely notice. The seller may cure within the contract time, or later if it reasonably expected acceptance (§§ 2-601, 2-508). | **MBE:** installment contracts and revocation of acceptance need substantial impairment (R-CTR-075, R-CTR-076). |
| 13 | **R-CTR-078, R-CTR-079** Risk of loss | Absent breach: agreement; shipment contract → delivery to carrier; destination → tender there; bailee → document or acknowledgment; otherwise merchant seller → buyer's receipt, non-merchant → tender (§ 2-509). Nonconforming tender keeps risk on the seller (§ 2-510). | **MBE:** risk does not follow title. |
| 14 | **R-CTR-089, R-CTR-092, R-CTR-095** Expectation damages | Loss in value + incidental + consequential − costs avoided. Consequential losses must be foreseeable at contracting, naturally or from special circumstances known to Δ (*Hadley*; Civ. Code § 3300). No recovery for avoidable losses. | **MBE:** foreseeability is judged at contracting, not breach. UCC seller gets incidental damages only. Certainty: R-CTR-093. |
| 15 | **R-CTR-096** Liquidated damages | Enforceable if reasonable in light of anticipated or actual loss and difficulty of proof; an unreasonably large sum is a penalty, limiting Π to actual loss. | **CA:** § 1671(b) presumes validity; consumer and residential-lease clauses are void unless damages are impracticable to fix [VERIFY V-107]. |
| 16 | **R-CTR-102** Buyer's UCC remedies | On non-delivery, repudiation, rightful rejection or revocation, the buyer may cancel, recover the price paid, and take cover − contract price or market (when buyer learned of breach) − contract price, plus incidental and consequential, minus expenses saved. | **MBE:** not covering does not bar market damages but can bar consequentials. Unique goods: specific performance (§ 2-716). |

## 2. Civil Procedure (CIV)

> **How it is tested**
> - **MBE:** I Jurisdiction and venue, III Pretrial, V Motions ≈ ⅔ of 25 (~17); II, IV, VI, VII ≈ ⅓ (~8) [S-040]. Federal court; FRCP as amended through Dec. 1, 2025.
> - **CA essay:** 13 essays, 13/31 (7/20 since Jul 2017). Tagged: SMJ 7, discovery 5, PJ 3.
> - **Approach:** recent forums were federal; add the CCP comparison when the call allows [S-084].

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/civil-procedure/rule-statements.md) · [`mbe-rules.md`](../03-subjects/civil-procedure/mbe-rules.md)

| # | Rule | Write this | MBE / CA note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-CIV-002** Federal question | § 1331 covers actions arising under federal law; the federal issue must appear in Π's well-pleaded complaint, not in an anticipated defense or a counterclaim (*Mottley*). | **MBE:** a federal defense does not support removal either. |
| 2 | **R-CIV-004, R-CIV-006, R-CIV-008** Diversity | Complete diversity plus > $75,000 exclusive of interest and costs, measured at filing (and removal). A corporation is a citizen of each state of incorporation and of its nerve center (*Hertz*). One Π aggregates all claims against one Δ; multiple Πs do not aggregate separate claims. | **MBE:** exactly $75,000 fails; later domicile changes don't matter; interpleader and CAFA need only minimal diversity. |
| 3 | **R-CIV-010, R-CIV-011** Supplemental | § 1367(a): claims sharing a common nucleus of operative fact. § 1367(b): in diversity-only cases, no jurisdiction over Π's claims against persons joined under Rules 14, 19, 20, 24 (or by Rule 19/24 Πs) if inconsistent with § 1332. | **MBE:** (b) bars only Π's claims; Δ's impleader and counterclaims are fine. Rule 20 co-Π short on amount may join (*Exxon*). |
| 4 | **R-CIV-013, R-CIV-014** Removal | Only Δs remove, to the district embracing the state court, within 30 days of formal service, with all served Δs consenting. No diversity removal with a served forum-state Δ or more than one year after filing, absent Π's bad faith (§§ 1441, 1446). | **MBE:** a Π facing a counterclaim cannot remove; § 1391 does not govern removal venue. |
| 5 | **R-CIV-017, R-CIV-021, R-CIV-022** Specific PJ | PJ needs a statute (forum long-arm via Rule 4(k)(1)(A)) and due process: Δ's own purposeful availment of or direction toward the forum, a claim arising out of or relating to those contacts, and fair play and substantial justice. Systematically serving the forum market for the very product that injured a resident there suffices (*Ford*). | **MBE:** Π's unilateral acts don't count; nonresidents can't borrow residents' links (*Bristol-Myers*). **CA:** cite CCP § 410.10 (constitutional limit). |
| 6 | **R-CIV-020** General PJ | Any claim may be heard where Δ is "at home": an individual's domicile; a corporation's state of incorporation and principal place of business; elsewhere only exceptionally (*Daimler*). | **MBE:** large, continuous sales do not make a corporation at home. Tag, consent and waiver still work (R-CIV-018). |
| 7 | **R-CIV-031, R-CIV-032** Venue | Venue lies where any Δ resides if all reside in one state; where a substantial part of the events or property is; or, only as a fallback, wherever any Δ is subject to PJ (§ 1391(b)). An entity resides wherever it is subject to PJ for the action. | **MBE:** venue is waivable (Rule 12(h)(1)); transfer: R-CIV-033, R-CIV-034. |
| 8 | **R-CIV-037, R-CIV-038** Erie | On state claims, apply state substantive law and federal procedure. A valid FRCP or statute on point controls (*Hanna*); otherwise ask whether ignoring state law is outcome-determinative under the twin aims, weighed against federal interests (R-CIV-039). | **CA:** write: "The federal court applies California substantive law and federal procedural law." |
| 9 | **R-CIV-047** Rule 12 waiver | PJ, venue, process and service defenses are waived if left out of the first Rule 12 motion or the answer. Failure to state a claim and failure to join a Rule 19 party survive through trial; SMJ is never waived. | **CA:** a general appearance waives PJ; use a motion to quash (CCP § 418.10). |
| 10 | **R-CIV-064** Discovery scope | Any nonprivileged matter relevant to a party's claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case; admissibility is not required (Rule 26(b)(1)). | **CA:** broader: relevant to the subject matter (CCP § 2017.010; R-CIV-110). |
| 11 | **R-CIV-066** Work product | Materials prepared in anticipation of litigation are discoverable only on substantial need and undue hardship; mental impressions stay protected (Rule 26(b)(3); *Hickman*). | **MBE:** underlying facts stay discoverable. **CA:** protection belongs to the attorney (R-CIV-112). |
| 12 | **R-CIV-085, R-CIV-086** Summary judgment | Granted if no genuine dispute of material fact and movant entitled to judgment as a matter of law, viewing evidence most favorably to the non-movant. A movant without the trial burden may show absence of evidence (*Celotex*); the non-movant must cite record materials. | **CA:** "no triable issue"; separate statement, 81 days' notice (R-CIV-113). |
| 13 | **R-CIV-078, R-CIV-079** Jury right | The Seventh Amendment preserves juries in suits at common law; the remedy (legal = money) matters most. Shared legal and equitable issues go to the jury first (*Beacon Theatres*). | **CA:** 1850 common law; courts may try equity first [VERIFY V-158]. |
| 14 | **R-CIV-087, R-CIV-088** JMOL | JMOL if no reasonable jury could find for the non-movant, after it is fully heard and before submission. Renew within 28 days after judgment, on the grounds raised before (Rule 50). | **MBE:** no pre-verdict motion means no renewed JMOL. **CA:** JNOV needs no prior motion (CCP § 629). |
| 15 | **R-CIV-094, R-CIV-095, R-CIV-096** Preclusion | Claim: a valid final judgment on the merits bars the same parties (or privies) from relitigating any claim from the same transaction. Issue: an issue actually litigated, decided and essential binds a party who had a full and fair opportunity; non-mutual offensive use is discretionary (*Parklane*). | **MBE:** jurisdiction and venue dismissals are not on the merits. **CA:** primary-rights theory; final only after appeal (R-CIV-114). |

## 3. Constitutional Law (CON)

> **How it is tested**
> - **MBE:** IV Individual rights ≈ ½ of 25 (~12–13); I–III ≈ ½ together [S-040].
> - **CA essay:** 13 essays, 13/31 (8/20 since Jul 2017). Tagged: case or controversy 4; takings, free exercise, school and employee speech, EP, DCC, P&I 2 each.
> - **Essay order:** justiciability → power → rights, naming the scrutiny and who bears the burden.

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/constitutional-law/rule-statements.md) · [`mbe-rules.md`](../03-subjects/constitutional-law/mbe-rules.md)

| # | Rule | Write this | MBE / CA note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-CON-008, R-CON-015, R-CON-016** Justiciability | Standing: concrete, particularized, actual or imminent injury, fairly traceable to Δ, likely redressable, per claim and remedy (*Lujan*). Ripeness: fitness and hardship. Mootness: live controversy, except repetition evading review, voluntary cessation, collateral consequences, class actions. | **CA:** standing first for every Π. **MBE:** generalized grievances fail (R-CON-013). |
| 2 | **R-CON-021, R-CON-022** Commerce power | Congress regulates channels, instrumentalities, and activities substantially affecting commerce; economic activity aggregates on a rational basis (*Wickard*, *Raich*). Noneconomic activity with attenuated effects fails (*Lopez*, *Morrison*); no compelled commerce (*NFIB*). | **MBE:** gun or violence law without economic activity fails. |
| 3 | **R-CON-046** Anti-commandeering | Congress may not compel states to legislate or administer federal programs, conscript state officers, or bar repeal of state laws (*New York*, *Printz*, *Murphy*). | **MBE:** generally applicable laws binding states and private parties alike are valid. |
| 4 | **R-CON-047, R-CON-048, R-CON-049** Dormant Commerce | Discrimination against interstate commerce is virtually per se invalid unless no reasonable nondiscriminatory alternative serves a legitimate local purpose. Evenhanded laws fall only if burdens clearly exceed local benefits (*Pike*). Market participants may favor locals. | **MBE:** no market-participant exception for Art. IV P&I (*Camden*). |
| 5 | **R-CON-051** Preemption | Valid federal law preempts expressly, by occupying the field, or by conflict (impossibility or obstacle). | **MBE:** stricter state standards survive if federal law is a floor. |
| 6 | **R-CON-053** Art. IV P&I | No discrimination against out-of-state citizens in fundamental rights (livelihood, important commercial activity) unless a substantial reason exists and the discrimination bears a substantial relationship to it. | **MBE:** corporations and aliens are not protected; recreation is not fundamental. |
| 7 | **R-CON-054** State action | Rights restrain only government; private conduct counts only if it performs a traditionally exclusive public function or the state is entangled with it. | **MBE:** licensing, funding or regulation is not enough. **CA:** state free-speech and privacy clauses reach some private actors. |
| 8 | **R-CON-057, R-CON-058** Procedural DP | Property needs a legitimate entitlement from law or contract. Process due follows *Mathews*: private interest, error risk and value of safeguards, government interest; at minimum notice and a hearing before a neutral decisionmaker. | **MBE:** at-will jobs are not property; reputation alone is not liberty. |
| 9 | **R-CON-060, R-CON-061** Substantive DP | A fundamental right must be deeply rooted in history and tradition (*Glucksberg*, *Dobbs*); burdens get strict scrutiny, else rational basis. Recognized: marriage, procreation, contraception, child-rearing, family living, intimate conduct. | **MBE:** no right to assisted suicide; abortion is not fundamental after *Dobbs*. |
| 10 | **R-CON-066, R-CON-067, R-CON-068** Equal protection | Strict scrutiny (government proves necessary to compelling interest): race, national origin, state alienage, fundamental rights. Intermediate (substantially related to important interest): sex, nonmarital birth. Otherwise rational basis, challenger's burden. Neutral laws need discriminatory purpose (*Washington v. Davis*). Racial classifications get strict scrutiny even if benign; race-conscious admissions without measurable objectives or an end point fail [VERIFY V-035]. | **CA:** sex and sexual orientation are suspect; art. I, § 31 bans race and sex preferences. |
| 11 | **R-CON-071** Sex | Government must show an exceedingly persuasive justification, substantially related to important objectives, not based on stereotypes (*Virginia*). | **MBE:** *Michael M.* upheld; VMI struck. **CA:** strict scrutiny (*Sail'er Inn*). |
| 12 | **R-CON-077, R-CON-078, R-CON-079** Takings | Public use (any public purpose, *Kelo*) and just compensation. Per se: permanent physical occupation or authorized physical invasion (*Loretto*, *Cedar Point*), or denial of all economic use beyond background nuisance law (*Lucas*). Otherwise *Penn Central*: impact, expectations, character. | **MBE:** big diminution alone is usually no taking. Exactions: R-CON-080. **CA:** "taken or damaged." |
| 13 | **R-CON-092 to R-CON-095** Speech framework | Content-based laws get strict scrutiny (least restrictive means); viewpoint discrimination is presumptively invalid anywhere. Content-neutral time, place, manner rules in public forums must be narrowly tailored to a significant interest with ample alternatives. Nonpublic forums: reasonable and viewpoint-neutral. | **MBE:** TPM tailoring does not require the least restrictive means. |
| 14 | **R-CON-102, R-CON-103** Students; employees | Schools may restrict speech forecast to cause substantial disruption (*Tinker*), plus lewd, school-sponsored and drug-promoting speech. Employees are protected only as citizens on public concerns, then *Pickering* balancing; duty speech is unprotected (*Garcetti*). | **MBE:** employer wins if it would have acted the same anyway. |
| 15 | **R-CON-087, R-CON-090** Religion | Free exercise: neutral, generally applicable laws need only rational basis (*Smith*); targeting, individualized exemptions or favoring comparable secular activity triggers strict scrutiny. Establishment: history and tradition plus no coercion; *Lemon* abandoned (*Kennedy*). | **CA:** note *Lemon* as the former test; display cases unsettled [VERIFY V-203]. |

## 4. Criminal Law and Procedure (CRM)

> **How it is tested**
> - **MBE:** V Constitutional protections ≈ ½ of 25 (~12–13); I–IV ≈ ½ together [S-040]. Use the majority view unless the question gives the MPC or a statute [VERIFY V-256].
> - **CA essay:** 15 essays, 15/31 (10/20 since Jul 2017). Tagged: search and seizure 9, confessions 4, attempt 3. Pairs with Evidence (2).
> - **MBE vs. CA:** never apply California rules on the MBE; on essays, add them as the call allows.

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/criminal-law-procedure/rule-statements.md) · [`mbe-rules.md`](../03-subjects/criminal-law-procedure/mbe-rules.md)

| # | Rule | Write this | MBE / CA note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-CRM-004, R-CRM-005** Mental states | Common law: specific intent (inchoate crimes, first-degree murder, assault, larceny, robbery, burglary, false pretenses), general intent (battery, rape, kidnapping), malice (murder, arson). MPC: purpose, knowledge, recklessness, negligence, element by element; silent statute → recklessness. | **MBE:** categories matter for intoxication and mistake (R-CRM-018, R-CRM-010). |
| 2 | **R-CRM-031, R-CRM-033** Murder | Unlawful killing with malice: intent to kill, intent to cause serious bodily injury, depraved heart, or felony murder. First degree: willful, deliberate, premeditated (brief reflection, cool mind), or statutory means or felonies. | **CA:** argue planning, motive and manner (*Anderson*); Pen. Code § 189(a) [VERIFY V-253]. |
| 3 | **R-CRM-039, R-CRM-040** Felony murder | A killing during an inherently dangerous felony is murder even if accidental. Limits: independent felony (assault merges), death before temporary safety, foreseeable, Δ guilty of the felony. | **CA:** non-killers liable only if aiding first-degree murder with intent to kill or major participants with reckless indifference (§ 189(e); R-CRM-043). |
| 4 | **R-CRM-034** Voluntary manslaughter | Intentional killing in sudden heat of passion from adequate provocation, before a reasonable person would cool and before Δ did. | **MBE:** words alone are inadequate. MPC: extreme emotional disturbance. Imperfect self-defense: R-CRM-023. |
| 5 | **R-CRM-020, R-CRM-021, R-CRM-022** Self-defense | Reasonable, proportional force against imminent unlawful force; deadly force only against imminent death or serious bodily injury. Majority: no retreat; MPC: retreat if completely safe, except at home. An aggressor must withdraw and communicate it (or the victim must escalate to deadly force). | **MBE:** no retreat duty unless stated. **CA:** presumed fear of residential intruders (Pen. Code § 198.5). |
| 6 | **R-CRM-044, R-CRM-046** Larceny; false pretenses | Larceny: trespassory taking and carrying away of another's tangible personal property with intent to permanently deprive. False pretenses: knowing false statement of past or present fact obtaining *title*; obtaining only *possession* is larceny by trick. | **MBE:** claim of right negates intent; intent to repay equivalent money does not. |
| 7 | **R-CRM-049** Robbery | Larceny from the victim's person or presence by force or threat of immediate harm; extortion uses threats of future harm. | **MBE:** force must precede or accompany the taking. **CA:** force to retain the property suffices (§ 211; *Estes*). |
| 8 | **R-CRM-060, R-CRM-061** Attempt | Specific intent to commit the target crime plus an act beyond preparation: dangerous proximity (common law) or a substantial step strongly corroborating intent (MPC, MBE default). | **MBE:** no attempted felony murder or reckless crimes; attempted murder needs intent to kill. |
| 9 | **R-CRM-065, R-CRM-067** Conspiracy | Agreement of two or more to commit an unlawful act, with intent to agree and achieve it, plus (majority) an overt act. Common law is bilateral, MPC unilateral. *Pinkerton*: liability for co-conspirators' foreseeable crimes in furtherance. | **MBE:** no bilateral conspiracy with an undercover agent; MPC rejects *Pinkerton*. |
| 10 | **R-CRM-069** Accomplice | One who aids or encourages with intent to aid and intent the crime succeed is liable as a principal; mere presence is not enough. | **MBE:** knowledge alone usually fails; principal need not be convicted. |
| 11 | **R-CRM-073, R-CRM-075** Search; standing | A search invades a reasonable expectation of privacy (*Katz*) or physically trespasses to gather information (*Jones*). Only one whose own privacy or property was invaded may object: owners, overnight guests, sole rental drivers. | **CA:** standing first per defendant and place; passengers may challenge the stop (*Brendlin*). |
| 12 | **R-CRM-077, R-CRM-078** Terry | Brief stop on reasonable suspicion from specific, articulable facts; pat-down for weapons if reasonably believed armed and dangerous; contraband plain by touch may be seized. | **MBE:** flight in a high-crime area suffices (*Wardlow*); manipulating a lump does not (*Dickerson*). |
| 13 | **R-CRM-083, R-CRM-084, R-CRM-086** Warrant exceptions | Incident to arrest: person and immediate area; car only if arrestee is unsecured and within reach or it is reasonable to believe evidence of the arrest offense is inside (*Gant*). Automobile: probable cause allows searching any container that could hold the object. Consent: voluntary, by actual or apparent authority. | **MBE:** no search incident to a citation; phones need warrants (*Riley*); present objector overrides (*Randolph*). |
| 14 | **R-CRM-090, R-CRM-091** Exclusion | Unconstitutionally obtained evidence and its fruits are excluded from the case-in-chief unless independent source, inevitable discovery or attenuation. Not applied to grand juries, civil or parole hearings, or impeachment. | **CA:** "Under Proposition 8, California excludes evidence only where the federal Constitution requires." |
| 15 | **R-CRM-094 to R-CRM-098** Miranda; Edwards | Warnings precede custodial interrogation (custody: not free to leave plus station-house pressure; interrogation: words or acts likely to elicit incrimination). Waiver must be knowing, voluntary, may be implied. An unambiguous request for counsel stops all questioning until counsel is present unless the suspect reinitiates; lapses after 14 days out of custody. | **MBE:** Terry stops are not custody; volunteered statements are not interrogation; ambiguous requests don't trigger *Edwards*. |

## 5. Evidence (EVD)

> **How it is tested**
> - **MBE:** II Relevancy ~⅓ of 25 (~8); I Presentation and V Hearsay ~¼ each (~6); III and IV ~⅙ (~4) [S-040]. Includes amended 801(d)(1)(A) (Dec. 1, 2026) [S-101].
> - **CA essay:** 15 essays, 15/31 (10/20 since Jul 2017). The call names CEC or FRE; read it first (R-EVD-001). Tagged: hearsay 3; party admissions, BER, business records, privileges 2 each.
> - **Method:** item by item: relevance → 403/352 → witness → authentication → BER → policy and privilege → hearsay → Confrontation.

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/evidence/rule-statements.md) · [`mbe-rules.md`](../03-subjects/evidence/mbe-rules.md)

| # | Rule | Write this | MBE / CA note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-EVD-002, R-EVD-003** Relevance; 403 | Relevant evidence has any tendency to make a fact of consequence more or less probable. Exclude if probative value is substantially outweighed by unfair prejudice, confusion, misleading, delay or cumulativeness (FRE 403; CEC § 352). | **CA:** fact must be disputed (§ 210); Prop 8 admits relevant criminal evidence except privileges, hearsay, §§ 352, 782, 1103 (R-EVD-004). |
| 2 | **R-EVD-035, R-EVD-037, R-EVD-038** Character | Character is inadmissible to prove conforming conduct. A criminal Δ may offer his own or the victim's pertinent trait by reputation or opinion; the prosecution may rebut and, if Δ attacked the victim's trait, offer Δ's same trait. | **CA:** § 1103 allows specific acts of the victim; § 1101 survives Prop 8 (*Ewoldt*). |
| 3 | **R-EVD-042** Other acts | Other acts are admissible not for propensity but for motive, opportunity, intent, preparation, plan, knowledge, identity or absence of mistake, if a jury could find them and 403 permits (FRE 404(b); *Huddleston*). | **CA:** *Ewoldt* scale: identity needs a signature, intent the least similarity. |
| 4 | **R-EVD-023, R-EVD-085** Prior inconsistent statements | Impeach without first showing the statement; extrinsic proof only after a chance to explain or deny (FRE 613(b)) and not on collateral matters. Under amended 801(d)(1)(A), a testifying witness's inconsistent statement is also substantive evidence [S-101]. | **CA:** § 770 also allows extrinsic proof if the witness is not excused; § 1235 has long allowed substantive use. **MBE:** older questions use the sworn-statement rule. |
| 5 | **R-EVD-025, R-EVD-026** Convictions | FRE 609: felonies impeach (403 for witnesses; for the accused only if probative value outweighs prejudice); dishonesty crimes always come in; convictions over 10 years old face a stricter test. | **CA:** criminal: moral-turpitude felonies and misdemeanor conduct, subject to § 352 (*Castro*, *Wheeler*); civil: § 788. |
| 6 | **R-EVD-054** Experts | Proponent shows it is more likely than not that the expert's knowledge will help, the testimony rests on sufficient data, uses reliable methods, and applies them reliably (FRE 702, 2023; *Daubert*). | **MBE:** basis and application are admissibility, not weight. **CA:** *Sargon*, *Kelly* (R-EVD-060). |
| 7 | **R-EVD-074, R-EVD-075** Policy exclusions | Subsequent remedial measures are inadmissible to prove negligence, defect or culpable conduct, but allowed for ownership, control, feasibility or impeachment (FRE 407). Compromise offers and negotiation statements are inadmissible to prove validity or amount (FRE 408). | **CA:** remedial measures are admissible in strict products cases (*Ault*); § 1152 also covers humanitarian offers. |
| 8 | **R-EVD-063** Attorney-client privilege | Client may refuse to disclose and prevent disclosure of confidential lawyer-client communications made for legal advice (*Upjohn*; CEC §§ 951–955). | **CA:** § 917 presumes confidentiality. Privilege ≠ ethical duty of confidentiality. |
| 9 | **R-EVD-078** Best evidence | To prove the content of a writing, recording or photo, the original is required unless excused; only when content is in issue (FRE 1002). | **CA:** "replaced by the secondary evidence rule" (R-EVD-080). |
| 10 | **R-EVD-083, R-EVD-084** Hearsay | An out-of-court assertion offered for its truth is inadmissible unless excepted. Not hearsay: operative words, effect on listener, circumstantial state of mind, impeachment. | **MBE:** non-assertive conduct and machine output are not statements. |
| 11 | **R-EVD-088** Party admissions | A party's own statement offered against it is admissible; personal knowledge and against-interest are not required (FRE 801(d)(2); CEC § 1220). | **MBE:** usable only against the declarant (*Bruton*). **CA:** no general employee-admission rule (R-EVD-090) [VERIFY V-401]. |
| 12 | **R-EVD-094, R-EVD-095** Excited utterance; state of mind | A statement about a startling event, made under its stress, is admissible (FRE 803(2); § 1240). So is a statement of then-existing intent, plan, emotion or physical condition, but not memory or belief offered to prove the past fact, except about one's will (FRE 803(3); *Hillmon*). | **CA:** § 1252 trustworthiness filter. **MBE:** belief about past acts excluded (*Shepard*). |
| 13 | **R-EVD-099** Business records | A record made at or near the time by a person with knowledge, kept as regular practice of a regular activity, shown by custodian or certification, is admissible unless the opponent shows untrustworthiness (FRE 803(6)). | **CA:** § 1271 excludes opinions and diagnoses; proponent must show trustworthiness. |
| 14 | **R-EVD-104** Against interest | An unavailable declarant's statement so contrary to pecuniary, proprietary or penal interest that a reasonable person would say it only if true; criminal penal-interest statements need corroboration (FRE 804(b)(3)). | **MBE:** unavailability required; blame-shifting parts excluded (*Williamson*). **CA:** § 1230 adds social interest. |
| 15 | **R-EVD-110, R-EVD-111** Confrontation | Testimonial statements of an absent declarant are barred unless the declarant is unavailable and Δ had a prior chance to cross-examine (*Crawford*). Testimonial means primary purpose to prove past events for prosecution, not to meet an ongoing emergency (*Davis*). | **CA:** Prop 8 cannot override it; analyze it after hearsay. |

## 6. Real Property (PRP)

> **How it is tested**
> - **MBE:** about ⅕ (~5) from each category: ownership, rights in land, contracts, mortgages, titles [S-040].
> - **CA essay:** 14 essays, 13/31 (8/20 since Jul 2017). Tagged: concurrent estates 3, recording acts 3; leases and easements 2 each.
> - **Approach:** common-law answer first, then California (USRAP, § 885.020, UVPRA, race-notice, adverse possession with taxes).

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/real-property/rule-statements.md) · [`mbe-rules.md`](../03-subjects/real-property/mbe-rules.md)

| # | Rule | Write this | MBE / CA note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-PRP-002, R-PRP-003** Defeasible fees | Durational words ("so long as," "until") create a fee simple determinable that ends automatically (possibility of reverter). Conditional words plus re-entry ("but if," "provided that") create a fee subject to condition subsequent, ended only by the grantor's election (right of entry). | **MBE:** ambiguity → condition subsequent. **CA:** FSD abolished; power of termination expires 30 years after recording unless preserved (§ 885.020; R-PRP-005). |
| 2 | **R-PRP-014, R-PRP-016** RAP | An interest is void unless it must vest, if at all, within 21 years after a life in being at creation; covers contingent remainders, executory interests, class gifts, options. | **MBE:** common law unless a statute is given. **CA:** USRAP 90-year wait-and-see, then reform (Prob. Code §§ 21205, 21220). |
| 3 | **R-PRP-020, R-PRP-021, R-PRP-024** Concurrent ownership | Joint tenancy needs the four unities and survivorship language; conveying one's share severs it into a tenancy in common; wills cannot sever. A cotenant in sole possession owes no rent absent ouster but must share third-party rents. | **MBE:** lien theory: one tenant's mortgage does not sever (R-PRP-022). **CA:** express declaration (§ 683); self-conveyance severs (*Riddle*). |
| 4 | **R-PRP-039, R-PRP-040** Assignment vs. sublease | Assignment transfers the whole remaining term (privity of estate with landlord); sublease transfers less. The original tenant stays liable in contract; assignees are liable only while holding the estate, unless they assumed the lease. | **MBE:** T assigns to A, A to B, B defaults: L sues T and B, not A unless A assumed. |
| 5 | **R-PRP-035, R-PRP-036, R-PRP-033** Tenant remedies; mitigation | Constructive eviction: substantial interference, notice, no cure, vacate in time. Habitability: residential premises fit throughout, non-waivable, tenant may stay. On abandonment, the modern majority requires the landlord to mitigate. | **CA:** *Green*; mitigation under § 1951.2 unless a lock-in clause (§ 1951.4) [VERIFY V-662]. |
| 6 | **R-PRP-050, R-PRP-051, R-PRP-052** Covenants; servitudes | Burden of a real covenant (damages): writing, intent, touch and concern, horizontal and vertical privity, notice. Benefit needs no horizontal privity. An equitable servitude (injunction) needs writing, intent, touch and concern and notice, but no privity. | **CA:** analyze both remedies; no servitude is implied from a common plan alone, but recorded CC&Rs suffice even if the deed is silent (R-PRP-053). |
| 7 | **R-PRP-062, R-PRP-063** Implied easements | Prior use: common ownership, apparent and continuous pre-severance use, reasonable necessity at severance. Necessity: common ownership and strict necessity (landlocked) at severance; ends when necessity ends. | **MBE:** inconvenience is not necessity. |
| 8 | **R-PRP-064** Prescription | Open and notorious, continuous, adverse use for the statutory period; exclusivity not required. | **CA:** 5 years, no tax payment (*Gilardi*); permission defeats it. |
| 9 | **R-PRP-080** Marketable title | Sellers impliedly promise title reasonably free from doubt at closing: no chain defects, unbargained encumbrances, or existing zoning violations. | **MBE:** zoning alone is not a defect; only a violation is. |
| 10 | **R-PRP-082** Equitable conversion | After a specifically enforceable contract, the buyer holds equitable title; under the majority, casualty risk passes to the buyer at signing (with insurance credit). | **CA:** UVPRA keeps risk on the seller until title or possession passes (§ 1662). |
| 11 | **R-PRP-090, R-PRP-100** Priorities | First in time, adjusted by the recording act. A purchase-money mortgage beats prior liens against the buyer; subordination and increased-burden modifications also adjust order. | **MBE:** seller's purchase-money mortgage usually beats lender's [VERIFY V-676]. |
| 12 | **R-PRP-101, R-PRP-102** Foreclosure | Foreclosure wipes out joined junior interests; seniors survive; omitted juniors keep their rights. Proceeds pay costs, the foreclosed debt, juniors in order, then the mortgagor; shortfall → deficiency unless barred. | **MBE:** seniors are not paid from proceeds. **CA:** limits deficiencies (R-PRP-104). |
| 13 | **R-PRP-105, R-PRP-106** Adverse possession | Actual, open and notorious, exclusive, adverse, continuous for the statutory period. | **CA:** 5 years plus paying all assessed taxes (CCP § 325); boundary strips usually fail on taxes. |
| 14 | **R-PRP-110** Delivery | A deed passes title only if the grantor intends it presently operative; handing over or recording presumes delivery. | **MBE:** returning a delivered deed does not reconvey. |
| 15 | **R-PRP-116, R-PRP-117** Recording; BFP | Race: first to record wins. Notice: a later BFP without notice wins. Race-notice (California): a later BFP must also record first. A BFP pays value without actual, record or inquiry notice. | **MBE:** read the statute; possession gives inquiry notice. **CA:** Civ. Code § 1214. |

## 7. Torts (TRT)

> **How it is tested**
> - **MBE:** II Negligence ≈ ½ of 25 (~12–13); I, III, IV ≈ ½ together [S-040]. Defaults: pure comparative negligence, joint and several liability.
> - **CA essay:** 13 essays, 12/31 (9/20 since Jul 2017). Tagged: property torts 4; misrepresentation, negligence, person torts 3 each. Pairs with Remedies (3).
> - **Approach:** Π v. Δ pairings with element headings; majority rule, then California (*Rowland*, *Knight*, *Barker*, Prop 51, *Thing*).

[`rule-statements.md`](../03-subjects/torts/rule-statements.md) · [`mbe-rules.md`](../03-subjects/torts/mbe-rules.md)

| # | Rule | Write this | MBE / CA note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-TRT-002, R-TRT-003, R-TRT-005** Torts to the person | Battery: intent to cause harmful or offensive contact (or apprehension) and resulting contact. Assault: same intent and reasonable apprehension of imminent contact. IIED: extreme, outrageous conduct intentionally or recklessly causing severe distress. | **MBE:** battery needs no awareness; assault does. **CA:** IIED conduct must target Π or occur in Π's known presence (*Christensen*). |
| 2 | **R-TRT-006, R-TRT-008** Property torts | Trespass to land: intentional entry (mistake no defense, no harm needed). Conversion: intentional dominion so serious Δ must pay full value. | **MBE:** good-faith buyer from a thief converts; lesser harm is trespass to chattels (R-TRT-007). **CA:** intangible intrusions are trespass only with physical damage or particulates (*Wilson*). |
| 3 | **R-TRT-010, R-TRT-012** Defenses | Reasonable force against reasonably feared force; deadly force only against death or serious harm. Property: reasonable nondeadly force after a request to stop; never deadly force or devices (*Katko*). | **MBE:** no privilege against an intruder privileged by necessity (R-TRT-017). |
| 4 | **R-TRT-018, R-TRT-019, R-TRT-020** Duty | Π proves duty, breach, actual and proximate cause, and damages, per pairing. Duty runs to foreseeable plaintiffs (*Palsgraf*). | **CA:** general duty under Civ. Code § 1714; exceptions only via *Rowland* factors, categorically. |
| 5 | **R-TRT-021, R-TRT-022** Affirmative duties | No duty to rescue or control others except via special relationship, creating the peril, undertaking a rescue (then act reasonably), or statute. | **MBE:** active driving is misfeasance, not omission. **CA:** *Brown v. USA Taekwondo* two-step. |
| 6 | **R-TRT-033, R-TRT-035** Breach shortcuts | Negligence per se: unexcused violation of a statute protecting Π's class from this harm sets duty and breach. Res ipsa: accident ordinarily needs negligence and Δ had exclusive control, so Π reaches the jury. | **CA:** both create presumptions (Evid. Code §§ 669, 646). **MBE:** licensing statutes rarely apply. |
| 7 | **R-TRT-044, R-TRT-046** Proximate cause | Liability extends to foreseeable types of harm to foreseeable plaintiffs; manner and extent need not be foreseen. Only unforeseeable, extraordinary intervening acts supersede. | **MBE:** the very risk that made Δ negligent never supersedes; eggshell plaintiff (R-TRT-045). |
| 8 | **R-TRT-047 to R-TRT-051** Premises | Undiscovered trespassers: avoid willful or wanton harm. Licensees: warn of known hidden dangers. Invitees: reasonable care, including inspection. | **CA:** "California rejects these categories (*Rowland*)"; status is a factor; recreational use: § 846. |
| 9 | **R-TRT-055** Bystander distress | Recovery only if closely related to the victim, present and aware of the injury as it occurs, and suffering serious distress beyond a disinterested witness's (*Thing*). | **MBE:** some states use zone of danger. **CA:** write *Thing* verbatim; cohabitant bar under review [VERIFY V-604]. |
| 10 | **R-TRT-070, R-TRT-041, R-TRT-072, R-TRT-073** Fault defenses | Pure comparative negligence reduces Π's recovery proportionally, never barring it; joint and several liability for indivisible injury. Knowing, voluntary risk acceptance usually merges into comparative fault. | **CA:** Prop 51: non-economic damages several only (R-TRT-042); *Knight* primary assumption of risk bars inherent-risk claims. |
| 11 | **R-TRT-063, R-TRT-064** Vicarious liability | Employers answer for employee torts within the scope of employment; hirers generally do not answer for independent contractors, except negligent hiring, inherently dangerous work or nondelegable duties. | **CA:** scope includes risks engendered by the job; *Privette* protects hirers against contractors' employees. |
| 12 | **R-TRT-081 to R-TRT-084** Products | Commercial sellers are strictly liable for physical harm from products defective when they left their control: manufacturing flaw, design (consumer expectations or risk-utility with a feasible alternative), or inadequate warnings. | **CA:** no "unreasonably dangerous" element (*Cronin*); both *Barker* tests and burden shift; *Soule* limits consumer expectations. |
| 13 | **R-TRT-092** Nuisance | A substantial and unreasonable non-trespassory interference with use and enjoyment of land; unreasonable if harm outweighs utility or Δ can pay and continue. | **CA:** pair with injunctions and hardship balancing; defenses: R-TRT-094. |
| 14 | **R-TRT-096, R-TRT-098, R-TRT-099** Defamation | A false, defamatory factual statement about Π, published to a third person, causing damage. Public figures prove actual malice by clear and convincing evidence; private figures on public matters prove negligence, plus actual malice for presumed or punitive damages. | **CA:** build the public/private grid; libel and slander per se: R-TRT-097. |
| 15 | **R-TRT-107** Misrepresentation | A knowingly or recklessly false statement of material fact, intended to induce and justifiably relied on, causing pecuniary loss. | **MBE:** Π's negligence is no defense; puffery is not fact. **CA:** deceit includes concealment and promises without intent (Civ. Code §§ 1709–1710). |
