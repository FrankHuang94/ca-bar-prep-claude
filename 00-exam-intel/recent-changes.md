# Recent changes (law in effect for July 27–28, 2027)

> **How to use this file.** Each subject's `outline.md` and `ca-distinctions.md` must reflect every item marked **Apply** below. `/update-exam-intel` re-checks this file monthly. Before the exam, re-run it in **June 2027** to catch anything adopted in spring 2027.

## 1. What law is tested: the cutoff question

| Component | Standard | Source |
|---|---|---|
| MBE: Evidence | Answer "according to the Federal Rules of Evidence, as currently in effect." | [S-040] |
| MBE: Civil Procedure | Assume "the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure as currently in effect" and the Title 28 sections on jurisdiction, venue and transfer. | [S-040] |
| MBE: other subjects | Generally accepted principles; NCBE's outline notes set defaults, e.g., Torts assumes pure comparative fault and joint and several liability. | [S-040] |
| NCBE lag policy | **None found.** No NCBE statement says, for example, that an amendment becomes testable one year after it takes effect. | [S-040] [VERIFY V-027] |
| California written section | **No stated cutoff found.** The default is "legal theories and principles of general application" unless the question asks for California law. | [S-077] [VERIFY V-027] |

**Working rule for this repo**
- Learn the law **in effect on July 27, 2027**.
- Where a rule changed recently, learn both versions and the change itself. A question may avoid the changed point, and MBE items are written well in advance. If a question clearly turns on the point, apply current law.

## 2. Federal Rules of Evidence (MBE and essays)

| Effective | Rule | Change | Exam impact | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dec 1, 2023 | **106** | Completeness now covers oral as well as written statements. The completing statement comes in over a hearsay objection. | **Apply** | [S-104] |
| Dec 1, 2023 | **615** | Court may also order excluded witnesses not to learn of trial testimony. | Apply | [S-104] |
| Dec 1, 2023 | **702** | The proponent must show by a preponderance ("more likely than not") that each requirement is met, and the opinion must stay within what reliable methodology supports. | **Apply, high-yield** | [S-104] |
| Dec 1, 2024 | **107** (new) | Illustrative aids are allowed if utility is not substantially outweighed by prejudice, confusion or delay. They are not evidence. | Apply | [S-105] |
| Dec 1, 2024 | **613(b)** | Extrinsic evidence of a prior inconsistent statement now needs a **prior** opportunity for the witness to explain or deny. This does not apply to an opposing party's statement under 801(d)(2). | **Apply** | [S-105] |
| Dec 1, 2024 | **801(d)(2)** | A predecessor-in-interest's statement is admissible against the successor. | Apply | [S-105] |
| Dec 1, 2024 | **804(b)(3)** | In criminal cases, corroboration is judged by the totality of circumstances, including evidence supporting or undermining the statement. | Apply | [S-105] |
| Dec 1, 2024 | **1006** | A summary is admissible whether or not the underlying evidence was admitted. | Apply | [S-105] |
| **Dec 1, 2026** | **801(d)(1)(A)** | **Pending.** Any prior inconsistent statement of a testifying witness is admissible **as substantive evidence**, subject to Rule 403 (and 613(b)). The old requirement that it was made under penalty of perjury at a proceeding or deposition is removed. Adopted by the Supreme Court Apr 8, 2026; takes effect unless Congress acts. | **Apply if in effect (very likely). Big change from the classic MBE answer.** | [S-101] [S-102] [VERIFY V-028] |
| — | 609 | A proposed amendment was **withdrawn** in 2026. | Current 609 applies | [S-106] |
| — | 707 (AI-generated evidence) | Sent back for further study; not adopted. | Not testable | [S-106] |

**Bridge to California.** If 801(d)(1)(A) takes effect, the FRE moves close to California's long-standing rule allowing substantive use of prior inconsistent statements. The `evidence/ca-distinctions.md` comparison table must be rewritten for it in Phase 4.

**California tort statutes**
- **MICRA, Civ. Code § 3333.2 (AB 35; effective Jan 1, 2023):** non-economic damages caps in medical-negligence cases rise each Jan 1 by $40,000 (non-death) and $50,000 (death). For 2027: **$510,000** (non-death) and **$700,000** (wrongful death); separate caps can apply by defendant category [S-701] [S-702] [VERIFY V-601]. Exam impact: low (Remedies/Torts damages note).
- **CCP § 377.34(b):** the temporary rule allowing pre-death pain-and-suffering damages in survival actions covered only actions filed from Jan 1, 2022 to Dec 31, 2025; SB 29 (2025) was not enacted, so for actions filed now the general bar applies [S-704] [S-723] [VERIFY V-602]. **Apply** (Torts wrongful death/survival).

**California criminal statutes**
- **Proposition 36 (Nov 2024; effective Dec 18, 2024):** permits aggregating multiple thefts to reach the grand-theft threshold, and felony charging of repeat petty theft (Pen. Code §§ 490.3, 666.1) [S-362] [S-363] [VERIFY V-257]. Exam impact: low; essays use general theft law.
