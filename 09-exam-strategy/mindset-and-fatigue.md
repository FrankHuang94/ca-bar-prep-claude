# Mindset and fatigue

> This file gives general, common-sense guidance only. It is not medical or mental-health advice. If sleep, mood or anxiety problems persist or affect daily life, talk to a doctor or a licensed professional. If you need testing accommodations, the State Bar's request deadline is the final filing deadline, and it recommends filing about 6 months ahead [S-069].

## 1. Nine months on top of a full-time job

You will study for about 37 weeks, mostly after work. The risk is not one bad week. It is **slow erosion**: sleep debt, skipped review and quiet guilt that turns into avoidance.

### The four non-negotiables
| Habit | Minimum standard | Why it matters for the exam |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleep** | A consistent bedtime and wake time, including weekends. Do not trade sleep for study hours as a routine. | Memory consolidation and exam-day alertness both depend on it. Short-sleep study hours are low-yield. |
| **Movement** | Some daily activity you enjoy: a walk at lunch, a run, the gym. | It breaks up sitting time and helps mood and focus. |
| **Weekly off-time** | **One fully off half-day each week**, with no flashcards. | It makes the other 6.5 days sustainable. Planned rest is not falling behind. |
| **Weekly review** | 15 minutes, every week (`/weekly-review`). | It turns vague guilt into a concrete re-plan. |

### Burnout signals: when you see two or more, adjust the plan instead of pushing harder
- You open the material and reread the same page without retaining it, several days in a row.
- MBE accuracy falls for two weeks while hours stay the same.
- You skip review sessions and do only "easy" tasks such as reading.
- You feel dread on Sunday evening about both work and study.
- Your sleep schedule slips by an hour or more on most nights.
- You get irritable with people around you about small things.

**What to do:**
- Take **two full days off**, then run `/weekly-review` to cut the next two weeks' load by about 20%.
- **Drop volume before you drop review.** Fewer new questions, but still review the ones you did.
- Talk to someone (partner, friend, a colleague who has taken a bar exam).

## 2. Mindset traps for experienced foreign-trained lawyers

You are a qualified lawyer with real transactional experience. That is an advantage: you already read documents carefully, you understand commercial context, and you work under deadlines. It also creates specific habits that cost points on this exam.

| Trap | How it shows up | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Perfectionism** | Polishing sentences, fixing typos, rewriting rule statements to be "complete". | Spelling and grammar are **not graded** [S-010]. Graders reward issues, rules and application ([grader-expectations.md](../00-exam-intel/grader-expectations.md)). A complete, plain answer beats an elegant, incomplete one. **Use "good enough to score" as the standard.** |
| **Over-reading the facts** | Looking for hidden traps, inventing facts ("maybe the contract had a clause..."), spending 20 minutes reading. | Take facts at face value. When a fact is ambiguous, argue both readings in the answer rather than resolving it in your head. Stop reading at 0:10 ([essay-method.md](essay-method.md)). |
| **Writing a memo, not an exam answer** | A background section, a long executive summary, footnote-like caveats, "subject to further diligence", recommendations to the client. | The grader wants **headings that follow the call, then IRAC**. No background section. No hedging without a conclusion. Each issue ends with "therefore... likely". |
| **Code-style deduction** | "Under § X the contract is void." A conclusion with no facts. | **Rule, because, facts, therefore** ([essay-method.md](essay-method.md) §5). Graders give "little or no credit" for conclusions alone [S-076]. |
| **One-sided analysis** | Stating "the answer" the way a court or a senior partner would. | Show the contest: "Plaintiff will argue...; however..." Then decide. |
| **"I should already know this"** | Embarrassment at missing basic Contracts or Civ Pro questions, and avoiding the subjects that show it. | Six of your subjects (four of them on the MBE) are new to you. Errors there are data, not a verdict. Log them and move on. |
| **Comparing with US JD students** | "They did three years of this." | Your benchmark is the pass line and your own trend ([readiness-dashboard.md](../10-progress/readiness-dashboard.md)), not other people. |
| **Translating in your head** | Reading English facts, thinking in Chinese legal concepts, then writing back in English. | Learn each rule as its English rule statement. Use the PRC bridge lines only to *connect* concepts, never as the rule. Terms are in `02-foundations/legal-english-glossary.md`. |

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: the PRC national legal exam (法考) and practice reward finding the governing article and stating the answer. California graders reward showing the reasoning path, including the losing side's best argument (approx.).

## 3. Handling a bad essay mid-exam

You will probably meet at least one essay where you do not recognize the issue, or where the facts seem to point nowhere. **This is normal and survivable.**

1. **The damage is capped.** Each answer is scored separately, 40–100 [S-010]. A 50 instead of 65 on one essay is roughly 30 total scaled points under the Feb 2026 formula. That is recoverable through the other six graded answers and the MBE ([format-and-scoring.md](../00-exam-intel/format-and-scoring.md) §2). **Letting it spill into the next essay is what turns one bad answer into a failing day.**
2. **Write something under every call.** Identify the subject, state the closest rule you know, and apply it to the facts. This earns at least 40 and often more [S-010]. Graders calibrate on sample answers before grading [S-010]. My inference (not a State Bar statement): if an essay is hard for you, it is probably hard for many applicants, and the calibration reflects that.
3. **Use the facts as a guide.** List the facts and ask what each could be relevant to. The facts usually point to the issues even when your memory does not.
4. **Hard stop at 60 minutes.** Then do the reset (§4) and start the next essay fresh.
5. **After the session:** do not discuss the essay with anyone, and do not look anything up. You cannot change it, and doing so will cost you energy for the next session.

## 4. Panic protocol (60 seconds)

If your mind goes blank, your heart races, or you notice you have read the same paragraph three times:

| Step | Action | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **S**top | Hands off the keyboard or pencil down. | 2 s |
| **B**reathe | Two or three slow breaths with a longer exhale than inhale. Feet flat, shoulders down. | 20 s |
| **O**rient | Look at the clock and your written stop time. Say the next concrete step: "Heading 2: rule, then facts." | 10 s |
| **A**ct | Do only that next step. Momentum returns with action, not with thinking. | — |

**Practise the protocol during timed practice**, so that it is automatic on exam day. If anxiety regularly disrupts practice sessions, that is a reason to talk to a professional early, not in July.

## 5. The two-day energy plan

| When | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Week before** | Shift to the exam schedule: wake time compatible with being seated by 8:20 a.m. [S-003]. Taper practice volume and keep short timed sets. No new material in the last 2–3 days. |
| **Monday (day before)** | Light: a site visit if offered ([exam-day-logistics.md](exam-day-logistics.md)), a one-page review, the packed bag, a normal dinner, your normal bedtime. Expect lighter sleep than usual. One restless night is common and should not decide your exam. |
| **Day 1 morning** | A breakfast you have tested in simulations. Arrive early and avoid group conversations about "what's going to be tested". |
| **Day 1 lunch** | Eat, hydrate, walk briefly. **Do not discuss the morning essays.** Glance at your PT method card only. |
| **Day 1 evening** | Upload if allowed ([exam-day-logistics.md](exam-day-logistics.md) §3). Eat a proper dinner. A **maximum of 30 minutes** of light MBE warm-up (10 easy questions, or your "rules I keep missing" list). Then stop. **Do not reread your essays or search for answers to Day 1 questions.** Normal bedtime. |
| **Day 2** | Same morning routine. The MBE is a stamina event: use the checkpoint resets ([05-mbe/strategy.md](../05-mbe/strategy.md) §5). Lunch is the same as Day 1: no discussing questions. |
| **After** | Upload by the deadline (if not done) [S-009]. Then rest. Results come months later [S-012]. |

**Food and drink:** nothing new on exam days. Use the same breakfast, lunch and caffeine habits you used on simulation days. The July 2026 rules allowed only water in the exam room [S-003]; check the 2027 bulletin [VERIFY V-050].

## 6. Rehearse the exam conditions
- At least **two full 2-day simulations** (`/simulate`) at real start times. One goal is the score; the other is learning **how your energy behaves** in hour 5 of Day 1 and hour 5 of Day 2.
- After each simulation, note what went wrong outside the law: hunger, cold room, a slow start, the post-lunch dip. Fix one thing for the next simulation.
- Use foam earplugs in some practice sessions if you plan to use them on exam day. Only foam was allowed in July 2026 [S-003].
