# Closing argument

> **How often this appears on California PTs:** to be filled in from [past-pt-index.csv](../past-pt-index.csv) in Phase 3. No frequency is stated until then.

## Purpose
Persuade the fact-finder (a **jury**, or a judge in a bench trial) after the evidence has closed. Show that the evidence satisfies, or fails to satisfy, each element the fact-finder must decide, under the **burden of proof** and the **jury instructions** supplied in the Library.

The File usually contains trial transcripts or witness summaries. The Library usually contains the jury instructions or elements and the cases on them.

## Format template

Write it as a **script to be spoken**, unless the task memo asks for an outline or another format.

```text
CLOSING ARGUMENT ON BEHALF OF [PLAINTIFF / DEFENDANT / THE PEOPLE]
[Case name, if given]

[1. THEME: 1-3 sentences, memorable, fact-based]
"Members of the jury, this case is about a promise made in writing and broken in silence."

[2. ROADMAP]
"The judge will instruct you that [plaintiff] must prove three things. Let's walk through
the evidence on each."

[3. BURDEN OF PROOF]
State the standard exactly as the Library's instruction gives it (e.g., preponderance; beyond a
reasonable doubt), and what it means in practice for this case.

[4. ELEMENT-BY-ELEMENT ARGUMENT: one heading per element / instruction]
Element 1: [quote or closely paraphrase the instruction]
  - Evidence: [witness/exhibit] said/showed [fact] (cite the transcript page or exhibit)
  - Why it proves the element: [inference]
  - Corroboration: [second source]
Element 2: ...

[5. CREDIBILITY AND THE OTHER SIDE'S CASE]
Address the strongest opposing evidence: inconsistencies, bias, lack of corroboration,
contradiction by documents. Use the Library's credibility instruction if there is one.

[6. DAMAGES / VERDICT FORM]  <- if relevant: the amount and the arithmetic from the evidence

[7. CONCLUSION AND REQUEST]
Return to the theme. Ask for a specific verdict: "...find [defendant] liable and award $___."
```

## Tone
- **Oral and persuasive:** short sentences, signposting ("First...", "Second..."), rhetorical questions used sparingly, plain words a juror understands.
- **Respectful** to the jury, the court and opposing counsel.

**Ethical limits on argument.** In trial, a lawyer may not allude to matters that will not be supported by admissible evidence, assert personal knowledge of facts in issue, or state a **personal opinion** on the justness of a cause, a witness's credibility, a civil litigant's culpability, or an accused's guilt or innocence (ABA Model Rule 3.4(e)). California rule 3.4(g) bars asserting personal knowledge of facts in issue and stating a personal opinion as to the guilt or innocence of an accused, but omits the ABA's justness-of-cause, witness-credibility and civil-culpability categories [S-550] [VERIFY V-450]. In a PT, apply whatever ethics rules the File and Library supply.
- **What to write:** "The evidence shows that Mr. Lee's account changed three times."
- **What not to write:** "I believe Mr. Lee is lying."

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Arguing facts that are not in the record | Use only the evidence in the File (transcripts, exhibits) and reasonable inferences from it. |
| Personal opinions ("I think", "I believe") on credibility or guilt | Attribute everything to the evidence. |
| Skipping an element the Library's instructions require | One heading per instruction element. |
| Misstating the burden of proof | Quote the Library's instruction. |
| Ignoring damaging testimony | Confront it: explain, minimize, or attack its reliability. |
| Writing a brief instead of a speech (citations, case analysis) | Jurors hear evidence and instructions, not case law. Cite record facts, not cases, unless the task says the argument is to a judge. |
| No specific verdict request | End with the verdict, and the amount if any. |

## Checklist
- [ ] Theme in the opening and again in the close.
- [ ] Roadmap tied to the Library's instructions or elements.
- [ ] Burden of proof stated correctly.
- [ ] Every element argued with specific record evidence (witness, exhibit, page).
- [ ] Opposing evidence and credibility addressed.
- [ ] No personal opinion or vouching; no facts outside the record.
- [ ] Specific verdict requested.
- [ ] Spoken style; format as the memo instructs.
