# Persuasive brief (memorandum of points and authorities, trial or appellate brief)

> **How often this appears on California PTs:** to be filled in from [past-pt-index.csv](../past-pt-index.csv) in Phase 3. No frequency is stated until then.

## Purpose
- Persuade a **court** to do something specific: grant or deny a motion, reverse or affirm, admit or exclude evidence.
- The reader is a busy judge who wants to know **what you want, why the law allows it, and why these facts require it.**

## Format template

Follow the task memo first. Many memos say to omit the caption, table of contents or statement of facts.

```text
[CAPTION]                     <- only if requested
[COURT NAME], [County], State of Columbia
[Plaintiff] v. [Defendant]    Case No. [ ]
[TITLE: e.g., MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES IN OPPOSITION TO DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT]

[INTRODUCTION]                <- optional; 3-5 sentences: what you ask for and the 2-3 reasons

[STATEMENT OF FACTS]          <- only if requested: accurate, but told from your client's side,
                                 with File citations

ARGUMENT
I.  [POINT HEADING 1: a full-sentence argument; see the formula below]
    [Rule paragraph: Library authority; the standard (e.g., the motion standard)]
    [Explanation: how the cases applied it: facts + holding]
    [Application: our facts, analogized or distinguished]
    [Rebuttal: the other side's best authority or fact, and why it fails]
    [Conclusion of the point]
    A. [Sub-heading for an element, if the point has several]
    B. ...
II. [POINT HEADING 2] ...

CONCLUSION
For the foregoing reasons, [client] respectfully requests that the Court [specific relief].

Dated: [ ]                     Respectfully submitted,
                               [Role per task memo, e.g., "Attorney for Plaintiff"]  <- no real name
```

## Point headings: the core skill

A point heading is a **complete, persuasive sentence** that tells the judge the conclusion, the legal reason, and the key fact. A judge who reads only your headings should understand your whole argument.

**Formula:** `[Result you want] + because + [legal rule or standard] + [decisive facts]`

| Weak (topic label) | Strong (point heading), original example |
|---|---|
| I. Personal jurisdiction | I. THE COURT HAS SPECIFIC JURISDICTION OVER DEFENDANT BECAUSE DEFENDANT SHIPPED THE DEFECTIVE HEATERS DIRECTLY TO COLUMBIA CUSTOMERS AND PLAINTIFF'S INJURY AROSE FROM ONE OF THOSE SALES. |
| II. Statute of limitations | II. PLAINTIFF'S CLAIM IS TIMELY BECAUSE THE LIMITATIONS PERIOD DID NOT BEGIN UNTIL SHE DISCOVERED THE CONTAMINATION IN MARCH. |
| A. Notice | A. The Tenant Received Adequate Notice Because the Landlord's Email Stated the Breach, the Cure Period, and the Termination Date. |

**Rules for headings:**
- Use **one heading per independent ground for relief.** Order them strongest first, unless logic requires otherwise (for example, a threshold issue first).
- Sub-headings for each element or factor when a point has several.
- Main headings may be in capitals; sub-headings in sentence or title case. Choose one style and keep it.
- If the task memo gives a heading format, use it.

## CREAC inside each point
- **C**onclusion: the heading, plus a one-sentence opening.
- **R**ule: the statute or test, cited to the Library.
- **E**xplanation: how the Library cases applied the rule, with their facts and holdings.
- **A**pplication: our facts, compared with those cases.
- **C**onclusion: what the court should therefore do on this point.

## Tone
- **Confident and formal.** Use "Plaintiff's claim is timely", not "Plaintiff's claim may arguably be timely".
- **Accurate.** Persuasion comes from choosing and framing facts, never from misstating them. In real practice a lawyer must not knowingly make a false statement of law or fact to a tribunal and, in the controlling jurisdiction, must disclose directly adverse controlling authority that opposing counsel has not disclosed (Cal. Rules of Prof. Conduct, rule 3.3(a)(1)–(2); ABA Model Rule 3.3(a)(1)–(2)). On the PT, that means **deal with adverse Library authority**: distinguish it, limit it, or argue that it was wrongly decided or has been superseded.
- Refer to your client sympathetically and by name ("Ms. Chen"). Refer to the opponent neutrally ("Defendant").

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Topic-label headings | Rewrite with the formula. |
| Ignoring the adverse case the Library obviously contains | It is there to be distinguished. Give it its own paragraph. |
| Arguing facts not in the File | Use only the record, plus reasonable inferences presented as such. |
| Forgetting the procedural standard (e.g., summary judgment: no genuine dispute of material fact) when the Library supplies one | State the standard in the first point and argue within it. |
| No specific relief requested | The Conclusion names the exact order sought. |
| An objective "on the other hand" tone throughout | Acknowledge counter-arguments in order to defeat them. |

## Checklist
- [ ] The document type and court match the task memo; the caption only if requested.
- [ ] Point headings are full persuasive sentences, one per ground.
- [ ] Every point has a rule plus a Library authority by name.
- [ ] Library cases are analogized or distinguished on their facts.
- [ ] Adverse authority and bad facts are confronted.
- [ ] A statement of facts only if requested, with File citations.
- [ ] Specific relief in the Conclusion; signed by role, with no real name.
