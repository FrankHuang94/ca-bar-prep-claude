# Drafting a contract (or selected provisions)

> **How often this appears on California PTs:** to be filled in from [past-pt-index.csv](../past-pt-index.csv) in Phase 3. No frequency is stated until then.

## Purpose
- Draft or revise contract provisions that carry out the client's **business deal** (from the File), are **enforceable** under the Library's law, and **protect the client** against the risks the File reveals.
- Often paired with a short memo explaining the drafting choices, or rejecting the other side's proposed language.

**Your edge:** M&A drafting experience. **Your risk:** over-drafting. Graders reward provisions that answer the task's specific concerns using the Library's rules, not full transaction boilerplate.

## Format template

Draft only what is requested. If the File contains a draft agreement, **mark up or replace the specific clauses** the task identifies, keeping its numbering and defined terms.

```text
[TITLE: e.g., SERVICES AGREEMENT]  /  [AMENDED SECTION 7: NON-COMPETITION]

[PARTIES]           <- only if drafting a full agreement
This Agreement is made on [date] between [Party A], a [Columbia corporation] ("Company"),
and [Party B] ("Consultant").

[RECITALS]          <- optional; short; the business purpose. Useful when the Library makes
                       intent or purpose relevant (e.g., protectable interest)

1. DEFINITIONS
   1.1 "Confidential Information" means ...
   1.2 "Territory" means ...

2. [OPERATIVE PROVISIONS]
   2.1 Obligation:  Consultant shall [act] by [time] in accordance with [standard].
   2.2 Discretion:  Company may [act] if [condition].
   2.3 Condition:   Company's obligation to pay is subject to [event] (a condition, not a promise).
   2.4 Prohibition: Consultant shall not [act] within the Territory for [period].

3. REPRESENTATIONS AND WARRANTIES       <- if the File reveals facts the client is relying on

4. REMEDIES / TERMINATION
   4.1 Liquidated damages: [amount/formula] (draft to the Library's reasonableness test; state
       why the amount is a reasonable forecast, if the explanatory memo requires it)
   4.2 Termination for cause: [defined events]; notice; cure period

5. GENERAL (only clauses the task makes relevant: severability or reformation if the Library
   allows partial enforcement; governing law; notices; entire agreement)

[SIGNATURE BLOCKS]  <- if a full agreement is requested

-----------------------------------------
[EXPLANATORY MEMO, if requested]
Section 2.4: limited to [scope/time/area] because [Library case] enforced a similar
restriction but [other Library case] struck down a broader one. ...
Rejected the other side's proposed § 4.1 because ...
```

## Drafting conventions
| Use | For | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **shall** | A duty of a party (subject = the party) | "Seller shall deliver..." |
| **may** | Discretion or a right | "Buyer may terminate..." |
| **shall not** | A prohibition | "Consultant shall not solicit..." |
| **is subject to / if ... then** | A **condition** (no breach if it fails; the obligation simply does not arise) | "Buyer's obligation to close is subject to receipt of..." |
| Defined terms | Every repeated concept, capitalized and used identically | "Confidential Information" every time, never "confidential data" |

> 🇨🇳 PRC bridge: 应 / 可以 / 不得 roughly track "shall", "may" and "shall not". US drafting keeps the three strictly apart, and separates a **condition** from a **promise**, because breach and non-occurrence of a condition have different legal consequences (approx.).

## Tone
- Precise, neutral wording inside the contract, even though the drafting choices favour your client.
- In the explanatory memo: objective and tied to the Library ("This provision is likely enforceable under *X* because...").

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Drafting the whole agreement when the task asks for two clauses | Draft only the clauses requested, and use the time on the explanatory memo. |
| Provisions that ignore the Library's enforceability test (e.g., an overbroad restraint, a liquidated-damages penalty) | Fit each clause to the Library's test, and explain the fit. |
| Inconsistent defined terms | Define once; repeat exactly. |
| Mixing promises and conditions | Use "shall" for duties and "subject to" for conditions. |
| Missing the client's real business concern (in the client's email or meeting notes) | List the client's concerns from the File before drafting. Every concern gets a clause or an explanation. |
| Silent rejection of the other side's language | Explain why, if the task asks. |

## Checklist
- [ ] Only the requested provisions, in the File's numbering and format where a draft exists.
- [ ] Every client concern in the File is addressed.
- [ ] Each provision meets the Library's enforceability rules (cite them in the memo).
- [ ] Duties (shall), discretion (may), prohibitions (shall not) and conditions (subject to) are used correctly.
- [ ] Defined terms are consistent.
- [ ] The explanatory memo, if asked, ties each choice to a Library authority by name.
- [ ] No real names other than the File's.
