# E-XO-013 — Three Forks Catering (Business Associations + Remedies)

## Facts

In 2021, Ana, Ben and Cole, three chefs, started a catering business in Oakland called "Three Forks Catering." They never signed a written agreement. Each contributed $30,000, all three cook and share management decisions, and they split the profits equally. They filed a fictitious business name statement listing all three as owners. Ana keeps the books and has signing authority on the business bank account, which is titled "Three Forks Catering." The business rents its commercial kitchen, month to month, from Lim, the building's owner, for $6,000 a month.

In March 2026, Lim emailed Ana at her Three Forks email address: "I'm retiring and want to sell the building. Since Three Forks is my tenant, I'm offering it to you first for $800,000." Ana told Ben and Cole that Lim "isn't interested in selling." She then formed AnaCo LLC, of which she is the sole member, and on April 15 AnaCo bought the building for $800,000. The $80,000 down payment came from $50,000 of Ana's own savings and $30,000 that Ana transferred from the Three Forks bank account and recorded in the books as a "loan to Ana." Ben and Cole never authorized the transfer. The remaining $720,000 was a bank loan secured by a recorded deed of trust on the building.

In June, AnaCo raised Three Forks' rent to $9,000 a month, and Ana paid the higher rent from the Three Forks account. Also in June, Ana formed Fork & Knife, a catering company that she owns alone and that operates on weekends from the same kitchen. Ana solicited Three Forks' two largest corporate clients, both of which moved their contracts to Fork & Knife. Over the following four months, Three Forks' profits fell by $60,000 compared with the same months in 2025, and Fork & Knife earned $45,000 in profit.

Ben and Cole discovered all of this in October 2026, when Lim mentioned at a neighborhood event that he was glad "Three Forks" had bought his building. When they confronted Ana, she said that the partnership "could never have come up with $800,000 anyway" and that Fork & Knife was "my own weekend side project." The building is now worth $1,000,000. AnaCo has signed a contract to sell the building to a developer for $1,000,000, with closing in three weeks; the developer knows nothing about the dispute.

## Call of the Question

1. Is Three Forks a partnership, and did Ana breach any duties to it or to Ben and Cole? Discuss.
2. What remedies can Three Forks, Ben and Cole obtain against Ana? Discuss.

## Model Answer

### Call 1 — Partnership and Ana's duties

#### A. Is Three Forks a partnership?

**Rule.** A general partnership is formed by the association of two or more persons to carry on as co-owners a business for profit, whether or not they intend to form a partnership; no writing or filing is required (RUPA § 202; Cal. Corp. Code § 16202). A person who receives a share of the profits of a business is presumed to be a partner.

**Application.** Ana, Ben and Cole each contributed capital, all three manage and cook, and they split profits equally, which raises the presumption of partnership; nothing suggests the profit shares were wages, rent or loan interest. Their fictitious business name statement lists all three as owners. The lack of a written agreement does not matter.

**Conclusion:** Three Forks is a general partnership at will, and Ana, Ben and Cole are partners. Each owes the partnership and the other partners fiduciary duties of loyalty and care.

#### B. Duty of loyalty

**Rule.** A partner's duty of loyalty includes (1) accounting to the partnership for, and holding as trustee for it, any property, profit or benefit derived from the conduct of the partnership business or the use of partnership property, including the appropriation of a partnership opportunity; (2) refraining from dealing with the partnership as or on behalf of a party having an interest adverse to it; and (3) refraining from competing with the partnership before its dissolution (RUPA § 404(b); Corp. Code § 16404(b)). Partners owe one another "the punctilio of an honor the most sensitive" (*Meinhard v. Salmon*). A partner may act in her own interest only after full disclosure and consent of the other partners.

**1. Appropriation of a partnership opportunity — the building.**
- Lim offered the building to Three Forks because it was his tenant ("Since Three Forks is my tenant, I'm offering it to you first"), sending the offer to Ana's Three Forks email. The opportunity came to the partnership, through Ana as its partner, and concerned the very premises where its business operates. It was a partnership opportunity.
- Ana did not disclose it; she affirmatively lied ("isn't interested in selling") and took it for her own LLC.
- Ana may argue that the partnership could not have afforded $800,000. But financial inability is generally no defense where the fiduciary concealed the opportunity and denied her partners the chance to raise the money or to consent; as in *Meinhard*, the partner who receives an opportunity because of the venture must at least disclose it.
- **Conclusion:** Ana breached the duty of loyalty and holds the building, in equity, as trustee for the partnership.

**2. Misuse of partnership funds.** Ana transferred $30,000 from the partnership account to fund the down payment, labelled it a "loan to Ana," and never obtained Ben's and Cole's consent. Using partnership property for a partner's own benefit is a loyalty breach. In addition, property acquired with partnership funds is presumed to be partnership property, even if titled in another's name, which supports the partnership's claim to the building.

**3. Dealing as an adverse party — the rent increase.** Through AnaCo, Ana became the partnership's landlord and raised its rent by 50%, then caused the partnership to pay that rent. She was on both sides of the transaction, acting for an interest adverse to the partnership, without disclosure or consent. This is a breach.

**4. Competition — Fork & Knife.** While still a partner, Ana formed a competing catering company, operated it from the same kitchen and solicited Three Forks' two largest clients. Competing with the partnership before dissolution is a breach of the duty of loyalty; soliciting its clients makes it flagrant.

#### C. Duty of care, good faith and information

A partner must refrain from grossly negligent or reckless conduct, intentional misconduct and knowing violations of law, and must act consistently with the obligation of good faith and fair dealing (RUPA § 404(c)–(d)). Partners must also furnish information the others need to exercise their rights (RUPA § 403). Ana's deliberate lie about Lim's offer and her secret transfers are intentional misconduct and a breach of good faith and of the duty to inform.

### Call 2 — Remedies

#### A. The action and an accounting

A partnership may sue a partner for breach of duty, and a partner may sue another partner for legal or equitable relief, with or without an accounting (RUPA § 405). An accounting is the equitable remedy by which a fiduciary must account for all receipts and profits that belong in conscience to the partnership. Three Forks (or Ben and Cole on its behalf) should seek an accounting of the building transaction, the rent paid to AnaCo, the $30,000 transfer and Fork & Knife's profits.

#### B. Constructive trust over the building

**Rule.** A constructive trust compels a defendant who holds title to property acquired by wrongdoing, such as a breach of fiduciary duty, to convey it to the plaintiff. California treats one who gains a thing by violation of a trust or other wrongful act as an involuntary trustee for the person who would otherwise have had it (Civ. Code § 2224). It requires identifiable property; it gives the plaintiff any increase in value and priority over unsecured creditors; and it is cut off by a transfer to a bona fide purchaser for value without notice.

**Application.**
- *Wrongful acquisition.* Ana usurped the partnership's opportunity by deceit and used $30,000 of partnership funds. The statute itself says a partner holds such benefits "as trustee" for the partnership.
- *Identifiable property.* The building is a specific asset. The $30,000 is traced directly from the partnership account into the down payment, and because the whole building is the usurped opportunity, the remedy can reach the whole building, not merely a proportionate share.
- *Holder.* Title is in AnaCo LLC, but AnaCo is Ana's wholly owned entity, formed to take the opportunity, and it took with full knowledge through Ana. It is not a bona fide purchaser and holds subject to the trust.
- *Terms.* Equity will condition the trust on fairness: the partnership takes the building subject to the bank's $720,000 deed of trust (the bank lent for value without notice and has priority) and must reimburse Ana's own $50,000 contribution. The partnership would thus receive a building worth $1,000,000, subject to the $720,000 loan, for a net equity of about $230,000 after reimbursing Ana, plus the appreciation going forward.

**Equitable lien — the alternative.** An equitable lien would secure only the $30,000 of partnership funds (plus interest), with a deficiency judgment if the property were worth less. Because the building has risen in value and the whole opportunity belonged to the partnership, the constructive trust is far more valuable. The partnership should choose the constructive trust.

#### C. Stopping the sale: TRO, preliminary injunction and lis pendens

The constructive trust will be lost if AnaCo closes its sale in three weeks to a developer who knows nothing of the dispute, because a bona fide purchaser for value takes free of equitable claims.

**Lis pendens.** Because the action claims an interest in real property, the partnership should immediately record a notice of pending action. Anyone who later acquires an interest takes with constructive notice and cannot be a bona fide purchaser.

**TRO and preliminary injunction.** The partnership should also seek an order barring AnaCo and Ana from selling or encumbering the building pending trial.
- *Likelihood of success.* Strong: Ana's lie, the email from Lim, and the unauthorized transfer document a clear breach.
- *Irreparable harm.* Land is unique; this building is the partnership's kitchen and place of business; if the sale closes to a bona fide purchaser, the constructive trust and the building's appreciation are lost, leaving only a money claim against Ana, whose ability to pay is uncertain.
- *Balance of hardships.* Ana's harm is delay in a sale, and the undertaking protects her and AnaCo if the injunction proves wrongful; the developer's contract simply waits. The partnership's harm is permanent loss of the property and possibly its premises.
- *Public interest.* Enforcing fiduciary duties serves the public interest.
- *California test.* California courts weigh the likelihood of prevailing and the relative interim harm on a sliding scale (*IT Corp. v. County of Imperial*); both factors favor the partnership.
- *Notice and bond.* Three weeks allows notice; an ex parte TRO is warranted only if closing becomes imminent. The partnership must post an undertaking for Ana's damages if the injunction proves wrongful.

**Conclusion:** The court will likely grant a preliminary injunction against the sale, and the partnership should record a lis pendens at once.

#### D. Injunction against competition

A permanent (and preliminary) injunction requires an inadequate legal remedy, a protectable interest, feasibility, a favorable balance of hardships and no defense. While Ana remains a partner, she is bound not to compete. Lost clients and goodwill are continuing and hard to measure, so damages are inadequate; an order forbidding Ana from soliciting Three Forks' clients or operating Fork & Knife while she is a partner is prohibitory and easy to enforce; and Ana has no legitimate interest in breaching her duty. (The statutory duty not to compete applies only before dissolution and ends when she dissociates (Corp. Code §§ 16404(b)(3), 16603(b)), so the injunction would last only until she dissociates or the partnership dissolves.)

#### E. Fork & Knife: disgorgement or lost-profit damages

- *Disgorgement.* Ana must account for and hold as trustee any profit derived from her breach. Fork & Knife's $45,000 profit, earned from diverted clients using the partnership's kitchen, is recoverable by restitution (Ana's gain).
- *Damages.* Alternatively, the partnership may recover its own lost profits, $60,000. Three Forks is an established business with a five-year history, so its lost profits can be proved with reasonable certainty by comparison to the same months in 2025. Ana will argue that some of the decline had other causes; she bears the risk of uncertainty created by her own wrong, but the partnership must still show the loss was caused by the diverted clients.
- *Election.* Both measures address the same harm (the diverted clients), so the partnership should elect the larger, $60,000 in damages, rather than recover both.

#### F. Rent overcharges

The $3,000-a-month increase that Ana caused the partnership to pay to her own LLC (at least $15,000 from June through October) is recoverable as restitution of a benefit obtained through self-dealing. If the court imposes a constructive trust on the building, all rent the partnership paid to AnaCo since April (a payment to itself in substance) should be credited in the accounting.

#### G. Punitive damages

Breach of fiduciary duty is a tort, not merely a breach of contract, so punitive damages are available on clear and convincing evidence of oppression, fraud or malice (Civ. Code § 3294). Ana's deliberate lie to her partners and secret diversion of the opportunity and funds is fraud. The partnership must offer evidence of Ana's financial condition, and the award must satisfy due-process limits.

#### H. Expulsion and best remedies

Ben and Cole may also seek judicial expulsion of Ana for willful, material breach of her duties, which would dissociate her; the partnership would then buy out her interest, offset by the damages she owes. Alternatively, they may seek dissolution and winding up.

**Best combination:** record a lis pendens and obtain a TRO/preliminary injunction now; at trial, a constructive trust over the building (conditioned on reimbursing Ana's $50,000 and subject to the bank loan); an accounting with recovery of the rent overcharges; $60,000 in lost-profit damages (or $45,000 disgorgement); an injunction against competition while Ana remains a partner; punitive damages; and, if desired, Ana's expulsion.

**Common mistakes**
1. Limiting the partnership to the $30,000 it lost. The usurped opportunity itself belongs to the partnership; a constructive trust reaches the building and its appreciation.
2. Forgetting to reimburse Ana's own $50,000 or to respect the bank's senior deed of trust. Equity conditions the trust on fairness.
3. Ignoring the impending sale. A bona fide purchaser would cut off the constructive trust; record a lis pendens and seek a preliminary injunction.
4. Treating AnaCo LLC as a separate innocent owner. It is Ana's wholly owned vehicle with full notice.
5. Recovering both $60,000 in damages and $45,000 in disgorgement for the same diverted clients.
