## Facts

Marguerite Lowe, a widow domiciled in California, had two adult sons, Felix and Gabe. On March 1, 2022, in the office of her lawyer, Dennis Hale, she signed a will in the presence of two of Dennis's employees, who both watched her sign and then signed as witnesses. The will provides: "I give my entire estate to the trustee of the Marguerite Lowe Trust, to be held and distributed under the terms of that trust as amended from time to time." It names Dennis as executor.

On April 15, 2022, Marguerite signed the Marguerite Lowe Trust, which Dennis drafted. It names Dennis as trustee. Marguerite transferred her brokerage account (worth $1.4 million) and a rental condominium to Dennis as trustee. The trust provides that Marguerite will receive income and principal as she requests during her life and that, at her death, the trustee shall distribute $100,000 to St. Brendan's Church and the rest equally to Felix and Gabe. It says nothing about revocation. Article 8 states: "This trust may be amended by a written instrument signed by me and by the trustee."

In 2023, Felix moved to Europe and rarely called. In June 2024, at Marguerite's request, Dennis drafted a "First Amendment to the Marguerite Lowe Trust" providing that (1) Felix will receive only $1,000, with the rest of his former share going to Gabe; and (2) "$150,000 shall be distributed to my attorney and trustee, Dennis Hale, in gratitude for his years of service." Marguerite, then 84 and described by her doctor as "fully lucid," signed the amendment in Dennis's office. Dennis did not sign it, but he kept the signed original in his trust file. No other lawyer advised Marguerite about the amendment. Marguerite had complained to Gabe that "Felix has abandoned me."

On March 3, 2025, Marguerite wrote entirely in her own hand on her personal stationery: "I have changed my mind about Felix. My trust should be split equally between my boys, as it was before. — Marguerite Lowe, March 3, 2025." She put the page in her desk next to her copy of the will. She did not send it to Dennis or tell him about it.

Marguerite died in October 2025. At her death she also owned, outside the trust, a bank account holding $80,000 and a car worth $20,000.

After her death, Dennis, as trustee:
- paid himself the $150,000 described in the First Amendment;
- sold the condominium for $500,000 and left the proceeds in a non-interest-bearing checking account for 14 months; and
- hired Hale Capital, an investment firm owned by his son, to manage the brokerage account for an annual fee of 2% of its value, without telling the beneficiaries that his son owns the firm. Comparable firms charge about 0.75%.

## Call of the Question

Answer according to California law.

1. What property passes under Marguerite's will, and how?
2. Was the trust validly amended in June 2024, and what is the effect of the March 2025 writing?
3. What claims do the trust beneficiaries have against Dennis as trustee?
4. How should the trust estate be distributed?

## Model Answer

### 1. The will and the pour-over

**Validity of the will.** An attested will must be in writing, signed by the testator, and signed during the testator's lifetime by two witnesses who were present at the same time and witnessed the signing, understanding that the instrument is the testator's will (Prob. Code § 6110). Marguerite signed her typed will in front of two employees who watched her sign and then signed as witnesses. The will is valid.

**Pour-over.** A will may devise property to the trustee of a trust identified in the will whose terms are set out in a written instrument executed **before, concurrently with, or within 60 days after** the will, even if the trust is unfunded, amendable or revocable. The property is administered under the trust as amended before or after the testator's death (§ 6300).
- *Identified in the will:* the will names the "Marguerite Lowe Trust."
- *Timing:* the trust was signed April 15, 2022, **45 days after** the will. Under the older uniform-act rule and under incorporation by reference, a trust that did not exist when the will was signed could not be incorporated, so the pour-over would have failed. California's 60-day window saves it.
- *Amendments:* § 6300 applies the trust as amended, including after the will was signed, and the will itself refers to the trust "as amended from time to time."

**Conclusion.** The will validly pours over Marguerite's probate estate, the $80,000 account and the $20,000 car (net of debts and expenses), to Dennis as trustee, to be distributed under the trust as validly amended. The brokerage account and condominium were already in the funded trust; they pass outside probate under the trust's terms.

### 2. The June 2024 amendment and the March 2025 writing

**A. The trust was amendable.** In California a trust is revocable unless the instrument expressly makes it irrevocable (§ 15400); the traditional rule, by contrast, treated a trust as irrevocable unless a power to revoke was reserved. Marguerite's trust is silent, so it was revocable. A revocable trust may be modified by the procedure for revocation unless the instrument provides otherwise (§ 15402).

**B. Method of amendment (*Haggerty*).** A revocable trust may be revoked, and therefore modified, (1) by any method stated in the instrument, or (2) by a writing, other than a will, signed by the settlor and delivered to the trustee during the settlor's lifetime, unless the instrument **explicitly** makes its own method exclusive (§ 15401(a); *Haggerty v. Thornton* (2024)).
- *Article 8 not followed.* The amendment needed signatures by Marguerite and the trustee. Dennis never signed it.
- *Not explicitly exclusive.* Article 8 says the trust "may be amended" by that method. It does not say "only" or "exclusively."
- *Statutory method satisfied.* Marguerite signed a written amendment (not a will) and delivered it to Dennis, the trustee, who received and kept it in his trust file, during her lifetime.

**The June 2024 amendment was validly executed.** Its two provisions must then be tested separately.

**C. Felix's challenge to his reduction.** Capacity is measured when the instrument is signed; for a simple, will-like amendment courts apply the testamentary-capacity test (§ 6100.5). Marguerite was "fully lucid," and a reduction of one child's share is a simple decision. Undue influence is pressure that overcomes the settlor's free will (*Rice v. Clark*; § 86). A presumption of undue influence arises when the contestant shows a confidential relationship, the beneficiary's active participation in procuring the instrument, and an **undue benefit to that beneficiary**. Dennis, her lawyer, had a confidential relationship and drafted the document. But Dennis gains nothing from Felix's reduction; Gabe does, and Gabe took no part in procuring it. Marguerite also had her own reason: she complained that "Felix has abandoned me." Undue influence voids only the tainted parts of an instrument (compare § 6104 for wills), so even a successful attack on Dennis's own gift would not reach Felix's reduction. **The reduction of Felix's share likely stands.**

**D. The $150,000 gift to Dennis.** A donative transfer to the person who drafted the instrument is presumed to be the product of fraud or undue influence (§ 21380). For the drafter (and the drafter's relatives and law partners) the presumption is **conclusive**. The statute applies to trusts and trust amendments as well as wills. It does not apply if the drafter is related to the transferor, or if an independent attorney counseled the transferor alone and signed a certificate of independent review (§§ 21382, 21384).
- Dennis drafted the First Amendment and is its donee.
- He is not related to Marguerite.
- No other lawyer advised her, so there is no certificate of independent review.

Dennis may argue that the gift was a genuine expression of gratitude from a lucid client. For a drafter, though, the presumption is conclusive, so evidence of her real wishes cannot save the gift. **The gift fails.** A transfer that fails under § 21380 passes as if the donee had predeceased the transferor without issue (§ 21386). The $150,000 stays in the trust and passes as part of the remainder.

**E. The March 2025 writing.**
- *Is it a will?* A will is valid as a holograph if the signature and the material provisions are in the testator's handwriting (§ 6111). The writing is entirely handwritten, signed and dated. If Marguerite intended it as a testamentary statement, it is a valid holographic codicil.
- *Can it amend the trust?* Under § 15401(a)(2), the statutory method requires a writing "other than a will," delivered to the trustee during the settlor's lifetime. California does not let a later will amend or revoke a living trust unless the trust itself allows amendment by will (*cf.* the Uniform Trust Code, under which a later will that expressly refers to the trust may amend it if the trust's stated method is not exclusive). Marguerite's trust does not provide for amendment by will; its only stated method is an instrument signed by her and the trustee, which this is not.
- *Felix's argument.* Felix will argue the page is not a will at all, but a signed writing amending the trust. Even so, the statutory method requires **delivery to the trustee during the settlor's lifetime**, and Marguerite left the page in her desk and never told Dennis about it. Article 8's method also failed, because Dennis did not sign. Either way, the writing fails as an amendment.
- *Effect on the will.* Even if the writing is a holographic codicil, it does not mention the will, and its only operative words concern "my trust." It does not change the will's pour-over, and the poured-over assets are still governed by the trust as validly amended.

**Conclusion.** The March 2025 writing does not amend the trust. However clearly it shows that Marguerite changed her mind, it fails for lack of the required method and delivery. Felix remains limited to $1,000.

### 3. Claims against Dennis as trustee

On Marguerite's death the trust became irrevocable, and Dennis owed fiduciary duties to St. Brendan's, Felix and Gabe.

**A. Paying himself the $150,000.** A trustee must administer the trust according to its valid terms and solely in the beneficiaries' interest, and must not use trust property for his own benefit (§§ 16000, 16002, 16004(a)). The gift was invalid by operation of law, and Dennis, as drafter and trustee, was charged with knowing § 21380. Paying himself from trust funds was a self-interested taking of trust property. **Breach of the duty of loyalty.**

**B. Hiring his son's firm.** A trustee breaches the duty of loyalty by entering a transaction in which he has a conflicting personal interest, such as hiring the trustee's own relatives or their businesses, unless the instrument authorizes it or the beneficiaries give informed consent. Separately, a trustee may delegate investment functions, but must use reasonable care in selecting the agent and setting the terms of the delegation, and must incur only reasonable costs (§§ 16052, 16047).
- *Loyalty.* Hale Capital is owned by Dennis's son, and Dennis did not disclose that fact. The trust does not authorize the arrangement, and no beneficiary consented.
- *Prudence.* Paying 2% a year when comparable firms charge 0.75% wastes trust assets every year. Dennis will argue that he chose a capable firm he trusted. But the conflict is the very reason his choice cannot be trusted, and nothing suggests Hale Capital offers services worth nearly three times the market rate.

**Breaches of loyalty and of prudent delegation.**

**C. Leaving $500,000 idle.** A trustee must make trust property productive (§ 16007) and invest it as a prudent investor, considering the trust's purposes and circumstances (§ 16047). Leaving $500,000 in a non-interest-bearing account for 14 months earned the trust nothing. Dennis might argue that he kept the cash available for distributions, but he made none, and even cash held for liquidity could have been placed in an insured interest-bearing account. **Breach.**

**D. Remedies.** For a breach, a trustee is chargeable with any loss resulting from the breach with interest, any profit he made through it, or any profit the trust would have earned but for the breach (§ 16440(a)). The beneficiaries may petition (§ 17200) to compel Dennis to:
- restore the $150,000 with interest; a constructive trust may be imposed on the funds if traceable, and taking trust property in bad faith may also expose him to statutory double liability (§ 859);
- repay the excess fees (the amount above a reasonable 0.75%), plus any profit his son's firm made through the conflict, if recoverable;
- pay the return the $500,000 would reasonably have earned over 14 months.

Dennis should also be **removed** for breach of trust (§ 15642). Because he drafted the trust and is its sole trustee, his status as a § 21380 drafter is also a ground for removal (§ 15642(b)(6)), unless the court finds that Marguerite intended him to serve and that this intent was not the product of fraud or undue influence; his attempt to take a gift under his own amendment makes that finding unlikely. The court may also reduce or deny his compensation.

### 4. Distribution of the trust estate

The trust estate consists of the brokerage account, the $500,000 condominium proceeds, the poured-over $80,000 and car (net of probate expenses), and Dennis's repayments. It is distributed under the trust as amended in June 2024, minus the failed gift to Dennis:
- **St. Brendan's Church:** $100,000.
- **Felix:** $1,000. The March 2025 writing did not restore his half.
- **Gabe:** the entire remainder, including the $150,000 that failed under § 21380 and Dennis's surcharges.

**Common mistakes**
1. Failing the pour-over because the trust was signed after the will. California allows 60 days.
2. Letting Dennis rebut the drafter presumption with evidence of Marguerite's gratitude. For the drafter it is conclusive.
3. Voiding the whole amendment because of Dennis's gift, instead of only the tainted transfer.
4. Treating a holographic will as an effective trust amendment, or ignoring the delivery requirement.
5. Lumping Dennis's conduct into one "breach of fiduciary duty" paragraph instead of separate duties with separate remedies.
