# E-PRP-005 — The car wash behind the gas station

## Facts

In 2012 Gloria owned two adjoining lots on Route 9 in Pine County: Parcel A, fronting the highway, where she ran a gas station and convenience store, and Parcel B, a vacant rectangular lot behind it. The only paved access to Parcel B was a driveway across Parcel A.

In 2012 Gloria sold Parcel B to Hank. The deed, which Gloria and Hank both signed and which was promptly recorded, describes both parcels and:

1. grants Hank, "his heirs and assigns, an easement over the existing driveway on Parcel A for ingress and egress to Parcel B";
2. provides that "no building or other structure shall be erected on Parcel B within 30 feet of its boundary with Parcel A"; and
3. provides that "the owner of Parcel B shall pay one-half of the cost of maintaining and repaving the driveway."

The deed states that the covenants in items 2 and 3 "are made for the benefit of Parcel A and shall bind Hank and every future owner of Parcel B." Gloria wanted the 30-foot strip kept open so that fuel tanker trucks could turn around and her station's sign would stay visible from the side road. In 2014 Hank put a small storage shed 10 feet from the boundary. Gloria never objected, and the shed is still there.

In 2016 Hank sold Parcel B to Ivy by a deed that did not mention the easement or the covenants. In 2017 Ivy opened a drive-through car wash on Parcel B, outside the 30-foot strip. Her customers reached it over the driveway. Ivy paid half the driveway maintenance costs until 2022. In 2020 Gloria sold Parcel A to Javier, who still runs the gas station.

In 2019 Pine County rezoned the Route 9 corridor "Neighborhood Commercial." Car washes are not allowed in the new zone. The ordinance provides that a use lawfully existing when it took effect may continue but "shall not be enlarged or expanded," and that any nonconforming car wash "shall be discontinued by December 31, 2029."

In 2023 the state highway department allowed Ivy to build a second driveway connecting Parcel B directly to Route 9. Customers now use both entrances. On weekends, lines of 15 or more cars waiting for the car wash back up onto the shared driveway, sometimes blocking tanker trucks delivering fuel to Javier's station.

From March to May 2024 Ivy built a second wash bay 12 feet from the boundary with Parcel A, at a cost of $180,000. Javier watched the construction from his station and said nothing until it was finished in June, when he demanded that Ivy remove the bay. Ivy has refused to pay any driveway costs since 2022. In 2025 Javier spent $16,000 repaving the driveway.

The county has issued Ivy a notice of violation for the second bay. Ivy has applied for a variance, stating that the bay is needed to shorten the lines and that she cannot recover her investment in the car wash by 2029.

## Call of the Question

1. Can Javier recover from Ivy for the driveway costs, and can he compel her to remove the second wash bay? Discuss.
2. What are Ivy's rights to use the driveway, and what relief, if any, may Javier obtain concerning it? Discuss.
3. Can the county stop Ivy from using the second wash bay, and must Ivy close the car wash by the end of 2029? Discuss.

## Model Answer

### 1. Javier's claims under the deed covenants

**A. The driveway costs: does the burden of the maintenance covenant run to Ivy as a real covenant?** A real covenant is a promise about land enforceable at law, by damages, between successors. The burden runs to a successor of the promisor if (1) the promise is in a writing satisfying the statute of frauds, (2) the parties intended it to bind successors, (3) it touches and concerns the land, (4) there was horizontal privity between the original parties, (5) there is vertical privity between the promisor and the successor, and (6) the successor had notice.

1. *Writing.* The covenant appears in the deed, which Hank signed and which was recorded.
2. *Intent.* The deed states that the covenants "shall bind Hank and every future owner of Parcel B."
3. *Touch and concern.* Ivy will argue that a promise to pay money is personal and does not touch the land. But an affirmative covenant to pay for the upkeep of a facility that serves the land touches and concerns it (*Neponsit*). The payments maintain the driveway that is Parcel B's access and part of Parcel A's land.
4. *Horizontal privity.* Gloria and Hank made the promise in the deed conveying Parcel B, a grantor–grantee relationship.
5. *Vertical privity.* Ivy took Hank's entire fee in Parcel B.
6. *Notice.* The deed was recorded in Parcel B's chain of title, giving Ivy record notice. Her payments until 2022 show actual knowledge as well. Hank's silent deed to Ivy does not matter.

California's statute reaches the same result: the instrument describes both parcels, states that successors are bound, concerns the repair and maintenance of the land, and was recorded (Civ. Code § 1468). **The burden runs to Ivy.**

**B. Does the benefit run to Javier?** The benefit runs to a successor of the promisee if the promise is in writing, the parties intended the benefit to run, the promise touches and concerns the benefited land, and there is (relaxed) vertical privity. The deed says the covenants are "for the benefit of Parcel A," maintenance of the driveway on Parcel A enhances that land, and Javier took Gloria's estate in Parcel A. **Javier may enforce the covenant.**

Ivy's new Route 9 entrance does not end her obligation. The covenant is not conditioned on Parcel B lacking other access, and Ivy's customers still use the driveway. **Javier recovers $8,000 (half of the $16,000 repaving), plus Ivy's share of any other maintenance he paid since 2022.**

**C. The second wash bay: is the setback restriction enforceable as an equitable servitude?** An equitable servitude is a promise about land use that equity enforces by injunction against a successor who takes with notice. The burden runs if the promise is in writing, the parties intended it to bind successors, it touches and concerns the land, and the successor had notice; no privity is required.

- *Writing and intent.* The recorded deed contains the restriction and binds "every future owner of Parcel B."
- *Touch and concern.* A negative restriction on building near the boundary limits the use of Parcel B and benefits Parcel A by keeping space for tanker trucks to turn and keeping the station's sign visible.
- *Notice.* The recorded deed gave Ivy record notice.
- *Violation.* The bay is a "building or other structure" 12 feet from the boundary, inside the 30-foot strip.
- *Standing.* Javier, as owner of the benefited parcel, may enforce.

**The restriction binds Ivy, and the bay violates it.**

**D. Ivy's defenses and the remedy.**
- *Acquiescence and abandonment.* Ivy will point to Hank's shed, 10 feet from the boundary since 2014, which Gloria never challenged. A servitude is abandoned only when violations are so widespread that its purpose is defeated, and a plaintiff who tolerated a minor violation is not barred from challenging a major one. A small storage shed does not block tanker turning or the sign the way a full wash bay does. **This defense likely fails.**
- *Changed conditions.* The rezoning and Ivy's new highway access do not affect the restriction's purpose; tankers still need room and the sign still needs visibility. **No changed conditions.**
- *Laches and estoppel.* Javier watched three months of construction without objecting while Ivy spent $180,000. Laches requires unreasonable delay that prejudices the defendant, and estoppel requires reasonable reliance on the plaintiff's conduct. Javier will respond that Ivy had record notice of the restriction and built at her own risk, that his silence was brief, and that he objected as soon as the bay was finished. A builder who knowingly violates a recorded restriction has a weak claim to equitable sympathy.

**Javier likely can enforce the restriction.** The usual remedy for an equitable servitude is an injunction, here an order to remove the bay. Because Javier stood by during construction, a court weighing the equities could instead award damages, but Ivy's knowing violation makes removal the more likely result.

### 2. The driveway easement

**A. Nature and transfer of the easement.** An express easement is created by a writing that satisfies the statute of frauds. It is appurtenant when it benefits a dominant parcel and passes with it. The benefit of an easement appurtenant passes automatically with the dominant estate, even if the deed is silent, and the burden binds a later owner of the servient estate who takes with notice.

The deed expressly grants an easement "for ingress and egress to Parcel B," so it is appurtenant to Parcel B (dominant) and burdens Parcel A (servient). It therefore passed to Ivy with Parcel B, although Hank's deed did not mention it. It binds Javier, who took Parcel A with record notice and could see the driveway in use. **Ivy holds an express easement appurtenant over the driveway.**

**B. Scope: traffic and queuing.** The scope of an express easement is set by its terms, read in light of the parties' intent and the reasonable, foreseeable development of the dominant estate. A change in degree, such as more traffic from normal development, is usually allowed; a change in kind, or a use that unreasonably increases the burden on the servient estate, is not.

- *Increased traffic.* A vacant commercial lot on a highway corridor was foreseeably going to be developed for business, so more cars passing over the driveway to reach the car wash is a change in degree within "ingress and egress."
- *Queuing.* Ivy will argue that waiting cars are still "entering" Parcel B. Javier will respond that an ingress-and-egress easement is a right of passage, not a waiting area. Lines of 15 or more stationary cars that block fuel tankers turn the driveway into a holding lane and unreasonably interfere with the servient owner's own use of his land.

**The queuing exceeds the scope of the easement.** The remedy for misuse is an injunction against the excess use, for example an order that Ivy keep waiting cars off the shared driveway by managing the line on her own lot or routing waiting cars through her Route 9 entrance. The remedy is not termination of the easement.

**C. Did the easement end?** An easement ends by its terms, merger, release, abandonment (nonuse plus intent to abandon), estoppel, prescription, the end of the necessity (for an easement by necessity), condemnation, or sale of the servient estate to a bona fide purchaser.

- *New access.* Javier will argue that Ivy's 2023 Route 9 entrance ended any need for the driveway. But this is an express easement, not an easement by necessity, so it does not end when another route becomes available.
- *Abandonment.* Ivy's customers still use the driveway, so there is no nonuse, let alone intent to abandon.
- *Misuse and unpaid maintenance.* Overuse is remedied by injunction, and Ivy's refusal to pay is remedied by damages on the covenant; neither terminates the easement.

**The easement continues**, but Javier may enjoin the blocking lines and collect the unpaid costs.

### 3. The zoning questions

**A. Nonconforming use; the second bay.** Zoning is a valid exercise of the police power if rationally related to a legitimate public purpose, which a commercial-zone use list easily is. A use that was lawful when a zoning restriction took effect may continue as a nonconforming use, but it may not be substantially expanded or changed, and it ends if abandoned or substantially destroyed.

Ivy's car wash opened lawfully in 2017, before the 2019 rezoning, so it is a lawful nonconforming use. The ordinance also says expressly that such a use "shall not be enlarged or expanded." Ivy will argue that the second bay only serves her existing customers more efficiently, within her existing lot, and that more customers is mere intensification. But a new structure that doubles the facility's capacity is a physical expansion of the nonconforming use, not ordinary repair or a natural increase in business. **The county may stop Ivy from using the second bay.**

**B. Variance.** A variance relieves an owner from a zoning requirement only where strict application causes unnecessary hardship arising from conditions unique to the property (size, shape, topography), the hardship is not self-created, and the variance will not harm the neighborhood or conflict with the plan. A use variance, which permits a use the zone does not allow, is disfavored; an area variance relaxes dimensional limits.

- *Type.* Ivy seeks permission to enlarge a use the zone prohibits, which functions as a use variance. California forbids variances that authorize a use not otherwise allowed in the zone (Gov. Code § 65906).
- *Unique hardship.* Parcel B is an ordinary rectangular lot. Ivy's hardships, long lines and an unrecovered investment, are economic, not conditions peculiar to the land.
- *Self-created.* Ivy built the bay in 2024, five years after the rezoning, without approval.

California also requires the agency's findings to bridge the evidence and its decision (*Topanga*), and no finding of unique hardship could be supported here. **The variance should be denied.**

**C. Amortization.** Many states, including California, allow a government to require a nonconforming use to end after a reasonable amortization period, judged by the owner's investment, the use's remaining useful life and the length of the period (*City of Los Angeles v. Gage*). The ordinance gives Ivy until the end of 2029, ten years after the rezoning and twelve years after the car wash opened. Ivy will argue that she cannot recover her investment by then. But the investment that counts is what she lawfully made before the rezoning. The $180,000 second bay was built in violation of the ordinance and does not lengthen the period.

Ivy may argue that forced closure is a taking. Some states treat amortization as requiring compensation, but California upholds reasonable amortization periods, and Ivy keeps her land for permitted uses, so a regulatory takings claim is weak. She also has no vested right: she never had a permit for the second bay. **Ivy will likely have to close the car wash by the end of 2029.**

**Common mistakes**
1. Assuming a deed that is silent about covenants and easements cuts them off. Recording gives notice, and appurtenant benefits pass automatically.
2. Saying an affirmative money covenant can never touch and concern the land.
3. Treating one tolerated shed as abandonment of the whole restriction.
4. Ending the express easement because Ivy now has other access. That rule applies to easements by necessity.
5. Treating the second bay as permitted intensification, or counting the post-rezoning investment in the amortization analysis.

**Accuracy notes.** Rules follow R-PRP-050 to -056, -060 to -068 and -070 to -073. The restriction is a setback covenant, chosen so that no restraint-of-trade question arises. California's statutory covenant requirements are stated from Civ. Code § 1468 (rulebook source S-769). Whether a court orders removal or awards damages after a plaintiff's silence during construction is a matter of equitable discretion; the answer predicts, but does not guarantee, removal.
